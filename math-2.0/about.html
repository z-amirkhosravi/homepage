    Purpose, mathematical evidence, attribution, and how the collection grows.

    A growing mathematical reference

    This wiki is the readable output of a Math 2.0 research project: a place to collect a coherent body of useful mathematics, whether established or new, and make its reasoning available to readers.

    Its first subject is quaternionic analysis and its connections to number theory. A reader should be able to enter through a definition, follow a construction through its proof, and discover related questions without reconstructing a research conversation.

    What a page contains#

    The mathematical core consists of separately boxed definitions, constructions, and results. A result states its hypotheses and conclusion, names the definitions and earlier results it uses, and carries its own formulation version and review status. Its proof appears directly beneath it.

    Discussion provides motivation, interpretation, and context. Examples and open questions are labeled separately. The “Hide discussion” control lets readers inspect the statements and proofs without that commentary. A result must remain interpretable from its stated hypotheses and explicit dependencies; essential assumptions must not be buried in discussion.

    A box identifies a precise mathematical claim. It does not certify that claim. Constructions link to the results establishing their well-definedness; theorem, lemma, proposition, and corollary labels do not turn an unreviewed argument into a checked proof.

    Pages are organized by subject and mathematical dependence. A single research investigation may contribute to several articles; an article may draw on several investigations.

    Evidence belongs to a statement#

    A proof supplied is an explicit mathematical argument available to inspect. It may still contain a gap or error. Independently reviewed means a recorded review of the exact statement and argument; it does not mean merely that the page has been edited.

    Formally verified means an exact statement checked by a proof assistant, with its source, assumptions, toolchain, and checking evidence linked. None of the results in this published collection currently carries that status.

    Definitions, conjectures, open questions, and numerical observations should be recognizable as such. Computation can support or refute a claim within its tested scope; it does not silently become a general proof.

    Attribution and new contributions#

    Existing constructions and results are attributed to their sources. A proposed new contribution needs both mathematical justification and a separate assessment of its relationship to the literature. The initial construction pages make no claim of novelty.

    The articles are written with AI assistance and include explicit mathematical derivations. Review status is recorded for each result; a linked review record identifies whether the reviewer was an AI agent or a human mathematician and states the scope of the check. The literature guide records sources, their relevance, and how closely they have been inspected.

    How the collection grows#

    The working research notebook preserves exploratory arguments, corrections, failed approaches, and branches that have not yet been followed. The wiki turns developed material into exposition for readers. Open directions can appear here with their status made explicit.

    As results change, their statements, proofs, dependencies, and evidence are revised together. Earlier formulations remain recoverable in version history.
