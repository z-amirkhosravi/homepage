    Expository

    Four-dimensional spin representations, five-dimensional standard representations, and published transfer theorems for quaternionic cusp forms.

    Langlands parameters and Galois representations

    Discussion

    A Hecke eigenvalue system of regular automorphic spinors has a natural place in the Langlands programme. For the quaternionic similitude group, the spin representation of the dual group has dimension four. For the derived group, the standard representation of the dual group has dimension five. These are related representations of the same arithmetic data, rather than dimensions determined by the spinor fibre Vn.

    There is a published existence theorem for the general-type cuspidal representations in this family when n≥3: LG3 specifies its hypotheses. It combines transfer from the quaternionic inner form with the Galois representations attached to genus-two Siegel modular forms. The qualifications at n=2, for endoscopic forms, and at the Hardy endpoint are explained separately below.

    Groups and the adelic input#

    Let D/ℚ be a definite quaternion algebra, H=diag⁡(1,−1), and let 𝐆 be the algebraic group

    𝐆(A)={g∈GL2⁡(D⊗ℚA):g∗Hg=ν(g)H,ν(g)∈A×}

    for a commutative ℚ-algebra A. After extending scalars to a splitting field of D, this is the symplectic similitude group. Its split inner form is therefore 𝐇=GSp4, and the identity 𝐆der=ker⁡ν descends from the corresponding identity for GSp4. Write 𝔸=ℝ×𝔸f for the adele ring of ℚ.

    Write Va=Syma⁡ℂ2; the compact group is Sp(1)×Sp(1). The central character notation means F(zg)=ω(z)F(g) and square integrability is taken modulo the centre. For weight n, set m=n−2.

    For n≥2, let Π be an irreducible unitary constituent

    Π⊂Lcusp2(𝐆(ℚ)\𝐆(𝔸),ω)

    with finite-order central character ω, whose archimedean restriction to Sp(1,1) contains the discrete series with minimal compact type V0⊠Vn or Vn⊠V0. We use n as the weight of this constituent.

    Discussion

    The similitude group above includes negative real multipliers. Its identity component modulo positive central scalars acts on the ball in the positive-similitude model. The full real group interchanges the two chiralities; a global representation can therefore contain both on restriction. Rösner–Weissauer §8 describes this explicitly.

    A function on a single quotient Γ\B must first be placed in the adelic space, with a level and central character, before applying LG3. This is additional data beyond a list of eigenvalues for selected rational double cosets. The theorem is stated for that adelic constituent; it does not infer its existence from algebraicity of a few eigenvalues.

    Definition LG-D2. Weak Equivalence and General Type [4, §§1, 9]

    1. Weak equivalence. Two automorphic representations of inner forms are weakly equivalent if their local representations are isomorphic at all but finitely many places where the groups are identified with the split form.

    2. CAP. A cuspidal Π of 𝐆(𝔸) is CAP if its local representations at almost all places agree with constituents of normalized parabolic induction from an automorphic representation of a Levi subgroup of a proper rational parabolic of 𝐇.

    3. Weak endoscopy. A cuspidal Π is weakly endoscopic if its unramified parameters at almost all places are the images of automorphic parameters for the proper elliptic endoscopic group GSO(2,2).

    4. General type. A cuspidal Π is of general type if it is neither CAP nor weakly endoscopic.

    LG-D2 · version 1

    In the four-dimensional spin representation, the parameters in the weakly endoscopic case are sums of two degree-two parameters with a common determinant. [4, §§1, 9]

    Why four dimensions, and why five also occurs#

    Proposition LG1. Dual groups and the exterior-square relation

    The complex dual groups are

    𝐆ˆ=GSpin5(ℂ)≃GSp4(ℂ),𝐆derˆ=SO5(ℂ).

    Let V be a four-dimensional representation of a group 𝒢 over a characteristic-zero field, with a nondegenerate alternating form

    b:V×V⟶M

    valued in a one-dimensional representation M, such that b is 𝒢-equivariant. Let c:Λ2V→M be c(v∧w)=b(v,w). Then

    V5=ker⁡(c)⊗M−1

    is five-dimensional and carries a nondegenerate 𝒢-invariant symmetric form with determinant-one action. Moreover,

    (Λ2V)⊗M−1≃𝟏⊕︎V5.

    Replacing (V,M) by (V⊗η,M⊗η2) for a character η leaves V5 unchanged.

    LG1 · version 1

    Uses adelic hypotheses.

    Proof of Proposition LG1

    The root datum of the simply connected group of type C2 is dual to the adjoint root datum of type B2, giving SO5. Including the similitude centre gives GSpin5, whose spin representation identifies it with GSp4. Inner twisting leaves these complex dual groups unchanged; their root-data Galois actions are trivial here.

    The contraction c is onto, so its kernel has dimension 6−1=5. In a symplectic basis, the inverse alternating form supplies an equivariant inclusion M↪Λ2V whose composite with c is a nonzero scalar. This splits the sequence in characteristic zero. Wedge product gives a symmetric pairing on Λ2V with values in Λ4V≃M2. The invariant line is nonisotropic; its orthogonal complement is ker⁡(c). Twisting this complement by M−1 gives the asserted nondegenerate invariant scalar pairing. Finally det⁡(V)=M2, hence det⁡(Λ2V)=M6 and det⁡(ker⁡c)=M5; the twist makes the determinant trivial. The character factors cancel in the last assertion.

    Discussion

    Thus a four-dimensional symplectic Galois representation gives a five-dimensional orthogonal one. The five-dimensional construction forgets central twists. Hecke data for the derived group consequently do not select a unique four-dimensional lift without extra central data.

    The weight dictionary#

    Proposition LG2. From spinor weight to Siegel weight [6, §§2.1–2.2]; [4, Prop. 8.3]

    For n≥2, the infinitesimal character of the discrete series in the adelic hypotheses is the Weyl orbit of (n,1) in the standard C2 coordinates. Its archimedean Langlands packet on the split group has a holomorphic member of Siegel weight

    (k1,k2)=(n+1,3),Symn−2⁡⊗det3⁡

    as a representation of GL2⁡.

    LG2 · version 1

    Uses adelic hypotheses, CP4, [6, §§2.1–2.2].

    Proof of Proposition LG2[6, §§2.1–2.2]

    The flat coefficient representation is Em=Symm⁡ℂ4, of highest weight (m,0). The half-sum of positive roots of C2 is (2,1), so its infinitesimal character is (m+2,1)=(n,1). The relative-cohomology calculation CP4 gives this same infinitesimal character for both chiral discrete series; dualizing Em does not change its derived-group highest weight.

    In the conventions of Weiss §2.1, the discrete-series parameter with infinitesimal character (m1,m2) has spin restriction to Wℂ with angular exponents ±(m1+m2) and ±(m1−m2). Section 2.2 gives holomorphic weight (m1+1,m2+2). Substitution of (m1,m2)=(n,1) gives (n+1,3). Since n≥2, these satisfy k1≥k2≥3, the cohomological Siegel-weight range.

    A published existence theorem in this family#

    Theorem LG3. Spin Galois representations for general-type constituents [4, Props. 8.3, 10.1, Thm. 11.5]; [6, Thm. 3.1, Remark 3.2]; [5]; [3]; [7]

    Let n≥3 and let Π be a general-type cuspidal automorphic constituent satisfying the adelic hypotheses. There are a number field E⊂ℂ, a finite set S of rational primes, and, for every finite place λ of E of residue characteristic ℓ, a continuous semisimple representation

    rΠ,λ:Gal⁡(ℚ/ℚ)⟶GSp4(Eλ)

    with the following properties. Here Eλ is the completion of E at λ, Eλ an algebraic closure, and comparisons with complex parameters use compatible embeddings of the algebraic coefficient fields.

    1. The representation is unramified at every p∉S∪{ℓ}.
    2. Let sp be the unitary-normalized unramified Satake conjugacy class of Πp at p∉S, with convention Lp(s,Π,spin)=det⁡(1−spin(sp)p−s)−1. Define
      Pp(T)=det⁡(1−p(n+1)/2spin(sp)T).
      Then Pp(T)∈E[T] is independent of λ, and for p∉S∪{ℓ},
      det⁡(1−rΠ,λ(Frobp)T)=Pp(T),
      where Frobp denotes arithmetic Frobenius.
    3. The representation at ℓ is de Rham, and is crystalline if ℓ∉S. With the convention that the cyclotomic character εℓ has Hodge–Tate weight 1, its Hodge–Tate weights are
      {0,1,n,n+1}.
    4. Its similitude character is χλεℓn+1, where χλ is the finite-order character attached to the central character of the split transfer, with the reciprocity convention in Weiss Remark 3.2. Each complex conjugate of a Frobenius eigenvalue at p∉S∪{ℓ} has absolute value p(n+1)/2.
    5. For ℓ≥5, rΠ,λ is irreducible.

    LG3 · version 1

    Uses adelic hypotheses, LG-D2, LG2, [4, Prop. 8.3, Prop. 10.1, Thm. 11.5], [6, Thm. 3.1, Remark 3.2].

    Proof of Theorem LG3[4, Props. 8.3, 10.1, Thm. 11.5]; [6, Thm. 3.1, Remark 3.2]; [5]; [3]; [7]

    Put m=n−2>0. The derived coefficient highest weight is (m,0), whose two coordinates are unequal. Rösner–Weissauer §8 and Proposition 8.3 show that its unitary cohomological archimedean representations are discrete series and contribute only in degree two. Thus it satisfies the inner form's regularity condition used in Theorem 11.5. This condition is specific to the inner form; it is not a claim that (m,0) is strictly dominant for every root of C2.

    To match the algebraic coefficient's central action in that theorem, use the cohomological normalization Π⊗|ν|𝔸−m/2, where |ν|𝔸 is the product of the local normalized absolute values. At a positive real scalar tI, this twist acts by t−m and cancels the action tm on Em. Apply Theorem 11.5 in that normalization and undo the same twist on the split side. Twisting preserves cuspidality and the general-type condition. This gives a cuspidal generic cohomological representation Π′ of GSp4(𝔸), with the same unramified factors outside a finite set and the corresponding archimedean packet. By Proposition 10.1 that global packet is a product of local packets. We can therefore replace its archimedean generic member by the holomorphic member without changing the finite factors. Call the resulting cuspidal representation Πhol. Its weight is (n+1,3) by LG2.

    Weiss Theorem 3.1 applies because n+1≥3 and k2=3. It supplies the symplectic compatible system, de Rham and crystalline assertions, purity, and irreducibility for ℓ≥5. It summarizes the constructions of Taylor, Laumon, and Weissauer and subsequent refinements; these are external theorem inputs, not consequences of the ball's de Rham complex.

    The Hodge–Tate formula in that theorem is {0,k2−2,k1−1,k1+k2−3}, which becomes {0,1,n,n+1}. Its local normalization is Πphol⊗|ν|p−(n+1)/2. Since |p|p=p−1, this multiplies the spin Satake eigenvalues by p(n+1)/2 and gives the displayed polynomial. Remark 3.2 gives the similitude character. Enlarge S to contain the quaternion discriminant, ramified places, and any exceptions in the weak transfer. The shared unramified parameters give all the stated assertions for Π.

    Discussion

    The named sources establish this existence direction without appealing to an unspecified complete global Langlands correspondence. The packet and transfer theorems, as well as the Galois construction, are substantial published inputs. Their application here has a supplied argument and awaits independent review; it is not Lean-verified.

    The normalization in LG3 is intrinsic to the unitary Satake parameter. It must not be identified silently with a chosen double-coset eigenvalue. SH1–SH4 establish integral operators on the ball model; comparison with an adelic Hecke polynomial additionally requires the local generators, their measures, the adelic realization, and its central character.

    Rational group cohomology gives a field of eigenvalues, as in CP9. A Galois representation is stronger data. In the route above it is obtained from the arithmetic geometry used for Siegel modular forms, after transfer, rather than by giving an unexplained Galois action to H2(Γ,Em).

    Corollary LG4. The five-dimensional standard Galois representation

    For r=rΠ,λ in LG3 and μ=sim⁡(r), the representation

    r5,λ=ker⁡(Λ2r⟶μ)⊗μ−1

    has image in SO5(Eλ). It is de Rham with Hodge–Tate weights

    {−n,−1,0,1,n},

    and is unchanged by a character twist of r.

    LG4 · version 1

    Uses LG1, LG3.

    Proof of Corollary LG4

    LG1 proves the dimension, form, determinant, and twist invariance. Taking exterior powers, kernels of the displayed direct-sum splitting, and tensor products preserves the de Rham property. The pairwise sums of 0,1,n,n+1 are 1,n,n+1,n+1,n+2,2n+1. Subtract the weight n+1 of μ, then remove the invariant zero-weight line. This gives the five displayed weights.

    Endoscopy and low weights#

    Proposition LG5. The Galois representation of a specified endoscopic system [2, exposé 355]; [1, Thm. 2.2.3, §2.4]

    Let n≥2. Let f and g be normalized holomorphic cuspidal eigenforms of weights n+2 and n, with the same finite-order nebentypus χ. At primes outside their levels write

    Pf,p(T)=1−ap(f)T+χ(p)pn+1T2,Pg,p(T)=1−ap(g)T+χ(p)pn−1T2.

    For a compatible coefficient embedding, let rf,λ and rg,λ be their two-dimensional Galois representations, normalized to have these arithmetic Frobenius polynomials. Then

    rλ=rf,λ⊕︎(rg,λ⊗εℓ)

    is symplectic with similitude character χλεℓn+1, Hodge–Tate weights {0,1,n,n+1}, and good-prime polynomial

    det⁡(1−rλ(Frobp)T)=Pf,p(T)Pg,p(pT).

    Every continuous semisimple four-dimensional Galois representation with the same polynomials at all but finitely many primes is isomorphic to rλ.

    LG5 · version 1

    Uses LG1, Deligne's Galois representations of holomorphic elliptic cuspidal eigenforms.

    Proof of Proposition LG5[2]; [1, Thm. 2.2.3, §2.4]

    For a two-dimensional representation the exterior-product alternating form has multiplier its determinant. Here the two determinants are both χλεℓn+1, so the orthogonal direct sum of the alternating forms is a nondegenerate alternating form with this multiplier. The Hodge–Tate weights of the classical representation are {0,n+1} and {0,n−1}; tensoring the second by εℓ shifts its weights to {1,n}. Arithmetic Frobenius acts on that twist by multiplication by p, proving the polynomial identity. Equality of polynomials gives equality of traces on a dense set by Chebotarev; continuity and the characteristic-zero Brauer–Nesbitt theorem give the final semisimple isomorphism.

    Discussion

    LG5 describes the rank-four representation when the motivic spin parameter is the indicated endoscopic sum. It is a conditional identification of that eigenvalue system, not a nonvanishing theorem for every proposed pair of elliptic forms or every quaternionic level. The possible elliptic weights n+2 and n also follow from the two real Weil-parameter exponents n+1 and n−1 in the proof of LG2. Cuspidal representations of a classical group can therefore have reducible spin Galois representations.

    At n=2, LG2 still gives Siegel weight (3,3) and hence Galois weights {0,1,2,3} whenever a cohomological cuspidal split transfer is available. The split-group Galois theorem includes this weight. What fails in the proof of LG3 is the automatic inner-form transfer criterion: the coefficient is trivial, and additional cohomological representations at infinity make the regularity hypothesis of Rösner–Weissauer Theorem 11.5 unavailable. Theorem 11.4 instead concerns an inner form compact modulo centre at infinity, so it cannot be substituted for our noncompact Sp(1,1) group. This leaves an additional transfer question for arbitrary general-type weight-two systems in this account; it is not a claim that Galois representations for them are impossible or unknown by every other method.

    The n=1 Hardy limit has no coefficient En−2 and is outside LG3. Results for holomorphic limits of discrete series on the split group do not by themselves identify or transfer that quaternionic endpoint. It requires a separate global analysis.

    Finally, an eigenvalue system determines the semisimple Galois representation through its good-prime characteristic polynomials, but need not distinguish individual automorphic forms within a packet. These results do not give a bijection between all quaternionic eigenfunctions and all four-dimensional Galois representations.

    Geometric realization#

    Automorphic cohomology and geometric realization compares the quaternionic period construction with modular curves and universal abelian varieties. It gives the obstruction to a first-cohomology realization and distinguishes a real family of polarized fibres from the algebraic geometry used after transfer.
