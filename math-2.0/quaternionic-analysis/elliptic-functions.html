    Quaternionic elliptic functions

    Discussion

    Summing translates of derivatives of the Cauchy–Fueter kernel produces quaternionic analogues of Weierstrass functions. This article develops three periodic functions and a zeta function with additive quasi-periods. Prerequisites are the kernel and Fueter regularity, Taylor's theorem, and uniform convergence of differentiated series.

    1. The lattice#

    Fix a full-rank lattice L⊂ℍ, written as

    L=ℤω0+ℤω1+ℤω2+ℤω3,

    where the four generators are linearly independent over ℝ.

    Put L∗=L∖{0}.

    Use the fixed coordinates q=t+∑j=13ejxj and the functions

    G(q)=q‾|q|4,Gj=∂xjG,j=1,2,3.

    Each bracket in the following lattice sums is a single summand; convergence is asserted for the bracketed sums.

    Discussion

    The naive sum of translates of Gj has borderline decay |λ|−4 in four dimensions. Subtracting the constant Gj(λ) gains an extra power of decay. The individual terms inside a bracket must not be split into divergent lattice sums.

    2. The elliptic functions#

    Construction E-C1. Regularized first-derivative lattice sums [1]; [2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019]

    For a full-rank lattice L⊂ℍ, j∈{1,2,3}, and q∈ℍ∖L, form the series

    ℘L,j(q)=Gj(q)+∑λ∈L∗[Gj(q+λ)−Gj(λ)].

    E-C1 · version 1

    Well-definedness: Convergence and regularity in E1; periodicity in E2.

    Discussion

    Here “elliptic” means lattice-periodic and Fueter-regular away from isolated singularities. E1 and E2 supply these properties for E-C1.

    Proposition E1. Convergence and regularity [1]; [2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019]

    Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice and j∈{1,2,3}. The series in E-C1 converges absolutely and uniformly on every compact subset of ℍ∖L. It may be differentiated term by term to every real order, with locally uniform convergence of the differentiated series. Its sum is both left and right Fueter-regular.

    For every ω∈L, the function

    q⟼℘L,j(q)−Gj(q−ω)

    extends to a smooth, left and right regular function on a neighborhood of ω.

    E1 · version 2

    Uses E-C1, F1, F2.

    Proof of Proposition E1[2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019]

    For a compact set K⊂ℍ∖L, choose R with |q|≤R on K. If |λ|>2R, the segment from λ to λ+q stays at distance at least |λ|/2 from 0. The derivative bound from the foundations page and the mean-value estimate give

    |Gj(λ+q)−Gj(λ)|≤CR|λ|−5.

    A full-rank lattice has O(r4) points in a ball of radius r: write L=Aℤ4 for an invertible real matrix A and use equivalence of norms. Summing over dyadic shells now shows ∑λ∈L∗|λ|−p<∞ for p>4. This proves absolute, locally uniform convergence.

    After m≥1 real derivatives, the constant subtraction vanishes and each tail term is bounded by CK,m|λ|−4−m. All differentiated series therefore converge locally uniformly. The finitely many terms omitted from these tail estimates are smooth away from their singularities. The standard theorem on locally uniform convergence of derivatives permits differentiation of the sum.

    Every summand is left and right regular by F1, including the subtracted constant. Termwise differentiation proves regularity of the sum. In a small ball around any lattice point, remove its one singular summand; the remaining series and its derivatives converge uniformly and are regular there.

    Proposition E2. Parity and periodicity [1]; [2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019]

    Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice and j∈{1,2,3}. For all q∈ℍ∖L and ω∈L,

    ℘L,j(−q)=℘L,j(q),℘L,j(q+ω)=℘L,j(q).

    E2 · version 2

    Uses E-C1, E1, F2.

    Proof of Proposition E2[2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019]

    Every first real partial derivative of ℘L,j is the absolutely convergent sum

    ∂xμ℘L,j(q)=∑λ∈L∂xμGj(q+λ),μ=0,1,2,3,x0=t.

    Reindexing this convergent sum shows that the derivatives are L-periodic. Hence

    Hω(q)=℘L,j(q+ω)−℘L,j(q)

    has zero real gradient. The complement of a discrete lattice in ℝ4 is path connected, so Hω is a constant cω.

    Since Gj is even, reindexing the absolutely convergent defining series by λ↦−λ shows that ℘L,j is even. Consequently

    Hω(−q−ω)=℘L,j(−q)−℘L,j(−q−ω)=−Hω(q).

    Both sides are constant in q, so cω=−cω, and therefore cω=0.

    Discussion

    The proof reindexes the absolutely convergent derivative series, then uses evenness to remove the possible constant increment. It does not reindex an unregularized borderline sum.

    3. The zeta function#

    Construction E-C2. Subtraction of the first Taylor polynomial [1]; [2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019]

    Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice. For λ∈L∗ and q=t+∑j=13ejxj, let the real differential at λ act by

    dGλ[q]=t∂tG(λ)+∑j=13xjGj(λ).

    For q∈ℍ∖L, form

    ζL(q)=G(q)+∑λ∈L∗[G(λ+q)−G(λ)−dGλ[q]].

    E-C2 · version 1

    Well-definedness: Convergence, regularity, and quasi-periodicity in E3.

    Lemma E-L1. Comparison with the regular linear coordinates

    For λ∈ℍ∖{0} and q=t+∑j=13ejxj, put zj(q)=tej−xj. Then

    dGλ[q]=−∑j=13Gj(λ)zj(q).

    E-L1 · version 1

    Uses E-C2, F1.

    Proof of Lemma E-L1

    Right regularity gives ∂tG(λ)=−∑jGj(λ)ej. Substitute this into the definition of dGλ[q] in E-C2 and use that t,xj are real.

    Discussion

    E-L1 expresses the real Taylor subtraction using regular linear coordinates. The order of quaternionic multiplication is explicit in the formula.

    Proposition E3. Convergence, derivatives, and quasi-periods [1]; [2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019]

    Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice. The series in E-C2 converges absolutely and locally uniformly on ℍ∖L, as do all its termwise real derivatives. Its sum is odd, is both left and right Fueter-regular, and for j∈{1,2,3} satisfies

    ∂xjζL(q)=℘L,j(q)(q∉L).

    There is a unique additive map ηL:L→ℍ such that, for every ω∈L and every q∉L,

    ζL(q+ω)−ζL(q)=ηL(ω).

    E3 · version 2

    Uses E-C2, E-C1, E1, E2, F1, F2.

    Proof of Proposition E3[2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019]

    Taylor's theorem bounds each bracket, uniformly on a compact set, by CK|λ|−5, since the second derivatives of G have degree −5. For first derivatives the summands are differences of first derivatives of G, again with decay |λ|−5. Derivatives of order m≥2 have decay |λ|−3−m. The argument in E1 proves convergence and permits all termwise differentiation.

    The first Taylor polynomial of a regular function is regular: applying either first-order constant-coefficient operator to it gives that operator applied to G, evaluated at λ, which is zero. The series is therefore left and right regular.

    Because G is odd and dG−λ=dGλ, replacing λ by −λ proves oddness of ζL. Differentiating with respect to xj gives exactly the defining series for ℘L,j.

    Now put Fω(q)=ζL(q+ω)−ζL(q). By E2, all three spatial derivatives of Fω vanish. Left regularity also gives

    ∂tFω=−∑j=13ej∂xjFω=0.

    Connectedness of ℍ∖L makes Fω constant. Finally, splitting the increment along ω+ν into two increments proves

    ηL(ω+ν)=ηL(ω)+ηL(ν).

    The constant increment determines ηL(ω) for each ω, so the resulting map is unique.

    Discussion

    The map ηL is the first period datum supplied by the construction. Further relations among its values require additional statements and proofs.

    4. Consequences and examples#

    Corollary E4. Half-period values of zeta

    For a full-rank lattice L⊂ℍ and ω∈L with ω/2∉L,

    ηL(ω)=2ζL(ω/2).

    E4 · version 1

    Uses E3.

    Proof of Corollary E4

    Set q=−ω/2 in the quasi-period identity of E3 and use the oddness of ζL.

    Corollary E5. Stationary points at half-periods

    Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice. For j∈{1,2,3} and ω∈L with ω/2∉L, the real differential of ℘L,j vanishes at ω/2:

    ∂xμ℘L,j(ω/2)=0(μ=0,1,2,3; x0=t).

    E5 · version 1

    Uses E1, E2.

    Proof of Corollary E5

    By E1 the derivatives exist. Differentiating the two identities in E2 shows that each first derivative is odd and L-periodic. Its values at ω/2 and −ω/2 are therefore both equal and negatives of one another, so they vanish.

    Corollary E6. Real dilation

    Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice, a>0 a real number, and j∈{1,2,3}. For all q∉L and ω∈L,

    ℘aL,j(aq)=a−4℘L,j(q),ζaL(aq)=a−3ζL(q),ηaL(aω)=a−3ηL(ω).

    E6 · version 1

    Uses E-C1, E-C2, E1, E3, F2.

    Proof of Corollary E6

    Reindex each convergent series using aλ∈aL and apply F2. In the zeta subtraction, dGaλ[aq]=a−3dGλ[q], since each first derivative of G has degree −4. Thus every bracket has the stated weight. Apply the resulting zeta identity to the difference at q+ω and q and use E3 to obtain the identity for η.

    Example. The standard lattice

    For L0=ℤ+ℤi+ℤj+ℤk, E2 gives periods 1,i,j,k. Additivity in E3 determines ηL0 from its four values on these generators.

    Since 1/2∉L0, E4 gives ηL0(1)=2ζL0(1/2) and E5 gives d℘L0,j(1/2)=0 for j=1,2,3. The zeta value is expressed by a convergent series here; no numerical evaluation is asserted.

    Discussion

    E6 describes real dilation. An action by general quaternions requires a separate transformation statement.

    Discussion. Sources and scope

    Sources and scope#

    The quaternionic Weierstrass construction is classical work of Fueter. Kraußhar's [2, §4, p. 1019, eq. (12)] displays the regularized first-derivative sum and attributes its quaternionic case to Fueter's 1939 paper. See the literature guide for the references and the remaining comparison with Fueter's original notation.

    This article supplies explicit arguments for its boxed formulations and makes no novelty claim. Each result carries its own review and formal-verification status.

    Representations and special values of the coefficients#

    Discussion

    The higher derivative identities express the regular part of these elliptic functions through convergent lattice sums. Varying the lattice gives Eisenstein functions on SL₄, while harmonic theta series express their scalar components through critical L-values.

    For a concrete first example, TH5 computes the coefficient of x12 in the regular part of ℘L0,1 for L0=ℤ+ℤi+ℤj+ℤk as 96iL(η(2τ)12,5), with a proof of the exact modular identity and its normalization.

    A separate group action, by Möbius transformations of the ball, leads to discrete series and the ordinary Fueter Hardy limit. The connections among these actions are a source of further questions.
