    Quaternionic Weierstrass Functions, Coefficients, and Quasi-Periods

    For a full-rank lattice in ℍ, regularized sums of the Cauchy–Fueter kernel and its derivatives define quaternionic analogues of the Weierstrass functions. We construct three periodic functions ℘L,j and a zeta function ζL with additive quasi-periods. The higher derivatives of ℘L,j at their singularities, after removal of the singular part, are given by absolutely convergent lattice Eisenstein sums.

    The quaternionic Weierstrass construction goes back to Fueter [1], as recorded by Kraußhar [2, §4, p. 1019]. The formulas below use full-lattice summation and real Taylor derivatives; their relation to Kraußhar's conventions is stated in the coefficient section. We assume the Cauchy–Fueter kernel and its regularity properties, Taylor's theorem, and the usual differentiation theorem for locally uniformly convergent series.

    Lattice and Kernel Conventions#

    Fix a full-rank lattice

    L=ℤω0+ℤω1+ℤω2+ℤω3⊂ℍ,

    where ω0,ω1,ω2,ω3 are linearly independent over ℝ. Put e1=i, e2=j, e3=k, and write

    q=t+∑j=13ejxj,G(q)=q‾|q|4,Gj=∂xjG.

    Sums over λ≠0 mean λ∈L∖{0}. Each bracketed expression in a regularized series is summed as a single term. The constant and linear subtractions ensure convergence; their separate lattice sums need not converge.

    The Weierstrass Functions#

    The first derivatives Gj have degree −4, which is the borderline decay for summation over a four-dimensional lattice. Subtracting Gj(λ) from each translated term gives the extra decay needed for convergence.

    Construction QW.1. [2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019] For a full-rank lattice L⊂ℍ, j∈{1,2,3}, and q∈ℍ∖L, form the series

    ℘L,j(q)=Gj(q)+∑λ∈L∖{0}[Gj(q+λ)−Gj(λ)].

    Proof of Proposition QW.2

    For a compact set K⊂ℍ∖L, choose R with |q|≤R on K. If |λ|>2R, the segment from λ to λ+q stays at distance at least |λ|/2 from 0. The derivative bound from the foundations page and the mean-value estimate give

    |Gj(λ+q)−Gj(λ)|≤CR|λ|−5.

    A full-rank lattice has O(r4) points in a ball of radius r: write L=Aℤ4 for an invertible real matrix A and use equivalence of norms. Summing over dyadic shells now shows ∑λ∈L∖{0}|λ|−p<∞ for p>4. This proves absolute, locally uniform convergence.

    After m≥1 real derivatives, the constant subtraction vanishes and each tail term is bounded by CK,m|λ|−4−m. All differentiated series therefore converge locally uniformly. The finitely many terms omitted from these tail estimates are smooth away from their singularities. The standard theorem on locally uniform convergence of derivatives permits differentiation of the sum.

    Every summand is left and right regular by Proposition QF.3, including the subtracted constant. Termwise differentiation proves regularity of the sum. In a small ball around any lattice point, remove its one singular summand; the remaining series and its derivatives converge uniformly and are regular there.

    Proposition QW.3. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice and j∈{1,2,3}. For all q∈ℍ∖L and ω∈L,

    ℘L,j(−q)=℘L,j(q),℘L,j(q+ω)=℘L,j(q).

    Proof of Proposition QW.3

    Every first real partial derivative of ℘L,j is the absolutely convergent sum

    ∂xμ℘L,j(q)=∑λ∈L∂xμGj(q+λ),μ=0,1,2,3,x0=t.

    Reindexing this convergent sum shows that the derivatives are L-periodic. Hence

    Hω(q)=℘L,j(q+ω)−℘L,j(q)

    has zero real gradient. The complement of a discrete lattice in ℝ4 is path connected, so Hω is a constant cω.

    Since Gj is even, reindexing the absolutely convergent defining series by λ↦−λ shows that ℘L,j is even. Consequently

    Hω(−q−ω)=℘L,j(−q)−℘L,j(−q−ω)=−Hω(q).

    Both sides are constant in q, so cω=−cω, and therefore cω=0.

    Higher Derivatives and Elliptic Coefficients#

    For a spatial multi-index ν=(n1,n2,n3)∈ℕ03, write

    |ν|=n1+n2+n3,∂ν=∂x1n1∂x2n2∂x3n3,Gν=∂νG.

    Let ϵj have a single 1 in position j, so that Gϵj=Gj. For |ν|≥2, the decay of Gν gives absolute convergence without subtraction. These higher sums describe the local coefficients of the functions just constructed.

    Construction QW.4. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice and let ν∈ℕ03 satisfy |ν|≥2. Form

    Eν(L)=∑λ∈L∖{0}Gν(λ),℘L,ν(q)=∑λ∈LGν(q+λ)−Eν(L)(q∉L).

    Proof of Proposition QW.6

    Differentiate the convergent series for hj from Proposition QW.2 and evaluate at q=0. The subtracted constants disappear, leaving ∑λ∈L∖{0}Gμ+ϵj(λ). This converges absolutely since |μ+ϵj|≥2.

    In particular, for μ=(μ1,μ2,μ3) with |μ|≥1, the coefficient of the spatial monomial x1μ1x2μ2x3μ3 in the real Taylor expansion of hj is

    Eμ+ϵj(L)μ1!μ2!μ3!.

    This convention differs from the expansion in Fueter polynomials in [2, eq. (13), p. 1020].

    Proof of Corollary QW.7

    By Lemma QF.4, Gν is odd when |ν| is even. The terms at λ and −λ cancel, and absolute convergence from Proposition QW.5 justifies pairing them.

    Thus order 3 is the first absolutely convergent derivative order not eliminated by parity. At odd derivative order, our full-lattice sum Eν(L) is twice Kraußhar's positive-semilattice sum [2, §4, eq. (8), p. 1018]. The regularized first-derivative series is his equation (12) [2, p. 1019]. These are the normalizations used when the coefficient formulas are applied to theta series.

    The Zeta Function and Its Quasi-Periods#

    For the zeta function, the kernel has degree −3, so we subtract its full first Taylor polynomial at each nonzero lattice point. This produces a primitive of the three Weierstrass functions. The classical derivative and quasi-period identities are recorded in [3, §2.1, p. 57].

    Construction QW.8. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice. For λ∈L∖{0} and q=t+∑j=13ejxj, let the real differential at λ act by

    dGλ[q]=t∂tG(λ)+∑j=13xjGj(λ).

    For q∈ℍ∖L, form

    ζL(q)=G(q)+∑λ∈L∖{0}[G(λ+q)−G(λ)−dGλ[q]].

    Lemma QW.9. For λ∈ℍ∖{0} and q=t+∑j=13ejxj, put zj(q)=tej−xj. Then

    dGλ[q]=−∑j=13Gj(λ)zj(q).

    Uses Construction QW.8, Proposition QF.3.

    Proof of Lemma QW.9

    Right regularity gives ∂tG(λ)=−∑jGj(λ)ej. Substitute this into the definition of dGλ[q] in Construction QW.8 and use that t,xj are real.

    This identity fixes the multiplication order in the comparison with those coordinates. The analytic properties and quasi-period map follow directly from the regularized series.

    Proposition QW.10. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice. The series in Construction QW.8 converges absolutely and locally uniformly on ℍ∖L, as do all its termwise real derivatives. Its sum is odd, is both left and right Fueter-regular, and for j∈{1,2,3} satisfies

    ∂xjζL(q)=℘L,j(q)(q∉L).

    There is a unique additive map ηL:L→ℍ such that, for every ω∈L and every q∉L,

    ζL(q+ω)−ζL(q)=ηL(ω).

    Proof of Proposition QW.10

    Taylor's theorem bounds each bracket, uniformly on a compact set, by CK|λ|−5, since the second derivatives of G have degree −5. For first derivatives the summands are differences of first derivatives of G, again with decay |λ|−5. Derivatives of order m≥2 have decay |λ|−3−m. The argument in Proposition QW.2 proves convergence and permits all termwise differentiation.

    The first Taylor polynomial of a regular function is regular: applying either first-order constant-coefficient operator to it gives that operator applied to G, evaluated at λ, which is zero. The series is therefore left and right regular.

    Because G is odd and dG−λ=dGλ, replacing λ by −λ proves oddness of ζL. Differentiating with respect to xj gives exactly the defining series for ℘L,j, the classical derivative identity [3, p. 57, following (2.20)].

    Now put Fω(q)=ζL(q+ω)−ζL(q). By Proposition QW.3, all three spatial derivatives of Fω vanish. Left regularity also gives

    ∂tFω=−∑j=13ej∂xjFω=0.

    Connectedness of ℍ∖L makes Fω constant. Finally, splitting the increment along ω+ν into two increments proves

    ηL(ω+ν)=ηL(ω)+ηL(ν).

    The constant increment determines ηL(ω) for each ω, so the resulting map is unique.

    Proof of Corollary QW.12

    By Proposition QW.2 the derivatives exist. Differentiating the two identities in Proposition QW.3 shows that each first derivative is odd and L-periodic. Its values at ω/2 and −ω/2 are therefore both equal and negatives of one another, so they vanish.

    Proof of Corollary QW.13

    Reindex each convergent series using aλ∈aL and apply Lemma QF.4. In the zeta subtraction, dGaλ[aq]=a−3dGλ[q], since each first derivative of G has degree −4. Thus every bracket has the stated weight. Apply the resulting zeta identity to the difference at q+ω and q and use Proposition QW.10 to obtain the identity for η.

    Example. The Standard Lattice

    For L0=ℤ+ℤi+ℤj+ℤk, Proposition QW.3 gives periods 1,i,j,k. Additivity in Proposition QW.10 determines ηL0 from its values on these four generators.

    Since 1/2∉L0, Corollary QW.11 and Corollary QW.12 give

    ηL0(1)=2ζL0(1/2),d℘L0,j(1/2)=0(j=1,2,3).

    The zeta value here is specified by its convergent defining series.

    Relations to Eisenstein Series, Theta Series, and Periods#

    The coefficient formula provides the link to the Eisenstein and Mellin formulas. As the lattice varies, its coefficient functions generate an Eisenstein module on SL4(ℝ). For a fixed lattice, the harmonic numerators give a Mellin formula; on even integral lattices, theta modularity identifies these component sums with critical values. In particular, the cubic-lattice calculation gives the coefficient of x12 in the regular part of ℘L0,1 as 96iL(η(2τ)12,5).

    These constructions vary the lattice or the theta variable. The Möbius action on the quaternionic ball instead concerns regular functions and the spinor discrete series or Fueter Hardy limit.

    Several further questions require information beyond the identities proved here. At derivative order 1, the lattice sum is not absolutely convergent, and its comparison with a theta Mellin integral or with quasi-periods requires a specified regularization and a comparison theorem. For other arithmetic lattices, the Hecke constituents of the resulting theta forms and the algebraic periods normalizing their critical values remain questions for further investigation.

    An action on L by elements of a quaternion algebra likewise requires a transformation law beyond the real-dilation identity Corollary QW.13; a transformation formula alone does not supply an arithmetic constraint.

    Further relations among the values of ηL, or their interpretation through residues and integrals on ℍ/L, require choices of normalization, orientation, and left or right regularity. The closed pairings and pullback identities provide local inputs for such comparisons. The literature guide collects the relevant sources.
