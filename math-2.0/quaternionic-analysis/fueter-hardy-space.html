    Expository

    Ordinary quaternion-valued regular functions, boundary induction, and the two limits of discrete series.

    The Fueter Hardy space

    Discussion

    The single-quaternion case has a boundary Hilbert norm. It realizes a genuine limit of discrete series, with an explicit splitting of the spinor principal series and a precise explanation of the failure of the ball volume norm.

    Boundary values and the conformal action#

    Put B={q∈ℍ:|q|<1}. Use q=t+ix+jy+kz, Dl=∂t+i∂x+j∂y+k∂z, and right complex scalars in ℍ. Let dσ be probability area on S3.

    Definition H-D1. The Regular Hardy Space

    The Fueter Hardy space consists of the smooth functions in

    ℋ+={h:B→ℍ:Dlh=0,‖h‖2=sup0<r<1⁡∫S3|h(rω)|2dσ(ω)<∞}.

    H-D1 · version 2

    For γ−1=(abcd), set

    q′=(aq+b)(cq+d)−1,J=cq+d,(Π(γ)h)(q)=|J|−2J−1h(q′).(H1)

    The action and boundary trace are established in H1.

    Theorem H1. An irreducible unitary representation on ordinary regular functions [1, Thm. 3.2]; [2, after Thm. 3.11]

    The Hardy boundary trace identifies ℋ+ isometrically with a closed invariant subspace of L2(S3,ℍ). Its action is strongly continuous, unitary and irreducible, with

    ℋ+|K=⨁ˆm≥0Vm⊠Vm+1.

    The central element −I acts by −1, so the representation does not descend to SO0(4,1).

    H1 · version 1

    Uses H-D1, SC3, DS5.

    Proof of Theorem H1[1, Thm. 3.2]; [2, after Thm. 3.11]

    Factorization DlDl=Δ makes every component harmonic. Write the homogeneous expansion as h=∑m≥0hm. Orthogonality on spheres gives

    ∫S3|h(rω)|2dσ=∑m≥0r2m‖hm‖L2(S3)2.

    The finite supremum is exactly the sum of the squared boundary norms. Thus the radial limits exist in L2, and the image is the closure of the regular polynomials. Conversely an L2 sum of these homogeneous boundary terms has a harmonic Poisson extension on B; interior convergence of derivatives preserves regularity. This identifies the complete Hardy space and its compact types by DS5 at n=1.

    The Möbius derivative on the boundary has length factor |J|−2, hence area Jacobian |J|−6. The squared norm of the fibre multiplier |J|−2J−1 is also |J|−6. Change of variables makes the action unitary on all boundary L2. The cocycle proves its group law; continuity on smooth boundary functions and density prove strong continuity. SC3 preserves the regular equation. Transforms of regular polynomials are regular and smooth up to the closed ball, since cq+d never vanishes there for a fixed ball automorphism. Their boundary values therefore stay in the Hardy subspace, which is consequently invariant by closure.

    For irreducibility, average over the first compact factor:

    ∫Sp(1)h(a−1q)da=h(0).

    This is the harmonic mean-value formula. Any nonzero invariant closed subspace has a vector nonzero somewhere. Translate that point to zero, average, and obtain a nonzero constant. The second compact factor acts irreducibly on the constants, so the subspace contains all constants. Its orthogonal complement, if nonzero, would contain them too, which is impossible. Finally insert J=−1 and q′=q to compute the central action.

    The principal series at parameter zero#

    Let P=MAN be the stabilizer of 1∈S3, with M≃Sp⁡(1) embedded diagonally in K, and write σ=V1. Normalize A by

    at=(cosh⁡(t/2)sinh⁡(t/2)sinh⁡(t/2)cosh⁡(t/2)).

    We use normalized induction: its sections satisfy

    F(gmatn)=e−3t/2σ(m)−1F(g).

    Proposition H-D2. The Boundary Model of Normalized Induction [1, §3, pp. 249–250]

    The formula (H1) extends to a unitary representation on ℬ=L2(S3,ℍ) and realizes

    I(σ,0)=IndMANG⁡(σ⊗1⊗1)(H3)

    with the normalized induction convention above.

    H-D2 · version 1

    Uses H1.

    Proof of Proposition H-D2cf. [1, §3, pp. 249–250]

    The boundary Jacobian of q↦γ−1q is |J|−6. The multiplier |J|−2J−1 has squared norm |J|−6, so change of variables preserves the boundary L2 norm. The group law follows from the automorphy-factor identity used in H1.

    At 1, the diagonal subgroup M acts on the fibre by its standard complex two-dimensional representation. With the parameter at, the positive restricted root has value t and multiplicity three, so ρ=3/2. For Π(at), formula (H1) uses at−1 and has J=e−t/2 at the fixed point 1. Its multiplier is therefore e3t/2, the inverse of the section character e−3t/2. These fibre actions identify the boundary model with the displayed induced representation.

    Theorem H2. The two Hardy summands of the spinor principal series [1, Thm. 3.2]; [2, after Thm. 3.11]

    For σ=V1 of M≃Sp(1), normalized induction at parameter zero splits as

    I(σ,0)=ℋ+⊕︎ˆℋ−,ℋ−=Cℋ+,(Cf)(ω)=ω‾f(ω‾).

    Both summands are irreducible and inequivalent. The second has compact types ⨁m≥0Vm+1⊠Vm.

    H2 · version 1

    Uses H1, H-D2.

    Proof of Theorem H2[1, Thm. 3.2]; [2, after Thm. 3.11]

    Peter–Weyl on S3≃Sp⁡(1) and Clebsch–Gordan give

    ℬ|K=⨁ˆm≥0[(Vm⊠Vm+1)⊕︎(Vm+1⊠Vm)].(H4)

    The two summands can be separated explicitly by the unitary Kelvin involution

    (Cf)(ω)=ω‾f(ω‾),C2=1.

    For k=(a,b), CΠ(a,b)C=Π(b,a). More generally

    CΠ(g)C=Π(WgW),W=(0110).(H5)

    Although W reverses the defining Hermitian form, conjugation by it preserves G. For a direct check write g−1=(abcd) and |q|=1. Applying CΠ(g)C gives multiplier

    |cq‾+d|−2q‾(aq‾+b)−1=|bq+a|−2(bq+a)−1

    and argument (cq‾+d)(aq‾+b)−1=(dq+c)(bq+a)−1. Here |cq‾+d|=|aq‾+b|=|bq+a| on the sphere. These are precisely the multiplier and argument for Π(WgW). In particular Cℋ+ is invariant and irreducible, with the compact factors exchanged. H1 and the preceding compact decomposition imply the orthogonal decomposition

    I(σ,0)=ℋ+⊕︎ˆℋ−,ℋ−=Cℋ+,ℋ−|K=⨁ˆm≥0Vm+1⊠Vm.(H6)

    The map C is complex-linear, preserves the boundary norm, and satisfies C2=1. The conjugation identity proves invariance and irreducibility of the second space. Their disjoint compact types imply orthogonality, and the full compact decomposition exhausts boundary L2, proving the displayed direct sum.

    Theorem H3. The limit-of-discrete-series identification [1, Thm. 3.2]; [2, after Thm. 3.11]

    The two summands in H2 are the two spinor limits of discrete series of Spin0⁡(4,1)≃Sp⁡(1,1). Their minimal compact types are V0⊠V1 and V1⊠V0.

    H3 · version 1

    Uses H2, [2], [1].

    Proof of Theorem H3[1, Thm. 3.2]; [2, after Thm. 3.11]

    H1–H2 identify the normalized spinor boundary representation and its two irreducible summands, distinguished by their half-spin minimal compact types. Camporesi–Pedon, printed pp. 250–252, Theorem 3.2 and its proof, identify these half-spin endpoint representations as limits of discrete series, using the standard classification. Liu–Zhang, printed p. 2996 immediately after Theorem 3.11, identify the regular ℋ1 model as their limit π1. This proves the classification using those published representation-theoretic results; the classification theorem itself is an external input, while H1–H2 supply the explicit realization and splitting.

    Integrability at the endpoint#

    Proposition H4. Failure of square-integrability and the Hardy replacement

    There is no nonzero regular function with finite ball integral ∫B|h|2s−1dv. The nonzero matrix coefficient between constant vectors of ℋ+ is in Lp(G) exactly for p>2 (among positive p); in particular the representation is not discrete series.

    H4 · version 1

    Uses H1, the coefficient lift.

    Proof of Proposition H4

    Write s=1−|q|2. The lift of a constant vector on the ball has size s3/2 on the standard section, whereas G/K has invariant measure proportional to s−4dv. Averaging the absolute pth power of its nonzero constant-vector matrix coefficient over the compact fibre gives a positive constant times

    ∫Bs3p/2−4dv.

    This is finite exactly when p>2. In particular these coefficients are not in L2(G). The attempted ball norm with density s−1dv also has no nonzero regular vectors, by monotonicity of spherical square means. The Hardy condition provides the correct nonzero Hilbert realization. For the first assertion, spherical square means M(r) are nondecreasing, either by the expansion in H1 or subharmonicity. If h≠0, then M(r0)>0 at some radius and

    ∫r01M(r)r31−r2dr=∞.

    For the coefficient, harmonic mean values identify pairing with a constant with evaluation at zero. On the section the lift is s3/2v. Compact integration of |⟨τ1(k)−1v,w⟩|p is a fixed strictly positive constant for nonzero v,w. The remaining radial integral converges exactly when 3p/2−4>−1, namely p>2.

    The exterior regular model#

    Proposition H5. Kelvin inversion and exterior regular functions

    Let ℋext consist of regular functions on |q|>1 with finite norm

    ‖u‖ext2=supR>1⁡R6∫S3|u(Rω)|2dσ(ω).

    The transform (𝒦h)(q)=|q|−2q−1h(q−1) is an isometric bijection ℋ+→ℋext. Its boundary trace is the involution C in H2, so the exterior boundary space is ℋ−. These exterior functions decay as O(|q|−3) at infinity.

    H5 · version 1

    Uses H1, H2, the Cauchy–Fueter kernel.

    Proof of Proposition H5

    Put G(q)=q‾/|q|4. Product and chain rules, with DlG=0, give

    Dl(G(q)h(q−1))=−q|q|6(Dlh)(q−1).

    To verify the coefficient in the chain rule, use d(q−1)v=−q−1vq−1. For the real orthonormal basis eμ=1,i,j,k, the adjoint of v↦q‾vq‾ is v↦qvq. Thus the coefficient of ∂νh in the product derivative is −qeν/|q|6, proving the identity. It proves regularity on the punctured domain. Also G(q)G(q−1)=1, so 𝒦2=1 where defined.

    For q=Rω, |G(q)|=R−3 and q−1=R−1ω‾. Therefore

    R6∫S3|(𝒦h)(Rω)|2dσ=∫S3|h(R−1ω)|2dσ.

    Taking suprema proves the isometry. Conversely the same identity sends an exterior function to a regular function on the punctured ball with bounded spherical square means near zero. Each harmonic component's spherical expansion has no singular term under this bound, so the singularity is removable. Its extension is regular and in ℋ+. At infinity the image of an interior function is O(R−3) since the interior function is bounded near zero. Taking the L2 boundary limit gives (Ch)(ω)=ω‾h(ω‾).

    Discussion

    The splitting H2 is analogous to the positive and negative Hardy spaces for the weight-one endpoint of SL2(ℝ). The elliptic coefficients use the same Cauchy kernel, but varying a lattice gives an SL4 action. A direct correspondence between these two representations remains open.
