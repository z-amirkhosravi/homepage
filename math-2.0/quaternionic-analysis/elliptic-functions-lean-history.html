    Quaternionic Weierstrass Functions and Quasi-Periods

    Summing translates of derivatives of the Cauchy–Fueter kernel produces quaternionic analogues of Weierstrass functions. This article develops three periodic functions and a zeta function with additive quasi-periods. Prerequisites are the kernel and Fueter regularity, Taylor's theorem, and uniform convergence of differentiated series.

    1. The lattice#

    Fix a full-rank lattice L⊂ℍ, written as

    L=ℤω0+ℤω1+ℤω2+ℤω3,

    where the four generators are linearly independent over ℝ.

    Put L∗=L∖{0}.

    Use the fixed coordinates q=t+∑j=13ejxj and the functions

    G(q)=q‾|q|4,Gj=∂xjG,j=1,2,3.

    Each bracket in the following lattice sums is a single summand; convergence is asserted for the bracketed sums.

    The naive sum of translates of Gj has borderline decay |λ|−4 in four dimensions. Subtracting the constant Gj(λ) gains an extra power of decay. The individual terms inside a bracket must not be split into divergent lattice sums.

    2. The elliptic functions#

    Construction QW.1. [2, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019] For a full-rank lattice L⊂ℍ, j∈{1,2,3}, and q∈ℍ∖L, form the series

    ℘L,j(q)=Gj(q)+∑λ∈L∗[Gj(q+λ)−Gj(λ)].

    Construction QW.1 · version 1

    Well-definedness: Convergence and regularity in Proposition QW.2; periodicity in Proposition QW.3.

    Earlier article. Current version; correspondence with the rewrite is unverified.

    Here “elliptic” means lattice-periodic and Fueter-regular away from isolated singularities. Proposition QW.2 and Proposition QW.3 supply these properties for Construction QW.1.

    Proposition QW.2. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice and j∈{1,2,3}. The series in Construction QW.1 converges absolutely and uniformly on every compact subset of ℍ∖L. It may be differentiated term by term to every real order, with locally uniform convergence of the differentiated series. Its sum is both left and right Fueter-regular.

    For every ω∈L, the function

    q⟼℘L,j(q)−Gj(q−ω)

    extends to a smooth, left and right regular function on a neighborhood of ω.

    Proposition QW.2 · version 2

    Uses Construction QW.1, Proposition QF.3, Lemma QF.4.

    Earlier article. Current version; correspondence with the rewrite is unverified.

    Proof of Proposition QW.2

    For a compact set K⊂ℍ∖L, choose R with |q|≤R on K. If |λ|>2R, the segment from λ to λ+q stays at distance at least |λ|/2 from 0. The derivative bound from the foundations page and the mean-value estimate give

    |Gj(λ+q)−Gj(λ)|≤CR|λ|−5.

    A full-rank lattice has O(r4) points in a ball of radius r: write L=Aℤ4 for an invertible real matrix A and use equivalence of norms. Summing over dyadic shells now shows ∑λ∈L∗|λ|−p<∞ for p>4. This proves absolute, locally uniform convergence.

    After m≥1 real derivatives, the constant subtraction vanishes and each tail term is bounded by CK,m|λ|−4−m. All differentiated series therefore converge locally uniformly. The finitely many terms omitted from these tail estimates are smooth away from their singularities. The standard theorem on locally uniform convergence of derivatives permits differentiation of the sum.

    Every summand is left and right regular by Proposition QF.3, including the subtracted constant. Termwise differentiation proves regularity of the sum. In a small ball around any lattice point, remove its one singular summand; the remaining series and its derivatives converge uniformly and are regular there.

    Proposition QW.3. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice and j∈{1,2,3}. For all q∈ℍ∖L and ω∈L,

    ℘L,j(−q)=℘L,j(q),℘L,j(q+ω)=℘L,j(q).

    Proposition QW.3 · version 2

    Uses Construction QW.1, Proposition QW.2, Lemma QF.4.

    Earlier article. Current version; correspondence with the rewrite is unverified.

    Proof of Proposition QW.3

    Every first real partial derivative of ℘L,j is the absolutely convergent sum

    ∂xμ℘L,j(q)=∑λ∈L∂xμGj(q+λ),μ=0,1,2,3,x0=t.

    Reindexing this convergent sum shows that the derivatives are L-periodic. Hence

    Hω(q)=℘L,j(q+ω)−℘L,j(q)

    has zero real gradient. The complement of a discrete lattice in ℝ4 is path connected, so Hω is a constant cω.

    Since Gj is even, reindexing the absolutely convergent defining series by λ↦−λ shows that ℘L,j is even. Consequently

    Hω(−q−ω)=℘L,j(−q)−℘L,j(−q−ω)=−Hω(q).

    Both sides are constant in q, so cω=−cω, and therefore cω=0.

    The proof reindexes the absolutely convergent derivative series, then uses evenness to remove the possible constant increment. It does not reindex an unregularized borderline sum.

    3. The zeta function#

    For the classical zeta function and its derivative and quasi-period identities, see [3, §2.1, p. 57].

    Construction QW.4. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice. For λ∈L∗ and q=t+∑j=13ejxj, let the real differential at λ act by

    dGλ[q]=t∂tG(λ)+∑j=13xjGj(λ).

    For q∈ℍ∖L, form

    ζL(q)=G(q)+∑λ∈L∗[G(λ+q)−G(λ)−dGλ[q]].

    Construction QW.4 · version 1

    Well-definedness: Convergence, regularity, and quasi-periodicity in Proposition QW.6.

    Earlier article. Current version; correspondence with the rewrite is unverified.

    Lemma QW.5. For λ∈ℍ∖{0} and q=t+∑j=13ejxj, put zj(q)=tej−xj. Then

    dGλ[q]=−∑j=13Gj(λ)zj(q).

    Lemma QW.5 · version 1

    Uses Construction QW.4, Proposition QF.3.

    Earlier article. Current version; correspondence with the rewrite is unverified.

    Proof of Lemma QW.5

    Right regularity gives ∂tG(λ)=−∑jGj(λ)ej. Substitute this into the definition of dGλ[q] in Construction QW.4 and use that t,xj are real.

    Lemma QW.5 expresses the real Taylor subtraction using regular linear coordinates. The order of quaternionic multiplication is explicit in the formula.

    Proposition QW.6. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice. The series in Construction QW.4 converges absolutely and locally uniformly on ℍ∖L, as do all its termwise real derivatives. Its sum is odd, is both left and right Fueter-regular, and for j∈{1,2,3} satisfies

    ∂xjζL(q)=℘L,j(q)(q∉L).

    There is a unique additive map ηL:L→ℍ such that, for every ω∈L and every q∉L,

    ζL(q+ω)−ζL(q)=ηL(ω).

    Proposition QW.6 · version 2

    Uses Construction QW.4, Construction QW.1, Proposition QW.2, Proposition QW.3, Proposition QF.3, Lemma QF.4.

    Earlier article. Current version; correspondence with the rewrite is unverified.

    Proof of Proposition QW.6

    Taylor's theorem bounds each bracket, uniformly on a compact set, by CK|λ|−5, since the second derivatives of G have degree −5. For first derivatives the summands are differences of first derivatives of G, again with decay |λ|−5. Derivatives of order m≥2 have decay |λ|−3−m. The argument in Proposition QW.2 proves convergence and permits all termwise differentiation.

    The first Taylor polynomial of a regular function is regular: applying either first-order constant-coefficient operator to it gives that operator applied to G, evaluated at λ, which is zero. The series is therefore left and right regular.

    Because G is odd and dG−λ=dGλ, replacing λ by −λ proves oddness of ζL. Differentiating with respect to xj gives exactly the defining series for ℘L,j, the classical derivative identity [3, p. 57, following (2.20)].

    Now put Fω(q)=ζL(q+ω)−ζL(q). By Proposition QW.3, all three spatial derivatives of Fω vanish. Left regularity also gives

    ∂tFω=−∑j=13ej∂xjFω=0.

    Connectedness of ℍ∖L makes Fω constant. Finally, splitting the increment along ω+ν into two increments proves

    ηL(ω+ν)=ηL(ω)+ηL(ν).

    The constant increment determines ηL(ω) for each ω, so the resulting map is unique.

    The map ηL is the first period datum supplied by the construction. Further relations among its values require additional statements and proofs.

    4. Consequences and examples#

    Corollary QW.7. For a full-rank lattice L⊂ℍ and ω∈L with ω/2∉L,

    ηL(ω)=2ζL(ω/2).

    Corollary QW.7 · version 1

    Uses Proposition QW.6.

    Earlier article. Current version; correspondence with the rewrite is unverified.

    Proof of Corollary QW.7

    Set q=−ω/2 in the quasi-period identity of Proposition QW.6 and use the oddness of ζL.

    Corollary QW.8. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice. For j∈{1,2,3} and ω∈L with ω/2∉L, the real differential of ℘L,j vanishes at ω/2:

    ∂xμ℘L,j(ω/2)=0(μ=0,1,2,3; x0=t).

    Corollary QW.8 · version 1

    Uses Proposition QW.2, Proposition QW.3.

    Earlier article. Current version; correspondence with the rewrite is unverified.

    Proof of Corollary QW.8

    By Proposition QW.2 the derivatives exist. Differentiating the two identities in Proposition QW.3 shows that each first derivative is odd and L-periodic. Its values at ω/2 and −ω/2 are therefore both equal and negatives of one another, so they vanish.

    Corollary QW.9. Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice, a>0 a real number, and j∈{1,2,3}. For all q∉L and ω∈L,

    ℘aL,j(aq)=a−4℘L,j(q),ζaL(aq)=a−3ζL(q),ηaL(aω)=a−3ηL(ω).

    Corollary QW.9 · version 1

    Uses Construction QW.1, Construction QW.4, Proposition QW.2, Proposition QW.6, Lemma QF.4.

    Earlier article. Current version; correspondence with the rewrite is unverified.

    Proof of Corollary QW.9

    Reindex each convergent series using aλ∈aL and apply Lemma QF.4. In the zeta subtraction, dGaλ[aq]=a−3dGλ[q], since each first derivative of G has degree −4. Thus every bracket has the stated weight. Apply the resulting zeta identity to the difference at q+ω and q and use Proposition QW.6 to obtain the identity for η.

    Example. The standard lattice

    For L0=ℤ+ℤi+ℤj+ℤk, Proposition QW.3 gives periods 1,i,j,k. Additivity in Proposition QW.6 determines ηL0 from its four values on these generators.

    Since 1/2∉L0, Corollary QW.7 gives ηL0(1)=2ζL0(1/2) and Corollary QW.8 gives d℘L0,j(1/2)=0 for j=1,2,3. The zeta value is expressed by a convergent series here; no numerical evaluation is asserted.

    Corollary QW.9 describes real dilation. An action by general quaternions requires a separate transformation statement.

    Sources and scope#

    The quaternionic Weierstrass construction is classical work of Fueter. Kraußhar's [2, §4, p. 1019, eq. (12)] displays the regularized first-derivative sum and attributes its quaternionic case to Fueter's 1939 paper. See the literature guide for the references and the remaining comparison with Fueter's original notation.

    This article supplies explicit arguments for its boxed formulations and makes no novelty claim. Each result carries its own review and formal-verification status.

    Representations and special values of the coefficients#

    The higher derivative identities express the regular part of these elliptic functions through convergent lattice sums. Varying the lattice gives Eisenstein functions on SL₄, while harmonic theta series express their scalar components through critical L-values.

    For a concrete first example, Theorem EM.13 computes the coefficient of x12 in the regular part of ℘L0,1 for L0=ℤ+ℤi+ℤj+ℤk as 96iL(η(2τ)12,5), with a proof of the exact modular identity and its normalization.

    A separate group action, by Möbius transformations of the ball, leads to discrete series and the ordinary Fueter Hardy limit. The connections among these actions are a source of further questions.
