    Quaternions and Fueter regularity

    Discussion

    The Cauchy–Fueter kernel is the starting point for the elliptic construction. This page fixes the conventions and collects the elementary facts needed later. Prerequisites are real multivariable calculus and quaternion multiplication.

    Quaternions and coordinates#

    Definition F-D1. Coordinates, conjugation, and norm

    Let ℍ be the real quaternion algebra with basis (1,e1,e2,e3)=(1,i,j,k) and multiplication i2=j2=k2=ijk=−1. Write

    q=t+e1x1+e2x2+e3x3,x0=t,e0=1.

    Set

    q‾=t−e1x1−e2x2−e3x3,|q|=(t2+x12+x22+x32)1/2.

    Thus qq‾=q‾q=|q|2. All partial derivatives below are ordinary real derivatives in these fixed coordinates.

    The regularity equation#

    Definition F-D2. Left and right Fueter regularity

    For an open set U⊂ℍ and a smooth function f:U→ℍ, define

    Dℓf=∂tf+∑j=13ej∂xjf,Drf=∂tf+∑j=13(∂xjf)ej.

    The function is left Fueter-regular if Dℓf=0, and right Fueter-regular if Drf=0 on U. “Regular” in these articles refers to Fueter regularity, with the side specified when needed.

    Discussion

    Multiplying either operator by a nonzero real scalar does not change its kernel. The side on which a quaternionic coefficient acts matters.

    Example. Products need not preserve left regularity

    The constant i and z2=tj−x2 are left regular, since Dℓi=0 and Dℓz2=j−j=0. But

    Dℓ(iz2)=ij−ji=2k≠0.

    Thus pointwise multiplication does not preserve left regularity.

    The Cauchy–Fueter kernel#

    Definition F-D3. Kernel and spatial derivatives

    For q∈ℍ∖{0}, define

    G(q)=q‾|q|4,Gj(q)=∂xjG(q)(j=1,2,3).

    There is no surface-area factor in this normalization.

    Proposition F1. Regularity of the kernel [1, §4, Lemma 2]

    The kernel G of F-D3 is both left and right Fueter-regular on ℍ∖{0}. For every multi-index α∈ℕ04, its real partial derivative ∂αG is also both left and right regular there.

    Proof of Proposition F1[1, §4, proof of Lemma 2]

    Put x0=t and e0=1, so q=∑μ=03eμxμ. Since Dℓq‾=4 and ∂xμ|q|−4=−4xμ|q|−6, the product rule gives

    DℓG=4|q|−4−4|q|−6∑μ=03eμq‾xμ=4|q|−4−4|q|−6qq‾=0.

    For Dr, the sum in the second term is instead ∑μq‾eμxμ=q‾q, with the same result. Real partial derivatives commute with both constant-coefficient operators, which proves the final assertion.

    Derivatives and decay#

    Lemma F2. Parity, homogeneity, and derivative bounds

    For every α∈ℕ04, there is a constant Cα such that, for all q≠0 and all real a>0,

    ∂αG(aq)=a−3−|α|∂αG(q),∂αG(−q)=(−1)|α|+1∂αG(q),|∂αG(q)|≤Cα|q|−3−|α|.

    In particular, G is odd, each Gj is even, and

    Gj(q)=−ej|q|4−4xjq‾|q|6.

    F2 · version 1

    Uses F-D1, F-D3.

    Proof of Lemma F2

    The defining formula gives G(aq)=a−3G(q) and G(−q)=−G(q). Differentiating these identities in the real coordinates proves the homogeneity and parity formulas.

    The smooth function ∂αG is bounded on the compact unit sphere. Write q=|q|u with |u|=1 and apply homogeneity to obtain the bound. Finally, the formula for Gj follows from ∂xjq‾=−ej and ∂xj|q|−4=−4xj|q|−6.

    Discussion

    A four-dimensional lattice sum with decay |λ|−p converges absolutely for p>4, as shown in E1. The first derivatives Gj are at the borderline p=4. The elliptic construction gains another power of decay by subtracting a constant term.

    Further constructions#

    Discussion

    On the ball, the regular equation has weighted symmetric-spinor realizations of discrete series and a Hardy-space realization of a limit of discrete series. For differential geometry, mixed left/right pairings produce closed three-forms. These articles use the conventions fixed here.
