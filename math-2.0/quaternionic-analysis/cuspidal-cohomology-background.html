    Degree-Two Cuspidal and Parabolic Cohomology for Sp(1,1)

    Use the group and spinor conventions, cuspidal condition, and complementary chirality. Thus n≥2, m=n−2, and Em=Symm⁡ℂ4 is the flat coefficient representation.

    Half-Space and Regular Spinors#

    Discussion

    We first describe the domain and the coefficient space. Regularity is a differential equation on that space; automorphy is a transformation law under a discrete group. The two conditions define the space ℳn(Γ) of regular automorphic forms.

    The Domain and Group Action#

    Write a quaternion as z=y+x, where x=x1i+x2j+x3k is purely imaginary. The domain is the quaternionic half-space

    ℋ={y+x∈ℍ:y>0}.

    We use the following realization of Sp(1,1):

    G={g∈GL2⁡(ℍ):g∗H0g=H0},H0=(0110).

    Here g∗ means quaternionic conjugate transpose. A matrix g=(abcd) acts by

    gz=(az+b)(cz+d)−1,J(g,z)=cz+d.

    The base point is 1∈ℋ. Its stabilizer is the maximal compact subgroup

    K=C{diag⁡(u,v):|u|=|v|=1}C−1,C=2−1/2(11−11).

    We label the two compact factors by this conjugation and write σ(k)=J(k,1) for the second factor. The action and its relation to the ball are established in Quaternionic Half-Space and Siegel Domains.

    Polynomial Coefficients#

    Fix an integer n≥1. The coefficient space is

    Vn=Symn⁡ℂ2={∑j=0npjXn−jYj:pj∈ℂ}.

    Thus a function f:ℋ→Vn is a polynomial in the auxiliary variables X,Y, with coefficient functions on ℋ. These auxiliary variables are distinct from the coordinates y,x1,x2,x3 of the domain.

    For a complex 2×2 matrix A, the symmetric-power action is

    (τn(A)p)(X,Y)=p(((X,Y)A)1,((X,Y)A)2).

    We represent quaternions by complex matrices through

    Q(y+x)=(y+ix1x2+ix3−x2+ix3y−ix1),

    and use I1=Q(i), I2=Q(j), I3=Q(k) and ϵ=(01−10). The symbol i denotes the complex scalar imaginary unit; i,j,k denote quaternion units. The map Q is called ι in the earlier articles.

    The coefficient norm is

    ‖p‖n2=∑j=0n(nj)−1|pj|2.

    This is the symmetric-tensor norm induced by the usual Hermitian norm on ℂ2. Consequently τn(Q(u)) is unitary when |u|=1, and its operator norm is |u|n for nonzero u.

    Regularity and Automorphy#

    Definition CP-H2. Regular Spinors

    Let n≥1 be an integer. A smooth function f:ℋ→Vn is a regular spinor of weight n if

    (∂yI2×2+∑a=13Ia∂xa)(fXfY)=0.(HS1)

    Here fX=∂f/∂X and fY=∂f/∂Y are derivatives in the auxiliary polynomial variables.

    CP-H2 · version 1

    For a smooth f:ℋ→Vn and g∈G, use the slash notation

    (f|ng)(z)=|J(g,z)|−2τn(Q(J(g,z)−1))f(gz).(HS2)

    The denominator identity gives (f|ng)|nh=f|n(gh). For the weights n≥2 used below, the covariance calculation in the two-form construction shows that the slash action preserves regularity. These facts allow regularity and a group transformation law to be imposed together.

    Definition CP-H0. Regular Automorphic Forms

    Let n≥1 be an integer and let Γ⊂G be a discrete subgroup. A regular automorphic form of weight n for Γ is a smooth function f:ℋ→Vn satisfying both conditions:

    1. f is regular.
    2. f|nγ=f for every γ∈Γ.

    CP-H0 · version 2

    The complex vector space of these forms is denoted by

    ℳn(Γ)={f∈C∞(ℋ,Vn):f is regular and f|nγ=f for all γ∈Γ}.
    Discussion

    The notation ℳn(Γ) imposes the differential equation and transformation law. Growth and cuspidality will be specified separately below, as is usual when passing from automorphic functions to spaces with prescribed behavior at the cusps.

    Growth and Finiteness Conditions#

    The growth condition uses the ordinary Euclidean norm on ℍ2:

    ‖(v1,v2)t‖=|v1|2+|v2|2,|a+bi+cj+dk|=a2+b2+c2+d2.

    For a matrix g∈G, its operator norm is the largest stretching factor for this positive norm:

    ‖g‖op=supv≠0⁡‖gv‖‖v‖.

    Set

    𝖧(g)=max⁡{1,‖g‖op,‖g−1‖op}.

    The indefinite form preserved by G has value 2Re⁡(v‾1v2); it is distinct from the Euclidean norm used to measure growth.

    For differential operators, write 𝔤=Lie⁡(G) and 𝔤ℂ=𝔤⊗ℝℂ. Right differentiation is given by

    D(T)F(g)=ddtF(gexp⁡(tT))|t=0(T∈𝔤).

    It extends complex-linearly and multiplicatively to the universal enveloping algebra U(𝔤ℂ). Its center is denoted by 𝒵=Z(U(𝔤ℂ)). Elements of 𝒵 therefore act as invariant differential operators on F.

    Definition CP-D0. Growth and Finiteness Conditions

    Let V be a finite-dimensional complex normed vector space and let F:G→V be smooth.

    1. Moderate growth. The function F has moderate growth if there are an integer A≥0 and a constant C>0 such that

      ‖F(g)‖≤C𝖧(g)A(g∈G).(MG)

    2. 𝒵-finiteness. The function F is 𝒵-finite if

      dimℂ⁡span⁡{D(z)F:z∈𝒵}<∞.(ZF)

    3. Uniform moderate growth. The function F has uniform moderate growth if there is an integer A≥0 such that, for every u∈U(𝔤ℂ), there is a constant Cu>0 with

      ‖D(u)F(g)‖≤Cu𝖧(g)A(g∈G).(UMG)

    4. Right K-finiteness. The function F is right K-finite if

      dimℂ⁡span⁡{g↦F(gk):k∈K}<∞.

    CP-D0 · version 4

    Discussion

    Moderate growth is a polynomial bound. Uniform moderate growth uses one exponent for every derivative, although the constants may depend on the derivative. The definition of a cusp form below requires moderate growth and 𝒵-finiteness; the rapid-decrease theorem then supplies the decay used in period integrals. These conventions agree with [1, §2].

    Finiteness as a Differential Equation#

    Discussion

    For classical Maass forms, the familiar spectral condition is a Laplace eigenvalue equation. The condition here has the same differential-equation interpretation, with two commuting central operators. The complexified Lie algebra is 𝔰𝔭4(ℂ), and the center of its universal enveloping algebra is a polynomial algebra on generators C2,C4 of degrees two and four. Put 𝒟2=D(C2) and 𝒟4=D(C4). The Harish–Chandra isomorphism [2, 0.2.7–0.2.9] gives these generators.

    Proposition CP-Z1. A Finite Differential Test for Central Finiteness

    Let V be a finite-dimensional complex vector space, let F:G→V be smooth, and choose algebra generators C2,C4 of 𝒵. Write 𝒟j=D(Cj). Then F is 𝒵-finite if and only if there are nonzero polynomials p2,p4∈ℂ[t] such that

    p2(𝒟2)F=0,p4(𝒟4)F=0.

    In particular, a joint eigenfunction of 𝒟2,𝒟4 is 𝒵-finite.

    CP-Z1 · version 1

    Uses Growth and finiteness; [2].

    Proof of Proposition CP-Z1

    For type C2, the Weyl group acts by signed permutations of two Cartan coordinates. Its invariant polynomials are the symmetric polynomials in λ12,λ22, generated by their sum and product. The Harish–Chandra isomorphism [2, 0.2.7–0.2.9] therefore gives 𝒵=ℂ[C2,C4].

    If the central orbit of F spans a finite-dimensional space, both operators preserve that space. Their characteristic polynomials annihilate it by Cayley–Hamilton.

    Conversely, let dj=deg⁡pj. If either polynomial is constant, the corresponding equation forces F=0. Otherwise polynomial division and commutation show that the central orbit is spanned by

    {𝒟2a𝒟4bF:0≤a<d2, 0≤b<d4}.

    This is a finite set. Linear polynomials give the eigenfunction case.

    Discussion

    Thus finiteness can be established by differential identities, without enumerating an infinite algebra. In a fixed homogeneous bundle these become equations for the coefficient functions on half-space, including the connection terms. They are not simply the ordinary scalar Euclidean Laplacian applied componentwise.

    For the weight-n quaternionic discrete series, the infinitesimal character is (n,1) in the Cartan coordinates of the weight dictionary. Normalize C2,C4 so their Harish-Chandra polynomials are λ12+λ22 and λ12λ22. A lift belonging to this representation then satisfies

    𝒟2F=(n2+1)F,𝒟4F=n2F.

    This follows by evaluating those polynomials at (n,1). In particular the matrix-coefficient construction of the weight-fourteen Poincaré series establishes its central finiteness through these eigenvalue equations. For a separately proposed function, the differential identities or its membership in the representation must still be established; regularity is not being used here as an unproved substitute for central finiteness.

    Vanishing Constant Terms#

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group of rational rank one, with 𝐆(ℝ)≃G, and let Γ⊂𝐆(ℚ) be a torsion-free congruence lattice. Put X=Γ\ℋ.

    For an integer n≥2 and f∈ℳn(Γ), its group lift is

    Ff(g)=|J(g,1)|−2τn(Q(J(g,1)−1))f(g1).(1)

    The lift satisfies Ff(gk)=τn(Q(σ(k)−1))Ff(g), so it is right K-finite. Indeed, k1=1 and the denominator cocycle gives J(gk,1)=J(g,1)J(k,1).

    Here rational cusps are Γ-orbits of proper rational parabolic subgroups Pξ=MξAξNξ; Nξ≃(ℝ3,+) is the unipotent radical. The quotient Haar measure is normalized to have total mass one.

    Definition CP-D1. Regular Automorphic Cusp Forms

    Let Γ be a torsion-free congruence lattice in the rational-rank-one group fixed above. For n≥2, a regular automorphic cusp form of weight n is a form f∈ℳn(Γ) whose lift satisfies all three conditions:

    1. Ff has moderate growth, measured using ‖⋅‖n.

    2. Ff is 𝒵-finite.

    3. At every rational cusp its constant term vanishes:

      ∫(Γ∩Nξ)\NξFf(ug)du=0(g∈G).(2)

    CP-D1 · version 4

    The space of regular automorphic cusp forms is denoted by 𝒮n−(Γ).

    Discussion

    Defining the constant term on the group makes it independent of the cusp coordinates. In a frame adapted to a cusp, it is the average over the three translation variables. The minus sign in 𝒮n−(Γ) labels the anti-self-dual two-form constructed below.

    Proposition CP0. Growth in a Cusp Frame

    Let Γ⊂G be discrete, let n≥1, and let f∈ℳn(Γ) have a group lift Ff of moderate growth with exponent A. Choose sξ∈G with sξ∞=ξ and set fξ=f|nsξ. Then

    Ff(sξnxay)=y(n+2)/2fξ(y+x).(HF)

    For every compact Q0⊂Im⁡ℍ there is Cξ,Q0>0 with

    ‖fξ(y+x)‖n≤Cξ,Q0y(A−n−2)/2(x∈Q0, y≥1).(H)

    CP0 · version 3

    Uses CP-D0, CP-D1, QH-D2.

    Proof of Proposition CP0

    The slash composition law gives Ffξ(g)=Ff(sξg). Since J(nxay,1)=y−1/2, homogeneity of τn gives (HF). The height 𝖧 is submultiplicative, 𝖧(ay)=y for y≥1, and 𝖧(nx) is bounded on Q0. Hence 𝖧(sξnxay)≤Cy. Substitute this bound in (MG) and divide (HF) by y(n+2)/2.

    Proposition CP1. Cuspidality and the Petersson Norm

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group of rational rank one with 𝐆(ℝ)≃Sp(1,1), and let Γ⊂𝐆(ℚ) be a torsion-free congruence lattice. Let n≥2 and let f∈ℳn(Γ) have a group lift of moderate growth that is 𝒵-finite. Then f∈𝒮n−(Γ) if and only if

    ‖f‖Γ,n2:=∫Γ\ℋyn−2‖f(y+x)‖n2dx1dx2dx3dy<∞.(3)

    For each rational cusp ξ, choose sξ∈G with sξ∞=ξ, set fξ=f|nsξ, and let Λξ⊂Im⁡ℍ be the lattice giving sξ−1(Γ∩Nξ)sξ={nλ:λ∈Λξ}. Its average

    Aξ(y)=1vol⁡(ℝ3/Λξ)∫ℝ3/Λξfξ(y+x)dx

    is independent of y. Finite norm implies Aξ=0 at every cusp. For a cusp form and every L≥0 there is Cξ,L such that

    ‖fξ(y+x)‖n≤Cξ,Ly−L−(n+2)/2(x in a fixed translation cell, y≥Yξ).(RD)

    CP1 · version 4

    Uses CP-D1, CP0; [1, Thm. A].

    Proof of Proposition CP1

    The height formula Re⁡(gz)=y/|J(g,z)|2, the norm of τn(Q(J)), and the real Jacobian |J(g,z)|−8 show that the density in (3) is invariant. Equivalently it is the squared invariant spinor norm yn+2‖f‖n2 times the invariant measure y−4dxdy.

    Average HS1 over the translation torus. Its spatial derivatives integrate to zero, so ∂XAξ′=∂YAξ′=0. Euler's identity for degree n>0 gives Aξ′=0. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the norm of the average by the mean squared norm. Thus finite (3) implies

    ‖Aξ‖n2∫Yξ∞yn−2dy<∞,

    which forces Aξ=0 for n≥2. In (HF), averaging over translations gives precisely the constant term at nxay. Every element of G is nxayk, and compact equivariance gives its vanishing at every g. Passing to the translation cover handles finite cusp holonomy.

    Conversely, the scalar components of Ff are smooth, left Γ-invariant, 𝒵-finite, of moderate growth, right K-finite and cuspidal. Miller–Schmid’s theorem [1, Thm. A] gives rapid decrease on each cusp Siegel set. The comparison 𝖧(sξnxay)≍y on a fixed translation cell converts it to ‖Ff(sξnxay)‖n≤CLy−L. Equation (HF) proves (RD), and then

    yn−2‖fξ(y+x)‖n2≤CL2y−4−2L.

    This is integrable in each cusp. There are finitely many cusps, and smoothness gives integrability on the compact core.

    The Condition on Group Cocycles#

    Put X=Γ\ℋ, m=n−2, Em=Symm⁡ℂ4, and ρ=Symm⁡R. Let ℰm=Γ\(ℋ×Em) be the flat bundle for the action (z,v)↦(γz,ρ(γ)v), with flat differential d∇. For each rational cusp, put Γξ=Γ∩Pξ.

    For j≥0, write Ωj(X,ℰm) for smooth bundle-valued j-forms and Ωcj(X,ℰm) for those with compact support; set both spaces to zero in degree −1. Define

    Hj(X,ℰm)=ker⁡(d∇:Ωj→Ωj+1)d∇Ωj−1,Hcj(X,ℰm)=ker⁡(d∇:Ωcj→Ωcj+1)d∇Ωcj−1.

    The inclusion of complexes induces jc:Hcj(X,ℰm)→Hj(X,ℰm).

    Definition CP-D2. Interior and Parabolic Cohomology

    Interior cohomology is the subspace

    H!j(X,ℰm):=im⁡jc⊆Hj(X,ℰm).(I)

    Parabolic cohomology is

    Hpar2(Γ,Em)=ker⁡(H2(Γ,Em)⟶⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,Em)).(6)

    CP-D2 · version 3

    Identify Hj(Γ,Em) with Hj(X,ℰm) by the de Rham comparison for the contractible universal cover ℋ. [4, §§8, 15]; [3, Thm. I.7.5, §§III.1, III.8].

    In inhomogeneous group-cochain notation, a two-cocycle c represents such a class exactly when, for every cusp, there is a one-cochain bξ:Γξ→Em such that

    c(u,v)=ρ(u)bξ(v)−bξ(uv)+bξ(u),u,v∈Γξ.(7)

    Indeed, membership in the kernel (6) says exactly that the restriction of c to each Γξ is a coboundary. Formula (7) is the inhomogeneous differential of a one-cochain.

    Discussion

    The notation H!2 denotes the image of jc in ordinary cohomology. A compactly supported closed form can be exact with a primitive that is not compactly supported, so the map jc need not be injective. Its image is a different object from its domain. CP2 identifies this image with the parabolic subspace. The companion article Parabolic Cohomology and Compact Support for Hyperbolic Four-Manifolds develops the topological comparison using the full stabilizers of geometric cusps, with a detailed proof of the relative-cohomology sequence and the cutoff construction.

    For a neat group, Γξ≃ℤ3; in general it is virtually ℤ3. In the classical surface case the cusp stabilizer is cyclic, and a degree-one cocycle becomes a coboundary there. Equation (7) is the corresponding degree-two condition. It does not ask the cocycle itself to vanish, and separate conditions on individual parabolic elements would be insufficient.

    Example. Why cyclic tests miss the condition

    Take n=2, so the coefficient representation is trivial, and let the cusp group be ℤ3. The cocycle c(u,v)=u1v2 has nonzero alternating part, so its class in H2(ℤ3,ℂ) is nonzero. Its restriction to every cyclic subgroup is nevertheless a coboundary: on the subgroup generated by a, take b(ka)=−k(k−1)a1a2/2. In fact H2(ℤ,E)=0 for every coefficient module E.

    Proposition CP2. Parabolic Cohomology Is Interior Cohomology

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group of rational rank one with 𝐆(ℝ)≃Sp(1,1), and let Γ⊂𝐆(ℚ) be a torsion-free congruence lattice. Put X=Γ\ℋ and Em=Symm⁡ℂ4, with m≥0. Then

    Hpar2(Γ,Em)=H!2(X,ℰm):=im⁡(Hc2(X,ℰm)→H2(X,ℰm)).(8)

    CP2 · version 2

    Uses CP-D2, the compact core of a finite-volume real hyperbolic manifold, and the relative cohomology exact sequence.

    Proof of Proposition CP2

    By the cusp decomposition [5, Prop. 4.2.17, Cor. 4.2.18], truncation gives a compact manifold X with boundary ∐[ξ]Yξ. Each Yξ is a compact flat three-manifold with contractible universal cover and fundamental group Γξ. The cusp collars identify the interior of X with X; the inclusion of a compact core into X is a homotopy equivalence. Extend ℰm over X using the same monodromy representation.

    The relative exact sequence contains

    H1(∂X,ℰm)→δH2(X,∂X;ℰm)→jH2(X,ℰm)→resH2(∂X,ℰm).

    The flat-coefficient comparison in M000003 identifies the second term with Hc2(X,ℰm); homotopy invariance identifies the third with H2(X,ℰm). Under these identifications j is jc. The aspherical boundary components identify the last term with ⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,Em), and their inclusions identify res with the restriction map in (6). Exactness gives

    Hpar2=ker⁡(res)=im⁡(jc)=H!2.

    The same sequence gives ker⁡(jc)=im⁡(δ), specifying exactly where compactly supported classes can disappear in ordinary cohomology.

    The Cohomological and Spectral Notation#

    Write 𝔤=𝔨⊕︎𝔭 for the Cartan decomposition determined by K.

    Definition CP-D3. Relative Lie Algebra Cohomology

    For a (𝔤ℂ,K)-module V, its relative cochain complex with coefficients Em is

    Cj(𝔤,K;V⊗Em)=HomK⁡(Λj𝔭ℂ,V⊗Em).

    The Lie algebra acts diagonally on V⊗Em. Its differential, evaluated on T0,…,Tj∈𝔭ℂ, is

    (dω)(T0,…,Tj)=∑i=0j(−1)iTi⋅ω(T0,…,Tˆi,…,Tj).

    The hat denotes omission of an argument. Denote the cohomology of this complex by Hj(𝔤,K;V⊗Em).

    CP-D3 · version 1

    The displayed differential is the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential for the symmetric pair: [𝔭,𝔭]⊆𝔨 makes the projected bracket terms zero.

    Let 𝒜cusp(Γ\G) consist of smooth complex functions on Γ\G that have moderate growth, are 𝒵-finite and right K-finite, and have vanishing constant terms at every rational cusp. Denote by κj the comparison map induced by their associated homogeneous-frame differential forms:

    κj:Hj(𝔤,K;𝒜cusp⊗Em)⟶Hj(X,ℰm).

    Definition CP-D3-CUSP. Cuspidal Cohomology

    Define the cuspidal subspace of ordinary cohomology by

    Hcuspj(Γ,Em):=im⁡(κj).

    CP-D3-CUSP · version 1

    Spectral Notation#

    Let Ldisc2(Γ\G) be the closed Hilbert sum of irreducible subrepresentations of the right regular representation on L2(Γ\G). Let Lcusp2 be the closure of 𝒜cusp in L2, and put

    Lres2=Ldisc2∩(Lcusp2)⟂.

    For a unitary representation V, write VK∞ for its smooth vectors that are K-finite. For an irreducible π, abbreviate πK=πK∞. For π in the discrete spectrum, MΓ(π)=HomG⁡(π,Ldisc2) is its multiplicity space, where HomG⁡ denotes bounded complex-linear intertwining maps.

    Discussion

    The comparison map κj is injective under the arithmetic hypotheses used here, by the external theorem cited in AC7. Defining its image does not prove that injection. Likewise, interior and cuspidal cohomology are different definitions; their equality in this particular degree is the content of CP5.

    Cohomology of the Discrete Series#

    Lemma CP4. One Cohomology Generator per Discrete-Series Copy

    Let n≥2, m=n−2, and let πn± be the two reflected discrete series. Then

    dim⁡Cj(𝔤,K;πn±⊗Em)={1j=2,0j≠2.(10)

    The unique cochain in degree two is, up to a nonzero scalar, the fiber map defining Ω±. Thus it generates relative Lie algebra cohomology in degree two.

    CP4 · version 1

    Uses CP-D3, the discrete-series compact decomposition, CP-C1.

    Proof of Lemma CP4

    Write Va⊠Vb for a representation of K=Sp(1)×Sp(1). The relevant decompositions are

    πn−|K=⨁r≥0Vr⊠Vr+n,Em|K=⨁a=0mVa⊠Vm−a,
    Λ0𝔭ℂ≃Λ4𝔭ℂ≃V0⊠V0,Λ1𝔭ℂ≃Λ3𝔭ℂ≃V1⊠V1,Λ2𝔭ℂ=(V2⊠V0)⊕︎(V0⊠V2).

    In the right tensor factor Vr+n⊗Vm−a, the smallest degree is r+a+2. Consequently degrees zero, one, three, and four have no cochains, and the V2⊠V0 part in degree two contributes none. The V0⊠V2 part forces r=a=0; Vn⊗Vn−2 contains V2 once. This proves (10) by the Clebsch–Gordan formula. The explicit fiber map is nonzero and equivariant by AC3, so it spans that line. There are no incoming or outgoing cochains. Reflection proves the other case. The resulting cohomology dimension agrees with [6, Thm. 7.1].

    The Correspondence#

    Theorem CP5. The Degree-Two Cuspidal and Parabolic Correspondence

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group with 𝐆(ℝ)≃Sp(1,1) and ℚ-rank one, and let Γ be a torsion-free congruence lattice in 𝐆. Put X=Γ\ℋ. For every integer n≥2, take the complex algebraic coefficient representation Em=Symm⁡ℂ4, m=n−2, with its flat bundle and the transported positive homogeneous metric hℋ of CP-H1. Then the explicit period construction gives a complex-linear isomorphism

    𝒮n−(Γ)⊕︎𝒮n+(Γ) → ∼  Hpar2(Γ,Em),(11)
    (f−,f+)⟼[Ωf−+Ωf++].

    Inside ordinary complex cohomology,

    Hpar2(Γ,Em)=H!2(X,ℰm)=Hcusp2(Γ,Em).

    With the spectral notation above,

    H2(𝔤,K;(Lres2(Γ\G))K∞⊗Em)=0.(11a)

    The restriction map in (6) induces an injection

    H2(Γ,Em)/Hcusp2(Γ,Em) ↪ ⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,Em).(11c)

    CP5 · version 4

    Uses Relative cohomology, cuspidal cohomology, spectral notation, CP2–CP4, AC6–AC7; the finite-coefficient harmonic spectral theorem of Borel–Garland/Borel–Casselman; [6, Thm. 7.1]; [7, Thm. 4.3].

    Proof of Theorem CP5

    The residual spectrum cannot contribute in degree two. The finite-coefficient harmonic spectral theorem identifies the space ℋ(2)2(X,ℰm) with

    ⨁π⊂Ldisc2(Γ\G)MΓ(π)⊗H2(𝔤,K;πK⊗Em),(11b)

    where MΓ(π) is the multiplicity space. The sum contributing to this degree is finite. This is the harmonic spectral decomposition [8, Prop. 4.4(i)], due to Borel–Garland [9, Prop. 5.6]. It is a theorem about square-integrable harmonic forms, not an identification of arbitrary ordinary cohomology with L2 cohomology. No continuous-spectrum harmonic term is omitted by (11b).

    Grobner’s classification [6, Thm. 7.1] says that only the two cohomological discrete series contribute in degree two. CP4 identifies them with πn±. The other possible unitary cohomological representations have cohomology only in degrees 0,4 or 1,3. In particular this remains true for the trivial coefficient system n=2.

    Every discrete series is tempered. Wallach’s theorem [7, Thm. 4.3] says that a tempered (𝔤,K)-module mapping to square-integrable automorphic forms has cuspidal image. It therefore places every occurrence of πn± in Lcusp2, not in the residual spectrum. This proves (11a) and identifies (11b) with cuspidal degree-two cohomology. This spectral exclusion is independent of the direct constant-term argument in CP1.

    Interior classes have the required harmonic representatives. Let [α]∈H!2, choosing α∈Ωc2(X,ℰm) with d∇α=0. Give X the complete half-space metric and the bundle its metric hℋ from CP-H1. Let dj be the closed L2 extension of d∇ in degree j, with Hilbert adjoint dj∗. The weak Hodge decomposition [10, §2.2, pp. 5–6] is

    L2Ω2=ℋ(2)2 ⊕︎ˆ im⁡d1 ⊕︎ˆ im⁡d2∗,ℋ(2)2=ker⁡d2∩ker⁡d1∗.

    Since d2α=0, α is orthogonal to im⁡d2∗, so its last component vanishes. Its harmonic projection η is smooth by elliptic regularity. Completeness permits approximation in the graph norm by compactly supported smooth forms: scalar cutoffs with gradients tending to zero commute with d∇ up to exterior multiplication by their differential. Consequently there exist uj∈Ωc1(X,ℰm) with

    d∇uj⟶α−ηin L2.

    Take any β∈Ωc2(X,ℰm∨) with d∇∨β=0, where ℰm∨ is the dual flat bundle. Parallel evaluation and Stokes give

    ∫Xev⁡(d∇uj∧β)=∫Xdev⁡(uj∧β)=0.

    On the compact support of β, the dual metric and Cauchy–Schwarz give a constant Cβ with

    |∫Xev⁡((α−η−d∇uj)∧β)|≤Cβ‖α−η−d∇uj‖L2⟶0.

    Thus ∫Xev⁡((α−η)∧β)=0 for every such β. Poincaré duality with local coefficients makes the pairing

    H2(X,ℰm)×Hc2(X,ℰm∨)⟶ℂ,([ω],[β])⟼∫Xev⁡(ω∧β)

    nondegenerate: X is oriented, has real dimension four, and has a finite compact core. Applying it to the smooth closed form α−η proves [α]=[η] in ordinary cohomology. This is the step converting the L2 limit into an equality of ordinary classes; it does not assume that the space of smooth exact forms is L2-closed.

    The spectral identification (11b) and the geometric comparison [8, §§4.2, 5.6] identify [η] with the image under κ2 of a cuspidal cohomology class. Thus

    H!2 ⊆im⁡(ℋ(2)2→H2) = Hcusp2.

    Conversely CP4 identifies each cuspidal cohomological generator with one of the explicit two-forms, and CP3 and reflection show that their restrictions to cusp stabilizers are exact. Consequently

    Hcusp2⊆Hpar2=H!2

    by CP2. These inclusions prove the equality of the three subspaces.

    Identifying the explicit period map. In each occurrence of πn−, the calculation in CP4 shows that the degree-two relative cochain is built entirely from the minimal compact type V0⊠Vn. Its fiber map is precisely the nonzero K-equivariant map

    λ−:Vn⟶Λ2𝔭ℂ∗⊗Em

    defined by AC3. Since Vn is irreducible, λ− is injective. Thus a harmonic form in this summand has homogeneous-frame coefficients λ−F(g), with a unique Vn-valued function F. Equivariance of λ− gives the equivariance law of the group lift, so F determines a half-space spinor f. Closedness of the form is equivalent to regularity of f by AC3. Its scalar coefficients belong to the cuspidal automorphic module; applying the fixed inverse of λ− on its image preserves zero constant terms, moderate growth, and central finiteness. Hence f∈𝒮n−(Γ). Reflection gives the same reconstruction for πn+.

    Conversely CP1 and AC6 send each cusp spinor to a square-integrable harmonic form, and CP4 identifies that map with this same fiber map in each cuspidal copy. The two chiralities lie in distinct Hodge-star eigenspaces, so their images have zero intersection. It follows that their sum identifies 𝒮n−⊕︎𝒮n+ with ℋ(2)2. The map from this harmonic space to ordinary cohomology is the injective cuspidal comparison κ2 on the spectral summands. The preceding interior-class argument identifies its image with Hpar2. Their composite is exactly (f−,f+)↦[Ωf−+Ωf++], proving both injectivity and surjectivity in (11).

    Finally ker⁡(res)=Hpar2=Hcusp2. Thus res factors through the quotient by Hcusp2, and the induced map has zero kernel. This proves (11c).

    Discussion

    This supplies the analogue of the global Eichler–Shimura step in the present setting. The proof uses harmonic representatives and classification instead of a separate numerical dimension comparison. At n=2 it identifies parabolic cohomology with self-dual and anti-self-dual square-integrable harmonic two-forms with trivial coefficients. The n=1 Hardy limit is outside (11): there is no coefficient system Sym−1⁡ℂ4 in this construction.

    Eisenstein Classes and the Scope of the Equality#

    Discussion

    Vanishing of boundary restriction is a cohomological condition. It does not, by itself, make an automorphic representative cuspidal. In general, interior cohomology can contain residual Eisenstein contributions. Grobner §6.3 explicitly warns about this distinction; a general theorem requiring regular highest weight cannot be applied to our family mμ1, whose highest weights lie on a wall.

    CP5 supplies the additional argument needed here: only tempered discrete series have degree-two unitary cohomology, and Wallach excludes them from the residual spectrum. Grobner §1.2 gives the automorphic decomposition H2=Hcusp2⊕︎HEis2, where the second summand is Eisenstein cohomology. His Theorem 6.1 allows nonzero non-square-integrable Eisenstein classes in ordinary H2. Applying the injection (11c) to this complementary summand shows that its boundary restriction is injective. This does not assert that the summand vanishes.

    The use of all cusp stabilizers, finite-volume arithmetic quotients, complex algebraic coefficients, and degree two is explicit in CP5. Neatness is unnecessary: the finite cusp holonomy is handled by the translation cover and transfer in CP3. The integrability bound n≥5 belongs to the dimension formula below, not to this cohomological correspondence.

    An Explicit Dimension Formula#

    Discussion

    The multiplicity formula used below is [6, Prop. 7.1]. Its derivation in §7.2 draws on Arakawa's work on [11] and [12]. Corollary CP6 translates the discrete-series multiplicities into dimensions of regular cusp spinors and parabolic cohomology using CP4 and CP5.

    Corollary CP6. Dimensions at Principal Congruence Level

    Let D/ℚ be a definite quaternion algebra with discriminant d(D), and fix a maximal order 𝒪. Define the ball realization 𝐆B by

    𝐆B(ℚ)={g∈GL2⁡(D):g∗diag⁡(1,−1)g=diag⁡(1,−1)},

    where g∗ is the quaternionic conjugate transpose. Fix D⊗ℚℝ≃ℍ, and identify the half-space real group by g↦CgC−1, transporting the rational structure. For an integer N≥3, let

    Γ(N)=C{g=(gij)∈𝐆B(ℚ):gij−δij∈N𝒪}C−1,N≥3.

    Write h=h(Γ(N)) for its number of cusps. For every integer n≥5,

    dimℂ⁡𝒮n−(Γ(N))=dimℂ⁡𝒮n+(Γ(N))=h[N3(n3−n)1440∏p|d(D)(p2+1)−1].(12)

    For the same N and n,

    dimℂ⁡Hpar2(Γ(N),En−2)=2h[N3(n3−n)1440∏p|d(D)(p2+1)−1].

    CP6 · version 3

    Uses CP4–CP5, and the external multiplicity formula [6, Prop. 7.1, §7.2].

    Proof of Corollary CP6

    The standard complex four-dimensional representation has highest weight μ1 in Grobner's compact-Cartan coordinates. Thus En−2 has highest weight (n−2)μ1, and his parameter ν is exactly n. His multiplicity formula [6, Prop. 7.1] gives the right-hand side of (12), with denominator 25325=1440, as the cuspidal multiplicity of A+(μ) for ν>4 and N≥3. By CP4 this multiplicity equals the dimension of one of our spinor spaces. It is not multiplied by dim⁡Vn=n+1: the space of equivariant maps from the minimal compact type into each discrete-series copy is one-dimensional.

    In the ball realization, the block-swap matrix WB=(0110) normalizes the entrywise principal group: it changes the Hermitian form by a sign and preserves the entrywise congruence condition. Its half-space conjugate is CWBC−1=D0, which exchanges the two discrete series, so their multiplicities agree. This removes any dependence on which sign is called plus in the source. Equation (11) gives the last assertion.

    The multiplicity formula is an external input, not a new derivation of the trace formula. Grobner [6, §7.2] writes it as formal degree times covolume minus h, using Arakawa’s unipotent contribution [12, Prop. 5.4].

    Coefficient Fields#

    Discussion

    The cohomological correspondence also gives an arithmetic constraint on Hecke eigenvalues. Its arithmetic input is a coefficient representation over a number field. This must be distinguished from the complex representation used to write the differential forms. The descent statement below uses [13, §20, especially 20.7]; the Hecke correspondence is the restriction–conjugation–transfer construction described in [14, §4.1].

    Proposition CP7. A Number-Field Model for Parabolic Cohomology

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group of rational rank one with 𝐆(ℝ)≃Sp(1,1), and let Γ⊂𝐆(ℚ) be a torsion-free congruence lattice. Let n≥2 and m=n−2. There exist a number field L⊂ℂ, an algebraic representation ρm,L:𝐆L→GL⁡(Em,L), and an isomorphism

    (Em,L,ρm,L)⊗Lℂ≃(Em,Rm),Em=Symm⁡ℂ4.(13)

    For every such choice define

    HL=ker⁡(H2(Γ,Em,L)⟶⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,Em,L)).(14)

    Then HL is finite-dimensional over L, and scalar extension induces an isomorphism

    HL⊗Lℂ≃Hpar2(Γ,Em).(15)

    For m=0 one may take L=ℚ. If m is odd, the complex representation Em of G has no real model, and consequently (13) has no model over ℚ.

    CP7 · version 2

    Uses flat coefficients, CP-D1, CP2, [13, 20.7–20.9].

    Proof of Proposition CP7

    Choose a finite extension L/ℚ splitting 𝐆. Such an extension exists: a splitting over ℚ is specified by finitely many algebraic coefficients. The split group is of type C2 and is simply connected, hence is isomorphic to Sp4 over L. Its symmetric-power representation on Symm⁡L4 has highest weight mω1, where ω1 is the highest weight of the standard four-dimensional representation. Highest-weight classification identifies its complexification with Em. Equivalently, Milne’s descent theorem [13, 20.7–20.9] descends this semisimple representation from ℂ to a splitting field. Fix one such identification in (13).

    For the quaternionic matrix group in CP6 this can be made explicit. Write D=(a,b)ℚ with generators I2=a, J2=b, IJ=−JI and a,b<0. Over L=ℚ(a) the matrices

    I⟼(a00−a),J⟼(0b10)(16)

    satisfy these relations and, together with the identity and their product, span M2(L). Thus they give a splitting D⊗L≃M2(L). Applying it to each entry of M2(D) gives the four-dimensional representation over L; take its mth symmetric power. After extension to ℂ, the two identifications of the split matrix algebra are conjugate, so this is isomorphic to the analytic block representation Rm. At m=0 the representation is trivial and already defined over ℚ.

    For finiteness and (15), use the compact core X and its boundary from CP2. They have finite CW structures, and their cellular cochains with the local system associated to Em,L are finite-dimensional L-vector spaces. These complexes compute the cohomology groups in (14), because their universal covers are contractible. Tensoring with ℂ is exact, so it commutes with their cohomology and the kernel of boundary restriction. This proves both assertions. This construction uses cellular cochains over L; de Rham forms enter only after scalar extension to ℂ.

    For the obstruction when m is odd, put J0v=diag⁡(ϵ,ϵ)v, with ϵ as in AC-C1. Directly from the quaternionic block matrices, J0R(g)=R(g)J0 and J02=−1. Its induced antilinear map Jm on Symm⁡ℂ4 satisfies Jm2=(−1)m. This symmetric power is an irreducible complex G-representation. By Schur's lemma, any invertible equivariant antilinear map is cJm for some c∈ℂ×. For odd m its square is −|c|2, never 1. A real model would supply an equivariant antilinear involution, which is impossible.

    Algebraicity of Eigenvalues#

    Corollary CP9. Hecke Eigenvalues Lie in a Number Field

    Let Γ, n≥2, L, and HL be as in CP7, and use the normalization in CP-D4. Let 0≠f∈𝒮nε(Γ), with ε∈{−,+}.

    If Tαεf=λαf, then

    det⁡(λαidHL⁡−Tα|HL)=0.(23)

    In particular λα is algebraic over ℚ.

    If {Tα:α∈A} is a commuting family of these operators and f is a simultaneous eigenvector, then

    Lf=L(λα:α∈A)(24)

    is a finite extension of L.

    CP9 · version 1

    Uses CP7, CP8, AC7.

    Proof of Corollary CP9

    Let v=[Ωf] for the minus space and v=[Ωf+] for the plus space. By the cuspidal injection AC7 and its reflected version, v≠0; by CP3 and reflection, v∈HL⊗Lℂ. Formula (20) and the trace calculation give Tαv=λαv. Thus λα is a root of the characteristic polynomial of the L-linear operator Tα on the finite-dimensional space HL. This proves (23), and algebraicity over L implies algebraicity over ℚ.

    For (24), let 𝕋L⊆EndL⁡(HL) be the unital L-algebra generated by the commuting family. It is finite-dimensional because it is an L-subspace of EndL⁡(HL). Its action on the nonzero common eigenline ℂv defines an L-algebra homomorphism χf:𝕋L→ℂ. Its image is a finite-dimensional L-algebra contained in a field. Multiplication by a nonzero element of that image is injective, hence surjective by finite-dimensionality, so the image is itself a field. It contains exactly the L-algebra generated by the eigenvalues, and therefore equals Lf. This proves [Lf:L]<∞.

    Discussion

    The algebraicity proof requires the injective period map and its Hecke equivariance; it does not require the surjectivity in CP5 or the exclusion of Eisenstein classes from interior cohomology. A Hecke-stable cohomology space over a number field is sufficient even if it contains additional classes.

    The L-structure in CP8 belongs to the sum of both chiralities. It does not by itself give an L-structure on each summand, or imply that the periods of an individual eigenform are algebraic. For odd n, CP7 even rules out a ℚ-model of the coefficient representation of this complex dimension. At n=2 the coefficients are trivial and one may work over ℚ throughout. The n=1 Hardy limit has no coefficient system En−2 in this construction.

    SH3 proves algebraic integrality for integral positive similitudes with a specified coefficient normalization. SH4 gives central counterexamples to an unrestricted integrality claim. Likewise, a scalar renormalization of each operator by an algebraic number preserves algebraicity of individual eigenvalues, but a common finite field for infinitely many operators requires control of all those normalization factors. Formula (24) applies to the explicit normalization above.
