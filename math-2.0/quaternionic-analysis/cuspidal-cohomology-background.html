    Classical Cuspidal and Parabolic Cohomology

    Throughout, use its group and spinor conventions, cuspidal condition, and complementary chirality. Thus n≥2, m=n−2, and Em=Symm⁡ℂ4 is the flat coefficient representation.

    The Condition on Group Cocycles#

    Put X=Γ\ℋ, m=n−2, Em=Symm⁡ℂ4, and ρ=Symm⁡R. Let ℰm=Γ\(ℋ×Em) be the flat bundle for the action (z,v)↦(γz,ρ(γ)v), with flat differential d∇. For each rational cusp, put Γξ=Γ∩Pξ.

    For j≥0, write Ωj(X,ℰm) for smooth bundle-valued j-forms and Ωcj(X,ℰm) for those with compact support; set both spaces to zero in degree −1. Define

    Hj(X,ℰm)=ker⁡(d∇:Ωj→Ωj+1)d∇Ωj−1,Hcj(X,ℰm)=ker⁡(d∇:Ωcj→Ωcj+1)d∇Ωcj−1.

    The inclusion of complexes induces jc:Hcj(X,ℰm)→Hj(X,ℰm).

    Definition CP-D2. Interior and Parabolic Cohomology

    Interior cohomology is the subspace

    H!j(X,ℰm):=im⁡jc⊆Hj(X,ℰm).(I)

    Parabolic cohomology is

    Hpar2(Γ,Em)=ker⁡(H2(Γ,Em)⟶⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,Em)).(6)

    CP-D2 · version 3

    Identify Hj(Γ,Em) with Hj(X,ℰm) by the de Rham comparison for the contractible universal cover ℋ. [4, §§8, 15]; [5, Thm. I.7.5, §§III.1, III.8].

    In inhomogeneous group-cochain notation, a two-cocycle c represents such a class exactly when, for every cusp, there is a one-cochain bξ:Γξ→Em such that

    c(u,v)=ρ(u)bξ(v)−bξ(uv)+bξ(u),u,v∈Γξ.(7)

    Indeed, membership in the kernel (6) says exactly that the restriction of c to each Γξ is a coboundary. Formula (7) is the inhomogeneous differential of a one-cochain.

    Discussion

    The notation H!2 denotes the image of jc in ordinary cohomology. A compactly supported closed form can be exact with a primitive that is not compactly supported, so the map jc need not be injective. Its image is a different object from its domain. CP2 identifies this image with the parabolic subspace. The companion article Parabolic Cohomology and Compact Support develops the topological comparison using the full stabilizers of geometric cusps, with a detailed proof of the relative-cohomology sequence and the cutoff construction.

    For a neat group, Γξ≃ℤ3; in general it is virtually ℤ3. In the classical surface case the cusp stabilizer is cyclic, and a degree-one cocycle becomes a coboundary there. Equation (7) is the corresponding degree-two condition. It does not ask the cocycle itself to vanish, and separate conditions on individual parabolic elements would be insufficient.

    Example. Why cyclic tests miss the condition

    Take n=2, so the coefficient representation is trivial, and let the cusp group be ℤ3. The cocycle c(u,v)=u1v2 has nonzero alternating part, so its class in H2(ℤ3,ℂ) is nonzero. Its restriction to every cyclic subgroup is nevertheless a coboundary: on the subgroup generated by a, take b(ka)=−k(k−1)a1a2/2. In fact H2(ℤ,E)=0 for every coefficient module E.

    Proposition CP2. Parabolic Cohomology Is Interior Cohomology [4, §§8, 15]; [5, Thm. I.7.5, §§III.1, III.8]; [7, Prop. 4.2.17, Cor. 4.2.18]

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group of rational rank one with 𝐆(ℝ)≃Sp(1,1), and let Γ⊂𝐆(ℚ) be a torsion-free congruence lattice. Put X=Γ\ℋ and Em=Symm⁡ℂ4, with m≥0. Then

    Hpar2(Γ,Em)=H!2(X,ℰm):=im⁡(Hc2(X,ℰm)→H2(X,ℰm)).(8)

    CP2 · version 2

    Uses CP-D2, the compact core of a finite-volume real hyperbolic manifold, and the relative cohomology exact sequence.

    Proof of Proposition CP2[5, Thm. I.7.5, §§III.1, III.8]; [4, §§8, 15]

    Truncate the finitely many cusps to obtain a compact manifold X with boundary ∐[ξ]Yξ. Each Yξ is a compact flat three-manifold with contractible universal cover and fundamental group Γξ. The cusp collars identify the interior of X with X; the inclusion of a compact core into X is a homotopy equivalence. Extend ℰm over X using the same monodromy representation.

    The relative exact sequence contains

    H1(∂X,ℰm)→δH2(X,∂X;ℰm)→jH2(X,ℰm)→resH2(∂X,ℰm).

    Compact-support/relative comparison identifies the second term with Hc2(X,ℰm); homotopy invariance identifies the third with H2(X,ℰm). Under these identifications j is jc. The aspherical boundary components identify the last term with ⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,Em), and their inclusions identify res with the restriction map in (6). Exactness gives

    Hpar2=ker⁡(res)=im⁡(jc)=H!2.

    The same sequence gives ker⁡(jc)=im⁡(δ), specifying exactly where compactly supported classes can disappear in ordinary cohomology.

    The Cohomological and Spectral Notation#

    Write 𝔤=𝔨⊕︎𝔭 for the Cartan decomposition determined by K.

    Definition CP-D3. Relative Lie Algebra Cohomology

    For a (𝔤ℂ,K)-module V, its relative cochain complex with coefficients Em is

    Cj(𝔤,K;V⊗Em)=HomK⁡(Λj𝔭ℂ,V⊗Em).

    The Lie algebra acts diagonally on V⊗Em. Its differential, evaluated on T0,…,Tj∈𝔭ℂ, is

    (dω)(T0,…,Tj)=∑i=0j(−1)iTi⋅ω(T0,…,Tˆi,…,Tj).

    The hat denotes omission of an argument. Denote the cohomology of this complex by Hj(𝔤,K;V⊗Em).

    CP-D3 · version 1

    The displayed differential is the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential for the symmetric pair: [𝔭,𝔭]⊆𝔨 makes the projected bracket terms zero.

    Let 𝒜cusp(Γ\G) consist of smooth complex functions on Γ\G that have moderate growth, are 𝒵-finite and right K-finite, and have vanishing constant terms at every rational cusp. Denote by κj the comparison map induced by their associated homogeneous-frame differential forms:

    κj:Hj(𝔤,K;𝒜cusp⊗Em)⟶Hj(X,ℰm).

    Definition CP-D3-CUSP. Cuspidal Cohomology

    Define the cuspidal subspace of ordinary cohomology by

    Hcuspj(Γ,Em):=im⁡(κj).

    CP-D3-CUSP · version 1

    Spectral Notation#

    Let Ldisc2(Γ\G) be the closed Hilbert sum of irreducible subrepresentations of the right regular representation on L2(Γ\G). Let Lcusp2 be the closure of 𝒜cusp in L2, and put

    Lres2=Ldisc2∩(Lcusp2)⟂.

    For a unitary representation V, write VK∞ for its smooth vectors that are K-finite. For an irreducible π, abbreviate πK=πK∞. For π in the discrete spectrum, MΓ(π)=HomG⁡(π,Ldisc2) is its multiplicity space, where HomG⁡ denotes bounded complex-linear intertwining maps.

    Discussion

    The comparison map κj is injective under the arithmetic hypotheses used here, by the external theorem cited in AC7. Defining its image does not prove that injection. Likewise, interior and cuspidal cohomology are different definitions; their equality in this particular degree is the content of CP5.

    Cohomology of the Discrete Series#

    Lemma CP4. One Cohomology Generator per Discrete-Series Copy [6, Thm. 7.1]

    Let n≥2, m=n−2, and let πn± be the two reflected discrete series. Then

    dim⁡Cj(𝔤,K;πn±⊗Em)={1j=2,0j≠2.(10)

    The unique cochain in degree two is, up to a nonzero scalar, the fiber map defining Ω±. Thus it generates relative Lie algebra cohomology in degree two.

    CP4 · version 1

    Uses CP-D3, the discrete-series compact decomposition, CP-C1.

    Proof of Lemma CP4[6, Thm. 7.1]

    Write Va⊠Vb for a representation of K=Sp(1)×Sp(1). The relevant decompositions are

    πn−|K=⨁r≥0Vr⊠Vr+n,Em|K=⨁a=0mVa⊠Vm−a,
    Λ0𝔭ℂ≃Λ4𝔭ℂ≃V0⊠V0,Λ1𝔭ℂ≃Λ3𝔭ℂ≃V1⊠V1,Λ2𝔭ℂ=(V2⊠V0)⊕︎(V0⊠V2).

    In the right tensor factor Vr+n⊗Vm−a, the smallest degree is r+a+2. Consequently degrees zero, one, three, and four have no cochains, and the V2⊠V0 part in degree two contributes none. The V0⊠V2 part forces r=a=0; Vn⊗Vn−2 contains V2 once. This proves (10) by the Clebsch–Gordan formula. The explicit fiber map is nonzero and equivariant by AC3, so it spans that line. There are no incoming or outgoing cochains. Reflection proves the other case.

    The Correspondence#

    Theorem CP5. The Degree-Two Cuspidal and Parabolic Correspondence [6, Thm. 7.1]; [11, Thm. 4.3]; [3, Prop. 4.4(i), Prop. 5.4]; [9, App. A, pp. 29–30]

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group with 𝐆(ℝ)≃Sp(1,1) and ℚ-rank one, and let Γ be a torsion-free congruence lattice in 𝐆. Put X=Γ\ℋ. For every integer n≥2, take the complex algebraic coefficient representation Em=Symm⁡ℂ4, m=n−2, with its flat bundle and the transported positive homogeneous metric hℋ of CP-H1. Then the explicit period construction gives a complex-linear isomorphism

    𝒮n−(Γ)⊕︎𝒮n+(Γ) → ∼  Hpar2(Γ,Em),(11)
    (f−,f+)⟼[Ωf−+Ωf++].

    Inside ordinary complex cohomology,

    Hpar2(Γ,Em)=H!2(X,ℰm)=Hcusp2(Γ,Em).

    With the spectral notation above,

    H2(𝔤,K;(Lres2(Γ\G))K∞⊗Em)=0.(11a)

    The restriction map in (6) induces an injection

    H2(Γ,Em)/Hcusp2(Γ,Em) ↪ ⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,Em).(11c)

    CP5 · version 4

    Uses Relative cohomology, cuspidal cohomology, spectral notation, CP2–CP4, AC6–AC7; the finite-coefficient harmonic spectral theorem of Borel–Garland/Borel–Casselman; [6, Thm. 7.1]; [11, Thm. 4.3]. Precise spectral references and inspection limits are in [3].

    Proof of Theorem CP5[9, App. A]; [6, Thm. 7.1]; [11, Thm. 4.3]

    The residual spectrum cannot contribute in degree two. The finite-coefficient harmonic spectral theorem identifies the space ℋ(2)2(X,ℰm) with

    ⨁π⊂Ldisc2(Γ\G)MΓ(π)⊗H2(𝔤,K;πK⊗Em),(11b)

    where MΓ(π) is the multiplicity space. The sum contributing to this degree is finite. This is the Borel–Garland result recorded in Borel–Casselman, Proposition 4.4(i), with its finite-coefficient scope explicitly recalled in Nair–Rai, Appendix A. It is a theorem about square-integrable harmonic forms, not an identification of arbitrary ordinary cohomology with L2 cohomology. No continuous-spectrum harmonic term is omitted by (11b).

    Grobner's Theorem 7.1 says that only the two cohomological discrete series contribute in degree two. CP4 identifies them with πn±. The other possible unitary cohomological representations have cohomology only in degrees 0,4 or 1,3. In particular this remains true for the trivial coefficient system n=2.

    Every discrete series is tempered. Wallach's Theorem 4.3 says that a tempered (𝔤,K)-module mapping to square-integrable automorphic forms has cuspidal image. It therefore places every occurrence of πn± in Lcusp2, not in the residual spectrum. This proves (11a) and identifies (11b) with cuspidal degree-two cohomology. This spectral exclusion is independent of the direct constant-term argument in CP1.

    Interior classes have the required harmonic representatives. Let [α]∈H!2, choosing α∈Ωc2(X,ℰm) with d∇α=0. Give X the complete half-space metric and the bundle its metric hℋ from CP-H1. Let dj be the closed L2 extension of d∇ in degree j, with Hilbert adjoint dj∗. The Hilbert-complex decomposition is

    L2Ω2=ℋ(2)2 ⊕︎ˆ im⁡d1 ⊕︎ˆ im⁡d2∗,ℋ(2)2=ker⁡d2∩ker⁡d1∗.

    Since d2α=0, α is orthogonal to im⁡d2∗, so its last component vanishes. Its harmonic projection η is smooth by elliptic regularity. Completeness permits approximation in the graph norm by compactly supported smooth forms: scalar cutoffs with gradients tending to zero commute with d∇ up to exterior multiplication by their differential. Consequently there exist uj∈Ωc1(X,ℰm) with

    d∇uj⟶α−ηin L2.

    Take any β∈Ωc2(X,ℰm∨) with d∇∨β=0, where ℰm∨ is the dual flat bundle. Parallel evaluation and Stokes give

    ∫Xev⁡(d∇uj∧β)=∫Xdev⁡(uj∧β)=0.

    On the compact support of β, the dual metric and Cauchy–Schwarz give a constant Cβ with

    |∫Xev⁡((α−η−d∇uj)∧β)|≤Cβ‖α−η−d∇uj‖L2⟶0.

    Thus ∫Xev⁡((α−η)∧β)=0 for every such β. Poincaré duality with local coefficients makes the pairing

    H2(X,ℰm)×Hc2(X,ℰm∨)⟶ℂ,([ω],[β])⟼∫Xev⁡(ω∧β)

    nondegenerate: X is oriented, has real dimension four, and has a finite compact core. Applying it to the smooth closed form α−η proves [α]=[η] in ordinary cohomology. This is the step converting the L2 limit into an equality of ordinary classes; it does not assume that the space of smooth exact forms is L2-closed.

    The spectral identification (11b) and its geometric comparison identify [η] with the image under κ2 of a cuspidal cohomology class. Thus

    H!2 ⊆im⁡(ℋ(2)2→H2) = Hcusp2.

    Conversely CP4 identifies each cuspidal cohomological generator with one of the explicit two-forms, and CP3 and reflection show that their restrictions to cusp stabilizers are exact. Consequently

    Hcusp2⊆Hpar2=H!2

    by CP2. These inclusions prove the equality of the three subspaces.

    Identifying the explicit period map. In each occurrence of πn−, the calculation in CP4 shows that the degree-two relative cochain is built entirely from the minimal compact type V0⊠Vn. Its fiber map is precisely the nonzero K-equivariant map

    λ−:Vn⟶Λ2𝔭ℂ∗⊗Em

    defined by AC3. Since Vn is irreducible, λ− is injective. Thus a harmonic form in this summand has homogeneous-frame coefficients λ−F(g), with a unique Vn-valued function F. Equivariance of λ− gives the equivariance law of the group lift, so F determines a half-space spinor f. Closedness of the form is equivalent to regularity of f by AC3. Its scalar coefficients belong to the cuspidal automorphic module; applying the fixed inverse of λ− on its image preserves zero constant terms, moderate growth, and central finiteness. Hence f∈𝒮n−(Γ). Reflection gives the same reconstruction for πn+.

    Conversely CP1 and AC6 send each cusp spinor to a square-integrable harmonic form, and CP4 identifies that map with this same fiber map in each cuspidal copy. The two chiralities lie in distinct Hodge-star eigenspaces, so their images have zero intersection. It follows that their sum identifies 𝒮n−⊕︎𝒮n+ with ℋ(2)2. The map from this harmonic space to ordinary cohomology is the injective cuspidal comparison κ2 on the spectral summands. The preceding interior-class argument identifies its image with Hpar2. Their composite is exactly (f−,f+)↦[Ωf−+Ωf++], proving both injectivity and surjectivity in (11).

    Finally ker⁡(res)=Hpar2=Hcusp2. Thus res factors through the quotient by Hcusp2, and the induced map has zero kernel. This proves (11c).

    Discussion

    This supplies the analogue of the global Eichler–Shimura step in the present setting. The proof uses harmonic representatives and classification instead of a separate numerical dimension comparison. At n=2 it identifies parabolic cohomology with self-dual and anti-self-dual square-integrable harmonic two-forms with trivial coefficients. The n=1 Hardy limit is outside (11): there is no coefficient system Sym−1⁡ℂ4 in this construction.

    Eisenstein Classes and the Scope of the Equality#

    Discussion

    Vanishing of boundary restriction is a cohomological condition. It does not, by itself, make an automorphic representative cuspidal. In general, interior cohomology can contain residual Eisenstein contributions. Grobner §6.3 explicitly warns about this distinction; a general theorem requiring regular highest weight cannot be applied to our family mμ1, whose highest weights lie on a wall.

    CP5 supplies the additional argument needed here: only tempered discrete series have degree-two unitary cohomology, and Wallach excludes them from the residual spectrum. Grobner §1.2 gives the automorphic decomposition H2=Hcusp2⊕︎HEis2, where the second summand is Eisenstein cohomology. His Theorem 6.1 allows nonzero non-square-integrable Eisenstein classes in ordinary H2. Applying the injection (11c) to this complementary summand shows that its boundary restriction is injective. This does not assert that the summand vanishes.

    The use of all cusp stabilizers, finite-volume arithmetic quotients, complex algebraic coefficients, and degree two is explicit in CP5. Neatness is unnecessary: the finite cusp holonomy is handled by the translation cover and transfer in CP3. The integrability bound n≥5 belongs to the dimension formula below, not to this cohomological correspondence.

    An Explicit Dimension Formula#

    Discussion

    The multiplicity formula used below is [6, Prop. 7.1]. Its derivation in §7.2 draws on Arakawa's work on [1] and [2]. Corollary CP6 translates the discrete-series multiplicities into dimensions of regular cusp spinors and parabolic cohomology using CP4 and CP5.

    Corollary CP6. Dimensions at Principal Congruence Level [6, Prop. 7.1, §7.2]; [1, Thm. 2]; [2, Prop. 5.4]

    Let D/ℚ be a definite quaternion algebra with discriminant d(D), and fix a maximal order 𝒪. Define the ball realization 𝐆B by

    𝐆B(ℚ)={g∈GL2⁡(D):g∗diag⁡(1,−1)g=diag⁡(1,−1)},

    where g∗ is the quaternionic conjugate transpose. Fix D⊗ℚℝ≃ℍ, and identify the half-space real group by g↦CgC−1, transporting the rational structure. For an integer N≥3, let

    Γ(N)=C{g=(gij)∈𝐆B(ℚ):gij−δij∈N𝒪}C−1,N≥3.

    Write h=h(Γ(N)) for its number of cusps. For every integer n≥5,

    dimℂ⁡𝒮n−(Γ(N))=dimℂ⁡𝒮n+(Γ(N))=h[N3(n3−n)1440∏p|d(D)(p2+1)−1].(12)

    For the same N and n,

    dimℂ⁡Hpar2(Γ(N),En−2)=2h[N3(n3−n)1440∏p|d(D)(p2+1)−1].

    CP6 · version 3

    Uses CP4–CP5, and the external multiplicity formula [6, Prop. 7.1, §7.2].

    Proof of Corollary CP6[6, Prop. 7.1]

    The standard complex four-dimensional representation has highest weight μ1 in Grobner's compact-Cartan coordinates. Thus En−2 has highest weight (n−2)μ1, and his parameter ν is exactly n. His Proposition 7.1 gives the right-hand side of (12), with denominator 25325=1440, as the cuspidal multiplicity of A+(μ) for ν>4 and N≥3. By CP4 this multiplicity equals the dimension of one of our spinor spaces. It is not multiplied by dim⁡Vn=n+1: the space of equivariant maps from the minimal compact type into each discrete-series copy is one-dimensional.

    In the ball realization, the block-swap matrix WB=(0110) normalizes the entrywise principal group: it changes the Hermitian form by a sign and preserves the entrywise congruence condition. Its half-space conjugate is CWBC−1=D0, which exchanges the two discrete series, so their multiplicities agree. This removes any dependence on which sign is called plus in the source. Equation (11) gives the last assertion.

    The multiplicity formula is an external input, not a new derivation of the trace formula. Grobner's §7.2 writes it as formal degree times covolume minus h; the unipotent contribution uses Arakawa (1984), Proposition 5.4.

    Coefficient Fields#

    Discussion

    The cohomological correspondence also gives an arithmetic constraint on Hecke eigenvalues. Its arithmetic input is a coefficient representation over a number field. This must be distinguished from the complex representation used to write the differential forms. The descent statement below uses [8, §20, especially 20.7]; the Hecke correspondence is the restriction–conjugation–transfer construction described in [10, §4.1].

    Proposition CP7. A Number-Field Model for Parabolic Cohomology [8, 20.7–20.9]

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group of rational rank one with 𝐆(ℝ)≃Sp(1,1), and let Γ⊂𝐆(ℚ) be a torsion-free congruence lattice. Let n≥2 and m=n−2. There exist a number field L⊂ℂ, an algebraic representation ρm,L:𝐆L→GL⁡(Em,L), and an isomorphism

    (Em,L,ρm,L)⊗Lℂ≃(Em,Rm),Em=Symm⁡ℂ4.(13)

    For every such choice define

    HL=ker⁡(H2(Γ,Em,L)⟶⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,Em,L)).(14)

    Then HL is finite-dimensional over L, and scalar extension induces an isomorphism

    HL⊗Lℂ≃Hpar2(Γ,Em).(15)

    For m=0 one may take L=ℚ. If m is odd, the complex representation Em of G has no real model, and consequently (13) has no model over ℚ.

    CP7 · version 2

    Uses flat coefficients, CP-D1, CP2, [8, 20.7–20.9].

    Proof of Proposition CP7[8, 20.7–20.9]

    Choose a finite extension L/ℚ splitting 𝐆. Such an extension exists: a splitting over ℚ is specified by finitely many algebraic coefficients. The split group is of type C2 and is simply connected, hence is isomorphic to Sp4 over L. Its symmetric-power representation on Symm⁡L4 has highest weight mω1, where ω1 is the highest weight of the standard four-dimensional representation. Highest-weight classification identifies its complexification with Em. Equivalently, Milne 20.7 descends this semisimple representation from ℂ to a splitting field. Fix one such identification in (13).

    For the quaternionic matrix group in CP6 this can be made explicit. Write D=(a,b)ℚ with generators I2=a, J2=b, IJ=−JI and a,b<0. Over L=ℚ(a) the matrices

    I⟼(a00−a),J⟼(0b10)(16)

    satisfy these relations and, together with the identity and their product, span M2(L). Thus they give a splitting D⊗L≃M2(L). Applying it to each entry of M2(D) gives the four-dimensional representation over L; take its mth symmetric power. After extension to ℂ, the two identifications of the split matrix algebra are conjugate, so this is isomorphic to the analytic block representation Rm. At m=0 the representation is trivial and already defined over ℚ.

    For finiteness and (15), use the compact core X and its boundary from CP2. They have finite CW structures, and their cellular cochains with the local system associated to Em,L are finite-dimensional L-vector spaces. These complexes compute the cohomology groups in (14), because their universal covers are contractible. Tensoring with ℂ is exact, so it commutes with their cohomology and the kernel of boundary restriction. This proves both assertions. This construction uses cellular cochains over L; de Rham forms enter only after scalar extension to ℂ.

    For the obstruction when m is odd, put J0v=diag⁡(ϵ,ϵ)v, with ϵ as in AC-C1. Directly from the quaternionic block matrices, J0R(g)=R(g)J0 and J02=−1. Its induced antilinear map Jm on Symm⁡ℂ4 satisfies Jm2=(−1)m. This symmetric power is an irreducible complex G-representation. By Schur's lemma, any invertible equivariant antilinear map is cJm for some c∈ℂ×. For odd m its square is −|c|2, never 1. A real model would supply an equivariant antilinear involution, which is impossible.
