    Research

    What survives of the modular-curve construction on a quaternionic quotient, including a real family of abelian varieties and the obstruction to a Jacobian realization.

    Automorphic cohomology and geometric realization

    Discussion

    For the quaternionic ball, flat coefficients, closed differential forms, periods, and Hecke correspondences survive. Even a real-analytic family of polarized abelian varieties can be constructed, as GE3 shows. The obstruction is to interpreting this homogeneous construction as a holomorphic algebraic family over the ball quotient. The arithmetic realization presently available through LG3 instead uses the different geometry of a Siegel modular threefold.

    The quaternionic differential-form construction#

    Discussion

    Let M=Γ\B, where B=Sp(1,1)/(Sp(1)×Sp(1)), and retain the hypotheses of AC7 for cuspidal injectivity. For n≥2, put m=n−2 and Em=Symm⁡ℂ4. The explicit construction gives

    f⟼Ωf∈Ω2(M,ℰm),d∇Ωf=0,

    and hence a Hecke-compatible class in Hpar2(Γ,Em).

    The decomposition n=(n−2)+2 has a precise local meaning. For the relevant rank-two spinor bundle S−,

    Symn⁡S−⟶Symn−2⁡S−⊗Sym2⁡S−≃Symn−2⁡S−⊗Λ−2T∗Mℂ.

    The moving inclusion S−↪ℰ1 then gives the flat-coefficient two-form. Here Λ−2 denotes the anti-self-dual two-forms for the stated orientation. The maps, connection derivatives, and closedness calculation are AC2–AC5.

    This is a decomposition into a coefficient tensor and a real differential two-form. It does not itself identify a holomorphic Hodge bundle or a Kodaira–Spencer isomorphism. In particular, the Hodge star on two-forms in real dimension four has square +1: its self-dual/anti-self-dual splitting is not the complex structure supplied by the Hodge star on one-forms of a Riemann surface.

    Proposition GE2. No invariant almost complex structure on the ball

    For G=Sp(1,1), K=Sp(1)×Sp(1), and B=G/K, one has

    EndK⁡(T0B)=ℝid.

    Consequently B has no G-invariant almost complex structure.

    GE2 · version 1

    Uses Quaternion multiplication and the isotropy action of the ball conventions.

    Proof of Proposition GE2

    Identify T0B with ℍ, where (a,b)∈K acts by v↦avb−1. A real-linear map commuting with all left multiplications by unit quaternions commutes with left multiplication by all quaternions. It is therefore right multiplication by c=A(1). Commuting also with right multiplication by every unit quaternion forces c to be real. An invariant almost complex structure would give such an endomorphism with square −id, impossible for a real scalar.

    Discussion

    This is the homogeneous obstruction: the quaternionic symmetric space cannot be the Hermitian symmetric domain of a Shimura datum. It does not claim that every individual quotient admits no conceivable unrelated complex structure. It does rule out the direct analogue with the natural transitive group action and homogeneous Hodge bundles.

    A real family of polarized abelian fourfolds#

    Proposition GE3. A torus family with polarized fibres and Hecke isogenies cf. [1, Ch. I, Thm. 2.8]

    Let D/ℚ be a definite quaternion algebra, 𝒪 an order in D, Vℚ=D2, Λ=𝒪2, and

    h(v,w)=v1w1−v2w2.

    Let G=Sp(hℝ)≃Sp(1,1), let K=Sp(1)×Sp(1) be its block-diagonal compact subgroup, and put B=G/K. Let Γ be a torsion-free finite-index subgroup of the rational isometries of h preserving Λ. Choose a nonzero u∈𝒪 of reduced trace zero, put u2=−d with d∈ℚ>0, and write 𝐢=u/d in D⊗ℝ≃ℍ. Define

    ψ(v,w)=TrdD/ℚ⁡(uh(v,w)),J0(v1,v2)=(−v1𝐢,v2𝐢).

    Extend ψ to Vℝ by real bilinearity. For x=gK∈B, set Jx=gJ0g−1. Then:

    1. ψ is a nondegenerate alternating rational form, integral on Λ, and Jx is well-defined.
    2. The complex torus Ax=(Vℝ,Jx)/Λ is a polarized abelian variety of complex dimension four, with Riemann form ψ.
    3. The quotient
      𝒜=Γ\(B×(Vℝ/Λ))⟶Γ\B,γ(x,[v])=(γx,[γv]),
      is a real-analytic family with fibres Ax and locally constant first homology Λ.
    4. If α∈M2(𝒪) is invertible over D and satisfies h(αv,αw)=νh(v,w) with ν∈ℚ>0, then [v]↦[αv] is a holomorphic isogeny Ax→Aαx, and its pullback multiplies the Riemann form by ν.

    GE3 · version 1

    Uses GE2, positive-similitude group, the Riemann bilinear criterion for a complex torus to be an abelian variety.

    Proof of Proposition GE3cf. [1, Ch. I, Thm. 2.8]

    For an order in a quaternion algebra, reduced traces and norms are integers and conjugation preserves the order. Thus ψ(Λ,Λ)⊂ℤ. Since u‾=−u and h(w,v)=h(v,w), invariance of reduced trace under conjugation and cyclic permutation gives ψ(w,v)=−ψ(v,w).

    The operator J0 squares to −1 and commutes with the block-diagonal compact group K, proving well-definedness of Jx. Direct multiplication gives

    ψ(J0v,J0w)=ψ(v,w),ψ(v,J0v)=2d(|v1|2+|v2|2)>0(v≠0).

    For the first identity use h(J0v,J0w)=−𝐢h(v,w)𝐢 and cyclicity of trace. These identities also prove nondegeneracy of ψ. Because g preserves h, it preserves ψ, so the same compatibility and positivity hold for every Jx. The Riemann bilinear criterion makes each complex torus projective, of dimension dimℝ⁡Vℝ/2=4.

    The formula Jγx=γJxγ−1 makes the fibre complex structures and polarizations descend to the smooth torus bundle. Analytic local sections of G→G/K show real-analytic dependence on x. Finally α/ν∈G, so Jαx=αJxα−1. Since αΛ⊂Λ has finite index, the induced complex-linear map is an isogeny. Its polarization formula is ψ(αv,αw)=νψ(v,w).

    Discussion

    GE3 depends on the chosen lattice and imaginary quaternion u. It supplies abelian varieties as fibres and isogenies above the usual Hecke correspondences. It is not a universal algebraic family over a number field: the base is a real manifold, and the construction gives a smooth, varying complex structure only in the vertical directions. No arithmetic model of this family or Galois action on the base cohomology follows from it.

    Its rational first-homology local system has rank eight. After complexification, D⊗ℂ≃M2(ℂ) splits D2⊗ℂ into two standard four-dimensional summands. Thus the complex coefficient representation used for spinors is closely related to this torus bundle, but it must not be called a rational rank-four first-cohomology bundle without additional descent data.
