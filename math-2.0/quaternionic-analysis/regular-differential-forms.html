    Expository

    Closed mixed-regular pairings, conformal pullbacks, and the hypotheses needed for automorphic quotient questions.

    Regular functions and differential forms

    Discussion

    Regular functions produce closed three-forms through a left/right pairing. The local identities and their conformal factors can be proved explicitly. A separate linear two-form construction gives flat coefficients and injective cohomology maps; the bilinear pairings below have their own quotient questions.

    A closed pairing of regular functions#

    Use q=t+ix+jy+kz and dv=dt∧dx∧dy∧dz. The Euclidean Hodge star acts on the real differential-form part, leaving quaternion coefficients in place. Applying the Euclidean Hodge star to each coordinate one-form gives

    Ω=∗dq=dx∧dy∧dz−idt∧dy∧dz+jdt∧dx∧dz−kdt∧dx∧dy.

    Products of quaternion-valued forms use exterior multiplication and the displayed coefficient order. For a quaternion-valued row g, write g∗(q)=g(q‾),t for its reflected conjugate-transpose column. In contrast, g(q),t involves no argument reflection. For a constant matrix, a superscript ∗ always means ordinary conjugate transpose.

    Theorem DF1. The mixed regular closed three-form

    For C1 quaternion-valued scalar functions u,f,

    d(uΩf)=((Dru)f+u(Dlf))dv,Dru=ut+uxi+uyj+uzk.

    If f,g are left Fueter-regular on U, then g∗ is right regular on U‾, and g∗Ωf is closed on U∩U‾. The same identity and conclusion hold entrywise for rows f,g, producing a matrix of three-forms.

    DF1 · version 1

    Uses DF-D1.

    Proof of Theorem DF1

    The scalar identity is [2, §4, eq. (4.1)]. Expand the exterior products in the specified orientation. The four terms of du∧Ω give (Dru)dv, while Ω∧df=−(Dlf)dv. Since deg⁡Ω=3, the Leibniz rule supplies another minus sign in d(uΩf), proving the formula with a plus between the two derivatives.

    For g∗(t,x,y,z)=g(t,−x,−y,−z), differentiate the reflection. Conjugating Dlg reverses quaternion multiplication and changes i,j,k to their negatives. The result is exactly Drg∗=(Dlg)∗, hence zero. Apply the identity with u=g∗, entry by entry for rows.

    Proposition DF2. Nonzero pairs and exactness on the ball

    If f,g are nonzero left-regular rows on the connected ball B, the matrix-valued form g∗Ωf is not identically zero. Nevertheless every component is exact on B.

    DF2 · version 1

    Uses DF1, harmonic unique continuation and the Poincaré lemma.

    Proof of Proposition DF2

    Choose a nonzero component of g∗. It is nonzero on some open set. If the matrix form vanished identically, its dx∧dy∧dz coefficient would force that component multiplied by each component of f to be zero there. Quaternions have no zero divisors, so f vanishes on the open set. Regular functions are harmonic by DlDl=Δ, hence real analytic; unique continuation would force f=0 on the ball, a contradiction. Exactness follows from DF1 and the Poincaré lemma [1, Cor. 4.1.1], applied to each real component on the ball. Nonvanishing as a differential form is therefore distinct from nonvanishing of a cohomology class.

    Pullback under ball automorphisms#

    Here γ=(abcd)∈Sp(1,1) itself acts by q′=γq=(aq+b)(cq+d)−1. Put

    J=cq+d,r=|J|,A=(a‾+qb‾)−1,s=1−|q|2.

    This differs from the inverse-element convention used to define a left action on functions; the pullback below is always along q↦γq.

    Proposition DF3. The one-, two-, and three-form pullbacks

    One has s(q′)=s(q)/r2, |A|=r−1, and

    γ∗dq=AdqJ−1,
    γ∗(dq‾∧dq)=r−2J‾−1(dq‾∧dq)J−1,
    γ∗Ω=r−4AΩJ−1.

    DF3 · version 1

    Uses DF-D1, DF-D2.

    Proof of Proposition DF3

    The relations defining Sp(1,1) give |cq+d|2−|aq+b|2=1−|q|2. They prove the formula for s(q′). Differentiation in the correct quaternion order gives

    dq′=(a−q′c)dqJ−1.

    Using the same group relations, a−q′c=(a‾+qb‾)−1=A and |a‾+qb‾|=|J|, yielding the first pullback and the norm of A. Conjugate it to get dq‾′=J‾−1dq‾A‾. The middle product A‾A=r−2 proves the second formula. The derivative is an orientation-preserving similarity of length factor r−2. For such a similarity in dimension four, pullback of the Hodge star on a one-form is its Hodge star after pullback multiplied by the square of the length factor. Apply this to dq to get the third formula. Left and right constant quaternion multiplication commute with the Hodge star on the differential-form part.

    Conditional automorphic pairings#

    Proposition DF4. Covariance with the scalar weights accounted for

    Fix n≥1, put C(u)=Sn−1(ι(u))t, and let f,g be quaternionic rows of length n. For an element γ suppose

    f(γq)=r2Jf(q)C(J),g∗(γq)=r2C(a+bq‾)∗g∗(q)A−1.

    Then the matrix-valued forms

    η=s2g∗dqf,ω=g∗Ωf

    satisfy

    γ∗η=C(a+bq‾)∗ηC(J),γ∗ω=C(a+bq‾)∗ωC(J).

    Separately, if both f and g obey the unreflected row law h(γq)=r2Jh(q)C(J), then

    θ=sg‾,t(dq‾∧dq)f,γ∗θ=C(J)∗θC(J).

    If f,g are regular, ω is closed.

    DF4 · version 1

    Uses DF3, symmetric-power convention.

    Proof of Proposition DF4

    Insert the two assumed row laws and DF3. The raw product g∗dqf picks up the scalar r4: the adjacent A−1A and J−1J cancel. Multiplication by s(q′)2=s(q)2/r4 removes it. The raw three-form has the additional r−4 from DF3 and needs no scalar correction. The remaining matrix factors have exactly the displayed order.

    For the second assertion, conjugate transpose of the unreflected law gives g(γq),t=r2C(J)∗g‾(q)tJ‾. DF3 then cancels J‾J‾−1 and J−1J, leaving the scalar r2. The factor s(q′)=s(q)/r2 cancels it. Closedness of ω is DF1. These computations are conditional on the stated transformation laws; they do not construct automorphic functions or a flat coefficient system.

    The proposition asserts closedness for ω; the covariance identities for η and θ do not establish their closedness.

    Proposition DF5. Reflection changes the subgroup

    Let W=(0110), γ†=WγW and Γ†=WΓW. Then

    γ†q=γq‾.

    If g obeys the unreflected row law for Γ†, its g∗ obeys the reflected law of DF4 for Γ. In particular a same-subgroup pairing is available under the additional hypothesis Γ†=Γ. Otherwise one may pair the two indicated subgroups or restrict both to their intersection.

    DF5 · version 1

    Uses DF-D2, DF4.

    Proof of Proposition DF5

    The block relations in γ∗diag⁡(1,−1)γ=diag⁡(1,−1) and its inverse imply

    (dq+c)(bq+a)−1=(qc‾+d‾)−1(qa‾+b‾),

    which is the asserted reflected fractional transformation. Also W normalizes the group because it negates the preserved diagonal form.

    For γ∈Γ apply the automorphy law for g at q‾ with element γ†. Its denominator is bq‾+a, of norm r. Conjugate and transpose the resulting row identity. The rightmost scalar becomes a+bq‾=a‾+qb‾=A−1, and the leftmost matrix becomes C(a+bq‾)∗. The result is precisely the assumed reflected law in DF4. This establishes both the formula and the subgroup qualification.

    Passing from the ball to a quotient#

    Proposition DF6. Full-ball Hilbert vectors are not automorphic sections

    Let n≥2, and let Γ⊂Sp(1,1) be an infinite discrete subgroup. The full-ball Hilbert space ℋn has no nonzero Γ-fixed vectors.

    DF6 · version 1

    Uses The discrete-series norm and action.

    Proof of Proposition DF6

    A fixed vector has a Γ-invariant norm density ‖h(q)‖2sn−2dv by the pointwise change-of-variables calculation in DS2. If h≠0, choose a small open set on which the norm integral is positive, away from nontrivial stabilizers of the effective action. Proper discontinuity gives a smaller open set with pairwise disjoint translates indexed by the quotient of Γ by its finite ineffective center. One can choose this set where h≠0 since that is an open set. Every translate has the same positive integral. There are infinitely many translates, contradicting the finite full-ball norm. Finite stabilizers do not affect the argument.

    Discussion

    A quotient construction needs spaces of sections on Γ\B, automorphic realizations on Γ\G, or appropriate distribution vectors. DF6 excludes identifying these with ordinary fixed vectors in the full-ball Hilbert space.

    DF1–DF5 supply local identities and conditional covariance for the bilinear pairings. Their further period theory still requires a specified flat coefficient bundle and a nonzero quotient class. The separate linear construction AC3–AC7 now supplies those steps for a different two-form associated to a single regular automorphic spinor. A varying transition matrix does not itself define a flat local system, and componentwise closedness in one chart does not settle those questions. This remains a research direction, linked to period relations.

    For the underlying left/right quaternionic analysis see [2]. The representation norm used here is developed in the discrete-series article.
