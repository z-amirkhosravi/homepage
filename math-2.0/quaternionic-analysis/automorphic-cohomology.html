    A Two-Form Construction for Regular Automorphic Spinors on the Quaternionic Ball

    A regular automorphic spinor on the quaternionic ball determines a closed two-form with values in a finite-dimensional flat bundle. On a compact quotient, a positive pairing proves that a nonzero spinor gives a nonzero cohomology class. At the lowest weight, the construction is simply the correspondence with anti-self-dual harmonic two-forms.

    The classical model is [8]: a holomorphic automorphic form of weight k gives the closed one-form f(z)(z,1)⊙(k−2)dz with coefficients in Symk−2⁡ℂ2. Here the base has real dimension four, the differential form has degree two, and the coefficient representation uses ℂ4. The formulas below prove this quaternionic construction directly.

    The definitions on this page use ball coordinates. For cusp calculations, the half-space formulation gives the group, lift and constant terms directly; Proposition PC.2 states the exact coordinate and coefficient conversion.

    Regular automorphic spinors#

    Let B={q=t+ix+jy+kz:|q|<1}, s=1−|q|2, and

    Q(q)=ι(q)=(t+ixy+iz−y+izt−ix),Vn=Symn⁡ℂ2=ℂ[X,Y]n.

    If M=(abcd), the symmetric-power action is

    τn(M)p(X,Y)=p(aX+cY,bX+dY).

    Let Γ be a discrete torsion-free subgroup of G=Sp(1,1). For γ=(abcd) put

    γq=(aq+b)(cq+d)−1,J=cq+d,j=ι(J),r=|J|.

    The integer n is positive. We use

    Dl=∂t+i∂x+jℍ∂y+k∂z,

    where jℍ is the quaternion unit, distinct from the matrix j; subscripts X,Y denote derivatives in the polynomial variables.

    Definition TF.1. For n≥1, a regular automorphic spinor of weight n is a smooth f:B→Vn satisfying, for every q∈B and γ∈Γ,

    Dl(fX−jℍfY)=0,f(γq)=r2τn(j)f(q).(TF.1)

    Definition TF.1 · version 1

    Write ℳn(Γ) for the vector space of regular automorphic spinors of weight n.

    Regularity is equivalent to

    (∂t+i∂x∂y+i∂z−∂y+i∂z∂t−i∂x)(fXfY)=0.(TF.2)

    Indeed, if A and B0 are the two entries on the left of (TF.2), expansion using ijℍ=k and jℍi=−k gives Dl(fX−jℍfY)=A−jℍB0. A quaternion of this form vanishes exactly when both complex entries vanish.

    Norms#

    For f=∑caXn−aYa, set

    ‖f‖n2=∑a=0n|ca|2(na),‖f‖Γ,n2=∫ℱ‖f(q)‖n2sn−2dv,

    where ℱ is a fundamental domain and dv=dtdxdydz. The finite-norm subspace is ℳn2(Γ). The invariance proved in Proposition TF.2 makes the integral independent of the fundamental domain.

    The space ℳn imposes regularity and the transformation law. Growth, infinitesimal finiteness, and cuspidality are additional conditions, specified by the growth and finiteness conditions and the cusp condition.

    Proposition TF.2. The factor r2τn(j) is a cocycle and defines a rank-n+1 vector bundle on Γ\B. For g=(abcd), the lift

    Ff(g)=|d|−2τn(ι(d)−1)f(g0)(TF.3)

    is left Γ-invariant and satisfies Ff(gk)=τn(ι(kR)−1)Ff(g) for k=diag⁡(kL,kR)∈K. At

    σ(q)=s−1/2(1qq‾1)

    one has Ff(σ(q))=s(n+2)/2f(q). The density defining ‖f‖Γ,n is Γ-invariant.

    Proposition TF.2 · version 1

    Uses Definition TF.1, ball pullbacks.

    Proof of Proposition TF.2

    The denominator satisfies J(g1g2,q)=J(g1,g2q)J(g2,q). Quaternion norm multiplicativity and the representation law for τn prove the cocycle identity and left invariance of (TF.3). Right multiplication by k replaces d by dkR and leaves g0 fixed, proving right equivariance. Substitution of d=s−1/2 proves the section formula. Finally, τn(j) multiplies norms by rn, s(γq)=s/r2, and the real Jacobian is r−8. Thus the density changes by r2n+4r−2n+4r−8=1.

    For arithmetic Γ, the usual automorphic conditions can be imposed on the scalar components of Ff: moderate growth, finiteness under the center of the enveloping algebra, and, for cusp forms, vanishing of every constant term

    ∫(Γ∩NP)\NPFf(ug)du=0

    for each proper rational parabolic P. Right K-finiteness follows from Proposition TF.2. These are conditions on the automorphic lift; the definition of a regular cusp form and the cuspidality–norm equivalence give the precise criterion in the stated arithmetic setting. The arithmetic theorem below assumes the lift is a smooth vector in the cuspidal L2 space, as well as an automorphic form in this sense.

    Embedding into a genuine flat bundle#

    Let E=ℂ4, with the block representation

    R(g)=(ι(a)ι(b)ι(c)ι(d)),H=diag⁡(I2,−I2).

    For ℓ≥0 write Eℓ=Symℓ⁡E, Rℓ=Symℓ⁡R, and

    Z(q)=(Q(q)I2),Zℓ(q)=Symℓ⁡Z(q).

    For ℓ≥0, let ℰℓ be the flat bundle associated to Rℓ|Γ on Γ\B: the quotient of B×Eℓ by (q,v)↦(γq,Rℓ(γ)v), with connection induced by ordinary differentiation on B.

    The bundle has rank (ℓ+33). The Hermitian form Hℓ is the symmetric tensor power of H, with the tensor-induced normalization. It is invariant because R(g)∗HR(g)=H for g∈Sp(1,1), and this identity passes to symmetric powers. The connection descends because the transition matrices Rℓ(γ) are constant. The rank formula follows by counting the degree-ℓ monomials in four variables.

    Proposition TF.3. For every γ∈G,

    Z(γq)j=R(γ)Z(q),Z(q)∗HZ(q)=−sI2.(TF.4)

    For f∈ℳn(Γ), sZnf is a section of ℰn, lying in its moving rank-n+1 subbundle Zn(Vn). The map f↦sZnf is injective.

    Proposition TF.3 · version 1

    Uses Flat coefficient conventions, flat coefficient bundle.

    Proof of Proposition TF.3

    Multiplying the two block matrices gives top block ι(aq+b) and bottom block j, which proves the first identity. The second follows from Q∗Q=|q|2I2. Combine (TF.1), (TF.4), and s(γq)=s/r2 to obtain (sZnf)(γq)=Rn(γ)(sZnf)(q). The bottom block of Z is the identity, so Zn is injective; s>0 on B.

    This already supplies the vector-bundle realization. The ambient bundle is flat, while its moving spinor subbundle need not be parallel. Differentiating sZnf gives an exact flat-bundle-valued one-form. To obtain interesting periods, we instead use the following two-form.

    The closed two-form#

    For n≥2, put m=n−2 and

    ϵ=(01−10),S=ϵ(dQ∗∧dQ).

    Here dQ∗ means the differential of the conjugate transpose. Matrix multiplication includes exterior multiplication of forms. The matrix S is symmetric by the explicit calculation (TF.8) in the proof of Theorem TF.5.

    Construction TF.4. For a smooth f:B→Vn, define

    Φn(f)=S11fXX+2S12fXY+S22fYYn(n−1),Ωf=ZmΦn(f).(TF.5)

    Construction TF.4 · version 1

    Well-definedness: Theorem TF.5 proves closedness, covariance, and injectivity.

    Then Φn(f) is a Vm-valued two-form, and Ωf takes values in Em. Auxiliary variables represent symmetric tensors: Zm replaces each of their ℂ2 tensor factors by its image under Z.

    Theorem TF.5. For every smooth f:B→Vn, n≥2,

    dΩf=0⟺Dl(fX−jℍfY)=0.(TF.6)

    The map f↦Ωf is pointwise injective and Ωf is anti-self-dual in the orientation dt∧dx∧dy∧dz. If f∈ℳn(Γ), then

    γ∗Ωf=Rm(γ)Ωf.(TF.7)

    Consequently Ωf descends to a closed two-form with values in the flat bundle ℰm.

    Theorem TF.5 · version 1

    Uses Construction TF.4, Definition TF.1, flat coefficient bundle.

    Proof of Theorem TF.5

    Set

    ω1=dt∧dx−dy∧dz,ω2=dt∧dy+dx∧dz,ω3=dt∧dz−dx∧dy.

    All three are anti-self-dual. Direct multiplication gives

    S=(−2ω2+2iω3−2iω1−2iω1−2ω2−2iω3).(TF.8)

    Thus Φn, and hence Ωf, is anti-self-dual. Its vanishing implies fXY=0, fXX+fYY=0, and fXX−fYY=0. All second derivatives vanish, so a homogeneous polynomial of degree n≥2 must be zero. Injectivity of Zm completes the pointwise assertion.

    For closedness, first take n=2 and f=aX2+2bXY+cY2. The four regularity residuals in (TF.2), after division by 2, are

    E1=at+iax+by+ibz,E2=−ay+iaz+bt−ibx,E3=bt+ibx+cy+icz,E4=−by+ibz+ct−icx.

    In the ordered basis (dt∧dx∧dy,dt∧dx∧dz,dt∧dy∧dz,dx∧dy∧dz) the coefficients of dΦ2(f) are

    (−2iE1+2iE4,−2E1−2E4,2iE2+2iE3,−2E2+2E3).

    This invertible change of variables proves equivalence with regularity. For general n, apply all auxiliary derivatives of total order n−2. They commute with base differentiation, and

    ∂αΦn(f)=2n(n−1)Φ2(∂αf).

    The quadratic case proves dΦn(f)=0 if and only if every such derivative of the regularity residual vanishes. That residual is homogeneous of degree n−1, so this is equivalent to its vanishing.

    It remains to differentiate the moving inclusion Zm. The required identity is algebraic, not a flatness assumption. For each row α=1,2 of dQ, expansion of (TF.8) gives

    dQα1∧S11=0,dQα2∧S22=0,dQα2∧S11+2dQα1∧S12=0,dQα1∧S22+2dQα2∧S12=0.(TF.9)

    Equivalently, dQα(i∧Sjk)=0. Every term of dZm∧Φn(f) contracts this identity with three symmetric indices of f. Thus dZm∧Φn(f)=0 for every f, and

    dΩf=ZmdΦn(f).

    Because Zm is injective, this proves (TF.6).

    For covariance, Proposition RF.3 gives

    γ∗(dQ∗∧dQ)=r−4j(dQ∗∧dQ)j−1.

    Since det⁡j=r2 and ϵj=(det⁡j)j−tϵ, it follows that

    γ∗S=r−2j−tSj−1.(TF.10)

    Contract this with f(γq)=r2j⊙nf(q). The two contracted tensor factors and the scalar weights cancel, leaving

    γ∗Φn(f)=τm(j)Φn(f).

    Finally apply (TF.4) in degree m to obtain (TF.7). The Hodge star on two-forms in dimension four is conformally invariant, so the same anti-self-duality holds for the ball's hyperbolic metric.

    Cohomology and periods#

    Theorem TF.6. For n≥2, (TF.5) defines a linear map

    ℳn(Γ)⟶HdR2(Γ\B,ℰn−2)≃H2(Γ,En−2),f⟼[Ωf].(TF.11)

    If Γ\B is compact, this map is injective. More precisely, let m=n−2 and let 𝒫Hm(α,β) denote the wedge pairing which applies Hm conjugate-linearly to coefficients of α and linearly to coefficients of β. Then

    𝒫Hm(Ωf,Ωf)=16(−1)m+1sm‖f(q)‖n2dv.(TF.12)

    Theorem TF.6 · version 1

    Uses Theorem TF.5, the de Rham theorem with flat coefficients.

    Proof of Theorem TF.6

    Theorem TF.5 establishes descent and closedness. The ball is contractible and Γ acts freely and properly, so its quotient is a classifying space for Γ. The de Rham comparison with flat coefficients and the classifying-space comparison give the identification; see the comparison construction and [3, §III.1, p. 59].

    The induced Hermitian form on Zm(Vm) is (−s)m times the standard positive form, by (TF.4). With Euclidean norms on two-forms,

    ‖Φn(f)‖2=16‖f‖n2.(TF.13)

    To verify the constant, the contraction in (TF.5) is equivariant for SU(2) acting on Vn, Vm, and the anti-self-dual forms. Its induced Hermitian form on the irreducible Vn is a scalar multiple of the standard one. On f=Xn, its image is XmS11; (TF.8) and ‖ωi‖2=2 give ‖S11‖2=16. Anti-self-duality now converts the norm identity into (TF.12).

    If Ωf=d∇β on a compact quotient, parallelness of Hm and d∇Ωf=0 imply

    𝒫Hm(Ωf,Ωf)=d𝒫Hm(β,Ωf).

    Its integral is zero by Stokes. But (TF.12) has a fixed sign and is strictly nonzero after integration when f≠0. Therefore [Ωf]≠0.

    Proposition TF.7. Fix q0∈B. For f∈ℳn(Γ) define, in the constant flat trivialization on B,

    cf(γ1,γ2)=∫[q0,γ1q0,γ1γ2q0]Ωf,

    where brackets denote oriented geodesically straightened simplices. This is an En−2-valued group two-cocycle representing (TF.11). Changing q0 changes it by a coboundary.

    Proposition TF.7 · version 1

    Uses Theorem TF.5, Theorem TF.6, Stokes' theorem.

    Proof of Proposition TF.7

    The four faces of the straightened tetrahedron with vertices q0,γ1q0,γ1γ2q0,γ1γ2γ3q0 give

    Rm(γ1)cf(γ2,γ3)−cf(γ1γ2,γ3)+cf(γ1,γ2γ3)−cf(γ1,γ2)=0.

    The first face uses (TF.7); the sum is zero by Stokes and closedness. Integrating over the prism homotopy between two choices of vertices proves the coboundary assertion. The augmented simplex chains are a free resolution; the comparison theorem [3, Thm. I.7.5] identifies this integration map with the comparison in Theorem TF.6.

    Square integrability and cusp forms#

    Theorem TF.8. Give Γ\B the complete hyperbolic metric s−2|dq|2. On E, let hq be −H on the negative plane Z(q)ℂ2 and +H on its H-orthogonal complement. Let hm be the induced positive metric on Em. These metrics descend to ℰm.

    For n≥2 and f∈ℳn2(Γ), Ωf is a square-integrable harmonic form for the flat differential and the metric hm, and

    ‖Ωf‖L2(hm)2=16‖f‖Γ,n2.

    Consequently f↦[Ωf] is injective into reduced L2 cohomology H(2)2(Γ\B,ℰm,hm).

    Theorem TF.8 · version 1

    Uses Theorem TF.5, Theorem TF.6, the complete-manifold Hilbert de Rham complex.

    Proof of Theorem TF.8

    The negative-plane splitting is G-equivariant by (TF.4), proving descent. If one argument lies in Zm(Vm), the coefficient pairings satisfy hm=(−1)mHm: in the symmetric tensor decomposition, the all-negative summand is orthogonal to every summand containing a positive factor. Thus (TF.13), Z∗HZ=−sI, and conformal invariance of the product of the squared two-form norm with volume prove the norm identity.

    For every compactly supported one-form β, the hm inner product of d∇β with Ωf is, up to a constant sign, the integral of 𝒫Hm(d∇β,Ωf). Closedness and parallelness of Hm make this integral zero by Stokes. Therefore d∇∗Ωf=0 weakly, hence smoothly. Also d∇Ωf=0. On this complete manifold compactly supported forms are a core for the closed differential, using cutoffs with gradients tending uniformly to zero. Hence Ωf is orthogonal to the closure of exact L2 forms. The weak Hodge decomposition [7, §2.2, pp. 5–6] identifies these harmonic forms with reduced cohomology; hence a nonzero such form has nonzero class.

    Corollary TF.9. Suppose Γ is a torsion-free arithmetic lattice in a rational form of Sp(1,1) of rational rank one. Let 𝒮n(Γ) consist of the regular automorphic spinors in Definition TF.1 whose lifts (TF.3) have scalar components in the smooth cuspidal L2 automorphic space, satisfying the quantified moderate-growth and central-finiteness conditions (CC.3) and (CC.4). For n≥2, the map

    𝒮n(Γ)⟶H2(Γ,En−2),f⟼[Ωf]

    is injective, and its image lies in the cuspidal subspace defined by the comparison map in cuspidal cohomology.

    Corollary TF.9 · version 1

    Uses Theorem TF.8; cuspidal injectivity [1, Cor. 5.5], also recalled in [5, §1.2].

    Proof of Corollary TF.9

    In the homogeneous K-frame, the map (TF.5) is a fixed K-equivariant linear map from Vn to Em⊗Λ2𝔭∗. This follows either from (TF.7) for G or by evaluating the construction at q=0 and translating. The flat-coefficient homogeneous frame uses Rm(g); its moving-frame differential is exactly the usual relative Lie algebra differential on coefficients twisted by Em. Thus the form determined by Ff belongs to the cuspidal relative Lie algebra cochain complex. It is closed by Theorem TF.5.

    If it were a coboundary in that complex, its primitive would give a square-integrable one-form on the quotient: the homogeneous-frame coefficient norm is fixed and positive, and all scalar coefficients of the primitive are smooth vectors in Lcusp2. This contradicts Theorem TF.8 for f≠0. The cuspidal class is therefore nonzero. Borel’s injection [1, Cor. 5.5] from cuspidal to ordinary cohomology therefore gives the conclusion.

    For general noncuspidal forms on a noncompact quotient, Theorem TF.6 still defines an ordinary cohomology class, and Theorem TF.8 proves a nonzero reduced L2 class when the norm is finite. These two facts alone do not prove injectivity into ordinary cohomology. Corollary TF.9 supplies that step under its arithmetic cuspidal hypotheses. Theorem CC.13 identifies the full parabolic image, using both chiralities and additional spectral inputs; it does not assert surjectivity onto all ordinary cohomology.

    The lowest weight and the Maxwell equation#

    Corollary TF.10. For n=2 the coefficient bundle is trivial. The map

    f=aX2+2bXY+cY2⟼Ωf=−4ibω1−2(a+c)ω2+2i(a−c)ω3(TF.14)

    is a bijection from ℳ2(Γ) to the smooth complex closed anti-self-dual two-forms on Γ\B. On a compact quotient these are the anti-self-dual harmonic forms and inject into ordinary H2. On a complete noncompact quotient the finite-norm correspondence is with square-integrable anti-self-dual harmonic two-forms.

    Corollary TF.10 · version 1

    Uses Theorem TF.5, Theorem TF.6, Theorem TF.8.

    Proof of Corollary TF.10

    For n=2, m=0 and Z0=1, so (TF.14) is (TF.5) expanded using (TF.8). It is an invertible linear map onto the three-dimensional complex space of anti-self-dual two-forms at each point. Theorem TF.5 makes closedness equivalent to regularity. Covariance, applied to the inverse pointwise map, makes invariance of the two-form equivalent to the automorphy law for f. For an anti-self-dual form, closedness implies coclosedness since ∗Ω=−Ω. On compact manifolds harmonic forms are closed and coclosed; on complete manifolds the same holds for L2 harmonic forms in the Hilbert-complex sense. Theorem TF.6 and Theorem TF.8 give the cohomology and norm assertions.

    Example. A local seed

    The constant spinor f=X2 gives Ωf=−2ω2+2iω3. It is nonzero, closed, and anti-self-dual. This is a local seed on B; it need not be automorphic for a specified lattice. The same periodization idea gives a Poincaré-series construction at weights n≥5. At weight two, compact quotients supply harmonic examples.

    Further Directions#

    The construction starts at n=2. At n=1, the exponent n−2 is negative, so it gives no finite-dimensional symmetric-power coefficient system for the Hardy limit. This is a limitation of this construction, not an impossibility theorem for other endpoint cohomology theories.

    [6, (1,1)] already places cohomological discrete series in degree two. The cuspidal correspondence now identifies (TF.5) with the corresponding cohomological generator and supplies the opposite chirality. The coefficient model and Hecke-equivariant period map give a number-field structure on the sum of both chiralities; the eigenvalue argument proves that simultaneous Hecke eigenvalues lie in a number field. The similitude extension constructs an integral coefficient lattice and proves algebraic-integrality for the specified Hecke semigroup. Specific arithmetic examples and triangle periods remain to develop from the available constructions. The bilinear three-forms in Fueter-Regular Functions and Closed Three-Forms remain a separate construction.
