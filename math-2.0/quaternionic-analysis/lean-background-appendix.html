    Harmonic Analysis and Compact Representations on the Quaternionic Ball

    This appendix supplies the analytic and compact representation results used in the weighted regular-spinor construction, together with the quaternionic description of rotations used in lattice covariance. All Laplacians below are Euclidean.

    Write q=t+ix+jy+kz, B={q∈ℍ:|q|<1}, s(q)=1−|q|2, and dv=dtdxdydz. Put U=Sp⁡(1)={a∈ℍ:|a|=1} and let da be its Haar probability measure. Integrals with values in a Banach space are Bochner integrals. For such a function u, distributional harmonicity on B means

    ∫B(Δφ)(q)u(q)dv(q)=0(φ∈Cc∞(B,ℝ)).(HA.1)

    We use Vn=ℂ[X,Y]n with inner product linear in its first argument and squared norm

    ‖∑j=0ncjXn−jYj‖n2=∑j=0n|cj|2(nj).(HA.2)

    The substitution action is τn(M)p(X,Y)=p((X,Y)M). Quaternion arguments of τn mean the matrices

    ι(t+ix+jy+kz)=(t+ixy+iz−y+izt−ix).(HA.3)

    For integers n≥2, write

    wn(q)=n(n−1)π2s(q)n−2,dμn=wndv|B.(HA.4)

    The polynomial-spinor space is

    ℬn={f∈L2(B,Vn,μn):Dl(Tnf)=0 distributionally},Tnp=pX−jpY,(HA.5)

    where Dl=∂t+i∂x+j∂y+k∂z acts on the quaternion coefficients. This is a closed complex subspace of the weighted L2 space. Its elements have zero distributional Laplacian: DlDl=Δ and the fixed map Tn is injective. These are the weak-kernel facts used in Theorem FD.4.

    Weyl Regularity#

    The scalar Weyl lemma is stated in [1, Thm. 14.1]. The following Banach-valued version uses a compactly supported signed kernel obtained by differentiating a regularized Newton potential.

    Theorem HA.1 (Weyl regularity). Let E be a real Banach space and let u∈Lloc1(B,E) satisfy (HA.1). There is a smooth harmonic function u~:B→E equal to u almost everywhere.

    Proof of Theorem HA.1

    Choose χ∈Cc∞(ℝ) with χ=1 on [−1,1] and χ=0 outside [−2,2]. Set

    h(v)=χ(v)−1v,P(q)=h(|q|2),

    using the zero extension for h near v=0. Thus h and P are smooth. For v≠0,

    h′(v)=vχ′(v)−χ(v)+1v2,h′′(v)=v2χ′′(v)−2vχ′(v)+2χ(v)−2v3.

    The four-dimensional radial Laplacian therefore gives

    ΔP(q)=8h′(|q|2)+4|q|2h′′(|q|2)=4χ′′(|q|2).(HA.6)

    The equality also holds at zero, where all terms vanish. The raw kernel χ′′(|q|2) is smooth and supported in B(0,2). Its mass is m=2vol⁡(B)=π2: indeed,

    ddrr2χ′(r2)−χ(r2)2=r3χ′′(r2),∫0∞r3χ′′(r2)dr=12,

    and the polar-coordinate factor in dimension four is 2π2. Hence

    K(q)=m−1χ′′(|q|2),Kr(q)=r−4K(q/r),Pr(q)=r−2P(q/r)(HA.7)

    satisfy ∫Krdv=1 and ΔPr=4mKr for r>0. No positivity of K is used.

    For |q|≥2r, Pr(q)=−|q|−2. Consequently Pr−Pρ is smooth with support in B(0,2max⁡(r,ρ)). If |a|+2max⁡(r,ρ)<1, apply (HA.1) to the test function (Pr−Pρ)(a−⋅). Each integral is defined by local integrability on its compact support, and (HA.6) gives

    Sru(a)=Sρu(a),Sru(a)=∫BKr(a−y)u(y)dv(y).(HA.8)

    We next recover u almost everywhere. Since K is bounded and supported in B(0,2), a constant C independent of r satisfies

    |Kr(y)|≤Cvol⁡(B(0,2r)).

    At every Lebesgue point a of an integrable localization of u, the mass-one identity implies

    ‖Sru(a)−u(a)‖≤Cvol⁡(B(a,2r))∫B(a,2r)‖u(y)−u(a)‖dv(y)⟶0.(HA.9)

    The Banach-valued Lebesgue differentiation theorem applies to locally Bochner-integrable functions [2]. In particular, (HA.9) holds almost everywhere along rk=1/(k+1).

    Define u~(a)=S(1−|a|)/4u(a). Fix 0<S<R<1 and extend 𝟏B(0,R)u by zero to ℍ. This extension is integrable. For |a|<S, (HA.8) gives

    u~(a)=S(R−S)/4(𝟏B(0,R)u)(a).(HA.10)

    The kernel in (HA.10) has a fixed positive radius; convolution with this smooth compactly supported kernel is smooth. Thus u~ is smooth on every smaller ball. For sufficiently large k, the localized convolution at radius rk also equals u~(a) by (HA.8). Its almost-everywhere limit is u(a) by (HA.9). Taking a countable cover by balls with rational radii less than one proves u=u~ almost everywhere on B.

    Finally, integration by parts twice against a compact smooth test identifies the distributional Laplacian of u~ with its classical Laplacian. The former is zero because u~=u almost everywhere. Injectivity of the locally integrable function-to-distribution map gives Δu~=0 almost everywhere; continuity makes it zero everywhere on B.

    Interior Derivatives and Bochner Integration#

    For a finite list 𝐯=(v1,…,vk) of real quaternion directions, write D𝐯=Dv1⋯Dvk, with the outer derivative listed first; the empty list gives the identity. A continuous representative of a weighted L2 class is unique on B, since wn is positive there and every nonempty open subset has positive measure. We always evaluate this representative.

    Proposition HA.2. Let n≥2, a∈B, and let 𝐯 be a finite list of directions in ℍ. Evaluation

    Ea,𝐯:ℬn⟶Vn,f⟼D𝐯f~(a)

    is a bounded complex-linear map. In particular,

    ‖D𝐯f~(a)‖n≤‖Ea,𝐯‖‖f‖L2(μn).(HA.11)

    For any measure space (Z,ν) and Bochner-integrable F:Z→ℬn,

    D𝐯(∫ZFdν)~(a)=∫ZD𝐯F(z)~(a)dν(z).(HA.12)

    Proof of Proposition HA.2

    For a smooth harmonic function u, Theorem HA.1 and equality of continuous representatives turn (HA.8) into u(a)=Sru(a) whenever r>0 and |a|+2r<1. Constant directional derivatives commute with the distributional Laplacian. Hence the same formula applies to D𝐯u.

    Put φ(y)=Kr(a−y). Repeated integration by parts gives

    D𝐯u(a)=∫Bφ𝐯(y)u(y)dv(y),φ𝐯=(−1)kDvk⋯Dv1φ.(HA.13)

    Each differentiation preserves compact support in B, so all products used in integration by parts are integrable. Equivalently, the adjoint tests obey φ()=φ and φ(v,𝐰)=(−Dvφ)𝐰; this records both the signs and the order.

    The function φ𝐯/wn, extended by zero outside its compact support, belongs to L2(μn). This follows from continuity and the positive minimum of wn on that support. Cauchy–Schwarz yields

    ‖∫Bφ𝐯fdv‖n≤‖φ𝐯wn‖L2(μn)‖f‖L2(μn).

    Thus the integral defines a bounded complex-linear map on the ambient L2 space, independent of its almost-everywhere representative. Restrict it to ℬn and use (HA.13) for its smooth harmonic representative, choosing r=(1−|a|)/4. This is Ea,𝐯 and proves (HA.11). Continuous linear maps commute with Bochner integrals [2], giving (HA.12).

    The Binary-Form Norm#

    The restriction of (HA.2) to the unit-quaternion representation is the usual invariant norm on homogeneous binary forms [4, §1.2, Prop. 1.12].

    ‖τn(a)p‖n=|a|n‖p‖n.(HA.14)

    Proof of Proposition HA.3

    For degree zero substitution fixes constants. Suppose the assertion holds in degree n−1, where n≥1. For a complex matrix M the polynomial chain rule gives

    ∂j(τn(M)p)=∑ℓ=01Mjℓτn−1(M)(∂ℓp),j=0,1.(HA.15)

    Here ∂0=∂X and ∂1=∂Y. The matrix ι(a) satisfies ι(a)∗ι(a)=|a|2I. Applying this identity to every pair of coefficients, with the degree-n−1 binomial weights, gives

    ∑j=01‖∑ℓ=01ι(a)jℓpℓ‖n−12=|a|2(‖p0‖n−12+‖p1‖n−12).

    The derivative norm identity proved in Proposition FD.1 is

    ‖pX‖n−12+‖pY‖n−12=n2‖p‖n2.(HA.16)

    Apply (HA.16) to τn(a)p, then (HA.15), the coefficient identity above, and the induction hypothesis. The result is

    n2‖τn(a)p‖n2=|a|2n(‖pX‖n−12+‖pY‖n−12)=n2|a|2n‖p‖n2.

    Cancel n2 and take nonnegative square roots. For |a|=1, the inverse action is τn(a−1), so norm preservation gives a unitary operator.

    Compact Support and Strong Continuity#

    Put G=Sp⁡(1,1), realized as the quaternion matrices preserving diag⁡(1,−1). If

    g−1=(abcd),Jg(q)=cq+d,qg=(aq+b)Jg(q)−1,

    the weighted action on L2(B,Vn,μn) is

    (πn(g)f)(q)=|Jg(q)|−2τn(Jg(q)−1)f(qg).(HA.17)

    The cocycle law and the weighted change-of-variables identity make this a unitary group action. Its restriction preserves ℬn by the covariance argument in Theorem FD.4.

    Proof of Lemma HA.4[2]

    It suffices to approximate a finite-measure simple function, and hence a function c𝟏A with μn(A)<∞. The measure is concentrated on B. By inner regularity choose a compact set S⊂A∩B such that μn(A∖S) is as small as prescribed. Thus

    ‖c𝟏A−c𝟏S‖L2(μn)=‖c‖nμn(A∖S)1/2

    is small.

    Choose an open neighborhood V of S with V⊂B, and a compact neighborhood C of S. Outer regularity and Urysohn's lemma supply a continuous function η, equal to one on S, taking values in [0,1], with support in a sufficiently small open neighborhood of S inside V∩int⁡C. The neighborhood can be chosen so that ‖cη−c𝟏S‖L2(μn) is arbitrarily small: its square is at most ‖c‖n2 times the measure of that neighborhood minus S.

    The closed support of cη lies in V∩C, hence is compact and contained in B. The triangle inequality completes the indicator approximation. Finite sums preserve continuity and compact support inside B; simple-function density then proves the result.

    The formal proof adapts Heather Macbeth's compact-support approximation in mathlib; its source retains her copyright and Apache 2.0 notice. Lean proof and notice.

    Proof of Proposition HA.5

    First take the class of a continuous compactly supported F:ℍ→Vn. Unitarity gives

    ‖πn(g)F−F‖22=2‖F‖22−2Re⁡⟨πn(g)F,F⟩2.(HA.18)

    We prove continuity of this matrix coefficient at g=1 by dominated convergence. Its pointwise integrand is continuous in g for each q∈B. For the blocks of g−1, the positive gap |d|−|c| gives |cq+d|≥|d|−|c| on B. The gap is continuous and equals one at the identity; choose a neighborhood of the identity on which it is at least δ>0.

    By (HA.14) and (HA.17), ‖πn(g)F(q)‖n≤δ−n−2‖F‖∞ there. Therefore

    |⟨πn(g)F(q),F(q)⟩n|≤δ−n−2‖F‖∞2𝟏supp⁡F(q).

    The right side is integrable and has fixed compact support. The matrix coefficient tends to ‖F‖22, and (HA.18) gives norm continuity at the identity. The group law and unitarity transport this conclusion to every g∈G.

    For arbitrary f, choose F from the dense class in Lemma HA.4. Then

    ‖πn(g)f−πn(g0)f‖2≤2‖f−F‖2+‖πn(g)F−πn(g0)F‖2.

    This proves continuity of each orbit. Joint continuity follows from the common Lipschitz bound ‖πn(g)f−πn(g)h‖2=‖f−h‖2. Restricting a continuous orbit map to the invariant subspace ℬn proves the last assertion.

    For the classical scalar mean-value theorem see [3, Thm. 1.4]. Here the average is over the unit-quaternion group; the proof differentiates that average and solves its radial equation.

    Proof of Proposition HA.6

    Haar probability on a compact group is right invariant and inversion invariant. For completeness, right translation of left Haar probability is again left Haar probability and hence agrees with it by uniqueness; inversion then has the same property. We use the compact Haar uniqueness theorem [2].

    Consider A(q)=∫UF(aq)da. On a smaller closed ball, the functions F(aq) and their first two derivatives in q are jointly continuous in (q,a). Their derivatives are uniformly bounded, since the closed ball and U are compact. These bounds are integrable in a. Dominated differentiation therefore gives

    DA(q)v=∫UDF(aq)(av)da,D2A(q)(v,w)=∫UD2F(aq)(av,aw)da.(HA.20)

    In this application the parameter space is the finite-dimensional real space ℍ, and the index measure is finite. Thus the compactness argument supplies exactly the local derivative bound required for differentiation under the integral.

    Left multiplication by a unit quaternion is a real linear isometry. Taking the trace of the Hessian in the orthonormal basis 1,i,j,k in (HA.20), and changing to its image under left multiplication, gives ΔA(q)=∫UΔF(aq)da=0. Right invariance of Haar gives A(bq)=A(q) for b∈U, so A(q)=M(|q|), where M(r)=A(r⋅1). Moreover A(0)=F(0) and DA(0)=0, the latter by replacing a with −a in the first equation of (HA.20).

    At q=r⋅1, 0<r<1, the radial Hessian entry is M′′(r). Each of the three unit tangential directions contributes M′(r)/r: differentiate

    DA(q)v=⟨q,v⟩ℝ|q|M′(|q|)

    in a direction v⟂q. Hence harmonicity gives

    rM′′(r)+3M′(r)=0.(HA.21)

    The function r3M′(r) has derivative zero on [0,1), including zero by differentiability of M′ there. Its value at zero is zero; the real-variable mean-value theorem makes it identically zero. Thus M′(r)=0 for r>0, also M′(0)=0, and M(r)=M(0). It follows that A(q)=F(0). Inversion invariance changes F(aq) to F(a−1q) and proves (HA.19).

    Lean averaging proof, radial equation, ODE lemma, and mean-value theorem.

    Proof of Proposition HA.8

    Every subspace of Vn is closed. For each e∈{i,j,k}, differentiate the orbit of p∈M under cos⁡t+esin⁡t. Its difference quotients lie in M, so the infinitesimal action of ι(e) preserves M. Taking complex linear combinations shows that the following three operators preserve M:

    H=X∂X−Y∂Y,E=X∂Y,L=Y∂X.(HA.23)

    Explicitly, if Ai,Aj,Ak are those three infinitesimal actions, then H=−iAi, E=(Aj−iAk)/2, and L=(−iAk−Aj)/2.

    Suppose M≠0. The complex endomorphism H|M has an eigenvector p≠0, say Hp=λp. On er=Xn−rYr, one has

    Her=(n−2r)er,Eer=rer−1,Ler=(n−r)er+1,(HA.24)

    with absent endpoint terms zero. The weights n−2r are distinct. Comparing coefficients in Hp=λp therefore shows that p is a nonzero scalar multiple of a single er. Hence er∈M. Applying E repeatedly, and dividing by the nonzero integer coefficients, gives e0=Xn∈M. Applying L repeatedly then gives every er. These monomials span Vn, so M=Vn.

    Lean proof.

    Proof of Proposition HA.9

    This is the specialization of Schur orthogonality [4, Thm. 1.22] to the representation in Proposition HA.8. We give the rank-one averaging proof.

    For w∈Vn, let Rwx=⟨x,w⟩nw and set

    Av=∫URτn(a)vda.

    The operator-valued integrand is continuous on the compact group, hence integrable. Unitarity gives Rτn(b)w=τn(b)Rwτn(b)−1. Left invariance of Haar then shows that Av commutes with every τn(b).

    A complex endomorphism T commuting with this representation is scalar: choose an eigenvalue λ of T. Its nonzero eigenspace is invariant, hence is all of Vn by Proposition HA.8. Apply this to Av. Since trace is a continuous linear functional on the finite-dimensional operator space,

    tr⁡Av=∫Utr⁡Rτn(a)vda=∫U‖τn(a)v‖n2da=‖v‖n2.

    Thus Av=(‖v‖n2/(n+1))I. Pairing Avu with u proves (HA.25). Finally, unitarity gives ⟨τn(a−1)u,v⟩n=⟨u,τn(a)v⟩n, and the first factor of K has mass one. This proves (HA.26).

    Proof of Lemma HA.10

    Write J0=diag⁡(1,−1). The identities g∗J0g=J0 and gJ0g∗=J0 imply d≠0, q0=bd−1∈B, c=q‾0a, and

    s(q0)|a|2=s(q0)|d|2=1.

    It follows that

    g=σ(q0)diag⁡(s(q0)1/2a,s(q0)1/2d).(HA.29)

    The two diagonal entries have norm one. Conversely, the origin of σ(q)k is q, and cancellation of σ(q) recovers k. All formulas and their inverses are continuous on their domains, proving the homeomorphism.

    Apply the norm identity for the last column to gσ(q), whose last column is s(q)−1/2(aq+b,cq+d)t. Its origin is gq. This gives the first formula in (HA.28), and also shows J(g,q)≠0.

    Differentiation with the quaternion multiplication order retained gives

    D(g⋅)(q)v=(a−(gq)c)vJ(g,q)−1.(HA.30)

    Set h=gσ(q) and A=a−(gq)c. The first-column relation for a group element is

    |h00−(h⋅0)h10|2=s(h⋅0).

    Indeed h10=h⋅0h00, so its left side is s(h⋅0)2|h00|2=s(h⋅0). Expanding the first column of gσ(q) gives

    h00−(gq)h10=s(q)1/2A.

    Together with the first formula of (HA.28), this yields |A|2=|J(g,q)|−2. Real left or right multiplication by a quaternion z has determinant |z|4, as follows by expanding its matrix in the basis 1,i,j,k. Applying this to the two factors in (HA.30) proves the determinant formula in (HA.28).

    The action of g−1 supplies the inverse change of variables. Since

    s(gq)−4|J(g,q)|−8=s(q)−4,

    that change of variables proves invariance of λ.

    Proof of Proposition HA.11

    The measure λ is σ-finite by exhaustion of B by smaller closed balls, and dk is finite. For g∈G and q∈B, let κ(g,q)∈K be the compact coordinate of gσ(q). Lemma HA.10 gives

    gσ(q)k=σ(gq)κ(g,q)k.(HA.32)

    The coordinate correction κ(g,q) is continuous. For any nonnegative measurable function on G, Tonelli and (HA.32) express its integral after left translation as an iterated integral. Left invariance of dk removes κ(g,q) in the inner integral; invariance of λ removes g in the outer one. The pushforward measure is therefore left invariant.

    On compact subsets of B the density s−4 is bounded, so λ is locally finite. It is positive on nonempty open sets because its density is strictly positive. Product Haar probability on K has the same open-set positivity. A compact subset of G has compact inverse image under the homeomorphism in Lemma HA.10, so the pushforward is finite on compact sets; the homeomorphism also preserves open-set positivity. These properties establish that it is a Haar measure.

    For continuous nonnegative ϕ, each compact fibre k↦ϕ(σ(q)k) is integrable. Tonelli for the pushforward gives

    ∫Gϕdg=∫B∫Kϕ(σ(q)k)dkdλ(q)=∫BQϕdv

    as extended nonnegative integrals. Finiteness of one side is equivalent to finiteness of the other, which proves the integrability equivalence. When finite, these are the ordinary real integrals in (HA.31).

    Proof of Proposition HA.12

    Multiplicativity of the quaternion norm makes ρ(a,b) an isometry, and the order of b−1 gives the homomorphism law. In the basis 1,i,j,k, direct determinant calculations give

    det⁡Lz=det⁡Rz=|z|4,det⁡C=−1,C(q)=q‾.

    Thus every ρ(a,b) has determinant one.

    If u is a unit quaternion, reflection in the hyperplane perpendicular to u is

    ru(q)=q−2⟨q,u⟩ℝu=−uq‾u.(HA.34)

    The formula follows by multiplying uq‾+qu‾=2⟨q,u⟩ℝ on the right by u. It writes ru as ρ(−u,u−1)C. Cartan–Dieudonné expresses every real orthogonal map as a product of hyperplane reflections [2]. Normalize each nonzero reflection vector to a unit quaternion. Using

    Cρ(a,b)=ρ(b,a)C,C2=1,

    induction on the product writes it as either ρ(a,b) or ρ(a,b)C. The second possibility has determinant −1. Every orientation-preserving isometry therefore lies in the image of ρ.

    If ρ(a,b)=1, evaluation at 1 gives a=b. The remaining identity says that a commutes with every quaternion. Commutation with i forces its j and k coefficients to vanish; commutation with j forces its i coefficient to vanish. Thus a is real, and |a|=1 gives a=b=1 or a=b=−1. Both pairs act trivially, proving the kernel assertion.

    Lean proof. The checked conclusion is this algebraic homomorphism, its surjectivity and kernel.

    The Matrix Maurer–Cartan Equation#

    For real matrix groups, the Maurer–Cartan form ω=h−1dh satisfies dω+ω∧ω=0 [6, §1.6, p. 18]. The following local formulation applies to the quaternionic section in the ball-connection calculation.

    Proposition HA.13 (Maurer–Cartan). Let E be a real normed vector space, A a finite-dimensional associative unital real normed algebra, and I a finite index set. Give M=MatI⁡(A) the norm ‖a‖=maxi,j∈I⁡‖aij‖, with value zero if I is empty. Let f,g:E→M and x∈E. Suppose that f is C2 on a neighborhood of x, that g is differentiable at x, and that

    g(y)f(y)=1for all y near x,f(x)g(x)=1.

    For y near x and u∈E, put θy(u)=g(y)Df(y)[u]. Then, for all constant vectors v,w∈E,

    D(θ⋅(w))(x)[v]−D(θ⋅(v))(x)[w]+θx(v)θx(w)−θx(w)θx(v)=0.(HA.35)

    Proof of Proposition HA.13

    Multiplication on the finite-dimensional space M is a continuous bilinear map. Hence the bilinear product rule gives

    D(ab)(x)[u]=a(x)Db(x)[u]+Da(x)[u]b(x)

    whenever a,b:E→M are differentiable at x. Apply this rule to the local inverse identity gf=1 [6, §1.6, p. 18]:

    g(x)Df(x)[u]+Dg(x)[u]f(x)=0.

    Right-multiply by g(x) and use f(x)g(x)=1. Associativity gives

    Dg(x)[u]=−g(x)Df(x)[u]g(x).(HA.36)

    Since f is C2 near x, the map Df is differentiable at x. For fixed w, evaluation at w is continuous linear, so the derivative of y↦Df(y)[w] in direction v is D2f(x)[v,w]. The product rule and (HA.36) now give

    D(θ⋅(w))(x)[v]=g(x)D2f(x)[v,w]−g(x)Df(x)[v]g(x)Df(x)[w]=g(x)D2f(x)[v,w]−θx(v)θx(w).(HA.37)

    The second Fréchet derivative of a C2 map is symmetric [2]. Subtract (HA.37) with v,w exchanged; the second-derivative terms cancel, leaving (HA.35).
