    Classical Automorphic Geometry and Hodge Weights

    The classical weight shift#

    Proposition GE1. The Hodge bundle and the two units of modular weight

    Let N≥5, let Y=Y1(N)ℂ, let X=X1(N)ℂ be its smooth compactification, and let C=X∖Y be the reduced cusp divisor. For the universal elliptic curve π:ℰ→Y, let

    ℋdR=R1π∗Ωℰ/Y∙,ω=π∗Ωℰ/Y1.

    Write ω also for its canonical extension over X. The Hodge filtration and the Kodaira–Spencer map give

    0⟶ω⟶ℋdR⟶ω−1⟶0on Y,ω⊗2≃ΩX1(log⁡C).

    For every integer k≥2,

    Sk(Γ1(N))=H0(X,ω⊗k(−C))≃H0(X,ω⊗(k−2)⊗ΩX1).

    GE1 · version 1

    Uses [2, Thm. 10.13.11].

    Proof of Proposition GE1

    The first exact sequence is the degree-one Hodge–de Rham sequence, with its quotient identified by the principal polarization and relative Serre duality. The Gauss–Manin connection followed by projection induces

    ω⟶(ℋdR/ω)⊗ΩY1≃ω−1⊗ΩY1.

    For the fine elliptic moduli problem this map is an isomorphism; its extension across the cusps is the Kodaira–Spencer isomorphism [2, Thm. 10.13.11]. Thus ω2(−C)≃ΩX1. Tensoring by ωk−2 and taking global sections proves the assertion, using the definition of cusp forms as sections vanishing on C.

    Discussion

    The bundle ω consists of invariant cotangent vectors of the elliptic fibres. Its square is the cotangent bundle of the modular curve, with the cusp correction. The Gauss–Manin connection is essential to this identification.

    The rank-two local system ℍ=R1π∗ℚ has this Hodge filtration. The geometric coefficient underlying a weight-k eigenform is Symk−2⁡ℍ, and the relevant cohomology is Hpar1(Y,Symk−2⁡ℍ). For k>2, Scholl's [5, Thm. 1.2.1] realizes it by a projector in degree k−1 of a compactified (k−2)-fold fibre product of the universal elliptic curve. Thus the general construction already uses higher cohomology rather than a quotient of a Jacobian.

    Theorem GE-J. The weight-two Jacobian quotient [6, Thm. 7.14]; [3, §2, p. 4]

    Let f∈S2(Γ1(N)) be a normalized cuspidal newform, let Ef be its Fourier-coefficient field, let J=Jac⁡(X1(N)) over ℚ, and let 𝕋⊂Endℚ⁡(J) be the Hecke algebra. Put

    If=ker⁡(𝕋⟶Ef),Bf=IfJ,Af=J/Bf,

    where IfJ is the connected abelian subvariety generated by the images of the endomorphisms in If. Then

    dim⁡Af=[Ef:ℚ],codimJ⁡Bf=[Ef:ℚ].

    In particular, if Ef=ℚ, then Af is an elliptic curve over ℚ.

    GE-J · version 1

    Uses Shimura's construction of the abelian variety of a newform; [3, §2, p. 4].

    Proof of Theorem GE-J

    The construction and its newform multiplicity-one input are imported from the cited classical theorem. To see the dimension mechanism, the cotangent space of J is H0(X1(N),Ω1)=S2(Γ1(N)). The cotangent space of the newform quotient is the span of the Galois conjugates of f, one dimension for each embedding of Ef in ℂ. Its dimension is therefore [Ef:ℚ]. Since Af is an abelian variety defined over ℚ, dimension one gives an elliptic curve with its identity as rational origin.

    Discussion

    A single complex eigenline need not define an abelian subvariety over ℚ. The full Galois orbit is used. The kernel has codimension one precisely in the rational-coefficient case of this construction, not for every weight-two newform.

    Geometry after transfer to the split group#

    Proposition GE4. The Siegel Hodge bundle and the cohomological degree

    Let S be a fine Siegel modular threefold over a number field, with universal principally polarized abelian surface π:𝒰→S. Let ℍ=R1π∗ℚ on S(ℂ) and let ℱ=π∗Ω𝒰/S1 be its rank-two Hodge bundle. Then

    ΩS1≃Sym2⁡ℱ,ΩS3≃(det⁡ℱ)3.

    For m≥0, the automorphic bundle of Siegel weight (m+3,3) is

    Symm⁡ℱ⊗(det⁡ℱ)3≃Symm⁡ℱ⊗ΩS3.

    For m≥1, H3(S(ℂ),Symm⁡ℍ) is a subquotient of Hm+3(𝒰Sm(ℂ),ℚ). The analogous statement holds for geometric étale cohomology over the number field, equivariantly for its absolute Galois group. For m=0, the corresponding group is H3(S) itself.

    GE4 · version 1

    Uses [4, §5.10], [7, Thm. 1.4].

    Proof of Proposition GE4

    The polarized Kodaira–Spencer isomorphism [4, Prop. 5.10.1] gives ΩS1≃Sym2⁡ℱ. On a diagonal change of basis with eigenvalues a,b, the determinant of its action on Sym2⁡ℱ is a2(ab)b2=(ab)3. This proves the canonical-bundle identity and hence the automorphic-bundle formula.

    For πm:𝒰Sm→S, relative Künneth identifies ℍ⊗m as the direct summand of Rmπm∗ℚ of degree one in each factor. Rational symmetrization gives its direct summand Symm⁡ℍ. This construction also exists in the geometric étale local systems. If implemented by permuting factors on the total cohomology, the degree-one Koszul signs must be included.

    The Leray spectral sequence has terms Ha(S,Rbπm∗ℚ) and converges to Ha+b(𝒰Sm,ℚ). Multiplication by a fixed integer r>1 on the abelian fibres acts by rb on Rb. A differential dt, t≥2, changes b to b−t+1, so its source and target have different eigenvalues and it is zero. Thus the spectral sequence degenerates, and its (3,m) term is a subquotient in total degree m+3. The same argument applies étale. The relative motivic construction underlying these coefficient summands is Ancona’s theorem [7, Thm. 1.4].

    Discussion

    For the transferred spinor family, m=n−2, so the split geometry places the coefficient cohomology in total degree m+3=n+1. This explains the weight n+1 of LG3. Boundary conditions and Hecke eigenspaces must still be imposed using the published cuspidal/intersection-cohomology construction. GE4 alone does not isolate an individual eigenform.

    The transfer matches Hecke systems on the quaternionic quotient and the Siegel threefold. It supplies no canonical map identifying their degree-two and degree-three cohomology groups or their periods. In particular, the real family in GE3, whose fibres have dimension four, is a different construction from the universal abelian surface used in GE4.

    An étale cohomological realization is also weaker than an explicit Chow motive cut out by an algebraic idempotent on a smooth projective variety. Wildeshaus's intersection-motive theorem requires derived highest weight (a,b) with a>b>0. Our coefficient (m,0) is outside that range for every m. Its relative coefficient motive is covered by Theorem 1.4, but the absolute intersection-motive conclusion cannot simply be imported. This is a limit of that particular theorem, not a nonexistence assertion about motives in these weights.

    Betti Hodge weights in this geometric discussion use ordinary cohomology. When comparing with the arithmetic-Frobenius Galois normalization of LG3, duals and the specified central twists must be respected. They do not change the cohomological-degree explanation or the Hodge–Tate obstruction below.

    Why an abelian-variety quotient is the wrong target#

    Proposition GE5. The Hodge–Tate obstruction to first cohomology

    Let n≥2 and let r be a four-dimensional de Rham representation of the absolute Galois group of a number field, with Hodge–Tate multiset {0,1,n,n+1} at one embedding. For every integer Tate twist t, neither r(t) nor r∨(t) is isomorphic to a subquotient of the first étale cohomology of an abelian variety, or its dual, over a finite extension of that field.

    GE5 · version 1

    Uses LG3, the Hodge–Tate decomposition for abelian varieties.

    Proof of Proposition GE5

    By the Hodge–Tate comparison [1, Thm. 2.2.3], the weights of first cohomology of an abelian variety are two consecutive integers, with the sign determined by the cyclotomic convention; its dual has the opposite pair. Every subquotient has weights drawn from that pair. The difference between the largest and smallest weights of r is n+1≥3. Integer twisting translates all weights by the same amount; duality reverses them; finite extension does not remove the weight multiset at an extending embedding. The difference therefore remains n+1 and cannot be at most one.

    Discussion

    At n=2, a geometric pure realization would have Hodge types (3,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,3), each with multiplicity one. These are of Calabi–Yau-threefold type as Hodge numbers; this observation does not produce a Calabi–Yau threefold, and the separate transfer qualification at n=2 in LG3 remains in force.

    The useful geometric questions are consequently about higher cohomology, algebraic correspondences, and period comparisons. An explicit relation between quaternionic two-form periods and the Siegel realization would add information beyond the existence of an algebraic Hecke spectrum. Neither an algebraic cycle realizing that comparison nor a compatible identity of periods has been constructed here.
