    The classical transformation law, cuspidality, level and character of rank-four harmonic theta series.

    Modularity of Harmonic Theta Series in Rank Four

    An even integral positive-definite lattice of rank four and a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of positive even degree ℓ determine a theta series of weight 2+ℓ. We state the classical transformation law for its discriminant-coset components and the scalar modularity theorem, using the normalization Q(x)=(x,x)/2.

    Lattices and Theta Series#

    Let L⊂ℝ4 be a full lattice with integral Euclidean pairing (x,y) and even squared norms (x,x) for x∈L. Put

    L∗={y∈ℝ4:(y,L)⊆ℤ},D=L∗/L,Q(x)=(x,x)2.

    The quadratic form and pairing induce maps Q:D→ℚ/ℤ and (⋅,⋅):D×D→ℚ/ℤ.

    Let H:ℝ4→ℂ be a homogeneous polynomial of even degree ℓ>0 satisfying ΔH=0 for the Euclidean Laplacian. For μ∈D and Im⁡τ>0, set

    Θμ(τ)=∑x∈L+μH(x)e2πiτQ(x),Θ(τ)=(Θμ(τ))μ∈D.(1)

    Here L+μ means the coset represented by any lift of μ to L∗. We write ΘL,H=Θ0. Gaussian decay gives locally uniform convergence, also after differentiation in τ.

    Harmonic Theta Transformation#

    Theorem 1. Transformation and Cuspidality [5, Props. 2–3, Cor. 2]

    Put k=2+ℓ. The theta vector Θ is a holomorphic cusp form of weight k for the lattice Weil representation, with transformation laws

    Θμ(τ+1)=e2πiQ(μ)Θμ(τ),(2)
    Θμ(−1/τ)=−τk|D|∑η∈De−2πi(μ,η)Θη(τ).(3)

    Proof of Theorem 1

    The translation law follows from Q(x)≡Q(μ)(modℤ) for x∈L+μ. For the inversion law, apply harmonic Poisson summation [5, Thm. 2]. With Fourier transform ϕˆ(y)=∫ℝ4ϕ(x)e−2πi(x,y)dx, the harmonic Gaussian has transform

    H(x)eπiτ(x,x)ˆ(y)=(−iτ)−2τ−ℓH(y)e−πi(y,y)/τ.

    This follows by differentiating the Gaussian transform: the lower terms are contractions of derivatives of H and vanish because ΔH=0.

    Apply the shifted-lattice Poisson formula at −1/τ and group the dual lattice into cosets of L. Its covolume factor is |D|−1/2, and the Gaussian factor is −τ2+ℓ since ℓ is even. Replacing the dual coset η by −η gives the phase in (3), because Θ−η=Θη.

    Every component has zero constant term: positive definiteness leaves only x=0 as a possible norm-zero term, and H(0)=0. Equations (2)–(3) express each modular transform as a linear combination of these components. Their exponential decay at infinity therefore proves cuspidality.

    Scalar Modularity#

    Let A be a Gram matrix of L and let NL be its level: the least positive integer such that NLA−1 is integral with even diagonal. Write

    χL(d)=(det⁡Ad)(gcd⁡(d,NL)=1)

    for the associated Kronecker character.

    Theorem 2. Level and Character of the Zero Component [5, Thm. 4]

    The scalar theta series belongs to S2+ℓ(Γ0(NL),χL). In particular, for (abcd)∈Γ0(NL),

    ΘL,H(aτ+bcτ+d)=χL(d)(cτ+d)2+ℓΘL,H(τ).(4)

    It is holomorphic on the upper half-plane and vanishes at every cusp of Γ0(NL).

    M000053 · version 1

    Uses The lattice and harmonic polynomial in (1), the level NL and character χL.

    In the matrix formulation of the cited theorem, choose a basis matrix B of L, so A=BtB, and put P(u)=H(Bu). For ΔA=∑i,j(A−1)ij∂ui∂uj, one has ΔAP=0. The quadratic exponent is utAu/2, and the character is ((−1)4/2det⁡Ad)=χL(d). Thus the scalar theorem uses exactly the normalization in (1).

    For the application to quaternionic elliptic coefficients, see the Mellin formula and cubic-lattice calculation. The automorphic representation of the weight-six example is treated with that example.
