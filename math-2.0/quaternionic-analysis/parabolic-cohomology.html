    Expository

    How restriction to cusp groups detects the image of compactly supported cohomology on a hyperbolic four-manifold.

    Parabolic Cohomology and Compact Support

    A closed differential form on a noncompact manifold can carry cohomology out along its ends. For a finite-volume hyperbolic manifold, each end is a cusp, with a compact cross-section on which one can restrict the class. The classes whose restrictions vanish are precisely those that admit compactly supported closed representatives. Group cohomology describes the same condition by restriction to the cusp subgroups; its kernel is called parabolic cohomology.

    This gives a useful target for period constructions. If a closed two-form with flat coefficients produces a group two-cocycle, one can test its parabolicity by finding a one-cochain primitive on each cusp group. The theorem below explains why this test detects compact support at the level of cohomology. Its proof also describes how to remove a form's tails in the cusps.

    The Geometry of the Ends#

    Let ℍ4 be real hyperbolic four-space, and let Γ⊂Isom⁡(ℍ4) be a discrete torsion-free group such that

    X=Γ\ℍ4

    is of finite volume. The metric on X is complete. Choose one parabolic fixed point ξ at infinity for each cusp, and write

    Γξ={γ∈Γ:γξ=ξ}

    for its full stabilizer.

    The cusp decomposition gives finitely many ends with cross-sections Yξ=Γξ\Sξ, where Sξ is a horosphere isometric to Euclidean three-space. Each Yξ is compact and flat. Truncation gives a compact core; reparametrizing its attached cusp collars gives a compact manifold X with interior X and boundary

    ∂X=∐[ξ]Yξ.

    The compactification has the same homotopy type as X. The geometric input is the finite-volume cusp theorem: [3, §4.2.5, Prop. 4.2.17, Cor. 4.2.18]. When X is compact, take X=X and an empty boundary.

    Fix a finite-dimensional complex vector space E and a representation ρ:Γ→GL⁡(E). It defines the flat bundle

    ℰ=Γ\(ℍ4×E),γ⋅(z,v)=(γz,ρ(γ)v).

    A lifted section is therefore an E-valued function satisfying f(γz)=ρ(γ)f(z). In a local parallel frame, the flat differential d∇ is the ordinary differential applied to each component. The same local system extends over X: on a cusp product, parallel transport along the interval identifies it with the pullback of its restriction to Yξ.

    The Two Cohomological Conditions#

    Definition 1. Interior Cohomology

    Let ℰ be a finite-dimensional complex flat bundle on a smooth manifold X, with flat differential d∇. Write Ωq(X,ℰ) for its smooth differential q-forms and Ωcq(X,ℰ) for the forms with compact support. Set both spaces to zero in negative degree. Define

    Hq(X,ℰ)=ker⁡(d∇:Ωq→Ωq+1)d∇Ωq−1,Hcq(X,ℰ)=ker⁡(d∇:Ωcq→Ωcq+1)d∇Ωcq−1.

    The inclusion of complexes induces the forget-support map

    jc:Hcq(X,ℰ)⟶Hq(X,ℰ).

    The interior cohomology is its image:

    H!q(X,ℰ)=im⁡(jc)⊆Hq(X,ℰ).

    M000001 · version 1

    Thus H!2 consists of ordinary classes with a compactly supported closed representative. The map jc can lose information: a compactly supported form may have an ordinary primitive but no compactly supported primitive. The exact sequence in the theorem will identify this kernel.

    For group cohomology we use left coefficients, so γ acts on E as ρ(γ). An inhomogeneous one-cochain is any function b:Γ→E, and a two-cochain is any function c:Γ2→E. Their differentials in the relevant degrees are

    (δb)(g,h)=ρ(g)b(h)−b(gh)+b(g),
    (δc)(g,h,k)=ρ(g)c(h,k)−c(gh,k)+c(g,hk)−c(g,h).

    The equation δc=0 defines two-cocycles, and H2(Γ,E) is their quotient by the two-coboundaries δb. Restriction of functions defines the maps for subgroups.

    Definition 2. Parabolic Cohomology

    Let Γ⊂Isom⁡(ℍ4) be discrete and torsion-free with finite-volume quotient, let E be a finite-dimensional complex left Γ-module, and let Γξ be the full stabilizer of a chosen point representing each cusp. The degree-two parabolic cohomology is

    Hpar2(Γ,E)=ker⁡(H2(Γ,E)→res⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,E)).

    For an empty set of cusps the target is zero.

    M000002 · version 1

    For a two-cocycle c, the condition in this definition is the existence, for every cusp, of a single function bξ:Γξ→E satisfying

    c(u,v)=ρ(u)bξ(v)−bξ(uv)+bξ(u)(u,v∈Γξ).(1)

    The same bξ must work for all pairs in that cusp group. Replacing c by c+δa replaces a possible primitive by bξ+a|Γξ, so this is a condition on the cohomology class.

    Parabolic Cohomology Is Interior Cohomology#

    The contractible universal cover of X identifies group cohomology with cohomology of the flat bundle. Denote this comparison by

    Φq:Hq(Γ,E)→ ∼ Hq(X,ℰ).

    Likewise, the universal cover Sξ≃ℝ3 identifies Hq(Γξ,E) with Hq(Yξ,ℰ|Yξ). These comparisons carry subgroup restriction to restriction on the cusp cross-sections, as we verify in the proof.

    Theorem 3. The Cusp Restriction Exact Sequence [1, §§8, 15]; [2, Thm. I.7.5, §§III.1, III.8]; [3, Prop. 4.2.17, Cor. 4.2.18]

    Let Γ⊂Isom⁡(ℍ4) be a discrete torsion-free group with finite-volume quotient X, and let E be a finite-dimensional complex representation of Γ, with associated flat bundle ℰ. Use the full cusp stabilizers Γξ. Under the comparison Φ2:H2(Γ,E)≃H2(X,ℰ),

    Φ2(Hpar2(Γ,E))=H!2(X,ℰ).(2)

    More precisely, the relative cohomology sequence of a cusp compactification gives the exact sequence

    H1(X,ℰ)→res⨁[ξ]H1(Γξ,E)→∂Hc2(X,ℰ)→jcH2(X,ℰ)→res⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,E).(3)

    Here res is subgroup restriction transported by the comparisons, and ∂ is the relative connecting homomorphism transported to compactly supported cohomology. In particular,

    ker⁡(jc)=im⁡(∂),H!2(X,ℰ)≃Hc2(X,ℰ)/im⁡(∂).(4)

    Uses Interior cohomology, parabolic cohomology, and the cusp decomposition.

    Proof of Theorem 3[2, Thm. I.7.5, §§III.1, III.8]; [1, §§8, 15]
    Proof detail

    Let Y=∂X. A cusp cross-section lifts to the equivariant inclusion Sξ↪ℍ4, so the comparisons from group cohomology to cohomology with local coefficients carry subgroup restriction to restriction on Y. Here the contractible covers give free resolutions ([2, Thm. I.7.5, §III.1]), and the Čech double-complex argument gives the de Rham comparison with flat coefficients ([1, Thms. 8.9, 15.8]). The expanded comparison explains the coefficient extension and naturality.

    The relative de Rham complex consists of forms on X whose pullbacks to Y vanish. Extension by zero includes the compactly supported forms on X in this complex. On a collar, integration in the normal direction gives a homotopy from the identity to pullback from Y. Applied to a closed relative form, it gives a primitive on the collar that vanishes at Y. Subtracting the differential of a cutoff times this primitive makes the form vanish near Y, without changing its relative class. The same adjustment to a relative primitive proves injectivity. Thus extension by zero identifies Hc2(X,ℰ) with H2(X,Y;ℰ), compatibly with their maps to ordinary cohomology.

    Every form on Y extends to X by parallel transport along a collar followed by a cutoff. Restriction is therefore surjective in each degree. Its kernel is the relative complex, so the associated long exact sequence, under the comparisons just described, is (3). Exactness identifies the kernel of cusp restriction with im⁡(jc) and the kernel of jc with im⁡(∂). This proves (2) and (4).

    The resulting condition has concrete representatives on both sides. A group cocycle restricts to zero in cohomology exactly when it has a one-cochain primitive as in (1). For a closed form with exact restriction to every cusp cross-section, the interval homotopy extends a primitive along each cusp. Subtracting its differential after multiplication by a cutoff equal to one sufficiently far out removes the tails. The resulting closed form has compact support and represents the original ordinary class.

    We first identify the restriction map in the relative cohomology sequence with cusp-group restriction. Keep the notation ℰ for the extended local system on X, and push the boundary a short distance into X along the collars. The resulting inclusion of a cusp cross-section lifts to the Γξ-equivariant inclusion Sξ↪ℍ4. Since both covering spaces are contractible, it induces the inclusion Γξ⊂Γ on fundamental groups, with the chosen lifts fixing the coefficient identifications.

    First compare group cohomology with singular cohomology. If Z is X or a cross-section, and G is its deck group, the augmented singular chain complex C∗(Z~;ℂ) is a free resolution of the trivial ℂ[G]-module: the action on lifted simplices is free, and the augmented complex is exact because Z~ is contractible. Thus

    Homℂ[G]⁡(C∗(Z~;ℂ),E)

    computes H∗(G,E) by the comparison theorem for projective resolutions ([2, Thm. I.7.5, p. 24]). It is also the singular cochain complex with local coefficients: a cochain assigns a vector to each lifted simplex, equivariantly under the deck group. We use the differential δu=u∘∂; Brown's signed Hom-complex convention has the same cocycles and coboundaries (§III.1, pp.57–59).

    For a subgroup H⊂G, a free ℂ[G]-resolution remains free after restriction to H. The map on lifted chains therefore computes subgroup restriction, by the uniqueness up to chain homotopy in the same comparison theorem. This is the functoriality described in [2, §III.8, pp. 78–80].

    To pass from singular cohomology to differential forms, we use a common Čech complex. Choose a locally finite good cover 𝒰 of Z: every nonempty finite intersection is contractible. Let ℒ denote the local system of parallel sections of ℰ. The Čech cochain space

    Cˇp(𝒰,ℒ)=∏i0<⋯<ipℒ(Ui0∩⋯∩Uip)

    has differential given by alternating restriction. For differential forms, consider the double complex ∏i0<⋯<ipΩq(Ui0∩⋯∩Uip,ℰ), with total differential δˇ+(−1)pd∇. Its augmented rows are exact by the partition-of-unity homotopy. Its columns have cohomology only in degree zero, where they are parallel sections: each intersection has a parallel frame and the Poincaré lemma applies componentwise. Computing total cohomology in the two orders identifies de Rham cohomology with H∗(Cˇ∗(𝒰,ℒ)).

    For singular cochains with local coefficients, form the analogous Čech double complex. Use chains whose simplices lie in a member of the cover. Subdivision and its prism homotopy identify their cohomology with ordinary singular cohomology. The augmented Čech rows are exact: for each such simplex, the nonempty set of cover members containing it gives a contractible augmented simplicial complex. On an intersection, the coefficient system is constant and contractibility gives cohomology only in degree zero, again equal to its parallel sections. Thus the same Čech complex computes singular cohomology.

    These are the double-complex arguments of [1, Prop. 8.8, Thm. 8.9, Prop. 15.2, Thm. 15.8]. Their stated comparison theorems use constant coefficients; the argument above explains the extension to the flat bundle used here. On overlaps, changes of parallel frame are locally constant and commute with differentiation and coefficient transport. No global trivialization is required.

    Restriction and refinement give maps of these augmented complexes. For the cross-section inclusion, refine its cover by the inverse images of a cover of X; the resulting pullback maps commute with both augmentations. A common refinement compares any two choices. Hence the comparison respects pullback to each cusp cross-section. Finally, pushing the boundary into a collar identifies the ordinary cohomology of X with that of its interior and identifies boundary restriction with restriction to that cross-section. This gives the absolute and boundary comparisons in (3).

    It remains to identify the relative term with compactly supported cohomology. Let i:∂X↪X denote boundary inclusion. Define

    Ωq(X,∂X;ℰ)={ω∈Ωq(X,ℰ):i∗ω=0}.(5)

    We use the cohomology of (5) as the relative de Rham group Hq(X,∂X;ℰ). A compactly supported form on X extends smoothly by zero near the boundary, giving a map into this complex.

    We prove that this map induces an isomorphism in degree two. Choose a collar Y×[0,ε), where Y=∂X, with boundary coordinate s=0. Parallel transport identifies the bundle there with the pullback of ℰ|Y. In this identification write a form as

    ω=α(s)+ds∧β(s),(Kω)(s)=∫0sβ(u)du.

    The operator K lowers degree by one. If p:Y×[0,ε)→Y is projection, differentiation and the fundamental theorem of calculus give

    d∇Kω+Kd∇ω=ω−p∗i∗ω.(6)

    The integral uses parallel transport along the interval, so it is defined for a flat bundle with arbitrary monodromy on Y.

    For a closed relative two-form ω, (6) says ω=d∇Kω on the collar. Choose a smooth scalar function χ equal to one near s=0 and supported in the collar. Extend χKω by zero outside the collar. Its boundary pullback is zero because Kω is zero at s=0. Therefore

    ω0=ω−d∇(χKω)(7)

    is in the same relative class and vanishes on a neighborhood of the boundary. Since X is compact, ω0|X has compact support. This proves surjectivity.

    For injectivity, suppose a compactly supported closed two-form ω0 is d∇η for a relative one-form η on X. On a sufficiently small collar, ω0=0, so d∇η=0 there. Since i∗η=0, (6) gives η=d∇Kη on that collar. With χ supported there, replace η by

    η0=η−d∇(χKη).

    Then η0 vanishes near the boundary and d∇η0=ω0. It is the required compactly supported primitive. Both directions used the inclusion of the compact-support complex, so the comparison takes the relative-to-absolute map to jc.

    We can now apply the relative cohomology sequence. Extending a form on Y by parallel transport along the collar, and multiplying by a cutoff equal to one at Y, proves degreewise surjectivity of i∗. Hence there is a short exact sequence of complexes

    0⟶Ω∗(X,Y;ℰ)⟶Ω∗(X,ℰ)→i∗Ω∗(Y,ℰ)⟶0.(8)

    Its long exact sequence contains

    H1(X,ℰ)⟶H1(Y,ℰ)→∂H2(X,Y;ℰ)⟶H2(X,ℰ)⟶H2(Y,ℰ).

    The group and de Rham comparisons identify the absolute and boundary terms, while extension by zero identifies the relative term with Hc2(X,ℰ). The resulting sequence is (3). In particular, its last map has kernel Φ2(Hpar2(Γ,E)), whereas exactness identifies that kernel with im⁡(jc).

    The connecting map has an explicit description. Represent a class of H1(Y,ℰ) by a closed form θ and extend it to θ~ on X. Then d∇θ~ is relative, and

    ∂[θ]=[d∇θ~]rel.(9)

    A different extension changes this by the differential of a relative form. A change of θ by an exact boundary form also leaves the relative class unchanged. Thus (9) is well-defined. Its absolute class is zero, and exactness says that every element of ker⁡(jc) arises this way. Exactness at H1(Y,ℰ) further says that a boundary class contributes zero under ∂ exactly when it extends as a cohomology class over X.

    Finally, we describe the condition in terms of cocycles and forms. For a group cocycle, the restriction of [c] is zero precisely when c|Γξ2=δbξ for some one-cochain, which is (1).

    For the form construction, let α be any closed two-form representing a class with zero cusp restrictions. Write a cusp end as Yξ×[0,∞), with height t increasing toward infinity, and let i0 be inclusion at t=0. Vanishing of the restriction gives a one-form θξ on Yξ such that

    i0∗α=d∇θξ.

    Use the same interval homotopy as in (6), now integrating from 0 to t. On the end it gives

    βξ=p∗θξ+Kα,d∇βξ=α.

    Choose a smooth χξ(t) equal to zero near t=0 and equal to one for all sufficiently large t. Extend χξβξ by zero across the base of the end. There are finitely many ends, so the form

    αc=α−d∇(∑[ξ]χξβξ)(10)

    is smooth, closed, compactly supported, and cohomologous to α. Every integral in this construction has a finite interval of integration. Thus the construction requires no decay estimate at infinity. It realizes the lift supplied by the relative exact sequence. Different choices can give different compact-support classes, but (4) identifies their difference as an element of im⁡(∂).

    Why the Whole Cusp Group Matters#

    Example. A Two-Cocycle on a Torus Cusp

    For a cusp group ℤ3 with trivial coefficients ℂ, use additive notation and set

    c(u,v)=u1v2.

    Bilinearity gives δc=0. A two-coboundary for this trivial action has the form b(v)−b(u+v)+b(u) and is symmetric in u,v. Our cocycle is not symmetric: c(e1,e2)=1 while c(e2,e1)=0. Consequently its class on ℤ3 is nonzero.

    Nevertheless it becomes a coboundary on every cyclic subgroup. For a nonzero a∈ℤ3, define on the subgroup generated by a

    b(ka)=−k(k−1)2a1a2(k∈ℤ).

    Then (δb)(ka,ℓa)=kℓa1a2=c(ka,ℓa). The zero subgroup causes no difficulty. Thus cyclic tests do not detect the two-dimensional obstruction on a cusp cross-section.

    The Role in Automorphic Cohomology#

    Suppose an automorphic construction supplies a closed two-form α with flat coefficients and a period two-cocycle c satisfying Φ2([c])=[α]. If α restricts to an exact form on every cusp cross-section, the cutoff formula (10) gives a compactly supported representative of [α], and [c] lies in parabolic cohomology. Equivalently, one can check this boundary condition by finding a one-cochain primitive for c on each full cusp group, as in equation (1).
