    Harmonic Theta Kernels and Quaternionic Coefficient Relations at Discriminant Five

    The coefficients of quaternionic elliptic functions are harmonic lattice sums. Their complete fixed-order family satisfies universal tensor compatibility, and a lattice's isometries impose further relations. This article exhibits a relation that survives both reductions: a nonzero harmonic weight fixed by every lattice isometry whose entire theta series vanishes.

    We work in the definite rational quaternion algebra

    B=(−2,−5)ℚ,u2=−2,v2=−5,uv=−vu,

    with the embedding u=2i, v=5j, uv=10k. Fix

    𝒪=ℤ⟨1,u,1+u+v2,2+u−uv4⟩,α=1+u+v2,I=α𝒪.(AT.1)

    This is a principal right ideal of a maximal order in the discriminant-five algebra, whose ideal class number is one. Our derivative indices refer to the fixed Euclidean coordinates q=t+xi+yj+zk:

    G(q)=q‾|q|4,Gν=∂xν1∂yν2∂zν3G,Eν(I)=∑λ∈I∖{0}Gν(λ).

    There is no division by ν!. For |ν|>1 the sum converges absolutely. Write [z]i,[z]j,[z]k for the real quaternionic coordinates of z.

    Theorem AT.1. For the marked ideal I in (AT.1),

    2[E(0,4,1)(I)]i+3[E(1,0,4)(I)]k+[E(2,0,3)(I)]i=0.(AT.2)

    The corresponding harmonic numerator has nonzero average under the full isometry group of I. Thus its vanishing lattice sum is an additional relation after imposing universal coefficient compatibility and lattice-isometry constraints.

    Theorem AT.1 · version 1

    Uses (AT.1), Theorem AT.3, Proposition AT.5.

    Proof of Theorem AT.1

    By Proposition AT.5, the full symmetry average of its numerator is a nonzero multiple of the even kernel weight of Theorem AT.3. That weight has zero sum on every norm shell. All three summands have denominator |λ|14, and absolute convergence permits summation over shells. This gives (AT.2). The nonzero average, and the explicit regular counterexample in the proof of Proposition AT.5, establish the final distinction.

    The proof identifies a five-dimensional proper-invariant harmonic space whose theta map has rank three. One kernel direction is odd under quaternionic conjugation; the other is invariant under every isometry. In weighted Brandt coordinates these are the off-diagonal coefficients between two distinct Hecke eigensystems. Classical harmonic theta modularity and Brandt theory supply the framework; the explicit kernel and its translation into (AT.2) are the calculation developed here. The exact certificate supplies the finite polynomial and lattice data used in the proofs.

    Norm Lattices and Harmonic Weights#

    For a fractional right ideal J in a definite rational quaternion algebra, let nJ>0 generate the fractional ideal of ℚ generated by its reduced norms. Write

    QJ(x)=nrd⁡(x)nJ.

    An algebraic quaternionic frame identifies the real norm space with ℍ. For a harmonic polynomial P in that frame, the raw ideal theta series is

    ΘJ,P(τ)=∑x∈JP(x)qτQJ(x),qτ=e2πiτ.

    The scalar i is central. Quaternion-valued polynomials are interpreted componentwise in ℍ⊗ℝℂ. The polynomial is evaluated on the unscaled ideal.

    For the numerator Hν(q)=|q|2r+4Gν(q), r=|ν|, the harmonic numerator formula gives degree r+1. The Mellin identity therefore reads

    Eν(J)=nJ−r−2L(ΘJ,Hν,r+2)(r≥3 odd).(AT.3)

    This normalization differs from using Q=|q|2/2 on an arbitrary Euclidean lattice: here the norm generator is part of the ideal data. At the ideal I in (AT.1), nI=2.

    Let SO(J) and O(J) denote its proper and full Euclidean lattice isometry groups. For either finite group ΓJ, the Reynolds average |ΓJ|−1∑g∈ΓJP∘g has the same norm-shell sums as P. This reduces theta computations to invariant harmonic weights. The universal tensor relations concern a different reduction: they identify the independent harmonic components before a lattice is chosen.

    For maximal orders at prime discriminant, the normalized norm form has the level and discriminant described by Pizer [3, Prop. 2.11]. We verify them directly in the present example below. The harmonic theta theorem then gives weight r+3, level five and trivial character; its application uses the even Gram matrix for the normalized norm.

    The Ideal and an Order-Three Coefficient#

    The algebra B is ramified at five and infinity. Its maximal-order class number is

    5−112+1−(−45)4+1−(−35)3=1

    by the classical class-number formula [3, Thm. 1.12]. The order in (AT.1) is closed under multiplication and has reduced discriminant five. These assertions, and the equality

    I=ℤ⟨2,2u,1+u+v2,6+5u−uv4⟩,

    are checked by rational multiplication and an integral unimodular basis change in the certificate. In this ideal basis, the Gram matrix of QI=N/2, where N=nrd⁡=|q|2, is

    AI=(4013082512223526),QI(z)=12ztAIz,det⁡AI=25.

    The matrix 5AI−1 is even integral and AI−1 is not integral. Thus the level is five; the character is trivial because the determinant is a square.

    Ordinary conjugation does not preserve I: its third basis vector has trace one, whereas 1∉I. Nevertheless the ideal has an orientation-reversing symmetry

    jI(x)=αα−1x.

    This is the transported conjugation of 𝒪, and satisfies jI2=1, jI(I)=I and N(jIx)=N(x). Failure of ordinary conjugation invariance does not mean absence of reflection symmetries.

    Define

    f5(τ)=η(τ)4η(5τ)45E2(5τ)−E2(τ)4,

    where η is Dedekind's eta function and E2(τ)=1−24∑n≥1σ1(n)qτn.

    Proposition AT.2. The form f5 is the normalized generator of S6(Γ0(5)), and

    ΘI,H(1,2,0)=(−602−12i+2410j+125k)f5.

    Consequently

    E(1,2,0)(I)=38L(f5,5)(−52−i+210j+5k),L(f5,5)>0.

    Proposition AT.2 · version 1

    Uses (AT.3), harmonic theta modularity.

    Proof of Proposition AT.2

    The eta-quotient criterion [1, Thms. 2.4–2.5] puts η(τ)4η(5τ)4 in S4(Γ0(5)): both congruence sums are 24, the character is trivial, and both cusp orders are one. The usual transformation formula for E2 [4, Thm. 5.8] cancels its nonmodular terms in 5E2(5τ)−E2(τ); this combination is holomorphic of weight two, including at the cusps. Thus f5∈S6(Γ0(5)). The dimension formula [2, Thm. 3.5.1], with genus zero, two cusps and two elliptic points of order two, gives −5+4+2=1.

    Let C=−602−12i+2410j+125k. Exact enumeration of the first three ideal norm shells gives theta coefficients C,2C,−4C. The eta expression begins qτ+2qτ2−4qτ3. Both sides are cusp forms of weight six and level five. The coefficient bound ⌊6⋅6/12⌋=3 proves equality [4, Thm. 9.18]; the certificate lists the finite shell calculation over ℚ(2,5). Equation (AT.3) gives the factor 2−5.

    On the imaginary axis the eta factors are positive. Moreover

    5E2(5τ)−E2(τ)4=1+6∑n≥1(σ1(n)−5σ1(n/5))qτn,

    where σ1(n/5)=0 unless 5|n. For n=5am with 5∤m, the expression in parentheses is σ1(m)>0. The Mellin integral for L(f5,5) is therefore strictly positive.

    For example, the ratio of the k and i components is −5. The proper-invariant harmonic quartic space has dimension one, as the certificate verifies. All its theta values therefore lie on one algebraic coefficient line. In degree six this space is larger, and the theta map itself supplies additional relations.

    The Degree-Six Theta Kernel#

    Transport polynomials from the ideal to its order by x=αa. Since N(α)=2,

    ∑x∈IP(x)qτN(x)/2=∑a∈𝒪P(αa)qτN(a).

    This similarity preserves harmonicity and identifies the proper and full isometry groups by conjugation. We can therefore compute the kernel on 𝒪.

    Write Γ+=SO(𝒪), ΓO=O(𝒪), and let ℋ6 be the harmonic sextics on the norm space of B. In rational coordinates a=t+xu+yv+zuv, harmonicity means

    (∂t2+12∂x2+15∂y2+110∂z2)P=0.

    The theta map is

    ϑ:ℋ6Γ+⟶S8(Γ0(5)),P⟼∑a∈𝒪P(a)qτN(a).

    Theorem AT.3. The proper and full isometry groups have orders 36 and 72, respectively, and

    dim⁡ℋ6Γ+=5,dim⁡ℋ6ΓO=4,rank⁡ϑ=3,dim⁡ker⁡ϑ=2.

    The kernel splits into a conjugation-even line fixed by the full group and a conjugation-odd line. Proper-group averages of the mixed order-five Fueter numerator components span all five proper-invariant directions.

    Theorem AT.3 · version 1

    Uses harmonic theta modularity, the exact certificate.

    Proof of Theorem AT.3

    The certificate supplies generators for Γ+ and conjugation, and verifies that their groups exhaust the integral isometries of the norm Gram matrix. In the 84-dimensional monomial space of sextics, the rational linear equations for harmonicity and invariance under the proper generators have rank 79. Adding conjugation invariance gives rank 80. Thus the dimensions are five and four.

    For the specified invariant basis P1,…,P5, the first four norm-shell rows are

    (0000636000036−720−72−324721440144816).(AT.4)

    The first three rows have rank three. Its two-dimensional kernel is generated in this basis by (0,0,1,0,0)t and (0,−1,0,1,0)t. Each corresponding theta series is a weight-eight level-five cusp form whose first four coefficients vanish. The bound ⌊8⋅6/12⌋=4 therefore makes both series identically zero [4, Thm. 9.18]. This proves the asserted rank and kernel dimension over ℚ, and hence over ℝ or ℂ.

    Conjugation has eigenspace dimensions four and one. Every odd weight cancels on each norm shell, so the odd line is in the kernel. The remaining kernel line is even. Finally, the certificate expresses five independent averaged mixed numerator components in the invariant basis, proving that they span the full domain.

    The odd kernel has the particularly short generator

    P−(t+xu+yv+zuv)=−ty(t−x+5z)(t+x−5z)(x−y+z)(x+y+z).

    The even generator will be specified by a small matrix below, avoiding a long sextic in the main text. Its expanded coefficients are included in the certificate.

    Weighted Brandt Matrices#

    Let V be the harmonic cubics on the trace-zero norm space B0, and let

    (ρ(a)F)(y)=F(a‾ya).

    This seven-dimensional multiplicative representation is the degree-six quaternionic representation. Its matrix entries are harmonic sextics in a, as in the classical construction [3, Prop. 2.10]. The order has six units. Put

    W=V𝒪×,Π=16∑ε∈𝒪×ρ(ε),C(a)=Πρ(a)|W.

    The class number one identifies W with the weighted Brandt module. We use columns for matrices in the following basis of W, writing y=Xu+Yv+Zuv:

    F1=−X2Z+3XY2−6XZ2−2Y2Z+3Z3,F2=X2Y+2XYZ−Y3+YZ2,F3=X3−15XZ2−15Y2Z+10Z3.

    Define

    B(n)=16∑a∈𝒪N(a)=nC(a).

    The division by six averages the unit generators. In particular B(1)=1. The matrix entries of ∑B(n)qτn are weight-eight level-five cusp forms by Pizer's harmonic Brandt construction [3, Def. 2.13 and Thm. 2.14]. The good-prime Hecke action is supplied by [3, Props. 2.22–2.23].

    The finite norm-shell calculation gives

    T:=B(2)=(210150−140−30−1),B(5)=diag⁡(−125,125,−125),

    and

    B(3)=78+2T−12T2,B(4)=T2−128,B(5)=−113−10T+12T2.

    Constants in these formulas denote scalar matrices. The Fischer inner product on W has positive Gram matrix

    GW=(107/125033/25024/125033/25027/5).

    It makes the displayed Brandt matrices self-adjoint.

    Let ℰ={A∈Endℚ⁡(W):[A,B(5)]=0} and put PA(a)=tr⁡(AC(a)). The matrix entries of C(a) are explicitly determined by the three cubics and the unit average; the certificate records their polynomial expansion.

    Proposition AT.4. The map A↦PA identifies ℰ with ℋ6Γ+, and

    ker⁡ϑ≃[T,ℰ].

    In the basis F1,F2,F3, this kernel consists of

    (a0223a+5b000b0−a)(a,b∈ℚ).

    Proposition AT.4 · version 1

    Uses Theorem AT.3, the displayed Brandt matrices.

    Proof of Proposition AT.4

    The polynomial coefficient matrix of the nine entries of C(a) has rank nine. Restricting A to the five-dimensional commutant of B(5) gives five independent polynomials invariant under the proper group. Exact substitution in the certificate verifies these claims. The dimension calculation of Theorem AT.3 therefore proves the identification. By definition,

    ϑ(PA)=6∑n≥1tr⁡(AB(n))qτn.(AT.5)

    The first four coefficients of [T,∑B(n)qτn] vanish by the displayed formulas. Every entry is a weight-eight level-five cusp form, so the four-coefficient bound proves [T,B(n)]=0 for every n. This step includes multiples of five and does not extend the good-prime theorem by assumption.

    The characteristic polynomial of T is (X+14)(X2−20X+24), with three distinct roots. Every B(n) is therefore diagonal in its eigenbasis. The matrices 1,T,B(3) span the full diagonal algebra, since B(3)=78+2T−T2/2. Within ℰ, the matrices orthogonal to this algebra under the trace pairing are exactly the two off-diagonal entries in the two-dimensional B(5)-eigenspace of eigenvalue −125. They form [T,ℰ]. Equation (AT.5) proves the kernel assertion. Solving the three trace equations in the original rational basis gives the displayed matrices.

    Choose

    A+=(105024750003410−105),A−=(−1650−6750001070165),

    and fix the even polynomial's normalization by

    P+(a)=17500tr⁡(A+C(a)).

    The certificate verifies PA−=−30000P−. Since GWA+=A+tGW and GWA−=−A−tGW, and quaternionic conjugation sends C(a) to its adjoint, these generators are respectively even and odd.

    Over ℚ(19), the eigenvalues of T are −14 and 10±219. The latter two have the same B(5) eigenvalue −125. The kernel consists of the two cross terms between their eigenlines: the adjoint-symmetric combination gives P+, and the skew-adjoint combination gives P−. This explains why one kernel direction persists after averaging over the full lattice isometry group.

    From the Even Kernel to the Mixed Relation#

    Transport the normalized even generator to the ideal by

    K+(q)=P+(α−1q).

    For |ν|=5, let Hν(q)=|q|14Gν(q) in the Euclidean coordinates fixed at the start. Define

    Hmix(q)=2[H(0,4,1)(q)]i+3[H(1,0,4)(q)]k+[H(2,0,3)(q)]i.

    Proposition AT.5. For the full isometry group of I,

    172∑g∈O(I)Hmix(gq)=−120005K+(q).(AT.6)

    The right-hand side is nonzero. Every norm-shell sum of Hmix is zero.

    Proposition AT.5 · version 1

    Uses Proposition AT.4, (AT.1).

    Proof of Proposition AT.5

    The kernel differentiation recurrence (EM.1) gives

    Hmix(t,x,y,z)=5760xz(2t4+3t2x2−7t2z2+x4−x2y2−4x2z2+6y4−11y2z2+3z4).

    Pull this polynomial back by q=αa, express a=t+xu+yv+zuv in the order basis, and substitute the 72 integral isometries given in the certificate. Dividing their polynomial sum by 72 gives −120005P+(a) coefficient by coefficient. The certificate supplies the polynomial coefficients, generating matrices and exact verification of this identity. Since A+≠0 and A↦PA is injective, the average is nonzero.

    The norm shells are stable under all isometries, so averaging preserves each shell sum. The theta series of K+ equals that of P+ after q=αa, because N(q)/2=N(a). It vanishes by Proposition AT.4. Thus every shell sum of Hmix is zero.

    This identity is additional to local Fueter compatibility. For example the function F(z)=G(1+i+k+z) is left and right regular near zero, whereas the same combination of its fifth derivatives is

    2[∂y4∂zF(0)]i+3[∂x∂z4F(0)]k+[∂x2∂z3F(0)]i=−1280243.

    Substitution into the displayed polynomial, followed by division by |1+i+k|14=37, verifies the value. The nonzero symmetry average also shows that isometry cancellation alone does not account for the relation.

    Equation (AT.6) is an identity after averaging; the unaveraged polynomial is not a multiple of K+. All terms in (AT.2) have denominator |λ|14 and degree −8 as functions of λ. Their shell sums can therefore be summed absolutely, completing the route from the polynomial identity to the elliptic coefficients.

    Surviving Special Values#

    The three surviving theta directions correspond to the three diagonal coefficients in the T-eigenbasis. Their normalized forms begin

    f0=qτ−14qτ2−48qτ3+68qτ4+125qτ5+⋯,f±=qτ+(10±219)qτ2+(10∓1619)qτ3+(48±4019)qτ4−125qτ5+⋯.

    The good-prime Brandt action identifies their Hecke eigensystems; the displayed bad-prime matrix gives the remaining coefficient at five. The dimension formula gives dim⁡S8(Γ0(5))=3 and dim⁡S8(SL2(ℤ))=0, so there are no oldforms at this level.

    The order-five coefficient family is an algebraic linear combination of L(f0,7) and L(f±,7), with the raw-ideal factor 2−7 from (AT.3). The relation proved above kills an entire theta series before taking its Mellin value. It does not compare these three surviving periods or identify their ratios. The contrast with the one-dimensional weight-six example is therefore a statement about the theta map and its arithmetic constituents, not a conclusion about transcendence or period proportionality.
