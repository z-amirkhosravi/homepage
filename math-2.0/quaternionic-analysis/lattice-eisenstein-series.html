    Eisenstein and harmonic-theta formulas for convergent quaternionic elliptic coefficients, including an explicit cubic-lattice critical value.

    Eisenstein and Mellin Formulas for Quaternionic Elliptic Coefficients

    Discussion

    Varying a four-dimensional lattice turns the elliptic coefficients into automorphic functions on a special linear group. We identify both their inducing data and the irreducible module they generate. This is a different group action from the Möbius action on the ball.

    Harmonic numerators of the kernel derivatives#

    Lemma EI1. Harmonic numerators in every derivative order

    For ν∈ℕ03, r=|ν|, and N(q)=|q|2, there is a quaternion-valued homogeneous polynomial Hν of degree r+1, with harmonic real components, such that

    Gν(q)=Hν(q)N(q)r+2.

    It obeys H0=q‾ and Hν+ej=N∂jHν−2(r+2)xjHν.

    Proof of Lemma EI1

    Start with H0=q‾. Differentiation gives the recurrence

    Hν+ej=N∂jHν−2(r+2)xjHν.(E3)

    It is polynomial and has degree r+2. If Hν is harmonic of degree r+1, then

    Δ(N∂jHν)=(4r+8)∂jHν,Δ(xjHν)=2∂jHν,

    so (E3) is harmonic. This proves the lemma for every order.

    Discussion

    The lattice sums Eν(L) and their relation to derivatives of the regular part of ℘L,j are established in N1–N3. They converge for r>1, and even r gives zero. We therefore focus on odd r≥3. Ordinary Taylor coefficients include the multi-index factorials.

    Homogeneous sections and Eisenstein summation#

    Set G4=SL4(ℝ), Γ=SL4(ℤ), and K4=SO(4). Vectors act on the right, and Lg=ℤ4g.

    For real w>4, write Iw for the space of smooth functions f:ℝ4∖{0}→ℂ such that

    f(tx)=|t|−wf(x)(x≠0, t∈ℝ×).

    The condition with t=−1 makes every f∈Iw even. The action of G4 is

    (R(g)f)(x)=f(xg).

    Homogeneity is preserved because (tx)g=t(xg), and R(g)R(h)=R(gh) follows from (xg)h=x(gh).

    Construction EI-C1. The lattice Eisenstein function

    For w>4 and f∈Iw, form

    ℰf(g)=∑m∈ℤ4∖{0}f(mg).

    EI-C1 · version 1

    Well-definedness: Convergence, smoothness, and equivariance in EI2.

    Let e4=(0,0,0,1) and

    P={(Ab0d):det⁡(A)d=1},Ff(g)=f(e4g).

    The primitive-vector regrouping in EI2 specializes, for a radial section, to the Epstein–Eisenstein identity [7, Lemma 2.9].

    Theorem EI2. A maximal-parabolic Eisenstein series

    For w>4, ℰf is smooth, left Γ-invariant, of moderate growth with all right derivatives, and satisfies ℰR(h)f(g)=ℰf(gh). Moreover

    ℰf(g)=2ζ(w)∑γ∈(P∩Γ)\ΓFf(γg).

    The section law is Ff(pg)=|d|−wFf(g). In normalized left-equivariant/right-translation induction, with δP(p)=|d|−4, the inducing character is χ(p)=|d|2−w. For an odd derivative order r≥3, the scalar components of Gν give w=r+3 and χ=|d|−(r+1).

    EI2 · version 1

    Uses homogeneous-function model, EI-C1.

    Proof of Theorem EI2

    The sum and all its g-derivatives converge uniformly on compact subsets of G4: differentiation preserves degree −w, and |mg|≫C|m| on a compact set C. Hence ℰf is smooth, left Γ-invariant, and

    ℰR(h)f(g)=ℰf(gh).(E6)

    Each scalar component of Gν is such an f, so EI-C1 includes the original coefficient sums. The bound |ℰf(g)|≤Cf‖g−1‖w∑m≠0|m|−w, and the same bound for right derivatives, gives moderate growth.

    Let P be the maximal parabolic consisting of matrices

    p=(Ab0d),det⁡(A)d=1,

    which stabilizes the row line ℝe4. Put Ff(g)=f(e4g). Then

    Ff(pg)=|d|−wFf(g),δP(p)=|d|−4.(E7)

    Every primitive integral row occurs as e4γ, and the stabilizer of the primitive line is P∩Γ. Grouping a nonzero row by its positive gcd and then identifying opposite primitive rows gives

    ℰf(g)=2ζ(w)∑γ∈(P∩Γ)\ΓFf(γg).(E8)

    This is a maximal-parabolic Eisenstein series in its region of absolute convergence. In the left-equivariant/right-translation convention F(pg)=δP(p)1/2χ(p)F(g), its normalized inducing character is

    χ(p)=|d|2−w=|d|−(r+1).(E9)

    Specifying the section law removes any ambiguity from authors who use the inverse character or opposite parabolic.

    The irreducible module selected by the coefficients#

    For even w≥6 put d=w−4, and let 𝒫d be the homogeneous degree-d polynomials on ℝ4, with action p(x)↦p(xg). Let dσ be Euclidean surface measure on S3.

    Definition EI-D2. The Vanishing-Moment Subspace

    For even w≥6, the vanishing-moment subspace of Iw is

    ℳw={f∈Iw:∫S3f(ω)p(ω)dσ(ω)=0 for every p∈𝒫d}.(E10)

    EI-D2 · version 1

    Write 𝒴l for complex harmonic homogeneous polynomials of degree l restricted to S3; Vl=Syml⁡ℂ2.

    Proposition EI3. Moment kernel and finite-dimensional quotient

    For even w≥6, the moment map gives an equivariant exact sequence

    0→ℳw→Iw→𝒫w−4∨→0.

    Its compact-finite kernel is ⨁j≥0𝒴w−2+2j. Every scalar derivative kernel of order r=w−3 lies in its minimal compact type 𝒴w−2.

    EI3 · version 1

    Uses EI-D2, EI1.

    Proof of Proposition EI3

    The pairing is bilinear, not Hermitian. Since the product has degree −4, angular change of variables under g∈SL4 shows

    ∫S3f(ωg)p(ωg)dσ(ω)=∫S3f(ω)p(ω)dσ(ω).

    Indeed for η=ωg/|ωg|, dσ(η)=|ωg|−4dσ(ω). Thus (E10) is invariant, and the pairing induces an exact sequence

    0⟶ℳw⟶Iw⟶𝒫w−4∨⟶0.(E11)

    Surjectivity follows from the nondegenerate spherical pairing on the finite-dimensional polynomial space. Harmonic decomposition of 𝒫w−4 gives

    (ℳw)K4=⨁j≥0𝒴w−2+2j,(E12)

    where the subscript means K4-finite vectors. The low harmonics 𝒴0,𝒴2,…,𝒴w−4 constitute the finite-dimensional quotient. By EI1, every scalar Fueter kernel derivative of order r=w−3 belongs to the minimal compact type 𝒴w−2 of this kernel.

    For the general real degenerate principal series, see [4, Thms. 1.1–1.2]. With normalized P3,1 induction, the restriction of |det⁡|w−2×1 to SL4(ℝ) has the section law |d|−w. EI4 gives the harmonic-transition argument for this restriction.

    Theorem EI4. Irreducibility and harmonic transitions

    The (𝔰𝔩4,SO(4))-module (ℳw)K4 is irreducible for every even w≥6. Its compact types are

    ⨁j≥0𝒴w−2+2j≃⨁j≥0Vw−2+2j⊠Vw−2+2j.

    EI4 · version 1

    Uses EI3, irreducibility of spherical harmonics under SO(4).

    Proof of Theorem EI4

    For a symmetric trace-free real matrix A, write

    QA(x)=xAxt,DAH=(xA)⋅∇H,TAH=∑ijAij∂i∂jH.

    Represent a compact type H∈𝒴l by fH(x)=|x|−w−lH(x). On S3 the infinitesimal action is

    dR(A)H=DAH−(w+l)QAH.(E13)

    It has harmonic degrees l+2,l,l−2. Its projection onto degree l−2 is exactly

    prl−2⁡dR(A)H=l+2−w4l(l+1)TAH.(E14)

    To see this, ΔDAH=2TAH and Δ2(QAH)=8TAH. Harmonic decomposition therefore gives degree-l−2 terms TAH/(2l) in DAH|S3 and TAH/[4l(l+1)] in (QAH)|S3, yielding (E14). The degree-l+2 term is −(w+l) times the top harmonic part of QAH.

    The lowering map vanishes precisely at l=w−2 and is nonzero at every subsequent degree in (E12); the raising map is nonzero at every degree. Nonzeroness can be seen over the complexified Lie algebra by taking H=(x1+ix2)l and quadratic forms (x1±ix2)2: the plus choice raises harmonically, the minus choice gives a nonzero TAH when l≥2. Each 𝒴l is an irreducible K4-module of multiplicity one. Any nonzero submodule contains a compact type; successive nonzero lowering and raising maps then give all of (E12). This proves irreducibility. The identification 𝒴l≃Vl⊠Vl follows from the usual Spin⁡(4) harmonic-polynomial representation; (−1,−1) acts trivially.

    Nonvanishing and the automorphic realization#

    Theorem EI5. The coefficient-generated automorphic module

    For odd r≥3, the scalar components of g↦Eν(ℤ4g), |ν|=r, generate an automorphic (𝔰𝔩4,SO(4))-module isomorphic to (ℳr+3)K4. The Eisenstein map is injective on this module. Its minimal compact type is 𝒴r+1.

    EI5 · version 1

    Uses EI1, EI2, EI4.

    Proof of Theorem EI5

    An intertwining sum could conceivably vanish on the entire module, so this needs a proof. Choose H∈𝒴w−2 with H(e4)≠0, and put

    ga=diag⁡(a,a,a,a−3),a→∞,fH(x)=|x|−2w+2H(x).

    The rows (0,0,0,k) contribute exactly 2ζ(w)a3wH(e4). All other rows satisfy |fH(x)|≤C|x|−w. For m′≠0,

    ∑k∈ℤ(a2|m′|2+a−6k2)−w/2≪a−w|m′|−w+a4−w|m′|1−w.

    Both sums over m′∈ℤ3∖0 converge for w>4. Consequently

    ℰfH(ga)=2ζ(w)a3wH(e4)+O(a4−w).(E15)

    The Eisenstein map is nonzero on the minimal type, hence injective on the irreducible module (E12). The scalar components of the order-r kernel derivatives contain a nonzero element of that type: for example ∂xrG(ix)=−i(−1)r(3)rx−r−3 for x>0. Its compact orbit spans the type and its enveloping-algebra orbit spans (E12).

    Discussion

    The fixed-order coefficients occupy the minimal compact type. General SL4 transformations generate the larger space (E12); they do not preserve the flat Fueter equation or act within the finite tuple of coefficients.

    This specifies an irreducible real-place Harish-Chandra module. It does not assert a unitary L2(Γ\G4) realization or a discrete series of SL4. The proof concerns the algebraic module of K-finite automorphic functions; a choice of globalization/topology or an adelic classification with finite-place constituents is additional work. A coefficient can vanish at a particular symmetric lattice even though the automorphic function is nonzero. For example cubic-lattice symmetries force numerous components to vanish.

    Corollary EI6. The first nonzero derivative order

    For r=3, the inducing degree is −6, the normalized character is |d|−4, the minimal compact type is V4⊠V4 of dimension 25, and the finite-dimensional quotient of I6 is 𝒫2∨ of dimension 10.

    EI6 · version 1

    Uses EI3, EI5.

    Proof of Corollary EI6

    Insert w=6 in EI3–EI5. The dimensions are (4+1)2=25 and (2+4−13)=10.

    Rotation covariance of the coefficient tuple#

    Proposition EI7. The coefficient action descends to the rotation group

    Let U−r−3 be the right-ℍ span of the order-r kernel derivatives, with r>1, and let ρ(a,b)f(q)=bf(a−1qb) for unit quaternions a,b. Set αL(f)=∑λ≠0f(λ). Then

    (σ(a,b)α)(f)=bα(ρ(a,b)−1f),αaLb−1=σ(a,b)αL.

    This is a genuine real representation on right-ℍ-linear functionals and factors through SO(4).

    EI7 · version 1

    Uses Convergent sums.

    Proof of Proposition EI7

    The identity G(a−1qb)=b−1G(q)a follows directly from the kernel formula. Differentiate it in any real coordinate directions. The chain rule expresses rotated order-r derivatives using all four real derivative directions, with constant right quaternion factors. The kernel is also right regular: direct differentiation gives ∂tG+∂xGi+∂yGj+∂zGk=0. This identity, and its derivatives, eliminate every time derivative in favor of spatial derivatives with right coefficients. Thus U−r−3 is invariant under ρ. The formula for ρ gives its group law and right-ℍ linearity. Reindexing the absolutely convergent sum gives the displayed identity. For composition, the two output factors multiply as b1b2, and the precomposition is ρ(k2−1)ρ(k1−1)=ρ((k1k2)−1). At (−1,−1), the input representation is −1, while the output also gets −1, so σ(−1,−1)α(f)=−α(−f)=α(f). The kernel of Spin⁡(4)→SO(4) therefore acts trivially.

    Discussion

    The arithmetic quotient is Γ\G4/K4; lattice stabilizers give the usual orbifold interpretation. The scalar-component model above avoids choosing local frames. The underlying induced representations are part of the theory studied by [4]; the proofs here specify our conventions directly. For the standard primitive-vector identity compare [7], §2.5. The following sections give the theta and special-value interpretation, including the cubic example.

    Discussion

    The coefficients of quaternionic elliptic functions can be converted into Mellin transforms of harmonic theta series. On the cubic lattice this leads to a specific newform and an exact, nonzero special value. The normalizations below distinguish the general even-lattice convention from the cubic example.

    Harmonic theta series and Mellin integration#

    Let L⊂ℝ4 be a full Euclidean lattice, H a scalar homogeneous harmonic polynomial of even degree ℓ>0, and N(x)=|x|2. In this article, L∗ denotes the Euclidean dual lattice. For an even integral lattice put Q=N/2 and D=L∗/L. In this case μ ranges over D, and L+μ denotes the corresponding coset in L∗.

    Definition TH-D1. Harmonic Theta Functions

    The harmonic theta function is

    θL,H(it)=∑λ∈LH(λ)e−πtN(λ)(t>0).

    If the Euclidean bilinear form is integral on L and N(λ) is even for every λ∈L, the coset theta functions are

    Θμ(τ)=∑λ∈L+μH(λ)e2πiτQ(λ),ΘL,H=Θ0,Im⁡τ>0.

    Theorem TH1. A convergent Mellin formula for each elliptic coefficient

    Let ν∈ℕ03 have odd order r≥3, and let H be any real component of the numerator Hν. For every full Euclidean lattice L,

    Eν,H(L):=∑λ≠0H(λ)N(λ)r+2=πr+2Γ(r+2)∫0∞tr+1θL,H(it)dt.

    Both the lattice sum and the interchange of summation and integration are absolutely convergent.

    Proof of Theorem TH1

    The numerator has degree r+1. Thus each absolute summand is bounded by a constant times |λ|−r−3, whose exponent exceeds the lattice dimension four. The gamma integral gives, for s=r+2,

    ∑λ≠0|H(λ)|∫0∞ts−1e−πtN(λ)dt=Γ(s)πs∑λ≠0|H(λ)|N(λ)s<∞.

    Fubini's theorem now gives the formula. The argument also applies to any homogeneous harmonic numerator of degree r+1, including linear combinations of the kernel numerators.

    Corollary TH3. Critical values with all normalizing factors fixed

    Under TH1's hypotheses, if L is even integral, then

    Eν,H(L)=2−(r+2)L(ΘL,H,r+2).

    The theta form has weight k=r+3, and the evaluation point is k−1. In particular the first potentially nonzero coefficients, of order r=3, evaluate weight-six cusp forms at s=5.

    TH3 · version 1

    Uses TH1, TH2.

    Proof of Corollary TH3

    Group the absolutely convergent lattice sum by m=Q(λ) and use N(λ)=2m. This gives exactly the factor 2−(r+2). TH2 gives the weight. The usual critical integers for the weight-k holomorphic cusp-form L-function are 1,…,k−1, from the archimedean gamma factors in the functional equation; hence this is the upper critical value in the classical normalization. No Hecke-eigenform hypothesis is required for the identity.

    The cubic lattice and a weight-six newform#

    Let L0=ℤ+ℤi+ℤj+ℤk and put

    H4(t,x,y,z)=t4+x4+y4+z4−12(t2+x2+y2+z2)2.

    In this example the Fourier exponent uses Q0(m)=|m|2, rather than N/2:

    ϑ(τ)=∑m∈ℤ4H4(m)e2πiτ|m|2,f(τ)=η(2τ)12.

    Here u=e2πiτ and η(τ)=u1/24∏n≥1(1−un). Expanding the product gives

    f(τ)=u∏n≥1(1−u2n)12=u−12u3+54u5−88u7−99u9+⋯.

    For this Fourier convention L(f,s)=∑m≥1amm−s when f=∑amum.

    Theorem TH4. The exact harmonic theta identity

    One has

    ϑ(τ)=4η(2τ)12,∑m∈ℤ4∖{0}H4(m)|m|10=4L(f,5).

    The normalized eta product f is the unique newform in S6(Γ0(4)), with trivial character.

    TH4 · version 1

    Uses TH2, TH-D2, the scalar theta, eta-quotient, and modular-form dimension theorems cited below.

    Proof of Theorem TH4

    First Δ∑xj4=12N and Δ(N2)=24N, so H4 is harmonic. To use TH2 without changing the Fourier convention, take the even Euclidean lattice 2L0, of Gram matrix 2I4. Its theta series with numerator H4 is 4ϑ, because H4(2m)=4H4(m). Its level is four, its determinant is sixteen, and the scalar theta transformation theorem [8, Thm. 4] gives weight 2+4=6 and character (16/⋅)=1 on Γ0(4). Hence ϑ∈S6(Γ0(4)).

    For the eta product use the eta-quotient criterion and cusp-order formula [1, Thms. 2.4–2.5]. For ∏δ|Nη(δτ)rδ the weight is 12∑rδ, the congruences are ∑δrδ≡∑(N/δ)rδ≡0(mod24), and the character is the Kronecker symbol associated to (−1)k∏δrδ. Here N=4, r2=12, and both sums equal 24. The character is trivial since 212 is a square. At a cusp of denominator c|4 the order is

    424∑δ|4gcd⁡(c,δ)2rδgcd⁡(c,4/c)cδ=1(c=1,2,4).

    Thus f is a nonzero cusp form of the same weight, level, and character.

    The dimension formula [3, Thm. 3.5.1], for even k>2, is

    dim⁡Sk(Γ)=(k−1)(g−1)+(k/2−1)c+⌊k/4⌋e2+⌊k/3⌋e3.

    For Γ0(4) the index is six, there are three cusps, no elliptic points, and genus zero. The formula gives −5+2⋅3=1. For level one the data (g,c,e2,e3)=(0,1,1,1) give dimension zero in weight six; for level two the data (0,2,1,0) also give zero. Therefore there is no oldspace at level four.

    The eight shortest lattice vectors have H4=1/2, so the first coefficient of ϑ is four. The first coefficient of f is one. Equality follows from the one-dimensional cusp space. Its normalized generator is a Hecke eigenform and is new because the oldspace is zero. Finally group the absolutely convergent harmonic lattice sum by |m|2 to obtain L(ϑ,5)=4L(f,5); TH1's convergence estimate applies to any harmonic quartic.

    Theorem TH5. An individual quaternionic elliptic coefficient

    With G(q)=q‾/|q|4, spatial derivative ∂x in the i direction, and the cubic lattice L0,

    E(3,0,0)(L0)=∑λ≠0∂x3G(λ)=192iL(η(2τ)12,5)≠0.

    For the elliptic function ℘L0,1 of E-C1, the coefficient of x2 in the real Taylor expansion of its regular part ℘L0,1−G1 at zero is 96iL(f,5).

    TH5 · version 1

    Uses TH4, N1, N2.

    Proof of Theorem TH5

    Differentiate the i component −x/N2 of G three times. It gives

    12N2−144x2N+192x4N5.

    The real, j, and k components have a coordinate of odd parity and vanish after summation over the sign-symmetric lattice. Absolute convergence justifies these cancellations and coordinate permutations. Average the displayed numerator over the four coordinate positions; its lattice sum is unchanged, while its value becomes

    12N2−36N2+48(t4+x4+y4+z4)=48H4.

    TH4 therefore gives E(3,0,0)=48i⋅4L(f,5). The Mellin identity

    L(f,5)=(2π)5Γ(5)∫0∞f(it)t4dt

    follows by the same absolute integration argument as TH1 (the harmonic coefficient sum converges at five). Every factor of η(2it)12 is positive for t>0, so this value is strictly positive. Finally N1 with μ=(2,0,0) gives the second derivative of the regular part. Division by 2! gives its ordinary Taylor coefficient.

    How the representations fit together#

    Discussion
    Object varied Group Result established here
    A regular function on the ball, with its point moved by Möbius transformations Sp(1,1) Discrete series for compatible spinors; the Hardy limit for one quaternionic value.
    The lattice in a kernel-derivative sum SL4(ℝ) An irreducible module of Eisenstein functions.
    The modular variable of the cubic harmonic theta form GL2, or the classical modular group TH4–TH6 identify a weight-six newform and its critical value.

    The theta and Mellin formulas provide the stated numerical bridge. An equivalence or functorial correspondence between the first two representations has not been established. An arbitrary lattice theta form need not be an eigenform and may vanish; the cubic example succeeds because its cusp space has dimension one. Its newform data also agree with [5, 4.6.a].

    Questions opened by the example#

    Discussion

    Which other arithmetic lattices and individual quaternionic coefficients select a single newform? Can lattice symmetries predict vanishing or isolate Hecke constituents? What algebraic periods normalize the resulting critical values, and how do these values interact with quaternionic multiplication? These questions remain open here.

    At derivative order r=1, the predicted theta weight is four and the evaluation point is three. TH1 does not apply: the original lattice sum is at the boundary of absolute convergence. Cuspidality still makes the theta Mellin integral meaningful on an even integral lattice, but comparison with a specified regularization or with elliptic quasi-periods needs a separate proof.
