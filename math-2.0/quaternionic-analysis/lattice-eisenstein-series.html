    Eisenstein and Mellin Formulas for Quaternionic Elliptic Coefficients

    Let L⊂ℍ be a full Euclidean lattice. Write q=t+xi+yj+zk, and consider the quaternionic Cauchy kernel and its spatial derivatives

    G(q)=q‾|q|4,Gν=∂xν1∂yν2∂zν3G,ν∈ℕ03.

    For r=|ν|>1, the lattice sum

    Eν(L)=∑λ∈L∖{0}Gν(λ)

    converges absolutely. These sums give derivatives of the regular parts of the quaternionic elliptic functions; ordinary Taylor coefficients require division by the corresponding multi-index factorial. The precise statements are the convergence and coefficient formulas. By parity, Eν(L)=0 for even r, so we concentrate on odd r≥3.

    The complete order-r tuple is an invertible quaternionic encoding of a symmetric trace-free tensor of rank r+1. We establish this identification, its similarity law and its realization by regular jets of the Weierstrass family on the universal real torus. The effective coefficient space has real dimension (r+2)2; the apparent quaternionic entries obey explicit compatibility relations (Proposition EM.2).

    We then study this family in two automorphic variables. As the unit-covolume lattice varies, the scalar components of the order-r coefficients generate an irreducible (𝔰𝔩4,SO(4))-module of Eisenstein functions. We identify it as the kernel of a spherical moment map, with minimal compact type the harmonics of degree r+1 (Theorem EM.8). For a fixed lattice, the same harmonic numerators express each coefficient as a Mellin integral. On the cubic lattice this leads to the explicit value

    E(3,0,0)(ℤ+ℤi+ℤj+ℤk)=192iL(η(2τ)12,5)≠0,

    where η is the Dedekind eta function (Theorem EM.18).

    The harmonic numerator calculation connects both descriptions. We identify the full theta kernel with the definite degree-zero Funke–Millson coefficient kernel and give its exact dilation integral. On arithmetic lattices the resulting theta map can have a kernel beyond the universal coefficient relations and lattice symmetries. The arithmetic companion exhibits such a kernel at discriminant five. The classical Weierstrass functions and their coefficient identities are supplied by the expository construction and coefficient formula; harmonic theta modularity is used from its reference article. The contributions here are the identifications and arguments connecting these inputs for this coefficient family.

    Throughout, a scalar component of a quaternion-valued function means one of its four real coordinate functions relative to 1,i,j,k. In forming complex representations, we regard these components as complex-valued functions. The scalar i in complex phases commutes with the quaternion units i,j,k.

    Harmonic Numerators of the Kernel Derivatives#

    Write ϵj for the spatial multi-index with a single 1 in position j.

    Lemma EM.1. For ν∈ℕ03 of order r=|ν|, there is a quaternion-valued homogeneous polynomial Hν of degree r+1, with harmonic real components, such that

    Gν(q)=Hν(q)|q|2r+4(q≠0).

    Writing N(q)=|q|2 and (x1,x2,x3)=(x,y,z), these numerators satisfy H0=q‾ and

    Hν+ϵj=N∂jHν−2(r+2)xjHν.(EM.1)

    The Complete Harmonic Tensor#

    The four real components of each Eν are constrained by a common harmonic potential. This identifies the effective coefficient space and distinguishes its universal relations from identities particular to a lattice.

    Write e0=1,e1=i,e2=j,e3=k, and let ∂0=∂t. For the real function U(q)=|q|−2, direct differentiation gives

    G=−12D‾U,D‾=∂t−i∂x−j∂y−k∂z.(EM.2)

    The function U is harmonic off zero. For m=r+1≥3, form the symmetric tensor

    T(m)(L)=∑λ∈L∖{0}(∇mU)(λ).

    Derivatives are evaluated before summation. They have degree −m−2<−4, so this tensor is absolutely convergent. No sum of the undifferentiated potential is used. Let Sym0m⁡ℝ4 denote symmetric trace-free tensors, with the Euclidean metric used to identify tensors and their duals.

    Proposition EM.2. For r≥2, the tensor T(r+1)(L) is trace-free, and its entries determine the complete order-r coefficient tuple by

    Eν(L)=−12(T0,ν−iT1,ν−jT2,ν−kT3,ν),|ν|=r.(EM.3)

    Here Ta,ν has one index a and the spatial indices prescribed by ν. Conversely, these quaternionic coordinates recover T. Their exact linear compatibility conditions are

    [Eμ+ϵa]b=[Eμ+ϵb]a(|μ|=r−1, a,b∈{1,2,3}),(EM.4)

    where [z]a is the real coefficient of ea in z, and ϵa is the ath spatial multi-index. The space of compatible tuples has real dimension (r+2)2.

    Proposition EM.2 · version 1

    Uses Lemma EM.1, (EM.2).

    Proof of Proposition EM.2

    Symmetry follows from commutation of partial derivatives; taking any trace gives a derivative of ΔU=0. Equation (EM.2) proves (EM.3). Its real components give every tensor entry with exactly one time index, and its imaginary components give all entries with no time index. Symmetry of the latter is precisely (EM.4). The trace identities

    T00A=−T11A−T22A−T33A

    then recover successively all entries with two or more time indices.

    There are no further restrictions on the prescribed entries. Equivalently, a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree r+1 is determined by arbitrary initial polynomials p(0,x,y,z) and ∂tp(0,x,y,z), of degrees r+1 and r. The recursion ∂t2p=−Δspp determines the remaining powers of t and terminates. The dimension is therefore

    (r+32)+(r+22)=(r+2)2.

    This tensor also gives a regular polynomial carrying the entire coefficient tuple. Namely, put

    pr+1,L(q)=T(r+1)(L)(q,…,q)(r+1)!,ℱr,L=−12D‾pr+1,L.

    Then ℱr,L has degree r, is both left and right Fueter-regular, and satisfies ∂νℱr,L(0)=Eν(L). These regular polynomials differ from the harmonic numerators Hν, which generally are not Fueter-regular.

    For completeness, the harmonic-to-regular map is invertible in positive homogeneous degree. If F=a+b1i+b2j+b3k has degree r and satisfies both Fueter equations, those equations give

    ∂ta=div⁡b,∂tb=−∇a,curl⁡b=0.

    The form adt−∑bjdxj is closed and co-closed. Its homogeneous potential, normalized by F=−D‾p/2, is

    p(q)=−2r+1Re⁡(qF(q)).

    Euler's identity verifies this formula and Δp=0. The general use of harmonic potentials to construct regular functions is classical [11, §5]; the formulas here fix its normalization for this coefficient family.

    Let πm be orthogonal projection onto Sym0m⁡ℝ4. The identity

    (∇mU)(q)=(−2)mm!πm(q⊗m)|q|2m+2(EM.5)

    identifies T(m) with the full harmonic lattice moment. To verify it, apply any harmonic homogeneous differential operator P(∂) of degree m to U(q)=∫0∞e−u|q|2du. Harmonicity gives P(∂)e−u|q|2=(−2u)mP(q)e−u|q|2; integration gives the constant in (EM.5). Pairing with all trace-free tensors proves the tensor identity.

    It follows that the real components of the polynomials Hν, |ν|=r, span all degree-(r+1) harmonics. One can also see the span directly: they are nonzero common multiples, with the component signs in (EM.3), of the harmonic projections of tXν,xXν,yXν,zXν, where X=(x,y,z). Write 𝒫d for all homogeneous polynomials of degree d. Modulo N𝒫r−1, where N=t2+x2+y2+z2, every degree-(r+1) polynomial has a unique representative A(x,y,z)+tB(x,y,z). These monomials span the representatives.

    For odd r≥3, Theorem EM.8 below shows that harmonic Eisenstein summation is injective. Hence (EM.4) exhausts the real linear relations valid for every lattice: an additional relation would give a nonzero harmonic numerator whose lattice sum vanished identically. Additional relations at a particular lattice remain possible and are the subject of the arithmetic companion.

    Homogeneous Sections and Eisenstein Summation#

    Put G4=SL4(ℝ), Γ=SL4(ℤ) and K4=SO(4). We identify ℍ with row vectors in ℝ4 using the coordinates (t,x,y,z). The lattice associated to g∈G4 is Lg=ℤ4g, so Γ\G4 parametrizes unit-covolume lattices.

    For real w>4, let Iw be the space of smooth complex-valued functions on ℝ4∖{0} satisfying

    f(tx)=|t|−wf(x)(t∈ℝ×),

    with right-translation action (R(g)f)(x)=f(xg). In particular these functions are even. For odd r≥3, each scalar component of Gν, |ν|=r, belongs to Ir+3.

    Construction EM.3. For w>4 and f∈Iw, set

    ℰf(g)=∑m∈ℤ4∖{0}f(mg).

    Construction EM.3 · version 1

    Well-definedness: Convergence, smoothness and equivariance in Proposition EM.4.

    Let e4=(0,0,0,1) and let P⊂G4 be the stabilizer of the row line ℝe4:

    P={(Ab0d):det⁡(A)d=1}.

    For f∈Iw, put Ff(g)=f(e4g). We use normalized induction with section law F(pg)=δP(p)1/2χ(p)F(g) and right translation by G4.

    Regrouping lattice vectors by their primitive lines relates ℰf to an Eisenstein series. For the radial section this is the Epstein–Eisenstein identity [9, Lemma 2.9]; the same regrouping applies to every even homogeneous section.

    Proposition EM.4. For w>4 and f∈Iw, ℰf is smooth and left Γ-invariant, has moderate growth with all right derivatives, and satisfies

    ℰR(h)f(g)=ℰf(gh).(EM.6)

    For p=(Ab0d)∈P, one has

    Ff(pg)=|d|−wFf(g),δP(p)=|d|−4.(EM.7)

    Moreover,

    ℰf(g)=2ζ(w)∑γ∈(P∩Γ)\ΓFf(γg).(EM.8)

    The normalized inducing character is

    χ(p)=|d|2−w;w=r+3 ⟹ χ(p)=|d|−(r+1).(EM.9)

    Proposition EM.4 · version 1

    Uses homogeneous-function model, Construction EM.3.

    Proof of Proposition EM.4

    Every right derivative of f remains homogeneous of degree −w. Since |mg|≫C|m| for g in a compact subset C⊂G4, the series and all its derivatives converge uniformly on compact sets. Reindexing gives left Γ-invariance, and the action gives (EM.6). The estimate

    |ℰf(g)|≤Cf‖g−1‖w∑m≠0|m|−w

    also holds with f replaced by any right derivative, proving moderate growth.

    The identity e4p=de4 gives the section law in (EM.7). Conjugation on the unipotent radical sends b to Abd−1, whose determinant is det⁡(A)d−3=d−4; hence δP(p)=|d|−4.

    Every primitive integral row is e4γ for some γ∈Γ, and P∩Γ stabilizes its line. Grouping nonzero rows by their positive gcd gives ζ(w); identifying opposite primitive rows gives the factor 2, since f is even. This proves (EM.8). Finally δP1/2χ=|d|−w gives (EM.9).

    The Moment Kernel and Its Compact Types#

    The homogeneous model contains a finite-dimensional quotient when w is an even integer. The derivative kernels lie in its kernel. We describe this kernel explicitly to identify the representation selected by the coefficients.

    For even w≥6, let 𝒫w−4 be the complex homogeneous polynomials of degree w−4 on ℝ4, with action p(x)↦p(xg). Its dual carries the contragredient action. With Euclidean surface measure dσ on S3, define the moment map

    momw⁡:Iw⟶𝒫w−4∨,momw⁡(f)(p)=∫S3f(ω)p(ω)dσ(ω).

    The pairing is complex bilinear. Write 𝒴l for the complex homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree l, identified with their restrictions to S3. Their homogeneous representatives in Iw are x↦|x|−w−lH(x) for H∈𝒴l. A subscript K4 denotes K4-finite vectors.

    Definition EM.5. For even w≥6, the vanishing-moment subspace of Iw is

    ℳw=ker⁡(momw⁡)={f∈Iw:∫S3f(ω)p(ω)dσ(ω)=0 for all p∈𝒫w−4}.(EM.10)

    Definition EM.5 · version 1

    Proposition EM.6. For even w≥6, the moment map gives a G4-equivariant exact sequence

    0⟶ℳw⟶Iw⟶momw⁡𝒫w−4∨⟶0.(EM.11)

    Its K4-finite kernel is

    (ℳw)K4=⨁j≥0𝒴w−2+2j.(EM.12)

    Every scalar component of an order-(w−3) kernel derivative lies in the minimal compact type 𝒴w−2.

    Proposition EM.6 · version 1

    Uses Definition EM.5, Lemma EM.1.

    Proof of Proposition EM.6

    The product fp is homogeneous of degree −4. For η=ωg/|ωg|, with g∈SL4(ℝ), the spherical Jacobian is dσ(η)=|ωg|−4dσ(ω). Consequently

    ∫S3f(ωg)p(ωg)dσ(ω)=∫S3f(ω)p(ω)dσ(ω).

    This proves equivariance into the contragredient dual and invariance of the kernel. The nondegenerate spherical pairing on 𝒫w−4 gives surjectivity.

    The restrictions of 𝒫w−4 are precisely the low harmonics 𝒴0,𝒴2,…,𝒴w−4. Orthogonality of distinct harmonic degrees gives (EM.12). By Lemma EM.1, an order-(w−3) derivative has harmonic numerator of degree w−2, proving the final assertion.

    These are degenerate principal series representations. For their general composition series, see [4, Thms. 1.1–1.2]. In that source's normalized P3,1 induction, restricting |det⁡|w−2×1 to SL4(ℝ) gives the section law |d|−w used here. The following calculation describes the harmonic transitions in this model.

    Put Vl=Syml⁡ℂ2. Under Spin⁡(4)=Sp⁡(1)×Sp⁡(1), one has 𝒴l≃Vl⊠Vl; the central element (−1,−1) acts trivially, so this is an SO(4) representation.

    Proposition EM.7. For every even w≥6, (ℳw)K4 is an irreducible (𝔰𝔩4,SO(4))-module, with compact types

    ⨁j≥0𝒴w−2+2j≃⨁j≥0Vw−2+2j⊠Vw−2+2j.

    Proposition EM.7 · version 1

    Uses Proposition EM.6, irreducibility of spherical harmonics under SO(4).

    Proof of Proposition EM.7

    For a symmetric trace-free real matrix A, set

    QA(x)=xAxt,DAH=(xA)⋅∇H,TAH=∑i,jAij∂i∂jH.

    Here the four real coordinates are indexed from 1 to 4. Differentiating fH(x)=|x|−w−lH(x) gives, on S3,

    dR(A)H=DAH−(w+l)QAH.(EM.13)

    The harmonic degrees present are l+2,l,l−2. If prl−2⁡ denotes projection onto degree l−2, then

    prl−2⁡dR(A)H=l+2−w4l(l+1)TAH.(EM.14)

    Indeed, ΔDAH=2TAH and Δ2(QAH)=8TAH. Harmonic decomposition gives degree-(l−2) terms TAH/(2l) in DAH|S3 and TAH/[4l(l+1)] in (QAH)|S3, yielding (EM.14). The degree-(l+2) term is −(w+l) times the top harmonic part of QAH.

    Thus lowering vanishes at l=w−2 and is nonzero at every higher degree in (EM.12), while raising is nonzero at every degree. To check nonvanishing, use the complexified Lie algebra and take H=(t+ix)l, where i is the complex scalar. The quadratic form (t+ix)2 gives a nonzero harmonic raising term, and (t−ix)2 gives a nonzero TAH for l≥2.

    Each 𝒴l is irreducible under K4 and occurs with multiplicity one. Any nonzero submodule therefore contains a compact type. Repeated lowering reaches 𝒴w−2, and repeated raising then reaches every type in (EM.12). This proves irreducibility.

    Nonvanishing and the Automorphic Realization#

    It remains to show that lattice summation does not annihilate the moment kernel. A degenerating family of lattices isolates one coordinate axis and gives a nonzero leading term.

    Theorem EM.8. For odd r≥3, Eisenstein summation defines an injective intertwining map

    ℰ:(ℳr+3)K4⟶C∞(Γ\G4),f⟼ℰf.

    Its image is the (𝔰𝔩4,SO(4))-module generated by the scalar components of

    g⟼Eν(ℤ4g),|ν|=r.

    This module is irreducible and has minimal compact type 𝒴r+1.

    Theorem EM.8 · version 1

    Uses Lemma EM.1, Proposition EM.4, Proposition EM.7.

    Proof of Theorem EM.8

    Put w=r+3. Choose H∈𝒴w−2 with H(e4)≠0, and set

    ga=diag⁡(a,a,a,a−3),fH(x)=|x|−2w+2H(x),a≥1.

    In ℰfH(ga), the rows (0,0,0,k) contribute 2ζ(w)a3wH(e4). For the remaining rows write m=(m′,k) with m′∈ℤ3∖{0}. The bound |fH(x)|≤C|x|−w and comparison of the sum over k with its maximum and integral give

    ∑k∈ℤ(a2|m′|2+a−6k2)−w/2≪a−w|m′|−w+a4−w|m′|1−w.

    Both sums over m′ converge for w>4, so

    ℰfH(ga)=2ζ(w)a3wH(e4)+O(a4−w).(EM.15)

    Hence the intertwining map is nonzero on (ℳw)K4 and is injective by Proposition EM.7.

    The scalar derivative kernels lie in its minimal compact type by Proposition EM.6, and at least one is nonzero: for x>0,

    ∂xrG(xi)=−i(−1)r(r+2)!2x−r−3.

    In fact the scalar components of the full tuple already span 𝒴r+1 by the tensor calculation after Proposition EM.2. In particular the K4-orbit of a nonzero such component spans 𝒴r+1, and its enveloping-algebra orbit spans (ℳr+3)K4. Equivariance now identifies the image as claimed.

    The fixed-order coefficients lie in the minimal compact type. Their translates under SL4(ℝ) generate the larger module; general linear changes of variables need not preserve the flat Fueter equation or the finite tuple of kernel derivatives. The result concerns a real-place Harish-Chandra module of automorphic functions. It makes no assertion of a unitary L2 realization, a discrete-series representation of SL4, or a classification of finite-place constituents. Individual components may still vanish at particular lattices.

    Corollary EM.9. For r=3, the homogeneous degree is −6, the normalized inducing character is |d|−4, and the minimal compact type is V4⊠V4, of dimension 25. The finite-dimensional quotient of I6 is 𝒫2∨, of dimension 10.

    Corollary EM.9 · version 1

    Uses Proposition EM.6, Theorem EM.8.

    Proof of Corollary EM.9

    Set w=6 in (EM.9), (EM.11) and (EM.12). The dimensions are (4+1)2=25 and (53)=10.

    Rotation Covariance of the Quaternionic Coefficients#

    The scalar model above describes the module generated under SL4. To describe rotations of the quaternion-valued coefficient tuple itself, we also need the action on its values.

    Fix r>1, and let U−r−3 be the right-ℍ span of the spatial derivatives Gν of order r. For unit quaternions a,b, consider

    (ρ(a,b)f)(q)=bf(a−1qb).

    On the right-ℍ-linear dual U−r−3∨=Homℍ⁡(U−r−3,ℍ), set

    (σ(a,b)α)(f)=bα(ρ(a,b)−1f).

    Absolute convergence defines a functional

    αL(f)=∑λ∈L∖{0}f(λ),αL(Gν)=Eν(L).

    Proposition EM.10. The space U−r−3 is invariant under ρ. The operators σ(a,b) define a real representation on U−r−3∨, trivial on (−1,−1), and

    αaLb−1=σ(a,b)αL.

    Thus the action on the coefficient functionals descends from Spin⁡(4) to SO(4).

    Proof of Proposition EM.10

    The kernel identity G(a−1qb)=b−1G(q)a shows, by differentiation, that rotated order-r derivatives are combinations of derivatives in the four real directions, with constant right quaternion factors. Direct differentiation also gives right regularity:

    ∂tG+∂xGi+∂yGj+∂zGk=0.

    This identity and its derivatives eliminate time derivatives in favor of spatial derivatives with right coefficients. Hence U−r−3 is invariant. The formula for ρ gives its group law and right-ℍ linearity.

    Reindexing the absolutely convergent lattice sum proves the covariance identity. In composing σ(a1,b1) and σ(a2,b2), the output factors multiply as b1b2 and precomposition is by ρ(a1a2,b1b2)−1. Thus σ is a representation. At (−1,−1), ρ acts as −1, so σ(−1,−1)α(f)=−α(−f)=α(f). The kernel of the spin covering acts trivially.

    Similarities and the Effective Coefficient Bundle#

    The rotation law extends to arbitrary two-sided similarities. Write a=|a|u, b=|b|v with u,v unit quaternions, and put c=|a|/|b|, so aLb−1=cR(L) for R(q)=uqv−1. For real directions h1,…,hr, write

    ℰr(L)(h1,…,hr)=∑λ∈L∖{0}Dh1⋯DhrG(λ).

    The spatial entries are Eν(L); the Fueter equations recover the entries involving the real direction.

    Proposition EM.11. For r>1, c>0 and R(q)=uqv−1 as above,

    ℰr(cRL)(h1,…,hr)=c−r−3vℰr(L)(R−1h1,…,R−1hr)u−1.(EM.16)

    Consequently covol⁡(L)(r+3)/4ℰr(L) is unchanged by positive dilation and transforms under rotations by the indicated real coefficient action.

    Proposition EM.11 · version 1

    Uses Proposition EM.10, Proposition EM.2.

    Proof of Proposition EM.11

    The kernel satisfies G(cRq)=c−3vG(q)u−1. Differentiation contributes c−r and applies R−1 to each direction. Summing over the lattice proves (EM.16). Covolume scales by c4, giving the last assertion. Changing (u,v) to (−u,−v) changes both output factors' signs and leaves the formula unchanged.

    There is a convenient harmonic model of the effective fibre. Let 𝒴r+1,ℝ be the real homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree r+1, and put

    𝔽r(g)(H)=∑m∈ℤ4∖{0}H(mg)|mg|2r+4.

    On this polynomial space define π(k)H(x)=H(xk) for k∈K4, and on its dual define τ(k)ℓ=ℓ∘π(k)−1. Direct substitution gives

    𝔽r(γgk)=τ(k)−1𝔽r(g).

    Thus 𝔽r is a section of the associated real orbifold bundle

    𝒱r=Γ\(G4×K4𝒴r+1,ℝ∨)⟶X=Γ\G4/K4,

    where (g,z)∼(gk,τ(k)−1z). The full tuple Eν recovers this section by the span established after Proposition EM.2. Its fibre has real dimension (r+2)2; its complexification has compact type Symr+1⁡ℂ2⊠Symr+1⁡ℂ2.

    The quaternionic coordinates do not make this an ℍ-linear representation. On the larger functional space used in Proposition EM.10,

    σ(u,v)(hα)=(vhv−1)σ(u,v)α.

    For odd r the effective real dimension is odd, which also excludes a quaternionic vector-space structure. The finite harmonic fibre records the order-r coefficients; the full SL4 module generated by translating those coefficients is the infinite-dimensional module of Theorem EM.8.

    The Weierstrass Family on the Universal Torus#

    Use the row-vector affine group, with the convention of [8, §2, (2.1)–(2.7)],

    J=G4⋉ℝ4,(g,q)(h,z)=(gh,qh+z),ΓJ=Γ⋉ℤ4.

    The quotient

    π:𝒯=ΓJ\J/K4⟶X

    is the universal family of oriented unit-covolume flat tori, understood as an orbifold family. Indeed, (γ,m)(g,q)=(γg,q+mg) and (g,q)(k,0)=(gk,qk).     For r≥1 and |ν|=r, use the subtracted Weierstrass normalization

    ℘ν,L(q)=Gν(q)+∑λ∈L∖{0}(Gν(q+λ)−Gν(λ)).(EM.17)

    𝒲r(q,L)(f)=f(q)+∑λ≠0(f(q+λ)−f(λ)),f∈U−r−3.

    𝒲r(cRq,cRL)=c−r−3σ(u,v)𝒲r(q,L)

    Proof of Proposition EM.12

    On compact sets away from the lattice, the differences in (EM.17) are O(|λ|−r−4). Differentiation in the lattice basis introduces integer-coordinate factors but also higher kernel derivatives, preserving this summable bound on compact sets of invertible bases. This proves smoothness in both variables and permits the vertical derivatives.

    For r≥2, the subtracted series is an absolutely convergent periodic sum minus a constant. At r=1, its first real derivatives are periodic, so each lattice increment is constant; evenness replaces that constant by its negative, proving periodicity as in Proposition QW.3. Reindexing gives basis invariance. Substitution into the series gives the rotation law, because v(ρ(u,v)−1f)(z)=f(uzv−1). Homogeneity gives the scale.

    For q′=Rq, the chain rule gives

    Dq′[vf(R−1q′)]=u(Dqf)(R−1q′).

    Indeed the coefficient of ∂qbf on the left is ∑aeavRab=R(eb)v=ueb. Thus the operator descends with target action β↦uβ∘ρ(u,v)−1. Every summand is left regular. The coordinate kernels are also right regular; arbitrary right quaternionic combinations need only be left regular.     The singularity is along a real codimension-four zero section. The assertion concerns a section on a real torus family with a vertical differential equation; it requires no complex structure on the nine-dimensional base.

    Harmonic Theta Series and Mellin Integration#

    We now keep the lattice fixed and use the harmonic numerator to express a coefficient as a Mellin integral. This argument requires Lemma EM.1, independently of the preceding representation-theoretic construction.

    For a full Euclidean lattice L⊂ℝ4 and a scalar homogeneous harmonic polynomial H of even degree ℓ>0, put

    θL,H(it)=∑λ∈LH(λ)e−πt|λ|2(t>0).

    If L is even integral, use Q(λ)=|λ|2/2 and write

    ΘL,H(τ)=∑λ∈LH(λ)e2πiτQ(λ)(Im⁡τ>0).

    These conventions give θL,H(it)=ΘL,H(it). Since Q is integral on L, write ΘL,H(τ)=∑m≥1ame2πimτ and

    L(ΘL,H,s)=∑m≥1amm−s

    where the Dirichlet series converges absolutely.

    The classical scalar theta theorem gives the weight, level and character in this normalization, while the coset transformation law supplies cuspidality.

    The Quaternion-Valued Theta Family#

    The scalar formulas assemble into a theta function for the full coefficient space. Write Wr+1=Sym0r+1⁡ℝ4 and let πr+1 be trace-free projection. For real directions h1,…,hr, define

    (JrT)(h1,…,hr)=(−2)r(r+1)!∑a=03⟨T,h1⊗⋯⊗hr⊗ea⟩e‾a.(EM.18)

    This real linear injection has image 𝒰r in the space of quaternion-valued symmetric r-linear maps. Its spatial coordinates satisfy (EM.4); they determine it by Proposition EM.2. The normalization in (EM.5) gives

    ℋr(q):=Jrπr+1(q⊗(r+1)),ℋr(q)(i[ν1],j[ν2],k[ν3])=Hν(q).

    Here the bracketed exponents mean repeated arguments. Under R(q)=uqv−1, the action on 𝒰r is

    (ρjet(u,v)F)(h1,…,hr)=vF(R−1h1,…,R−1hr)u−1.

    Contraction and Rq=vq‾u−1 prove that Jr intertwines this action with the orthogonal action on trace-free tensors.

    Use a central complex scalar i for the modular variable, commuting with the quaternion units. The faithful theta family takes values in 𝒰r,ℂ=𝒰r⊗ℝℂ:

    𝚯L,r(τ)=∑λ∈Lℋr(λ)eπiτ|λ|2.(EM.19)

    It is defined for every full Euclidean lattice and Im⁡τ>0. When L is even integral, its scalar components satisfy the harmonic theta theorem, with weight r+3; retaining all lattice cosets gives the finite Weil representation. For odd r≥3 the harmonic degree is positive and even, so these forms are cuspidal.

    Proposition EM.16. For every full Euclidean lattice L and odd r≥3,

    ℰr(L)=πr+2Γ(r+2)∫0∞tr+1𝚯L,r(it)dt.(EM.20)

    The integral is an absolutely convergent integral in the real coefficient space 𝒰r. For even integral L, with Fourier exponents Q(λ)=|λ|2/2,

    ℰr(L)=2−r−2L(𝚯L,r,r+2).

    The vector L-function means the Dirichlet series of its Fourier coefficients.

    Uses Proposition EM.14, (EM.18), (EM.19).

    Proof of Proposition EM.16

    The numerator has norm O(|λ|r+1). Integrating the norm of each term against tr+1dt gives a constant times |λ|−r−3, summable over the lattice. The finite-dimensional Fubini theorem therefore applies, and the gamma integral gives the complete derivative tensor. Grouping by Q(λ) gives the second formula. On the imaginary axis all exponential factors are real, so the integral lies in 𝒰r itself.

    An actual quaternion-valued modular function at general τ requires a choice of complex slice. Choose a unit imaginary quaternion I and identify a+ib with a+Ib. Applying h⊗z↦zIh to each spatial coordinate of (EM.19) gives

    ΘL,νI(τ)=∑λ∈LeπIτI|λ|2Hν(λ),τI=Re⁡τ+IIm⁡τ.

    All scalar phases and modular automorphy factors act on the left. Thus if the scalar theta components have character χ and weight r+3,

    ΘL,νI(γτ)=χ(d)(cτI+d)r+3ΘL,νI(τ)

    for γ=(abcd) in their modular group. This follows by applying the same left-complex-linear map to the scalar component laws. It is not a claim of a simultaneous complex-linear SO(4) coefficient action on this smaller target: the irreducible source has complex dimension (r+2)2, whereas

    dimℂI⁡ℍ(r+22)=(r+1)(r+2).

    A nonzero equivariant map between them would be injective, which is impossible. On the imaginary axis the phases are real; the fixed-slice and commuting-complexification formulas then give exactly the same quaternionic Mellin values.

    The Theta Kernel and the Dilation Integral#

    The coefficient kernel has a precise realization in the theta correspondence. In the Funke–Millson construction take signature (4,0), genus one and coefficient representation Wr+1. The orthogonal symmetric space is a point, so the form degree is zero. Their coefficient operators and harmonic projection [3, §5.2, Defs. 5.1 and 5.5] specialize to

    φ0,[r+1](x)=πr+1(x⊗(r+1))e−π|x|2.(EM.21)

    To check the normalization, the creation operators are Aa=12(xa−(2π)−1∂a). For φ0=e−π|x|2,

    φ0−1Aa(pφ0)=xap−14π∂ap.

    The generating function gives P(A)φ0=(e−Δ/(8π)P)(x)φ0. For harmonic P this is P(x)φ0, proving (EM.21) after trace-free projection. Applying the explicit map Jr gives precisely the kernel ℋr(x)e−π|x|2 of (EM.19). This identifies the coefficient vector, not only its modular weight.

    The same Schwartz function gives the lattice Eisenstein family by a dilation integral. For H∈𝒴r+1 and a>0, put

    ϑH(a,g)=∑m∈ℤ4∖{0}H(amg)e−π|amg|2.

    The substitution t=a2 in Proposition EM.14 gives the exact real-place identity

    ∑m≠0H(mg)|mg|2r+4=2πr+2Γ(r+2)∫0∞ϑH(a,g)ar+3daa.(EM.22)

    Absolute convergence follows from the same integrated majorant. In the oscillator convention ω(m(a))ϕ(x)=a2ϕ(ax), the theta kernel includes a2, and the character in the integral is instead ar+1.

    The two operations belong to dual pairs in the same symplectic space. For a rational four-space V, put 𝕎=V⊕︎V∗ with its canonical alternating form. The actions

    g(v,λ)=(gv,λ∘g−1),a(v,λ)=(av,a−1λ)

    give (GL(V),GL1)⊂Sp(𝕎). A nondegenerate quadratic form on V identifies 𝕎 with V⊗ℚ2 and gives (O(V),SL2)⊂Sp(𝕎). The inclusions are

    O(V)⊂GL(V),GL1⟶SL2,a⟼diag⁡(a,a−1).

    This is the seesaw relating the harmonic theta construction to scalar dilation in the Schrödinger model. Formula (EM.22) fixes its real-variable constant. A globally normalized adelic identity would also require finite Schwartz functions and Haar measures.

    For positive-definite rational V, the arithmetic orthogonal data is a finite genus class set. Choose a lattice L, its finite stabilizer Kf⊂O(V)(𝔸f), and genus representatives Lj with groups Γj=O(Lj). The usual finite model for harmonic-weight algebraic modular forms is

    O(V)(ℚ)\O(V)(𝔸f)/Kf,⨁j𝒴r+1Γj;

    see [5, Prop. 2.3 and Lemma 2.4]. Its mass-weighted theta map is

    (Pj)j⟼∑j1|Γj|∑x∈LjPj(x)e2πiτQ(x).

    A section supported at one class gives that lattice's theta series, with the indicated stabilizer factor. Its Mellin integral is the corresponding harmonic contraction of 𝔽r(Lj). For oriented shapes use the proper groups throughout. Unit-covolume normalization multiplies order-r coefficients by covol⁡(Lj)(r+3)/4. An orthogonal genus class and a one-sided quaternionic ideal class retain different data; the arithmetic companion fixes the ideal norm and its map to the norm lattice explicitly.

    The Cubic Lattice and a Weight-Six Newform#

    Let L0=ℤ+ℤi+ℤj+ℤk, and put

    H4(t,x,y,z)=t4+x4+y4+z4−12(t2+x2+y2+z2)2.

    For this example define

    ϑ(τ)=∑m∈ℤ4H4(m)e2πiτ|m|2,f(τ)=η(2τ)12.

    The exponent in ϑ uses |m|2, rather than |m|2/2. Equivalently, it comes from the even lattice 2L0; the scaling factor in the polynomial is accounted for in the proof below.

    With u=e2πiτ, the Dedekind eta function is η(τ)=u1/24∏n≥1(1−un). Hence

    f(τ)=u∏n≥1(1−u2n)12=u−12u3+54u5−88u7−99u9+⋯.

    As above, L(f,s)=∑m≥1amm−s for f=∑amum.

    Theorem EM.17. One has

    ϑ(τ)=4η(2τ)12,∑m∈ℤ4∖{0}H4(m)|m|10=4L(f,5).

    The eta product f=η(2τ)12 is the unique normalized newform in S6(Γ0(4)), with trivial character.

    Uses cubic-lattice conventions, scalar theta modularity, the eta-quotient and dimension formulas cited in the proof.

    Proof of Theorem EM.17

    Writing N=t2+x2+y2+z2, one has Δ(t4+x4+y4+z4)=12N and Δ(N2)=24N, so H4 is harmonic. Apply Schoeneberg's scalar modularity theorem [10, p. 519, (16)] to L=2L0, with Gram matrix A=2I4. Its level is four: 4A−1=2I4 is even integral, while 2A−1=I4 is not. The character is trivial, since det⁡A=16 and (16d)=1 for odd d.

    Now Q(2m)=|m|2 and H4(2m)=4H4(m), so ΘL,H4=4ϑ. The theta theorem, with harmonic degree four, gives ϑ∈S6(Γ0(4)).

    For f, the eta-quotient criterion [1, Thms. 2.4–2.5] gives weight six. At level four its two required congruence sums both equal 24, and its character is trivial because 212 is a square. The cusp-order formula gives order 1 at each of the three cusps, of denominators 1,2,4. Thus f belongs to the same cusp space.

    For Γ0(4), the index is six, the genus is zero, there are three cusps and no elliptic points. The dimension formula [2, Thm. 3.5.1] gives

    dim⁡S6(Γ0(4))=−5+2⋅3=1.

    At levels one and two the genus is again zero. Level one has one cusp and one elliptic point of each order 2 and 3; level two has two cusps and one elliptic point of order 2. The same formula gives dimensions −5+2+1+2=0 and −5+4+1=0, respectively. Therefore the level-four space has no oldforms.

    Each of the eight shortest vectors of ℤ4 has H4=1/2, so ϑ begins with 4u, whereas f begins with u. The one-dimensionality proves ϑ=4f and makes f a normalized Hecke eigenform; the absence of oldforms makes it new. Finally, grouping by |m|2 gives the lattice-sum identity. Absolute convergence follows from the harmonic-quartic estimate in Proposition EM.14.

    The Automorphic Representation of the Cubic Example#

    The classical newform correspondence attaches to f=η(2τ)12 an irreducible cuspidal representation πf of GL2(𝔸ℚ), of conductor four and trivial central character [7, §§2.1–2.2]. Its archimedean factor is the weight-six discrete series; on restriction to SL2(ℝ), the holomorphic summand has lowest weight six [7, §1.1].

    For the finite standard L-function in unitary normalization,

    L(f,s)=L(πf,s−5/2).

    The shift is (6−1)/2, corresponding to the normalized Hecke eigenvalues ap/p5/2 [7, p. 1182]. Thus Theorem EM.18 also reads E(3,0,0)(L0)=192iL(πf,5/2). The weight-six, level-four newform data agree with [6, 4.6.a].

    The two automorphic descriptions concern different variables. The SL4 module arises by varying the lattice in Eν(L); the GL2 representation arises from the modular variable of the cubic harmonic theta series. The Mellin formula relates their values. For comparison, the Möbius action on the quaternionic ball concerns regular functions and the spinor discrete series or Hardy limit. No equivalence or functorial correspondence between that ball representation and the lattice module is established here.

    Further Questions#

    For a general arithmetic lattice, the harmonic theta series may vanish or have several Hecke constituents. The cubic calculation isolates one newform because its cusp space is one-dimensional. The discriminant-five companion gives a second explicit mixed coefficient in weight six and identifies an additional theta-kernel relation in weight eight. These results distinguish universal tensor compatibility, lattice symmetry and arithmetic cancellation. They do not identify the periods of distinct surviving eigenforms.

    At derivative order r=1, the harmonic numerator predicts theta weight four and evaluation at s=3, but the lattice sum is at the boundary of absolute convergence. The Mellin formula proved above does not cover this order. Cuspidality still makes the theta Mellin integral meaningful for an even integral lattice; identifying it with a specified regularization or with elliptic quasi-periods requires a separate argument.
