    Regular cusp spinors on quaternionic half-space, their Mellin period cocycles, an explicit weight-fourteen example, and arithmetic cohomology.

    Cusp Forms, Mellin Transforms and Parabolic Cohomology

    Discussion

    A regular quaternionic cusp form produces a closed two-form with flat coefficients. Its periods over triangles therefore form a group two-cocycle. Mellin transforms enter these surface periods through a primitive whose coefficients are incomplete Mellin integrals. Their compatibility is expressed by cocycle and inversion identities.

    Half-Space and Regular Spinors#

    Discussion

    We first describe the domain and the coefficient space. Regularity is a differential equation on that space; automorphy is a transformation law under a discrete group. The two conditions define the space ℳn(Γ) of regular automorphic forms.

    The Domain and Group Action#

    Write a quaternion as z=y+x, where x=x1i+x2j+x3k is purely imaginary. The domain is the quaternionic half-space

    ℋ={y+x∈ℍ:y>0}.

    We use the following realization of Sp(1,1):

    G={g∈GL2⁡(ℍ):g∗H0g=H0},H0=(0110).

    Here g∗ means quaternionic conjugate transpose. A matrix g=(abcd) acts by

    gz=(az+b)(cz+d)−1,J(g,z)=cz+d.

    The base point is 1∈ℋ. Its stabilizer is the maximal compact subgroup

    K=C{diag⁡(u,v):|u|=|v|=1}C−1,C=2−1/2(11−11).

    We label the two compact factors by this conjugation and write σ(k)=J(k,1) for the second factor. The action and its relation to the ball are established in Quaternionic Half-Space and Siegel Domains.

    Polynomial Coefficients#

    Fix an integer n≥1. The coefficient space is

    Vn=Symn⁡ℂ2={∑j=0npjXn−jYj:pj∈ℂ}.

    Thus a function f:ℋ→Vn is a polynomial in the auxiliary variables X,Y, with coefficient functions on ℋ. These auxiliary variables are distinct from the coordinates y,x1,x2,x3 of the domain.

    For a complex 2×2 matrix A, the symmetric-power action is

    (τn(A)p)(X,Y)=p(((X,Y)A)1,((X,Y)A)2).

    We represent quaternions by complex matrices through

    Q(y+x)=(y+ix1x2+ix3−x2+ix3y−ix1),

    and use I1=Q(i), I2=Q(j), I3=Q(k) and ϵ=(01−10). The symbol i denotes the complex scalar imaginary unit; i,j,k denote quaternion units. The map Q is called ι in the earlier articles.

    The coefficient norm is

    ‖p‖n2=∑j=0n(nj)−1|pj|2.

    This is the symmetric-tensor norm induced by the usual Hermitian norm on ℂ2. Consequently τn(Q(u)) is unitary when |u|=1, and its operator norm is |u|n for nonzero u.

    Regularity and Automorphy#

    Definition CP-H2. Regular Spinors

    Let n≥1 be an integer. A smooth function f:ℋ→Vn is a regular spinor of weight n if

    (∂yI2×2+∑a=13Ia∂xa)(fXfY)=0.(HS1)

    Here fX=∂f/∂X and fY=∂f/∂Y are derivatives in the auxiliary polynomial variables.

    CP-H2 · version 1

    For a smooth f:ℋ→Vn and g∈G, use the slash notation

    (f|ng)(z)=|J(g,z)|−2τn(Q(J(g,z)−1))f(gz).(HS2)

    The denominator identity gives (f|ng)|nh=f|n(gh). For the weights n≥2 used below, the covariance calculation in the two-form construction shows that the slash action preserves regularity. These facts allow regularity and a group transformation law to be imposed together.

    Definition CP-H0. Regular Automorphic Forms

    Let n≥1 be an integer and let Γ⊂G be a discrete subgroup. A regular automorphic form of weight n for Γ is a smooth function f:ℋ→Vn satisfying both conditions:

    1. f is regular.
    2. f|nγ=f for every γ∈Γ.

    CP-H0 · version 2

    The complex vector space of these forms is denoted by

    ℳn(Γ)={f∈C∞(ℋ,Vn):f is regular and f|nγ=f for all γ∈Γ}.
    Discussion

    The notation ℳn(Γ) imposes the differential equation and transformation law. Growth and cuspidality will be specified separately below, as is usual when passing from automorphic functions to spaces with prescribed behavior at the cusps.

    Growth and Finiteness Conditions#

    The growth condition uses the ordinary Euclidean norm on ℍ2:

    ‖(v1,v2)t‖=|v1|2+|v2|2,|a+bi+cj+dk|=a2+b2+c2+d2.

    For a matrix g∈G, its operator norm is the largest stretching factor for this positive norm:

    ‖g‖op=supv≠0⁡‖gv‖‖v‖.

    Set

    𝖧(g)=max⁡{1,‖g‖op,‖g−1‖op}.

    The indefinite form preserved by G has value 2Re⁡(v‾1v2); it is distinct from the Euclidean norm used to measure growth.

    For differential operators, write 𝔤=Lie⁡(G) and 𝔤ℂ=𝔤⊗ℝℂ. Right differentiation is given by

    D(T)F(g)=ddtF(gexp⁡(tT))|t=0(T∈𝔤).

    It extends complex-linearly and multiplicatively to the universal enveloping algebra U(𝔤ℂ). Its center is denoted by 𝒵=Z(U(𝔤ℂ)). Elements of 𝒵 therefore act as invariant differential operators on F.

    Definition CP-D0. Growth and Finiteness Conditions

    Let V be a finite-dimensional complex normed vector space and let F:G→V be smooth.

    1. Moderate growth. The function F has moderate growth if there are an integer A≥0 and a constant C>0 such that

      ‖F(g)‖≤C𝖧(g)A(g∈G).(MG)

    2. 𝒵-finiteness. The function F is 𝒵-finite if

      dimℂ⁡span⁡{D(z)F:z∈𝒵}<∞.(ZF)

    3. Uniform moderate growth. The function F has uniform moderate growth if there is an integer A≥0 such that, for every u∈U(𝔤ℂ), there is a constant Cu>0 with

      ‖D(u)F(g)‖≤Cu𝖧(g)A(g∈G).(UMG)

    4. Right K-finiteness. The function F is right K-finite if

      dimℂ⁡span⁡{g↦F(gk):k∈K}<∞.

    CP-D0 · version 4

    Discussion

    Moderate growth is a polynomial bound. Uniform moderate growth uses one exponent for every derivative, although the constants may depend on the derivative. The definition of a cusp form below requires moderate growth and 𝒵-finiteness; the rapid-decrease theorem then supplies the decay used in period integrals. These conventions agree with [5, §2].

    Finiteness as a Differential Equation#

    Discussion

    For classical Maass forms, the familiar spectral condition is a Laplace eigenvalue equation. The condition here has the same differential-equation interpretation, with two commuting central operators. The complexified Lie algebra is 𝔰𝔭4(ℂ), and the center of its universal enveloping algebra is a polynomial algebra on generators C2,C4 of degrees two and four. Put 𝒟2=D(C2) and 𝒟4=D(C4). The [7] gives these generators; a finite test for the definition follows.

    Proposition CP-Z1. A Finite Differential Test for Central Finiteness [7, 0.2.7–0.2.9]

    Let V be a finite-dimensional complex vector space, let F:G→V be smooth, and choose algebra generators C2,C4 of 𝒵. Write 𝒟j=D(Cj). Then F is 𝒵-finite if and only if there are nonzero polynomials p2,p4∈ℂ[t] such that

    p2(𝒟2)F=0,p4(𝒟4)F=0.

    In particular, a joint eigenfunction of 𝒟2,𝒟4 is 𝒵-finite.

    CP-Z1 · version 1

    Uses Growth and finiteness; [7].

    Proof of Proposition CP-Z1[7, 0.2.7–0.2.9]

    For type C2, the Weyl group acts by signed permutations of two Cartan coordinates. Its invariant polynomials are the symmetric polynomials in λ12,λ22, generated by their sum and product. The Harish-Chandra isomorphism therefore gives 𝒵=ℂ[C2,C4].

    If the central orbit of F spans a finite-dimensional space, both operators preserve that space. Their characteristic polynomials annihilate it by Cayley–Hamilton.

    Conversely, let dj=deg⁡pj. If either polynomial is constant, the corresponding equation forces F=0. Otherwise polynomial division and commutation show that the central orbit is spanned by

    {𝒟2a𝒟4bF:0≤a<d2, 0≤b<d4}.

    This is a finite set. Linear polynomials give the eigenfunction case.

    Discussion

    Thus finiteness can be established by differential identities, without enumerating an infinite algebra. In a fixed homogeneous bundle these become equations for the coefficient functions on half-space, including the connection terms. They are not simply the ordinary scalar Euclidean Laplacian applied componentwise.

    For the weight-n quaternionic discrete series, the infinitesimal character is (n,1) in the Cartan coordinates of the weight dictionary. Normalize C2,C4 so their Harish-Chandra polynomials are λ12+λ22 and λ12λ22. A lift belonging to this representation then satisfies

    𝒟2F=(n2+1)F,𝒟4F=n2F.

    This follows by evaluating those polynomials at (n,1). In particular the matrix-coefficient construction of the weight-fourteen Poincaré series establishes its central finiteness through these eigenvalue equations. For a separately proposed function, the differential identities or its membership in the representation must still be established; regularity is not being used here as an unproved substitute for central finiteness.

    Vanishing Constant Terms#

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group of rational rank one, with 𝐆(ℝ)≃G, and let Γ⊂𝐆(ℚ) be a torsion-free congruence lattice. Put X=Γ\ℋ.

    For an integer n≥2 and f∈ℳn(Γ), its group lift is

    Ff(g)=|J(g,1)|−2τn(Q(J(g,1)−1))f(g1).(1)

    The lift satisfies Ff(gk)=τn(Q(σ(k)−1))Ff(g), so it is right K-finite. Indeed, k1=1 and the denominator cocycle gives J(gk,1)=J(g,1)J(k,1).

    Here rational cusps are Γ-orbits of proper rational parabolic subgroups Pξ=MξAξNξ; Nξ≃(ℝ3,+) is the unipotent radical. The quotient Haar measure is normalized to have total mass one.

    Definition CP-D1. Regular Automorphic Cusp Forms

    Let Γ be a torsion-free congruence lattice in the rational-rank-one group fixed above. For n≥2, a regular automorphic cusp form of weight n is a form f∈ℳn(Γ) whose lift satisfies all three conditions:

    1. Ff has moderate growth, measured using ‖⋅‖n.

    2. Ff is 𝒵-finite.

    3. At every rational cusp its constant term vanishes:

      ∫(Γ∩Nξ)\NξFf(ug)du=0(g∈G).(2)

    CP-D1 · version 4

    The space of regular automorphic cusp forms is denoted by 𝒮n−(Γ).

    Discussion

    Defining the constant term on the group makes it independent of the cusp coordinates. In a frame adapted to a cusp, it is the average over the three translation variables. The minus sign in 𝒮n−(Γ) labels the anti-self-dual two-form constructed below.

    Proposition CP0. Growth in a Cusp Frame

    Let Γ⊂G be discrete, let n≥1, and let f∈ℳn(Γ) have a group lift Ff of moderate growth with exponent A. Choose sξ∈G with sξ∞=ξ and set fξ=f|nsξ. Then

    Ff(sξnxay)=y(n+2)/2fξ(y+x).(HF)

    For every compact Q0⊂Im⁡ℍ there is Cξ,Q0>0 with

    ‖fξ(y+x)‖n≤Cξ,Q0y(A−n−2)/2(x∈Q0, y≥1).(H)

    CP0 · version 3

    Uses CP-D0, CP-D1, QH-D2.

    Proof of Proposition CP0

    The slash composition law gives Ffξ(g)=Ff(sξg). Since J(nxay,1)=y−1/2, homogeneity of τn gives (HF). The height 𝖧 is submultiplicative, 𝖧(ay)=y for y≥1, and 𝖧(nx) is bounded on Q0. Hence 𝖧(sξnxay)≤Cy. Substitute this bound in (MG) and divide (HF) by y(n+2)/2.

    Proposition CP1. Cuspidality and the Petersson Norm [5, Thm. A, Thms. 2.23–2.24]

    Let 𝐆/ℚ be a simply connected semisimple group of rational rank one with 𝐆(ℝ)≃Sp(1,1), and let Γ⊂𝐆(ℚ) be a torsion-free congruence lattice. Let n≥2 and let f∈ℳn(Γ) have a group lift of moderate growth that is 𝒵-finite. Then f∈𝒮n−(Γ) if and only if

    ‖f‖Γ,n2:=∫Γ\ℋyn−2‖f(y+x)‖n2dx1dx2dx3dy<∞.(3)

    For each rational cusp ξ, choose sξ∈G with sξ∞=ξ, set fξ=f|nsξ, and let Λξ⊂Im⁡ℍ be the lattice giving sξ−1(Γ∩Nξ)sξ={nλ:λ∈Λξ}. Its average

    Aξ(y)=1vol⁡(ℝ3/Λξ)∫ℝ3/Λξfξ(y+x)dx

    is independent of y. Finite norm implies Aξ=0 at every cusp. For a cusp form and every L≥0 there is Cξ,L such that

    ‖fξ(y+x)‖n≤Cξ,Ly−L−(n+2)/2(x in a fixed translation cell, y≥Yξ).(RD)

    CP1 · version 4

    Uses CP-D1, CP0; [5, Thm. A].

    Proof of Proposition CP1[5, Thm. A, Thms. 2.23–2.24]

    The height formula Re⁡(gz)=y/|J(g,z)|2, the norm of τn(Q(J)), and the real Jacobian |J(g,z)|−8 show that the density in (3) is invariant. Equivalently it is the squared invariant spinor norm yn+2‖f‖n2 times the invariant measure y−4dxdy.

    Average HS1 over the translation torus. Its spatial derivatives integrate to zero, so ∂XAξ′=∂YAξ′=0. Euler's identity for degree n>0 gives Aξ′=0. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the norm of the average by the mean squared norm. Thus finite (3) implies

    ‖Aξ‖n2∫Yξ∞yn−2dy<∞,

    which forces Aξ=0 for n≥2. In (HF), averaging over translations gives precisely the constant term at nxay. Every element of G is nxayk, and compact equivariance gives its vanishing at every g. Passing to the translation cover handles finite cusp holonomy.

    Conversely, the scalar components of Ff are smooth, left Γ-invariant, 𝒵-finite, of moderate growth, right K-finite and cuspidal. Miller–Schmid's Theorem A gives rapid decrease on each cusp Siegel set. The comparison 𝖧(sξnxay)≍y on a fixed translation cell converts it to ‖Ff(sξnxay)‖n≤CLy−L. Equation (HF) proves (RD), and then

    yn−2‖fξ(y+x)‖n2≤CL2y−4−2L.

    This is integrable in each cusp. There are finitely many cusps, and smoothness gives integrability on the compact core.

    The Closed Two-Form and the Ball-Coordinate Dictionary#

    For n≥2 put m=n−2, Em=Symm⁡ℂ4. If g is a quaternionic matrix, let R(g) be its complex block matrix obtained by applying Q to each entry, and put ρ(g)=Rm(g)=Symm⁡R(g). Define

    Z(z)=(Q(z)I2×2),Zm(z)=Symm⁡Z(z),S=ϵ(dQ∗∧dQ),
    ΦK(p)=K11pXX+2K12pXY+K22pYYn(n−1),Ωf=ZmΦS(f).(HS3)

    Here K is a symmetric 2×2 matrix with scalar or differential-form entries; the same contraction defines ΦK in either case.

    Proposition CP-C2. The Closed Covariant Two-Form

    Let n≥2 and let f:ℋ→Vn be smooth. For regular f, dΩf=0 and ∗Ωf=−Ωf, in the orientation dy∧dx1∧dx2∧dx3. For smooth f and g∈G,

    Ωf|ng=ρ(g)−1g∗Ωf.(HS4)

    In particular γ∗Ωf=ρ(γ)Ωf for f∈ℳn(Γ).

    CP-C2 · version 1

    Uses CP-H0, AC3.

    Proof of Proposition CP-C2

    The coefficients of S are constant two-forms. The direct exterior differentiation and auxiliary-variable contraction in AC3 give dΩf=0 if and only if HS1, and give anti-self-duality. This is a local coordinate identity on the quaternionic variable, so the same computation applies on ℋ.

    For j=Q(J(g,z)) and r=|J(g,z)|, matrix multiplication and differentiation give

    Z(gz)j=R(g)Z(z),g∗S=r−2j−tSj−1.

    One may check the second identity on the translations, positive dilations, compact rotations and inversion generating G; the denominator composition law then preserves it under products. Substituting HS2 cancels the scalar factor and the two contracted tensor factors, leaving HS4. Since Zm is injective and AC3's closedness criterion is an equivalence, HS4 also proves preservation of regularity.

    Proposition CP-H1. Exact Comparison with Ball Conventions

    Let q∈B={|q|<1}, z=Cq=(1+q)(1−q)−1, and let fB be a ball-model spinor of weight n. Its half-space representative is

    f(z)=2−(n+2)/2|1−q|2τn(Q(1−q))fB(q).(4)

    For Γ=CΓBC−1 this gives an isomorphism of regular automorphic cusp spaces with the transported rational structure. It satisfies

    C∗Ωf=ρ(C)ΩfB,‖f‖Γ,n2=22−n‖fB‖ΓB,n2,(5)

    where the ball norm is ∫ΓB\B(1−|q|2)n−2‖fB(q)‖n2dv(q). If hB is the coefficient metric of AC6, its transported half-space metric is

    hzℋ(v1,v2)=hC−1zB(ρ(C)−1v1,ρ(C)−1v2),v1,v2∈Em.

    CP-H1 · version 1

    Uses CP-C2, QH2.

    Proof of Proposition CP-H1

    The inverse Cayley denominator is (z+1)/2; its inverse is (1−q)/2. Applying the slash formula to this change of basis gives (4). The identities for Z,S in CP-C2 also hold for this Cayley matrix between the two realizations, proving the two-form identity and regularity. The group lifts satisfy

    Ff(g)=2−(n+2)/2FfB(C−1gC).

    Conjugation transports parabolics, coefficient actions, growth and central finiteness; changing variables in the normalized constant term preserves its vanishing. Finally

    y=1−|q|2|1−q|2,dv(z)=16|1−q|−8dv(q),‖f(z)‖n2=2−(n+2)|1−q|2n+4‖fB(q)‖n2.

    Their product proves the norm factor in (5).

    Discussion

    All constructions below use the half-space normalization (4). The ball formulation remains useful for compact-type decompositions and centered Poincaré seeds; CP-H1 gives the exact conversion, including the scalar that affects periods. Group cohomology is transported by the constant coefficient map ρ(C), so the change of coordinates does not change the underlying correspondence.

    Fourier Modes at a Cusp#

    Proposition CP-M1. Polarized Fourier Expansion cf. [3, §3.1, Lemmas 1–3]

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ), and assume infinity is a rational cusp with translation lattice Λ⊂ℝ3. Put w=n+2 and

    Λ∗={λ∈ℝ3:⟨λ,μ⟩∈ℤ for every μ∈Λ}.

    There are vectors Aλ∈Vn such that

    f(y+x)=∑0≠λ∈Λ∗Aλe−2π|λ|ye2πi⟨λ,x⟩.(M1)

    If (aλ,bλ)t spans the positive eigenspace of the Hermitian matrix iQ(λ1i+λ2j+λ3k), then Aλ is a scalar multiple of (aλX+bλY)n. Moreover

    ‖f(y+x)‖n≤Cfy−w/2,‖Aλ‖n≤Cf′|λ|w/2.(M2)

    The Fourier series and all its derivatives converge locally uniformly for y>0.

    CP-M1 · version 1

    Uses CP-H0, CP1.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M1cf. [3, §3.1]

    The invariant norm of the lift is bounded on a compact core and tends to zero in every cusp by (RD). Thus it is bounded globally on Γ\G. Equation (HF) gives the first bound in M2.

    Applying the conjugate quaternionic differential operator to HS1 gives the Euclidean Laplace equation for fX,fY. Euler's identity then gives Δf=0. A Fourier coefficient aλ(y) therefore satisfies

    aλ′′−(2π|λ|)2aλ=0.

    Rapid decrease excludes the growing solution, and CP1 excludes the zero mode. Substitution of aλ(y)=Aλe−2π|λ|y into HS1 yields

    (iQ(λ)−|λ|I2×2)((Aλ)X(Aλ)Y)=0.

    The matrix has a one-dimensional kernel, since Q(λ)2=−|λ|2I. Equality of mixed partial derivatives and homogeneity force the stated power of a linear polynomial. Evaluate the Fourier coefficient at y=|λ|−1 and apply the first bound in M2 to obtain the second. Polynomial coefficient growth combined with e−2π|λ|y proves convergence with every derivative on compact subsets.

    Discussion

    All nonzero lattice directions occur in M1; the positive radial frequency is |λ|. Opposite directions have different polarization lines. Summing by |λ| can therefore discard directional information even when the complete Fourier expansion determines the form. The relation between Fueter regularity and polarized exponential modes also appears in [3, §3.1]; the symmetric-spinor calculation used here is the proof above.

    Complete Mellin Transforms and Inversion#

    Proposition CP-M2. The Height Mellin Transform

    Let n≥2 and let f∈𝒮n−(Γ) be a regular automorphic cusp form. Suppose 0 and ∞ are rational cusps of Γ. Let Λ be the translation lattice at infinity, let Aλ be the Fourier coefficients in the polarized expansion, and put w=n+2. Define

    Λf(s)=∫0∞f(y)ys−1dy,𝒟f(s)=∑0≠λ∈Λ∗Aλ|λ|−s.

    The first integral is entire in s∈ℂ. The series converges absolutely for Re⁡s>w/2+3, and in that half-plane

    Λf(s)=(2π)−sΓ(s)𝒟f(s).(M3)

    Here Γ(s)=∫0∞e−tts−1dt for Re⁡s>0. With W=(0110), one has

    Λf(s)=Λf|nW(w−s)(M4)

    and

    Λf(s)=∫1∞(f(y)ys−1+(f|nW)(y)yw−s−1)dy.(M5)

    CP-M2 · version 2

    Uses CP-M1, CP1, QH-D2.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M2

    The lattice has O(R3) points of length at most R, so M2 gives absolute convergence of the series in the stated half-plane. There one may integrate M1 term by term, obtaining M3. At infinity the Fourier expansion decays exponentially. On the positive real axis,

    (f|nW)(y)=y−wf(1/y).

    The form f|nW is cuspidal at infinity for the conjugated lattice, because 0 is a cusp of the original group. This gives exponential decay at the other endpoint after y↦1/y, up to a power of y. Both estimates are uniform on compact sets of s and remain integrable after multiplication by any power of log⁡y. They prove entire dependence. Substitution in the two halves of the integral proves M4–M5.

    Discussion

    The series 𝒟f is vector-valued and has positive real frequencies. When a scaling makes |λ|2 integral, it can be grouped into ∑NBNN−s/2. The construction and functional equation require no Hecke-eigenform assumption. An Euler product would be additional arithmetic structure, requiring a suitable scalar or algebra-valued coefficient system and multiplicativity.

    If W does not normalize Γ, M4 relates forms on conjugate lattices. In the eigencondition f|nW=ηf, it becomes Λf(s)=ηΛf(w−s).

    Proposition CP-M3. Directional Complete Transforms

    Let n≥2 and let f∈𝒮n−(Γ) be a regular automorphic cusp form, where 0 and ∞ are rational cusps of Γ. Write Λ for the translation lattice at infinity, Aλ for its Fourier coefficients, and w=n+2. Let ω=ω0+𝝎 be a unit quaternion with ω0>0. Then

    ℳf(s;ω)=∫0∞f(rω)rs−1dr

    is entire. For Re⁡s>w/2+3,

    ℳf(s;ω)=(2π)−sΓ(s)∑λ≠0Aλ(|λ|ω0−i⟨λ,𝝎⟩)−s.(M6)

    The complex power uses the logarithm in the right half-plane. Its inversion law is

    ℳf|nW(s;ω)=τn(Q(ω−1))ℳf(w−s;ω−1).(M7)

    CP-M3 · version 2

    Uses CP-M1, CP-M2.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M3

    Insert M1 on the ray rω; its exponential has real decay rate 2π|λ|ω0>0. Absolute convergence in the stated half-plane permits the gamma integral, proving M6. At zero, inversion takes the ray to r−1ω−1; the cusp estimates and periodicity in a translation frame give integrable decay. The slash factor is r−wτn(Q(ω−1)). Substitution gives M7.     Proof of Proposition CP8

    Rational elements commensurate an arithmetic group: in an integral faithful representation, clearing the finitely many denominators of α and α−1 gives a principal congruence subgroup contained in the intersection. Thus the coverings have finite degree. The coefficient identification is well-defined because for δ∈Γα,

    ρm,L(α)−1ρm,L(αδα−1)=ρm,L(δ)ρm,L(α)−1.

    Pullback, this identification, and trace are all L-linear. Both covering maps are proper. Pullback therefore preserves compact support, as does trace, whose support is contained in the image of the original support. These maps commute with the map from compactly supported to ordinary cohomology. Hence (17) preserves its image, which is HL by the compact-core argument of CP2 over L.

    The normalization in (17)–(19) is a sum, with no division by the covering degree. Both the coefficient transport and this normalization matter when comparing with Hecke operators in other conventions. The rational elements here belong to 𝐆(ℚ). Similitude Hecke operators extend the coefficient and slash actions, with separate cohomological and integral normalizations.

    Algebraicity of Eigenvalues#

    Corollary CP9. Hecke Eigenvalues Lie in a Number Field

    Let Γ, n≥2, L, and HL be as in CP7, and use the normalization in CP-D4. Let 0≠f∈𝒮nε(Γ), with ε∈{−,+}.

    If Tαεf=λαf, then

    det⁡(λαidHL⁡−Tα|HL)=0.(23)

    In particular λα is algebraic over ℚ.

    If {Tα:α∈A} is a commuting family of these operators and f is a simultaneous eigenvector, then

    Lf=L(λα:α∈A)(24)

    is a finite extension of L.

    CP9 · version 1

    Uses CP7, CP8, AC7.

    Proof of Corollary CP9

    Let v=[Ωf] for the minus space and v=[Ωf+] for the plus space. By the cuspidal injection AC7 and its reflected version, v≠0; by CP3 and reflection, v∈HL⊗Lℂ. Formula (20) and the trace calculation give Tαv=λαv. Thus λα is a root of the characteristic polynomial of the L-linear operator Tα on the finite-dimensional space HL. This proves (23), and algebraicity over L implies algebraicity over ℚ.

    For (24), let 𝕋L⊆EndL⁡(HL) be the unital L-algebra generated by the commuting family. It is finite-dimensional because it is an L-subspace of EndL⁡(HL). Its action on the nonzero common eigenline ℂv defines an L-algebra homomorphism χf:𝕋L→ℂ. Its image is a finite-dimensional L-algebra contained in a field. Multiplication by a nonzero element of that image is injective, hence surjective by finite-dimensionality, so the image is itself a field. It contains exactly the L-algebra generated by the eigenvalues, and therefore equals Lf. This proves [Lf:L]<∞.

    Discussion

    The algebraicity proof requires the injective period map and its Hecke equivariance; it does not require the surjectivity in CP5 or the exclusion of Eisenstein classes from interior cohomology. A Hecke-stable cohomology space over a number field is sufficient even if it contains additional classes.

    The L-structure in CP8 belongs to the sum of both chiralities. It does not by itself give an L-structure on each summand, or imply that the periods of an individual eigenform are algebraic. For odd n, CP7 even rules out a ℚ-model of the coefficient representation of this complex dimension. At n=2 the coefficients are trivial and one may work over ℚ throughout. The n=1 Hardy limit has no coefficient system En−2 in this construction.

    SH3 proves algebraic integrality for integral positive similitudes with a specified coefficient normalization. SH4 gives central counterexamples to an unrestricted integrality claim. Likewise, a scalar renormalization of each operator by an algebraic number preserves algebraicity of individual eigenvalues, but a common finite field for infinitely many operators requires control of all those normalization factors. Formula (24) applies to the explicit normalization above.

    Further Arithmetic Questions#

    Discussion

    The Mellin construction and weight-fourteen example above give explicit spinors and triangle periods. Further arithmetic questions include effective dimensions at n=2,3,4, explicit Hecke spectra, comparisons with adelic Hecke generators at each level, and the arithmetic of periods within each chirality. An integral coefficient lattice for the matrix-order semigroup is constructed in SH3. The Poincaré and theta-lift constructions provide places to start. Neither the correspondence nor the dimension formula asserts that every cusp form is an Arakawa theta lift.
