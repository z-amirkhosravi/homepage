    Poincaré series for regular Sp(1,1) cusp spinors, their incomplete-Mellin period cocycles, and an explicit nonzero weight-fourteen example.

    Explicit Regular Cusp Forms and Period Cocycles for Sp(1,1)

    A regular quaternionic cusp form produces a closed two-form with flat coefficients. Its periods over triangles therefore form a group two-cocycle. Mellin transforms enter these surface periods through a primitive whose coefficients are incomplete Mellin integrals. Their compatibility is expressed by cocycle and inversion identities.

    Setup#

    Let n≥2, m=n−2, and Em=Symm⁡ℂ4. Let Γ be a torsion-free congruence lattice in a rational-rank-one form of G≃Sp⁡(1,1), acting on ℋ={y+x∈ℍ:y>0}. Use the half-space action and polynomial coefficients, the growth and finiteness conditions, and the cuspidal condition. Write 𝒮n−(Γ) for that cusp space.

    The closed Em-valued two-form Ωf, its coefficient embedding Zm and covariance are given in CP-C2; CP-H1 identifies them with the ball model. The Fourier expansion and complete Mellin formulas fix the analytic conventions used below.

    The Closed Two-Form and the Ball-Coordinate Dictionary#

    For n≥2 put m=n−2, Em=Symm⁡ℂ4. If g is a quaternionic matrix, let R(g) be its complex block matrix obtained by applying Q to each entry, and put ρ(g)=Rm(g)=Symm⁡R(g). Define

    Z(z)=(Q(z)I2×2),Zm(z)=Symm⁡Z(z),S=ϵ(dQ∗∧dQ),
    ΦK(p)=K11pXX+2K12pXY+K22pYYn(n−1),Ωf=ZmΦS(f).(HS3)

    Here K is a symmetric 2×2 matrix with scalar or differential-form entries; the same contraction defines ΦK in either case.

    Proposition CP-C2. The Closed Covariant Two-Form

    Let n≥2 and let f:ℋ→Vn be smooth. For regular f, dΩf=0 and ∗Ωf=−Ωf, in the orientation dy∧dx1∧dx2∧dx3. For smooth f and g∈G,

    Ωf|ng=ρ(g)−1g∗Ωf.(HS4)

    In particular γ∗Ωf=ρ(γ)Ωf for f∈ℳn(Γ).

    CP-C2 · version 1

    Uses CP-H0, AC3.

    Proof of Proposition CP-C2

    The coefficients of S are constant two-forms. The direct exterior differentiation and auxiliary-variable contraction in AC3 give dΩf=0 if and only if HS1, and give anti-self-duality. This is a local coordinate identity on the quaternionic variable, so the same computation applies on ℋ.

    For j=Q(J(g,z)) and r=|J(g,z)|, matrix multiplication and differentiation give

    Z(gz)j=R(g)Z(z),g∗S=r−2j−tSj−1.

    One may check the second identity on the translations, positive dilations, compact rotations and inversion generating G; the denominator composition law then preserves it under products. Substituting HS2 cancels the scalar factor and the two contracted tensor factors, leaving HS4. Since Zm is injective and AC3's closedness criterion is an equivalence, HS4 also proves preservation of regularity.

    Proposition CP-H1. Exact Comparison with Ball Conventions

    Let q∈B={|q|<1}, z=Cq=(1+q)(1−q)−1, and let fB be a ball-model spinor of weight n. Its half-space representative is

    f(z)=2−(n+2)/2|1−q|2τn(Q(1−q))fB(q).(4)

    For Γ=CΓBC−1 this gives an isomorphism of regular automorphic cusp spaces with the transported rational structure. It satisfies

    C∗Ωf=ρ(C)ΩfB,‖f‖Γ,n2=22−n‖fB‖ΓB,n2,(5)

    where the ball norm is ∫ΓB\B(1−|q|2)n−2‖fB(q)‖n2dv(q). If hB is the coefficient metric of AC6, its transported half-space metric is

    hzℋ(v1,v2)=hC−1zB(ρ(C)−1v1,ρ(C)−1v2),v1,v2∈Em.

    CP-H1 · version 1

    Uses CP-C2, QH2.

    Proof of Proposition CP-H1

    The inverse Cayley denominator is (z+1)/2; its inverse is (1−q)/2. Applying the slash formula to this change of basis gives (4). The identities for Z,S in CP-C2 also hold for this Cayley matrix between the two realizations, proving the two-form identity and regularity. The group lifts satisfy

    Ff(g)=2−(n+2)/2FfB(C−1gC).

    Conjugation transports parabolics, coefficient actions, growth and central finiteness; changing variables in the normalized constant term preserves its vanishing. Finally

    y=1−|q|2|1−q|2,dv(z)=16|1−q|−8dv(q),‖f(z)‖n2=2−(n+2)|1−q|2n+4‖fB(q)‖n2.

    Their product proves the norm factor in (5).

    All constructions below use the half-space normalization (4). The ball formulation remains useful for compact-type decompositions and centered Poincaré seeds; CP-H1 gives the exact conversion, including the scalar that affects periods. Group cohomology is transported by the constant coefficient map ρ(C), so the change of coordinates does not change the underlying correspondence.

    Fourier Modes at a Cusp#

    For Clifford-valued plane-wave representations of the fundamental solution, see [8, §3.1, Lemmas 1–3].

    Proposition CP-M1. Polarized Fourier Expansion

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ), and assume infinity is a rational cusp with translation lattice Λ⊂ℝ3. Put w=n+2 and

    Λ∗={λ∈ℝ3:⟨λ,μ⟩∈ℤ for every μ∈Λ}.

    There are vectors Aλ∈Vn such that

    f(y+x)=∑0≠λ∈Λ∗Aλe−2π|λ|ye2πi⟨λ,x⟩.(M1)

    If (aλ,bλ)t spans the positive eigenspace of the Hermitian matrix iQ(λ1i+λ2j+λ3k), then Aλ is a scalar multiple of (aλX+bλY)n. Moreover

    ‖f(y+x)‖n≤Cfy−w/2,‖Aλ‖n≤Cf′|λ|w/2.(M2)

    The Fourier series and all its derivatives converge locally uniformly for y>0.

    CP-M1 · version 1

    Uses CP-H0, CP1.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M1

    The invariant norm of the lift is bounded on a compact core and tends to zero in every cusp by (RD). Thus it is bounded globally on Γ\G. Equation (HF) gives the first bound in M2.

    Applying the conjugate quaternionic differential operator to HS1 gives the Euclidean Laplace equation for fX,fY. Euler's identity then gives Δf=0. A Fourier coefficient aλ(y) therefore satisfies

    aλ′′−(2π|λ|)2aλ=0.

    Rapid decrease excludes the growing solution, and CP1 excludes the zero mode. Substitution of aλ(y)=Aλe−2π|λ|y into HS1 yields

    (iQ(λ)−|λ|I2×2)((Aλ)X(Aλ)Y)=0.

    The matrix has a one-dimensional kernel, since Q(λ)2=−|λ|2I. Equality of mixed partial derivatives and homogeneity force the stated power of a linear polynomial. Evaluate the Fourier coefficient at y=|λ|−1 and apply the first bound in M2 to obtain the second. Polynomial coefficient growth combined with e−2π|λ|y proves convergence with every derivative on compact subsets.

    All nonzero lattice directions occur in M1; the positive radial frequency is |λ|. Opposite directions have different polarization lines. Summing by |λ| can therefore discard directional information even when the complete Fourier expansion determines the form. The relation between Fueter regularity and polarized exponential modes also appears in [8, §3.1]; the symmetric-spinor calculation used here is the proof above.

    Complete Mellin Transforms and Inversion#

    Proposition CP-M2. The Height Mellin Transform

    Let n≥2 and let f∈𝒮n−(Γ) be a regular automorphic cusp form. Suppose 0 and ∞ are rational cusps of Γ. Let Λ be the translation lattice at infinity, let Aλ be the Fourier coefficients in the polarized expansion, and put w=n+2. Define

    Λf(s)=∫0∞f(y)ys−1dy,𝒟f(s)=∑0≠λ∈Λ∗Aλ|λ|−s.

    The first integral is entire in s∈ℂ. The series converges absolutely for Re⁡s>w/2+3, and in that half-plane

    Λf(s)=(2π)−sΓ(s)𝒟f(s).(M3)

    Here Γ(s)=∫0∞e−tts−1dt for Re⁡s>0. With W=(0110), one has

    Λf(s)=Λf|nW(w−s)(M4)

    and

    Λf(s)=∫1∞(f(y)ys−1+(f|nW)(y)yw−s−1)dy.(M5)

    CP-M2 · version 2

    Uses CP-M1, CP1, QH-D2.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M2

    The lattice has O(R3) points of length at most R, so M2 gives absolute convergence of the series in the stated half-plane. There one may integrate M1 term by term, obtaining M3. At infinity the Fourier expansion decays exponentially. On the positive real axis,

    (f|nW)(y)=y−wf(1/y).

    The form f|nW is cuspidal at infinity for the conjugated lattice, because 0 is a cusp of the original group. This gives exponential decay at the other endpoint after y↦1/y, up to a power of y. Both estimates are uniform on compact sets of s and remain integrable after multiplication by any power of log⁡y. They prove entire dependence. Substitution in the two halves of the integral proves M4–M5.

    The series 𝒟f is vector-valued and has positive real frequencies. When a scaling makes |λ|2 integral, it can be grouped into ∑NBNN−s/2. The construction and functional equation require no Hecke-eigenform assumption. An Euler product would be additional arithmetic structure, requiring a suitable scalar or algebra-valued coefficient system and multiplicativity.

    If W does not normalize Γ, M4 relates forms on conjugate lattices. In the eigencondition f|nW=ηf, it becomes Λf(s)=ηΛf(w−s).

    Proposition CP-M3. Directional Complete Transforms

    Let n≥2 and let f∈𝒮n−(Γ) be a regular automorphic cusp form, where 0 and ∞ are rational cusps of Γ. Write Λ for the translation lattice at infinity, Aλ for its Fourier coefficients, and w=n+2. Let ω=ω0+𝝎 be a unit quaternion with ω0>0. Then

    ℳf(s;ω)=∫0∞f(rω)rs−1dr

    is entire. For Re⁡s>w/2+3,

    ℳf(s;ω)=(2π)−sΓ(s)∑λ≠0Aλ(|λ|ω0−i⟨λ,𝝎⟩)−s.(M6)

    The complex power uses the logarithm in the right half-plane. Its inversion law is

    ℳf|nW(s;ω)=τn(Q(ω−1))ℳf(w−s;ω−1).(M7)

    CP-M3 · version 2

    Uses CP-M1, CP-M2.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M3

    Insert M1 on the ray rω; its exponential has real decay rate 2π|λ|ω0>0. Absolute convergence in the stated half-plane permits the gamma integral, proving M6. At zero, inversion takes the ray to r−1ω−1; the cusp estimates and periodicity in a translation frame give integrable decay. The slash factor is r−wτn(Q(ω−1)). Substitution gives M7. The same bounds with logarithmic factors prove entire dependence, uniformly for ω in compact subsets of the open hemisphere.

    Both Chiralities#

    Let D0=diag⁡(1,−1), θ(g)=D0gD0, and r(z)=z‾. Equip θΓ with the rational structure transported from that of Γ by θ. Define the complex vector space 𝒮n+(Γ):=𝒮n−(θΓ), with the following realization by differential forms on Γ\ℋ. For f+∈𝒮n−(θΓ) set

    Ωf++=Rm(D0)−1r∗Ωf+.(9)

    Proposition CP-C1. The Complementary Chirality

    Let n≥2 and f+∈𝒮n−(θΓ). The form Ωf++ is closed, satisfies γ∗Ωf++=ρ(γ)Ωf++ for γ∈Γ, and satisfies ∗Ωf++=Ωf++. The corresponding minimal compact type is Vn⊠V0.

    CP-C1 · version 3

    Uses CP-D1, AC3.

    Proof of Proposition CP-C1

    The identity r∘γ=θ(γ)∘r and the representation identity ρ(θ(γ))=Rm(D0)ρ(γ)Rm(D0)−1 give

    γ∗Ωf++=Rm(D0)−1r∗θ(γ)∗Ωf+=ρ(γ)Ωf++.

    The constant coefficient map and pullback commute with the flat differential, proving closedness. The map r is an orientation-reversing isometry of the half-space, so ∗r∗=−r∗∗ on two-forms. Since ∗Ωf+=−Ωf+ by AC3, the pulled-back form is self-dual. Conjugation by D0 exchanges the two factors of K and carries rational parabolics and their unipotent radicals to those for the transported rational structure. Change of variables in the constant-term integrals preserves their vanishing; conjugation also preserves polynomial growth and central finiteness. Thus the transported space has the claimed cuspidality and compact type.

    The plus space is not defined by complex-conjugating the coefficients of the minus space. Already at n=2, complex conjugation preserves anti-self-duality. Orientation reversal is the required operation. For a general lattice, θΓ need not equal Γ, and no equality of the two dimensions is assumed.

    Incomplete Mellin Integrals Give a Primitive#

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ) and assume infinity is a rational cusp. For y>0, x∈Im⁡ℍ and s∈ℂ, define

    Mf(s;x;y)=∫y∞f(t+x)ts−1dt.(M8)

    Expand the polynomial map Zm(y+x):Symm⁡ℂ2→Em as

    Zm(y+x)=∑j=0myjPj(x),Ka=2ϵIa.

    Set

    αf(y+x)=−∑a=13∑j=0mPj(x)ΦKa(Mf(j+1;x;y))dxa.(M9)

    Proposition CP-MC1. The Incomplete-Mellin Primitive

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ) and assume infinity is a rational cusp. The integral M8 is entire in s, and dαf=Ωf. It has the Fourier expression

    Mf(s;x;y)=∑λ≠0Aλe2πi⟨λ,x⟩(2π|λ|)−sΓ(s,2π|λ|y),(M10)

    where Γ(s,u)=∫u∞e−tts−1dt for u>0.

    CP-MC1 · version 1

    Uses CP-C2, CP-M1.

    Proof of Proposition CP-MC1

    Put r0=min0≠λ∈Λ∗⁡|λ|>0 and w=n+2. By M1–M2, for every coordinate multi-index β and ε>0,

    ‖∂βf(t+x)‖n≤Cβ∑λ≠0|λ|w/2+|β|e−2π|λ|t≤Cε,βe−πr0t(t≥ε).

    The last inequality follows by splitting the exponential into two factors and summing one at t=ε. The bound is uniform in real x. For s in a compact subset of ℂ, multiplication by ts−1(log⁡t)r, for any integer r≥0, leaves an integrable majorant on [ε,∞). This justifies termwise integration in M10, entire dependence on s, and all horizontal derivatives. Height derivatives follow by differentiating the lower endpoint. Thus M8 is smooth in (y,x), locally uniformly with its s-derivatives.

    Write

    Ωf=dy∧A(y,x)+B(y,x)

    with horizontal forms A of degree one and B of degree two. Since S(∂y,∂xa)=2ϵIa, the expression M9 is exactly −∫y∞A(t,x)dt. Closedness of Ωf gives ∂yB=dxA. Its polynomial coefficient factors and cusp decay imply B(t,x)→0. Thus

    ∂yαf=A,dxαf=−∫y∞dxA(t,x)dt=B(y,x).

    The preceding estimates justify differentiation of these integrals. The factors Pj(x) are polynomial, and the moments in M9 have 0≤j≤m. Hence the coefficients of αf and every coordinate derivative tend to zero exponentially, up to a polynomial factor in y, uniformly for x in a compact set. The same estimates apply to B. The two identities prove dαf=Ωf.

    Here the word “incomplete” refers to the lower endpoint y. The horizontal point x need not be a boundary cusp. The arguments 1,…,n−1 in M9 come from the polynomial coefficient embedding Zm; the surface parameterization below gives a different list, 2,…,n.

    From a Two-Form to a Cocycle of Periods#

    Proposition CP-M4. Triangle Periods and Their Relations

    Assume infinity is a rational cusp. Fix o∈ℋ and let f∈𝒮n−(Γ). For g,k∈Γ, let [o,go,gko] be the oriented geodesic triangle and put

    cf,o(g,k)=∫[o,go,gko]Ωf=∫∂[o,go,gko]αf.(M11)

    It satisfies

    ρ(g)cf,o(k,l)−cf,o(gk,l)+cf,o(g,kl)−cf,o(g,k)=0.(M12)

    Changing the base point changes cf,o by a coboundary

    (δa)(g,k)=ρ(g)a(k)−a(gk)+a(g).

    Its class in H2(Γ,Em) is the class of Ωf and determines f uniquely in 𝒮n−(Γ).

    CP-M4 · version 1

    Uses CP-C2, CP-MC1.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M4

    Stokes proves M11. Apply it to the geodesic tetrahedron [o,go,gko,gklo]. Its first face is g[o,ko,klo]; covariance converts its integral to ρ(g)cf,o(k,l). The other three faces give M12 with their boundary signs. A prism between the two equivariant choices of vertices gives the base-point coboundary. The comparison theorem [6, Thm. I.7.5] identifies simplex integration with the de Rham/group-cohomology comparison for the contractible universal cover.

    To apply AC7, its smooth-vector hypothesis must hold for the lift Ff. Its scalar components are smooth, cuspidal, of moderate growth and centrally finite by the definition of the cusp space. By [13, Thm. 2.23 and Rem. 2.18], each is a finite sum of smooth vectors in cuspidal L2 representations. Right differentiation replaces a smooth vector v by dπ(u)v, for u∈U(𝔤ℂ); [13, Thm. 2.24] gives rapid decay of these derivatives, hence square integrability in every cusp. Thus the lift lies in (Lcusp2)∞ componentwise. The exact transport in CP-H1 preserves these hypotheses and the two-form class. AC7 therefore gives [Ωf1]=[Ωf2]⇒f1=f2.

    A two-cocycle is a function of a pair of group elements. In a finite cellular description, its coordinates belong to two-cells, with compatibility equations from three-cells; they are also subject to changes by one-cochain coboundaries. The collection of incomplete-Mellin integrals in M11 is therefore organized by M12 into one cohomological object.

    For the classical full modular group, the corresponding differential form has degree one. In even weight, pass to PSL2(ℤ)=⟨S,T:S2=(ST)3=1⟩. A one-cocycle P with P(T)=0 is determined by P(S), because P(gk)=P(g)+ρ(g)P(k). The relators still impose

    (1+ρ(S))P(S)=0,(1+ρ(ST)+ρ(ST)2)P(S)=0.

    These follow by applying the one-cocycle law to the two relators. Thus the single period polynomial retains the cocycle equations. This compression depends on the full modular group and its cusp normalization; it is not a property of every Fuchsian lattice. The quaternionic surface construction displays the higher cohomological degree explicitly.

    The Parabolic Condition for the Constructed Period Class#

    For each rational cusp ξ, write Γξ=Γ∩Pξ. Use the ordinary cohomology conventions and interior and parabolic cohomology. In the following statement, equation (7) is the subgroup-coboundary criterion.

    Proposition CP3. Cusp Forms Satisfy the Parabolic Cocycle Condition

    If f∈𝒮n−(Γ), then [Ωf]∈Hpar2(Γ,Em). In particular its triangle-integral two-cocycle satisfies (7).

    CP3 · version 1

    Uses CP-D1, CP-D2, AC3–AC5.

    Proof of Proposition CP3

    Move the cusp to infinity, transport the group and form, and write f for its cusp-frame representative. Work on a translation cover of the cross-section at fixed height y. Untwist the flat coefficient monodromy by writing its tangential restriction in the periodic frame

    α(x)=ρ(nx)−1Ωf(x,y).

    The connection in that frame is

    dN=d+∑j=13Njdxj,

    where the Nj=dρ(∂xj) commute and are nilpotent. Equivariance of the two-form construction implies that α(x) is a fixed linear function of Ff(nxay) at this height: translating by nx translates the tangent coframe and multiplies the flat coefficient by ρ(nx). Hence (2) says its zero Fourier coefficient is zero. Also dNα=0.

    For a nonzero Fourier mode ℓ∈Λ∗, choose j with ℓj≠0. Choose r with Njr=0. The operator Aj=2πiℓjI+Nj has inverse

    Aj−1=12πiℓj∑a=0r−1(−Nj2πiℓj)a.

    For

    dN,ℓ=∑kdxk∧(2πiℓk+Nk),Hℓ=Aj−1ι∂xj,

    the identity ι∂xj(dxk∧ω)+dxk∧ι∂xjω=δjkω, together with commutation of the Nk, gives

    dN,ℓHℓ+HℓdN,ℓ=Aj−1Aj=I.

    Thus for each closed mode αℓ, the one-form βℓ=Hℓαℓ satisfies dN,ℓβℓ=αℓ. Choose j with |ℓj|=maxk⁡|ℓk|≥‖ℓ‖/3. The finite inverse formula gives ‖Hℓ‖≤C‖ℓ‖−1 for sufficiently large ‖ℓ‖. Smoothness of α on the torus gives ‖αℓ‖≤CM(1+‖ℓ‖)−M for every M; the same rapid decay holds for βℓ. Hence β(x)=∑ℓ≠0βℓe2πiℓ(x) and all its derivatives converge uniformly, and dNβ=α.

    For the finite-index inclusion Λ⊂Γξ, restriction followed by transfer satisfies cor⁡∘res⁡=[Γξ:Λ]I [6, Prop. III.9.5(ii)]. Multiplication by this positive integer is invertible over ℂ, so restriction is injective. The full cusp-stabilizer class therefore vanishes. The de Rham/group-cohomology comparison and AC5 give (7).

    Period Functions on Group Generators#

    For the period-cohomology correspondence for Maass forms on PSL2(ℝ), with its principal-series coefficient modules, see [7, Thm. B].

    Proposition CP-M5. A Function-Valued Period Cocycle

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ), assume infinity is a rational cusp, take αf from CP-MC1, and fix o∈ℋ. Put 𝒜=C∞(ℋ,Em) with left action

    (TgF)(z)=ρ(g)F(g−1z),

    and regard Em as its submodule of constant functions. Define

    bg(z)=∫[o,z](g∗αf−ρ(g)αf),Bg(z)=bg(g−1z),Bg=BgmodEm.(M13)

    The first integral is path-independent and the functions are real-analytic in the interior. They satisfy

    bgk(z)=bg(kz)+ρ(g)bk(z)−bg(ko),(M14)
    Bgk=Bg+TgBk.(M15)

    The constants κf(g,k)=bg(ko) form an Em-valued two-cocycle and

    cf,o=δaf+κf,af(g)=∫[o,go]αf.(M16)

    For the connecting map of 0→Em→𝒜→𝒜/Em→0,

    ∂[B]=[κf]=[Ωf].(M17)

    The class [B] is independent of the primitive and its normalization, is zero after restriction to each cusp stabilizer, and determines f uniquely in the cusp space.

    CP-M5 · version 1

    Uses CP-MC1, CP-M4.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M5

    The one-form βg=g∗αf−ρ(g)αf is closed by covariance of Ωf. Contractibility makes its integral path-independent. For the integer moments in M9, the identity

    Γ(j+1,u)=j!e−u∑a=0juaa!

    and the polynomial coefficient bound in M2 give locally convergent analytic Fourier series. Hence αf, and local primitives of βg, are real-analytic.

    On Em-valued forms use Tgη=ρ(g)(g−1)∗η, extending the function action. Then dBg=αf−Tgαf. Pullbacks reverse composition, giving βgk=k∗βg+ρ(g)βk. Integrating and imposing bg(o)=0 proves M14. Substitution gives

    TgBk−Bgk+Bg=κf(g,k),

    which proves M15 and M17. The group coboundary squared is zero, so κf is a two-cocycle. Integrating the three edges in M11 gives M16: the translated edge contributes ρ(g)af(k)+bg(ko).

    If αf is replaced by αf+dψ, the normalization bg′(o)=0 gives

    Bg′−Bg=ψ−Tgψ−ψ(go)+ρ(g)ψ(o).

    The last two terms are constant functions. With the convention (δψ)(g)=Tgψ−ψ, the change is −δψ. All primitives on ℋ differ by such an exact form. Changing the base point from o to o′ replaces bg by bg−bg(o′), so it also changes Bg only by a constant.

    For p∈Γ∞, the form βp=p∗αf−ρ(p)αf is horizontal and closed. Write the action as p(y,x)=(λy,λRx+t) with λ>0 and R∈SO(3). The decay proved in CP-MC1 implies decay of the coefficients of βp as y→∞ on compact horizontal sets. If βp=∑ava(y,x)dxa, the dy∧dxa coefficients of dβp=0 give ∂yva=0. Decay forces va=0, hence bp=Bp=0. This applies to the full stabilizer, including its finite cusp holonomy.

    At a rational cusp ξ, choose s∈G with s∞=ξ. The form fξ=f|ns is cuspidal for s−1Γs with its transported rational structure. Construct its primitive by CP-MC1 and set

    αξ=ρ(s)(s−1)∗αfξ.

    Covariance HS4 gives dαξ=Ωf on all of ℋ. Applying the preceding stabilizer argument to s−1Γξs and transporting back gives p∗αξ=ρ(p)αξ for every p∈Γξ. Contractibility supplies ψξ∈𝒜 with αf−αξ=dψξ. The normalized cocycle for αξ vanishes on Γξ, so the primitive-change formula yields

    Bp=ψξ−Tpψξ=−(δψξ)(p),p∈Γξ.

    Thus the degree-one restriction is a coboundary at every cusp. Finally M17 and CP-M4 prove injectivity.

    The connecting map in M17 has a concrete meaning: choose representatives Bg of the function classes, take their group coboundary, and obtain constant coefficient vectors. Those constants recover the surface periods up to a coboundary.

    For a presentation Γ=⟨s1,…,sd|ℛ⟩, one function class per generator suffices for M15. For each relator r1⋯rℓ=1, the functional equation is

    ∑j=1ℓTr1⋯rj−1Brj=0,Bs−1=−Ts−1Bs.(M18)

    These follow by repeated use of M15. They provide a function-valued description of the same form detected by the surface-period class. An independent analytic characterization of which generator functions arise from regular cusp forms remains a further question. Function-valued parabolic cocycles also occur in the classical Maass-form theory of [7], with different coefficient modules.

    Cusp-Normalized Surface Periods#

    Proposition CP-M6. Moving the Vertices to Cusps

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ). For a rational cusp η set

    cf,η(g,k)=∫[η,gη,gkη]Ωf,(M19)

    with value zero on degenerate triangles. This integral converges absolutely and satisfies M12. If

    aη,o(g)=∫[η,o,go]Ωf−∫[η,gη,go]Ωf,

    then cf,o−cf,η=δaη,o. Moreover cf,η(g,k)=0 if either g or k fixes η.

    CP-M6 · version 1

    Uses CP1, CP-C2, CP-M4.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M6

    Near each ideal vertex, move that cusp to infinity. The triangle then lies in a vertical plane with bounded horizontal width near infinity. The spinor decays faster than every inverse power of height, while the fixed coefficient embedding and the coordinate transports contribute only polynomial factors. This proves integrability and makes the truncation edges, and the truncation faces of three-simplices, tend to zero. Stokes therefore proves the cocycle identity for ideal simplices as for compact ones. The prism between the vertex maps g↦go and g↦gη has the displayed one-cochain as its two-dimensional term, yielding the coboundary formula. Finally, if gη=η or kη=η, two of the triangle's vertices coincide.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M7

    In the vertical plane the triangle has 0<t<a and y2+t2>at. With y=rcos⁡θ and t=rsin⁡θ, this becomes

    μλ(ω)=2π(|λ|Re⁡ω−i⟨λ,Im⁡ω⟩),

    Poincaré series from constant spinors#

    Construction AS-C1. Periodizing a constant regular spinor

    Let Γ⊂G be discrete and torsion-free, n≥5, and v∈Vn. Write γ=(aγbγcγdγ). Define

    PΓ,n,v(q)=∑γ∈Γ|cγq+dγ|−2τn(ι((cγq+dγ)−1))v.(1)

    AS-C1 · version 1

    Well-definedness: AS1 proves convergence and regular automorphy; AS2 gives a nonvanishing criterion.

    The seed v is regular because its derivatives in the ball coordinates vanish.

    Theorem AS1. Convergence, regularity, and arithmetic cuspidality

    Let Γ⊂Sp(1,1) be discrete and torsion-free, let n≥5, and let v∈Vn. The series (1) converges absolutely, locally uniformly with every derivative, and defines an element of ℳn(Γ). If Γ is a congruence lattice in a rational form of G, its automorphic lift is cuspidal, square-integrable, and satisfies the usual automorphic growth and finiteness conditions.

    AS1 · version 1

    Uses AS-C1, the corrected action in the discrete-series model, and, for the cusp conclusion, [15, Lemma 4-2(i)].

    Proof of Theorem AS1

    For a nonzero quaternion J, the coefficient norm gives

    ‖|J|−2τn(ι(J−1))v‖n=|J|−(n+2)‖v‖n.

    The relation |dγ|2−|cγ|2=1 implies, for |q|≤r<1,

    |cγq+dγ|≥(1−r)|dγ|.

    For the hyperbolic metric s−2|dq|2, write tγ=d(0,γ0). Then |dγ|=cosh⁡tγ. Discreteness and torsion-freeness give a separated orbit of 0: the stabilizer is trivial, and every bounded displacement set contains finitely many group elements. Packing disjoint small balls around the orbit gives

    #{γ:tγ≤T}=O(e6T),

    since the radial volume density is a constant multiple of sinh3⁡(2t)dt. Consequently

    ∑γ∈Γ|dγ|−(n+2)<∞(n>4).(2)

    This proves local uniform absolute convergence. For derivatives, factor cq+d=d(1+d−1cq). The quaternion d−1c lies in the unit ball. After taking out the factor |d|−(n+2), every derivative of the summand is bounded uniformly for |q|≤r, independently of γ; the inverse denominator stays away from zero. Equation (2) therefore proves convergence with every derivative.

    Each summand is the corrected translate πn(γ−1)v, hence regular. The denominator cocycle J(γδ,q)=J(γ,δq)J(δ,q) and reindexing the sum give the required automorphy law.

    For cuspidality, set

    kn,v(g)=|dg|−2τn(ι(dg−1))v.

    By the constant-vector matrix-coefficient identity in the discrete-series model, its scalar components are smooth, K-finite matrix coefficients and are finite under the center of the enveloping algebra. Their absolute values are bounded by a constant times (cosh⁡t)−(n+2). The Cartan integral

    ∫0∞(cosh⁡t)−(n+2)sinh3⁡(2t)dt

    is finite precisely for n>4. The lift of (1) is ∑γkn,v(γg). Muić’s Poincaré-series theorem [15, Lemma 4-2(i)] gives cuspidality and the automorphic conditions, componentwise. Square integrability follows from [15, Lemma 4-1(e)].

    Ensuring that the series is nonzero#

    Nonvanishing in a separating tower is also given by [15, Lemma 4-2(iii)] for a fixed nonzero K-finite matrix coefficient of an integrable discrete series.

    Theorem AS2. Deep levels and high weights

    Let Γ⊂Sp(1,1) be discrete and torsion-free and let n≥5. Put

    TΓ,n=∑γ≠1|dγ|−(n+2).

    If TΓ,n<1, the linear map v↦PΓ,n,v is injective. In particular it produces n+1 linearly independent regular automorphic spinors. This criterion holds in each of the following situations:

    • Fix n≥5 and a decreasing sequence Γj⊂Γ0 of discrete torsion-free groups with ⋂jΓj={1}. It holds for all sufficiently large j.
    • Fix a discrete torsion-free Γ. It holds for every sufficiently large integer n.

    For congruence lattices these are cusp forms by AS1. In particular every fixed n≥5 occurs at sufficiently deep level in a separating congruence tower.

    AS2 · version 2

    Uses AS1.

    Proof of Theorem AS2

    The identity term at q=0 is v, and the other terms give

    ‖PΓ,n,v(0)−v‖n≤TΓ,n‖v‖n.

    Thus T<1 makes evaluation of the Poincaré series an invertible linear map on Vn.

    In the first case, dominate the sum by the convergent series for Γ0. Each nonidentity element eventually leaves Γj, so dominated convergence gives TΓj,n→0. In the second case, |dγ|>1 for γ≠1: equality would put γ in the compact stabilizer of 0, whose intersection with a torsion-free discrete group is trivial. Each term tends to zero as n→∞, and the convergent n=5 series dominates. Again T→0.

    The bound n≥5 is the convergence range of this simple periodization argument. It is not a nonexistence result at smaller weights. Nor does AS2 promise a nonzero form at every prescribed weight and level. Torsion and the center also matter: if −I∈Γ, the transformation law forces every odd-weight spinor to vanish. Passing to a torsion-free level removes that particular obstruction.

    A Nonzero Weight-Fourteen Example#

    The following example specifies the lattice as well as the form. It uses an effective orientation-preserving group of hyperbolic four-space. At the even weights below, the central sign in its spin cover acts trivially on both spinors and flat coefficients. Thus all formulas are independent of a choice of sign for a lift. The group-cohomology comparison is unchanged by this finite central kernel over ℂ.

    Let L=diag⁡(−1,1,1,1,1) and

    Proposition CP-E1. Geometry and Congruence of the Example Group

    The color map in CP-E0 is a homomorphism. Its kernel is orientation-preserving, torsion-free, and has index 16 in W2. It contains the principal level-four subgroup

    W4={g∈W2:g≡I(mod4)},[ΓL:W4]=64.

    A fundamental domain is the regular ideal 24-cell obtained from the chamber

    P={v∈ℍL4:vi≥0, vi+vj≤v0 (i<j)}

    by all sixteen spatial coordinate-sign changes. The boundary points 0,∞,i/2 are cusps of Γ.

    CP-E1 · version 1

    Uses CP-E0; the level-two reflection-group description in [11, §4].

    Proof of Proposition CP-E1

    The reflection-group theorem [11, §4, pp. 9–10] identifies W2 with the time-orientation-preserving integral Lorentz group congruent to I modulo two, with chamber P. Its Coxeter relations are that the ten reflections have order two and perpendicular-wall reflections commute. All colors satisfy these relations, and the four Ai prove surjectivity. Every color has odd coordinate sum. Thus zero color implies even reflection parity and orientation preservation.

    To check torsion, the maximal sets of mutually perpendicular walls are: the four Ai; for each three-element index set {i,j,k}, the set {Bij,Bik,Bjk,Al} with l complementary; for each i, the three Bij with j≠i; and the six sets {Ai,Aj,Bkl} with complementary pairs. Substituting the displayed colors gives independent columns over 𝔽2 in each of these fifteen cases. A finite subgroup fixes a hyperbolic point; after moving it to P, its stabilizer is generated by the walls through that point. The color map is injective on such a stabilizer, so its kernel has no torsion. The coordinate-sign subgroup supplies sixteen coset representatives; the union of their chambers is the ideal 24-cell.

    For the congruence assertion, set Dg=(g−I)/2(mod2). Matrix multiplication adds these matrices modulo two. Put

    βij=(Dg)ij,αi=(Dg)ii+∑j≠iβmin⁡(i,j),max⁡(i,j).

    Then

    χ(g)=∑iαie‾i+∑i<jβijχ(Bij),

    as is checked on the ten generators. Their ten Dg-matrices are independent: the six off-diagonal spatial entries detect the Bij, then the four diagonal entries detect the Ai. Hence |W2/W4|=210, proving the indices and congruence inclusion. The points q=1,−1 are rational isotropic boundary lines and hence cusps; their half-space images are ∞,0. The element a below sends 0 to i/2, proving the last assertion.

    This example involves thirteen radial Mellin arguments, 2,3,…,14, with angle-dependent endpoints. Its height transform has the complete functional equation s↔16−s. The surface transform has the separate inversion equation M21, and its period value obeys the group relations M12. These are three explicit structures available for studying the same cusp form.

    The cusp-normalized representative in E5 is cohomologous to the interior-base-point representative by CP-M6. The nonvanishing result E3 proves that the full cohomology class is nonzero; it does not by itself assert that this particular (a,a) coordinate is nonzero. Formula E6 gives the coordinate exactly, without a numerical evaluation or an identification with special values of a scalar L-function.

    Further Arithmetic Questions#

    The Mellin construction and weight-fourteen example above give explicit spinors and triangle periods. Further arithmetic questions include effective dimensions at n=2,3,4, explicit Hecke spectra, comparisons with adelic Hecke generators at each level, and the arithmetic of periods within each chirality. An integral coefficient lattice for the matrix-order semigroup is constructed in SH3. The Poincaré construction and theta-lift constructions provide places to start. Neither the correspondence nor the dimension formula asserts that every cusp form is an Arakawa theta lift.
