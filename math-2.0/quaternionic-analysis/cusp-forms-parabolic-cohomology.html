    Expository

    Regular cusp spinors on quaternionic half-space, their Mellin period cocycles, an explicit weight-fourteen example, and arithmetic cohomology.

    Cusp Forms, Mellin Transforms and Parabolic Cohomology

    Discussion

    A regular quaternionic cusp form produces a closed two-form with flat coefficients. Its periods over triangles therefore form a group two-cocycle. Mellin transforms enter these surface periods through a primitive whose coefficients are incomplete Mellin integrals. Their compatibility is expressed by cocycle and inversion identities.

    Setup#

    Let n≥2, m=n−2, and Em=Symm⁡ℂ4. Let Γ be a torsion-free congruence lattice in a rational-rank-one form of G≃Sp⁡(1,1), acting on ℋ={y+x∈ℍ:y>0}. Use the half-space action and polynomial coefficients, the growth and finiteness conditions, and the cuspidal condition. Write 𝒮n−(Γ) for that cusp space.

    The closed Em-valued two-form Ωf, its coefficient embedding Zm and covariance are given in CP-C2; CP-H1 identifies them with the ball model. The Fourier expansion and complete Mellin formulas fix the analytic conventions used below.

    Incomplete Mellin Integrals Give a Primitive#

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ) and assume infinity is a rational cusp. For y>0, x∈Im⁡ℍ and s∈ℂ, define

    Mf(s;x;y)=∫y∞f(t+x)ts−1dt.(M8)

    Expand the polynomial map Zm(y+x):Symm⁡ℂ2→Em as

    Zm(y+x)=∑j=0myjPj(x),Ka=2ϵIa.

    Set

    αf(y+x)=−∑a=13∑j=0mPj(x)ΦKa(Mf(j+1;x;y))dxa.(M9)

    Proposition CP-MC1. The Incomplete-Mellin Primitive

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ) and assume infinity is a rational cusp. The integral M8 is entire in s, and dαf=Ωf. It has the Fourier expression

    Mf(s;x;y)=∑λ≠0Aλe2πi⟨λ,x⟩(2π|λ|)−sΓ(s,2π|λ|y),(M10)

    where Γ(s,u)=∫u∞e−tts−1dt for u>0.

    CP-MC1 · version 1

    Uses CP-C2, CP-M1.

    Proof of Proposition CP-MC1

    The positive lower endpoint and exponential decay at infinity prove convergence, entire dependence and M10 by termwise integration. Write

    Ωf=dy∧A(y,x)+B(y,x)

    with horizontal forms A of degree one and B of degree two. Since S(∂y,∂xa)=2ϵIa, the expression M9 is exactly −∫y∞A(t,x)dt. Closedness of Ωf gives ∂yB=dxA. Its polynomial coefficient factors and cusp decay imply B(t,x)→0. Thus

    ∂yαf=A,dxαf=−∫y∞dxA(t,x)dt=B(y,x).

    The integrals can be differentiated on compact sets by the Fourier estimates. These two identities prove dαf=Ωf.

    Discussion

    Here the word “incomplete” refers to the lower endpoint y. The horizontal point x need not be a boundary cusp. The arguments 1,…,n−1 in M9 come from the polynomial coefficient embedding Zm; the surface parameterization below gives a different list, 2,…,n.

    Period Functions on Group Generators#

    For the period-cohomology correspondence for Maass forms on PSL2(ℝ), with its principal-series coefficient modules, see [2, Thm. B].

    Proposition CP-M5. A Function-Valued Period Cocycle

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ), assume infinity is a rational cusp, take αf from CP-MC1, and fix o∈ℋ. Put 𝒜=C∞(ℋ,Em) with left action

    (TgF)(z)=ρ(g)F(g−1z),

    and regard Em as its submodule of constant functions. Define

    bg(z)=∫[o,z](g∗αf−ρ(g)αf),Bg(z)=bg(g−1z),Bg=BgmodEm.(M13)

    The first integral is path-independent and the functions are real-analytic in the interior. They satisfy

    bgk(z)=bg(kz)+ρ(g)bk(z)−bg(ko),(M14)
    Bgk=Bg+TgBk.(M15)

    The constants κf(g,k)=bg(ko) form an Em-valued two-cocycle and

    cf,o=δaf+κf,af(g)=∫[o,go]αf.(M16)

    For the connecting map of 0→Em→𝒜→𝒜/Em→0,

    ∂[B]=[κf]=[Ωf].(M17)

    The class [B] is independent of the primitive and its normalization, is zero after restriction to each cusp stabilizer, and determines f uniquely in the cusp space.

    CP-M5 · version 1

    Uses CP-MC1, CP-M4.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M5

    The one-form βg=g∗αf−ρ(g)αf is closed by covariance of Ωf. Contractibility makes its integral path-independent. For the integer moments in M9, the identity

    Γ(j+1,u)=j!e−u∑a=0juaa!

    and the polynomial coefficient bound in M2 give locally convergent analytic Fourier series. Hence αf, and local primitives of βg, are real-analytic.

    Pullbacks reverse composition, giving βgk=k∗βg+ρ(g)βk. Integrating and imposing bg(o)=0 proves M14. Substitution gives

    TgBk−Bgk+Bg=κf(g,k),

    which proves M15 and M17. The group coboundary squared is zero, so κf is a two-cocycle. Integrating the three edges in M11 gives M16: the translated edge contributes ρ(g)af(k)+bg(ko).

    If αf is replaced by αf+dψ, then Bg changes by ψ−Tgψ modulo constants, a coboundary. All primitives on ℋ differ this way. A change of the normalization of bg only adds constants.

    For the primitive based at infinity and p∈Γ∞, the form p∗αf−ρ(p)αf is horizontal and closed. Since p acts by (y,x)↦(λy,λRx+t), its coefficients tend to zero as y→∞. Closedness makes these horizontal coefficients independent of y, so the form vanishes. Thus Bp=0. At another cusp choose the primitive obtained in its own half-space chart. It differs from αf by an exact one-form, so the restriction of B is a coboundary there. Finally M17 and CP-M4 prove injectivity.

    Discussion

    The connecting map in M17 has a concrete meaning: choose representatives Bg of the function classes, take their group coboundary, and obtain constant coefficient vectors. Those constants recover the surface periods up to a coboundary.

    For a presentation Γ=⟨s1,…,sd|ℛ⟩, one function class per generator suffices for M15. For each relator r1⋯rℓ=1, the functional equation is

    ∑j=1ℓTr1⋯rj−1Brj=0,Bs−1=−Ts−1Bs.(M18)

    These follow by repeated use of M15. They provide a function-valued description of the same form detected by the surface-period class. An independent analytic characterization of which generator functions arise from regular cusp forms remains a further question. Function-valued parabolic cocycles also occur in the classical Maass-form theory of [2], with different coefficient modules.

    Cusp-Normalized Surface Periods#

    Proposition CP-M6. Moving the Vertices to Cusps

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ). For a rational cusp η set

    cf,η(g,k)=∫[η,gη,gkη]Ωf,(M19)

    with value zero on degenerate triangles. This integral converges absolutely and satisfies M12. If

    aη,o(g)=∫[η,o,go]Ωf−∫[η,gη,go]Ωf,

    then cf,o−cf,η=δaη,o. Moreover cf,η(g,k)=0 if either g or k fixes η.

    CP-M6 · version 1

    Uses CP1, CP-C2, CP-M4.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M6

    Near each ideal vertex, move that cusp to infinity. The triangle then lies in a vertical plane with bounded horizontal width near infinity. The spinor decays faster than every inverse power of height, while the fixed coefficient embedding and the coordinate transports contribute only polynomial factors. This proves integrability and makes the truncation edges, and the truncation faces of three-simplices, tend to zero. Stokes therefore proves the cocycle identity for ideal simplices as for compact ones. The prism between the vertex maps g↦go and g↦gη has the displayed one-cochain as its two-dimensional term, yielding the coboundary formula. Finally, if gη=η or kη=η, two of the triangle's vertices coincide.

    Proposition CP-M7. The Mellin Transform of an Ideal Triangle

    Let f∈𝒮n−(Γ) and suppose 0,∞,au are cusps of Γ, where a>0 and u is a unit imaginary quaternion. Orient 𝒯a,u=[0,∞,au] by dy∧dt in z=y+tu. Put

    𝒫f,a,u(s)=∫𝒯a,u(|z|/a)sΩf.

    This is entire and 𝒫f,a,u(0)=∫𝒯a,uΩf. Set

    ωθ=cos⁡θ+usin⁡θ,Zm(rω)=∑j=0mrjBj(ω),Ku=2ϵQ(u),
    ℳf(v;ω;l,b)=∫lbf(rω)rv−1dr.

    Then

    𝒫f,a,u(s)=a−s∑j=0m∫0π/2Bj(ωθ)ΦKu(ℳf(s+j+2;ωθ;asin⁡θ,a/sin⁡θ))dθ.(M20)

    For a unit imaginary v perpendicular to u, put

    ga,u=(0ava−1v0).

    It belongs to G, and the transforms satisfy

    𝒫f|nga,u,a,u(s)=−ρ(ga,u)−1𝒫f,a,u(−s).(M21)

    CP-M7 · version 1

    Uses CP-C2, CP-M6.

    Proof of Proposition CP-M7

    In the vertical plane the triangle has 0<t<a and y2+t2>at. With y=rcos⁡θ and t=rsin⁡θ, this becomes

    0<θ<π/2,asin⁡θ<r<a/sin⁡θ.

    The pullback of S on (∂r,∂θ) is rKu. Expanding Zm and integrating gives M20. Cusp decay proves absolute convergence; the factor (|z|/a)s and its derivatives in s have at most polynomial-logarithmic growth near zero and infinity and are bounded near the third vertex on compact sets of s. This proves entire dependence.

    One checks ga,u∗H0ga,u=H0 and ga,u2=−I. On the indicated plane it acts as

    rωθ⟼a2r−1ωθ.

    It preserves the triangle, interchanges its radial bounds and reverses its orientation. Apply HS4 and change variables; the weight changes from (r/a)s to (r/a)−s, giving M21.

    Discussion

    For a fixed interior angle, the Fourier expression for the finite radial integral is a difference of incomplete gamma integrals. In particular, with

    μλ(ω)=2π(|λ|Re⁡ω−i⟨λ,Im⁡ω⟩),

    each mode contributes

    Aλ∫lbe−μλ(ω)rrv−1dr.

    On compact angular subintervals the sums can be integrated term by term. At the cusp endpoint θ=π/2, M20 is defined by the convergent integral of the original cusp form; an exchange with the Fourier sum needs an additional uniform estimate.

    At s=0, M20 uses the radial arguments 2,…,n. These values form the cocycle. For a freely varying s, the weighted form need not remain closed: d(rsΩf)=srsdlog⁡r∧Ωf. Thus M21 is a functional equation of surface transforms; M12 is the cocycle relation for their period values. The two assertions have different domains.

    A Nonzero Weight-Fourteen Example#

    The following example specifies the lattice as well as the form. It uses an effective orientation-preserving group of hyperbolic four-space. At the even weights below, the central sign in its spin cover acts trivially on both spinors and flat coefficients. Thus all formulas are independent of a choice of sign for a lift. The group-cohomology comparison is unchanged by this finite central kernel over ℂ.

    Construction CP-E0. The Colored Reflection Group

    Let L=diag⁡(−1,1,1,1,1) and

    ℍL4={v∈ℝ5:vtLv=−1, v0>0}.

    Write e0,…,e4 for the coordinate vectors. For νtLν=1, put rν=I−2ννtL. Set

    Ai=rei,Bij=re0+ei+ej(1≤i<j≤4),W2=⟨Ai,Bij⟩.

    Let e‾1,…,e‾4 be the basis of 𝔽24. Define χ:W2→𝔽24 by χ(Ai)=e‾i and

    Pair (i,j) Color χ(Bij)
    (1,2) e‾1
    (1,3) e‾3
    (1,4) e‾4
    (2,3) e‾2
    (2,4) e‾1+e‾2+e‾3
    (3,4) e‾1+e‾2+e‾4

    Put ΓL=ker⁡χ. Identify v∈ℍL4 with the ball point

    q=v1+v2i+v3j+v4kv0+1,

    and then with z=Cq∈ℋ. Let Γ⊂G/{±I} be the resulting group acting on ℋ.

    CP-E0 · version 1

    Well-definedness: CP-E1.

    Proposition CP-E1. Geometry and Congruence of the Example Group

    The color map in CP-E0 is a homomorphism. Its kernel is orientation-preserving, torsion-free, and has index 16 in W2. It contains the principal level-four subgroup

    W4={g∈W2:g≡I(mod4)},[ΓL:W4]=64.

    A fundamental domain is the regular ideal 24-cell obtained from the chamber

    P={v∈ℍL4:vi≥0, vi+vj≤v0 (i<j)}

    by all sixteen spatial coordinate-sign changes. The boundary points 0,∞,i/2 are cusps of Γ.

    CP-E1 · version 1

    Uses CP-E0; the level-two reflection-group description in [4, §4].

    Proof of Proposition CP-E1

    The reflection-group theorem [4, §4, pp. 9–10] identifies W2 with the time-orientation-preserving integral Lorentz group congruent to I modulo two, with chamber P. Its Coxeter relations are that the ten reflections have order two and perpendicular-wall reflections commute. All colors satisfy these relations, and the four Ai prove surjectivity. Every color has odd coordinate sum. Thus zero color implies even reflection parity and orientation preservation.

    To check torsion, the maximal sets of mutually perpendicular walls are: the four Ai; for each three-element index set {i,j,k}, the set {Bij,Bik,Bjk,Al} with l complementary; for each i, the three Bij with j≠i; and the six sets {Ai,Aj,Bkl} with complementary pairs. Substituting the displayed colors gives independent columns over 𝔽2 in each of these fifteen cases. A finite subgroup fixes a hyperbolic point; after moving it to P, its stabilizer is generated by the walls through that point. The color map is injective on such a stabilizer, so its kernel has no torsion. The coordinate-sign subgroup supplies sixteen coset representatives; the union of their chambers is the ideal 24-cell.

    For the congruence assertion, set Dg=(g−I)/2(mod2). Matrix multiplication adds these matrices modulo two. Put

    βij=(Dg)ij,αi=(Dg)ii+∑j≠iβmin⁡(i,j),max⁡(i,j).

    Then

    χ(g)=∑iαie‾i+∑i<jβijχ(Bij),

    as is checked on the ten generators. Their ten Dg-matrices are independent: the six off-diagonal spatial entries detect the Bij, then the four diagonal entries detect the Ai. Hence |W2/W4|=210, proving the indices and congruence inclusion. The points q=1,−1 are rational isotropic boundary lines and hence cusps; their half-space images are ∞,0. The element a below sends 0 to i/2, proving the last assertion.

    Proposition CP-E2. An Explicit Nonzero Poincaré Series

    For v∈V14 define the half-space seed and its periodization by

    ϕv(z)=28|z+1|−2τ14(Q((z+1)−1))v,hv(z)=∑γ∈Γ(ϕv|14γ)(z).(E1)

    For either lift γ=(aγbγcγdγ), this is

    hv(z)=28∑γ∈Γ|Lγ(z)|−2τ14(Q(Lγ(z)−1))v,Lγ(z)=(aγ+cγ)z+bγ+dγ.(E2)

    The series converges absolutely locally with derivatives and defines a regular cusp form. It satisfies

    ‖hv(1)−2−8v‖14≤342−8‖v‖14,‖hv(1)‖14≥2−10‖v‖14.(E3)

    In particular v↦hv is injective. For W in CP-M2,

    hv|14W=hv,Λhv(s)=Λhv(16−s).(E4)

    CP-E2 · version 1

    Uses CP-E1, CP-H1, AS1–AS2; [6, Lemma 4-2(i)].

    Proof of Proposition CP-E2

    The Cayley image (4) of the constant ball seed v is ϕv, since 1−q=2(z+1)−1. The product of the two denominator factors in ϕv|γ is Lγ(z) in the indicated order, proving E2. The constant-seed construction AS1 gives convergence with derivatives and regularity. Its lift is a matrix coefficient of the weight-fourteen discrete series, which is integrable because 14>4; Muić’s theorem [6, Lemma 4-2(i)] gives cuspidality for this congruence lattice. Summing over the spin inverse image gives twice E1; the even central character allows the displayed effective-group sum.

    For the nonzero bound, work briefly at the ball center oL=(1,0,0,0,0). Torsion-freeness makes γ↦γoL injective. For γ≠1 write

    γoL=(2N−1,2z1,2z2,2z3,2z4),|z|2=N(N−1),N≥2.

    Each integer coordinate has absolute value at most N−1; choosing three leaves at most two choices for the fourth. There are therefore fewer than 16N3 possible orbit points. For a ball lift, |dγ|2=N, and the summand at the center has norm N−8‖v‖14. The tail is at most

    16∑N=2∞N−5≤16(2−5+∫2∞t−5dt)=34.

    The center value transforms by hv(1)=2−8Fv(0), proving E3.

    The spatial sign change q↦−q lies in W2 and normalizes ker⁡χ. A ball lift is k=diag⁡(−1,1); it fixes the constant seed under slash. Reindexing the absolutely convergent Poincaré sum gives Fv|k=Fv. Since CkC−1=W, this proves the first identity in E4; CP-M2 proves the second.

    Discussion

    E1–E2 specify the form without a Fourier-coefficient computation. For reproducible spin lifts, associate to a Lorentz vector ν=(ν0,…,ν4) the complex matrix

    𝒞(ν)=(ν0I2×2−Q(ν1+ν2i+ν3j+ν4k)Q(ν1+ν2i+ν3j+ν4k)∗−ν0I2×2).

    For an even reflection word, its product gives a ball spin lift, up to central sign; conjugate by R(C) to obtain the half-space lift. This prescription follows from the Clifford identity 𝒞(ν)2=−νtLνI and its reflection-conjugation formula. At weight fourteen the two choices of sign give identical terms. The form hX14 is a specified nonzero cusp form; no Hecke-eigenvector choice is built into its definition.

    Proposition CP-E3. The (a,a) Period as a Surface Mellin Integral

    Let h=hX14, E12=Sym12⁡ℂ4, and choose the element with half-space lift

    a=(0−i−i−2)∈G.

    Then a∈Γ, a∞=0 and a2∞=i/2. With ch,∞ from M19,

    ch,∞(a,a)=−𝒫h,1/2,i(0).(E5)

    Writing ωθ=cos⁡θ+isin⁡θ and Z12(rω)=∑j=012rjBj(ω), its value is

    ch,∞(a,a)=4i14⋅13∑j=012∫0π/2Bj(ωθ)[∫12sin⁡θ12sin⁡θhXY(rωθ)rj+1dr]dθ.(E6)

    The formula is an equality in the 455-dimensional coefficient space E12.

    CP-E3 · version 1

    Uses CP-E2, CP-M6, CP-M7.

    Proof of Proposition CP-E3

    The Lorentz transformation A1B12 has zero color and ball lift

    (−1+i11−1−i).

    Conjugation by C gives the displayed half-space lift. Its fractional action sends ∞ to zero and zero to i/2. Thus the ideal triangle in M19 is [∞,0,i/2], with the opposite orientation to 𝒯1/2,i=[0,∞,i/2]. This proves E5. Finally

    Φ2ϵQ(i)(p)=−4i14⋅13pXY.

    Substitute this into M20 at s=0 and reverse the sign from E5 to obtain E6. The dimension is (153)=455.

    Discussion

    This example involves thirteen radial Mellin arguments, 2,3,…,14, with angle-dependent endpoints. Its height transform has the complete functional equation s↔16−s. The surface transform has the separate inversion equation M21, and its period value obeys the group relations M12. These are three explicit structures available for studying the same cusp form.

    The cusp-normalized representative in E5 is cohomologous to the interior-base-point representative by CP-M6. The nonvanishing result E3 proves that the full cohomology class is nonzero; it does not by itself assert that this particular (a,a) coordinate is nonzero. Formula E6 gives the coordinate exactly, without a numerical evaluation or an identification with special values of a scalar L-function.

    Further Arithmetic Questions#

    Discussion

    The Mellin construction and weight-fourteen example above give explicit spinors and triangle periods. Further arithmetic questions include effective dimensions at n=2,3,4, explicit Hecke spectra, comparisons with adelic Hecke generators at each level, and the arithmetic of periods within each chirality. An integral coefficient lattice for the matrix-order semigroup is constructed in SH3. The Poincaré and theta-lift constructions provide places to start. Neither the correspondence nor the dimension formula asserts that every cusp form is an Arakawa theta lift.
