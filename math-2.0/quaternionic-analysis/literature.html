    Expository

    Literature on Fueter regularity, quaternionic elliptic functions, lattice sums, and number-theoretic connections.

    Sources and further reading

    This guide collects literature for the foundations, elliptic functions, representation constructions, and special-value formulas. It focuses on Fueter-regular and monogenic functions. Works using other analytic settings are identified separately where relevant.

    The notes record the scope of reading, not a verdict on correctness. “Metadata” means publication details were checked; “abstract” or “selected passages” identifies how much mathematical content was inspected. No entry here represents a completed proof audit. The initial selection dates from 17 October 2026. Article-local bibliographies, added on 29 October 2026, identify sources at the statements and proofs that use them; this reading guide supplements those citations.

    Attribution of the construction#

    The quaternionic Weierstrass construction is due to Fueter. A directly inspected reference is Kraußhar (2001), §4, p. 1019, equation (12): the regularized derivative lattice sum is displayed there, with its quaternionic case credited to Fueter (1939), reference [16]. Read the passage.

    A detailed comparison with Fueter's original formulas is still needed, especially for the zeta normalization and quasi-period conventions. The proofs in this wiki concern the exact formulations displayed here; a historical citation does not verify those proofs.

    Foundations and periodic functions#

    Work Relevance to the wiki Reading so far
    R. Fueter (1934–35), Die Funktionentheorie der Differentialgleichungen Δu = 0 und ΔΔu = 0 mit vier reellen Variablen. Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici 7, 307–330. Foundational work on quaternionic function theory and its differential equations. Publisher metadata inspected. The volume spans 1934–35; the publisher labels it 1934, while Sudbery cites 1935. Full text not reviewed.
    R. Fueter (1935–36), Über die analytische Darstellung der regulären Funktionen einer Quaternionenvariablen. Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici 8, 371–378. Regular polynomial expansions and the linear coordinates used in the Taylor theory. Indexed opening passage inspected; full scan not reviewed.
    R. Fueter (1936–37), Die Singularitäten der eindeutigen regulären Funktionen einer Quaternionenvariablen. I. Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici 9, 320–334. Historical starting point for the singularity theory underlying lattice constructions. Archive metadata and indexed opening inspected; full scan not reviewed.
    R. Fueter (1939), Über vierfachperiodische Funktionen. Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik 48, 161–169. Original source credited for the quaternionic Weierstrass construction by Kraußhar (2001). DOI-registry metadata checked; attribution located in the later paper. Original formulas still to compare.
    R. Fueter (1945), Über die Quaternionenmultiplikation regulärer vierfachperiodischer Funktionen. Experientia 1, 57. Early source to consult for quaternion multiplication in the periodic theory. Publisher metadata and bibliography inspected; the one-page communication itself not reviewed.
    A. Sudbery (1979), Quaternionic analysis. Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society 85 (2), 199–225. An entry point to the regular-function theory and its historical development. Publisher extract, bibliography, and selected regularity and differential-form passages inspected; full article not audited.

    Automorphic and number-theoretic directions#

    Work Relevance to the wiki Reading so far
    R. S. Kraußhar (2001), On a New Type of Eisenstein Series in Clifford Analysis. Zeitschrift für Analysis und ihre Anwendungen 20 (4), 1007–1029. Open text. Kernel derivatives, regularized Weierstrass sums, Laurent coefficients, and Eisenstein series. Selected passages in §§3–5 and bibliography inspected, including equation (12) on p. 1019.
    R. S. Kraußhar (2004), Generalized Analytic Automorphic Forms in Hypercomplex Spaces. Birkhäuser, Frontiers in Mathematics. A book-length route through translation-group Eisenstein series, modular forms, and multiplication of lattices. Publisher description and contents inspected. In particular, pp. 49–115 and 117–149 are relevant chapters to read.
    R. S. Kraußhar, J. Ryan, and Qiao Yuying (2004), Harmonic, Monogenic and Hypermonogenic Functions on Some Conformally Flat Manifolds…. Author version, arXiv:math/0404337. Cylinders and tori; Mittag–Leffler and Laurent theory for monogenic functions. Abstract inspected. Hypermonogenic parts use a different differential equation; transfer to the wiki's setting needs care.
    A. Krieg (1988), Eisenstein-series on the four-dimensional hyperbolic space. Journal of Number Theory 30, 177–197. A theta-series connection in a related hyperbolic automorphic setting. A lead for comparison with the harmonic-theta direction. Indexed publisher abstract inspected; full text not reviewed. It does not by itself establish the proposed theta relation for this wiki's lattice sums.

The classical comparison for the two-form construction. §§1–5 inspected in the full text. Later arithmetic and Hecke arguments not audited.
    Michio Kuga and Goro Shimura (1960), On vector differential forms attached to automorphic forms. J. Math. Soc. Japan 12, 258–270. A further comparison for vector-valued automorphic differentials. Publisher metadata inspected; full argument not yet read.
    Harald Grobner (2010), The Automorphic Cohomology and the Residual Spectrum of Hermitian Groups of Rank One. Int. J. Math. 21, 255–278. Author version. §§1.1–1.2 and 7: automorphic cohomology of Sp(1,1); Theorem 7.1 locates the two cohomological discrete series in degree two. The author version’s §§1.1–1.2, 4.1, 5.1–5.3, 6.1–6.3 and 7.1–7.2 inspected. CP5–CP6 match the coefficient family and unordered chirality pair, and import Proposition 7.1 with its exact level and weight restrictions. The classification and trace-formula proofs have not been independently audited.
    Harald Grobner (2008), A Growth Condition for Cuspidal Cohomology of Arithmetically Defined Quaternionic Hyperbolic n-Manifolds, extended preprint, ESI 2043. §1.2 states the finite-coefficient cuspidal-to-ordinary cohomology injection used in AC7, citing Borel, Stable real cohomology of arithmetic groups II (1981), Corollary 5.5. Introduction, §§1.1–1.5 and 2.4–2.5 inspected, including the weight/coefficient dictionary and degree-2r discrete-series cohomology in QH4. Borel's original proof and the later growth computations were not audited.

    Harmonic comparison and cusp dimensions#

    Work Role and precise use Reading so far
    Stephen D. Miller and Wilfried Schmid (2012), On the rapid decay of cuspidal automorphic forms. Advances in Mathematics 231, 940–964. §2, (2.9)–(2.10), defines moderate and uniform moderate growth. Theorem A gives the rapid decrease on Siegel sets used in CP1; Theorems 2.23–2.24 specify the smooth-vector version. Exact arXiv v1 retained; definitions and these theorem statements inspected. The full proof and its Averbuch input have not been independently audited. The cusp-frame conversion in CP0 and the integrability estimate in CP1 are supplied in the article.
    Nolan R. Wallach (1984), On the constant term of a square integrable automorphic form, in Operator Algebras and Group Representations, Vol. II, Monographs and Studies in Mathematics 18, Pitman, 227–237. Theorem 4.3: a tempered module realized by square-integrable automorphic forms has cuspidal image. Together with the degree-two classification, this excludes residual contributions in CP5. Original scan retained; introduction, setup, Theorem 4.3 and proof (printed pp. 233–234) inspected, including the rendered theorem page. Underlying Jacquet-module results not independently reconstructed.
    Armand Borel and William Casselman (1983), L²-cohomology of locally symmetric manifolds of finite volume. Duke Math. J. 50, 625–647. Proposition 4.4(i), due to Borel–Garland, identifies finite-coefficient square-integrable harmonic forms with discrete-spectrum cohomology; Proposition 5.4 gives the geometric comparison. External input in CP5. Original full text not retrieved. The precise finite-coefficient statement and these references were inspected in Nair–Rai, Appendix A, pp. 29–30. The use concerns harmonic forms; it does not identify arbitrary ordinary classes with square-integrable forms.
    Arvind Nair and Ankit Rai, Automorphic Lefschetz properties for noncompact arithmetic manifolds, author-hosted preprint. Appendix A explicitly recalls the general finite-coefficient harmonic/discrete-spectrum comparison used in CP5. 35-page author PDF retained on 17 October 2026; title, abstract, and Appendix A, pp. 29–30, inspected. Its Lefschetz theorems are not used here.
    Tsuneo Arakawa (1982), On automorphic forms of a quaternion unitary group of degree two. J. Fac. Sci. Univ. Tokyo Sect. IA Math. 28, 547–566. Reference in Grobner. Historical dimension-formula source. The extended growth paper points to Theorem 2; the directly inspected formula used in CP6 is Grobner’s Proposition 7.1. Identified through Grobner’s primary papers; original full text not inspected. Some catalogues date volume 28/issue 3 to 1981; the cited publication year is 1982.
    Tsuneo Arakawa (1984), On certain automorphic forms of Sp(1,q), in Automorphic Forms of Several Variables, Katata 1983, Birkhäuser, 1–48. Reference and use in Grobner. Proposition 5.4 supplies the unipotent correction in the dimension calculation; the real-place parameter is matched in CP6. Citation and use inspected in Grobner §7.2; original proof not audited.

    Harmonic theta series, newforms, and L-values#

    Work Role and precise use Reading so far
    Bruno Schoeneberg (1939), Das Verhalten von mehrfachen Thetareihen bei Modulsubstitutionen. Mathematische Annalen 116, 511–523. Historical attribution; the modern formulation used here is Cohen–Strömberg, Chapter 14. Classical harmonic theta transformation theory, the setting for TH2. Bibliographic entry in Cohen–Strömberg checked; original paper not audited. The rank-four Poisson calculation is supplied in TH2.
    Henri Cohen and Fredrik Strömberg (2017), Modular Forms: A Classical Approach. Graduate Studies in Mathematics 179, AMS. Chapters 5 and 7 for dimensions, Chapters 10–13 for Hecke theory, Dirichlet series, and newforms, Chapter 14 for theta functions. Standard scalar theta and dimension theorems are explicit external inputs in TH4. Publisher contents and bibliography inspected; full book not audited.
    Ken Ono (2004), The Web of Modularity: Arithmetic of the Coefficients of Modular Forms and q-series. CBMS Regional Conference Series 102, AMS. Chapter 1 supplies the standard eta-quotient modularity and cusp-order criteria used explicitly in TH4. Publisher record and contents checked; the complete book proof was not audited. TH4 records the exact congruences, character, and cusp orders needed here.
    Stephen S. Gelbart (1975), Automorphic Forms on Adele Groups. Annals of Mathematics Studies 83, Princeton University Press; linked digital reissue 2016. §§3–6 develop adelization, local/global representations, cusp forms, and Hecke theory. The classical newform correspondence is the external input in TH6. Publisher description and contents inspected; no independent proof of that general correspondence is claimed here.
    The LMFDB Collaboration, weight-six, level-four space 4.6.a. Records the one-dimensional newspace, zero oldspace, coefficients, and unique newform 4.6.a.a. Used as a data cross-check for TH4. Space record inspected on 17 October 2026. The individual form page presented a browser challenge. The eta identity is proved using modularity and dimension, rather than database agreement. This is a database reference, not a published proof.

This is an author preprint. §§2.4–2.5, Lemma 2.9 were inspected for the primitive-vector/Epstein-to-Eisenstein comparison. The nonspherical summation identity in EI2 is proved directly.     Further comparisons#

    A detailed historical comparison of E-C1 and E-C2 with Fueter's original definitions remains useful, especially for kernel normalization, left/right conventions, and the zeta subtraction. The 1945 multiplication note is a separate starting point for the arithmetic action.

    The convergent harmonic theta and critical-value formulas now appear in TH1–TH5. Order-one regularization, arithmetic multiplication, and a direct relation between the ball and lattice representations remain open. The references here distinguish the published inputs from those questions.

    Half-space geometry and Mellin period functions#

    Work Role and precise use Reading so far
    David A. Vogan, Jr. (1981), Representations of Real Reductive Lie Groups, introductory chapter, §0.2. The Harish-Chandra isomorphism identifies the center of the enveloping algebra with Weyl-invariant polynomials. Used for the two central generators and the finite differential test. The map, isomorphism statement and infinitesimal-character convention in 0.2.7–0.2.9 inspected. The degree-two and degree-four generators follow from the signed-permutation Weyl group in type C2; the finite-span criterion is proved in the article.
    Jouni Parkkonen and Frédéric Paulin (2019), Counting and equidistribution in quaternionic Heisenberg groups. §§3 and 6 give Siegel-domain, horospherical and Heisenberg conventions, compared with QH1–QH3. The model, metric (8), coordinates (18) and Heisenberg law inspected. The arithmetic counting theorems are not used. The matrix and Cayley identities in QH1–QH3 are proved directly.
    Denis Constales and Rolf Sören Kraußhar (2002), Representation Formulas for the General Derivatives of the Fundamental Solution to the Cauchy–Riemann Operator in Clifford Analysis and Applications. Z. Anal. Anwendungen 21, 579–597. §3.1, Lemmas 1–3: Fourier plane waves and Clifford regularity. Related to the spinor Fourier calculation CP-M1. Introduction pp.580–581 and those lemmas inspected. The symmetric-spinor and cusp estimates used here are proved in CP-M1.
    Roelof Bruggeman, John Lewis and Don Zagier (2013 author version), Period functions for Maass wave forms and cohomology. A comparison for function-valued parabolic cocycles and automorphic reconstruction. Introduction pp.3–6 and Theorem B inspected. Its group and coefficient modules differ from those of CP-M5; that quaternionic construction is proved separately.
    Dubravko Ivanšić, Hyperbolic structure on a complement of tori in the 4-sphere, author version. §4 describes the ten-reflection level-two group and ideal 24-cell, used in CP-E1. It attributes the reflection-group identification to Ratcliffe–Tschantz, The Volume Spectrum of Hyperbolic 4-Manifolds, Experimental Mathematics 9 (2000), 101–125. PDF pp.9–10 inspected. The original Ratcliffe–Tschantz full proof was not independently audited. The particular color kernel, congruence condition and nonvanishing estimate are calculated in CP-E1–CP-E2.
