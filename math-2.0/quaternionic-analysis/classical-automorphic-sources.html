    Arakawa Lifts and Weight-Two Forms on Quaternionic Ball Quotients

    Arakawa theta lifts from classical forms#

    [2], takes an elliptic cusp form and an automorphic form on a definite quaternion algebra and constructs a vector-valued automorphic form on GSp(1,1). Their Theorem 4.1 treats even n>6. Restricting to the isometry group and fixing the finite adelic component gives a form on an arithmetic ball quotient. The kernel calculation below identifies its real-place condition with our regular-spinor model.

    [3] proves that some such lifts are nonzero. An arbitrary pair of input forms need not have nonzero lift.

    Proposition AL.1. Use the same quaternion embedding ι in both models. Put

    C=12(11−11).

    It takes B to {z:Re⁡z>0} by q↦(q+1)(1−q)−1. For a half-space group element h=(ABChDh) define

    D∗(h)=12(h1+1)(Ch+Dh),ωn(h)=|D∗(h)|−2τn(ι(D∗(h)−1)).

    For a ball group element g with lower-right block dg,

    D∗(CgC−1)=dg.(AL.1)

    Let n>4 and let ℱ be a smooth bounded, left Λ-invariant half-space group function with right compact transformation ℱ(hk)=τn(ι(Ck+Dk))−1ℱ(h) and reproducing identity

    ℱ(g)=c∫ωn(h−1g)ℱ(h)dh,(AL.2)

    with c and Haar measure normalized as in that identity. Then, for Γ=C−1ΛC, the function

    f(q)=s−(n+2)/2ℱ(Cσ(q)C−1)

    is regular and has exactly the automorphy law of Definition TF.1. The assertion applies after restricting an adelic form satisfying [2, Def. 2.1] to a fixed finite component. One may restrict further to a torsion-free finite-index subgroup to obtain the torsion-free quotient convention.

    Proposition AL.1 · version 1

    Uses Proposition TF.2, the corrected discrete-series action, and [2, §2, Def. 2.1].

    Proof of Proposition AL.1

    Conjugation by C changes the ball's form diag⁡(1,−1) to the half-space form (0110) up to sign. Direct multiplication shows that the sum of all four blocks of CgC−1 is 2dg. Since

    D∗(h)=12(A+B+Ch+Dh),

    this proves (AL.1), with quaternion multiplication in its displayed order. For g=diag⁡(a,b), the denominator at 1 of CgC−1 is b. The compact type is therefore our right-hand factor Vn, with no change of the integer n.

    Write F(g)=ℱ(CgC−1). Equation (AL.1) turns (AL.2) into

    F(g)=c∫G|dh−1g|−2τn(ι(dh−1g−1))F(h)dh.

    After setting g=σ(q) and multiplying by s−(n+2)/2, the integrand is precisely πn(h)cF(h)(q), the translated constant spinor with value F(h). It is regular. Boundedness of F, the integrable Cartan estimate of Theorem PC.14, and its derivative bounds on compact subsets of B justify differentiation under the integral. Hence f is regular. Right compact equivariance and left Γ invariance give the ball automorphy law by Proposition TF.2. The resulting section is nonzero whenever this restricted group function is nonzero.

    This identifies the theta-lift outputs with the present definition by a kernel computation, rather than by their compact types alone. The arithmetic construction and its cusp property remain external inputs from the cited literature. These lifts offer a promising route to Hecke eigenvalues, Fourier coefficients, and periods; a specific nonzero pair is a useful next example to work out.

    The geometric supply at weight two#

    Corollary AL.2. Let X=Γ\B be compact and connected, with Γ torsion-free. Then

    dimℂ⁡ℳ2(Γ)=b2−(X)=χ(X)2−1+b1(X).(AL.3)

    Here b2− uses the orientation in Corollary TF.10. For a finite cover Y→X of degree D,

    dimℂ⁡ℳ2(π1Y)≥Dχ(X)2−1.

    Thus covers of unbounded degree supply nonzero weight-two forms and unbounded dimensions.

    Corollary AL.2 · version 1

    Uses Corollary TF.10, Hodge theory, Poincaré duality, and the Hirzebruch signature and Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorems.

    Proof of Corollary AL.2

    Corollary TF.10 and Hodge theory identify the space with complex anti-self-dual harmonic two-forms. A real hyperbolic four-manifold has signature zero [1, §2.2]. Explicitly, for an orthonormal coframe its curvature matrix has entries Θij=Kθi∧θj, so tr⁡(Θ∧Θ)=0 and the first Pontryagin form vanishes. The signature theorem gives b2+=b2−. Poincaré duality gives χ=2−2b1+b2, proving (AL.3). Finally χ(Y)=Dχ(X), b1(Y)≥0, and Chern–Gauss–Bonnet [1, eq. (1)] gives χ(X)>0 for a compact hyperbolic four-manifold. This proves the bound and growth assertion. Such covers exist, for example along a separating tower in a cocompact arithmetic lattice.

    The three constructions serve different purposes. Poincaré series prove existence and give explicit convergent sums. Theta lifts supply arithmetic structure. At weight two, topology measures the space exactly on compact quotients. The cuspidal correspondence identifies the parabolic image using both chiralities, and the dimension formula gives explicit principal-level dimensions for n≥5. The weight-fourteen example specifies a congruence lattice, proves nonvanishing by an explicit tail estimate, and writes its (a,a) period as a surface Mellin integral. Low-weight noncompact dimensions remain a further question.
