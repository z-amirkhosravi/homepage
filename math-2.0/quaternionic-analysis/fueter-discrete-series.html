    Expository

    Weighted regular spinors, explicit group actions, compact types, and proofs of irreducibility and square-integrability.

    Fueter regularity and discrete series

    Discussion

    Fueter regularity realizes an entire family of discrete-series representations when the coefficients are compatible symmetric spinors. The construction below gives the Hilbert spaces, the group action, and the proofs. The case of one unrestricted quaternionic value is the Hardy limit discussed in the following article.

    The group and its coefficient spaces#

    Definition DS-D1. Ball, symmetric spinors, and compatible quaternionic tuples

    Put G=Sp⁡(1,1), K=Sp⁡(1)L×Sp⁡(1)R, B={q∈ℍ:|q|<1}, and s(q)=1−|q|2. Explicitly G={g∈GL2(ℍ):g∗diag⁡(1,−1)g=diag⁡(1,−1)}, where g∗ is conjugate transpose. Its action is gq=(aq+b)(cq+d)−1 and K consists of diag⁡(a,b) with |a|=|b|=1. All representations and Hilbert spaces below are complex. For quaternion coefficients, the complex scalar acts on the right.

    Let Vn=ℂ[X,Y]n with

    (τn(M)p)(X,Y)=p(aX+cY,bX+dY),M=(abcd),

    and invariant norm

    ‖∑k=0nckXn−kYk‖n2=∑k=0n|ck|2(nk).

    A quaternion acts through

    ι(t+ix+jy+kz)=(t+ixy+iz−y+izt−ix).

    Write a quaternion-valued homogeneous polynomial of auxiliary degree n−1 as

    h(q;X,Y)=∑k=0n−1hk(q)Xn−1−kYk=A(q;X,Y)−jB(q;X,Y),

    where A,B have complex coefficients. Auxiliary variables commute with quaternion coefficients. Define the fibre

    Wn={A−jB:AY=BX}⊂ℍ[X,Y]n−1.(C1)

    It has complex dimension n+1, embedded in the complex dimension 2n space ℍn. The condition is pointwise in q and couples the quaternion components algebraically. The first compact factor acts trivially on Vn. Let Tnp=pX−jpY and 𝒯n=Tn/n for n≥1. For a quaternion coefficient row of length n, use the squared norm ∑k=0n−1|hk|2/(n−1k); the auxiliary variables X,Y commute with coefficients. Write Dl=∂t+i∂x+j∂y+k∂z, coefficientwise, with x=x1,y=x2,z=x3 in the foundations article.

    DS-D1 · version 1

    Proposition DS1. The spinor-to-Fueter isometry cf. [1, §§2–3]

    For n≥1, 𝒯n:Vn→Wn is a complex-linear isometric isomorphism, with inverse A−jB↦XA+YB. In particular dimℂ⁡Wn=n+1.

    DS1 · version 1

    Uses DS-D1.

    Proof of Proposition DS1

    The polynomials fX/n and fY/n satisfy the compatibility condition, and for h=A−jB∈Wn the inverse is particularly simple:

    𝒯n−1h=XA+YB.(C5)

    Differentiating (C5), using AY=BX and Euler's identity, gives (XA+YB)X=nA and (XA+YB)Y=nB. This proves the inverse formula. For a monomial Xn−kYk, the squared norm of Tn's image is

    (n−k)2(n−1k)+k2(n−1k−1)=n2(nk),

    with absent endpoint terms omitted. Images of distinct monomials are orthogonal for the underlying complex inner product, proving the isometry.

    Weighted Hilbert spaces and the action#

    Definition DS-D2. The weighted regular spaces

    For n≥2, set Cn=n(n−1)/π2 and

    ℋn={h:B⟶Wn:Dlh=0,‖h‖ℋn2=Cn∫B∑k=0n−1|hk(q)|2(n−1k)s(q)n−2dv(q)<∞},(C2)

    where Dl=∂t+i∂x+j∂y+k∂z acts coefficientwise. The functions in this definition are smooth; equivalently one may impose the equation distributionally in the indicated L2 space. Equivalently, via DS1, use

    ℬn={f∈L2(B,Vn,dμn):Dl(Tnf)=0},dμn=Cnsn−2dv.(C6)

    The equation may be read distributionally. The measure is a probability measure, as follows from ∫Bsn−2dv=π2/[n(n−1)].

    DS-D2 · version 1

    Construction DS-C1. The Möbius representation [1, §§2.1–3.4]

    For γ∈G, write

    γ−1=(abcd),q′=(aq+b)(cq+d)−1,J=cq+d.

    Let Sm(M) be the coefficient-column matrix of τm(M) in descending monomials; in particular S1(M)=M. Regard h=(h0,…,hn−1) as a row. Define

    (Πn(γ)h)(q)=|J|−2J−1h(q′)Sn−1(ι(J−1))t.(C3)

    The matrix entries multiply quaternion coefficients on the right, while J−1 multiplies on the left. On polynomial spinors the corresponding formula is

    (πn(γ)f)(q)=|J|−2τn(J−1)f(q′).(C7)

    DS-C1 · version 1

    Well-definedness: The group law, covariance, and unitarity in DS2.

    Theorem DS2. Completeness, covariance, and unitarity [1, §§2.1–3.4]

    For n≥2, ℋn and ℬn are nonzero complete Hilbert spaces. Their distributional kernels have smooth harmonic representatives. The formulas in DS-C1 define strongly continuous unitary group representations, and 𝒯nπn=Πn𝒯n.

    DS2 · version 1

    Uses DS1, DS-D2, DS-C1, SC3.

    Proof of Theorem DS2[1, §§2.1–3.4]

    If Dl(Tnf)=0 distributionally, apply Dl=∂t−i∂x−j∂y−k∂z. Since DlDl=Δ and Tn is a fixed injective linear map, Δf=0 distributionally. Weyl's lemma gives a smooth harmonic representative. In particular all its coefficient functions are harmonic.

    For a convergent sequence in the weighted L2 norm, convergence also holds in L2 on each compact subset, because the weight is strictly positive there. The distributional equation passes to the limit, as does the fixed algebraic fibre constraint. Consequently (C6) is closed and complete. Interior harmonic estimates make evaluation at each point continuous. This also justifies differentiating and averaging its vectors inside the ball.

    Quaternion arguments of τn always mean their ι matrices. The cocycle J(g1g2,q)=J(g1,g2q)J(g2,q) proves the group law with the indicated inverse-element convention. The polynomial chain rule shows

    𝒯nπn(γ)=Πn(γ)𝒯n,

    including the inverse and transpose in (C3).

    For covariance of the differential equation, lift f to

    Ff(g)=|d|−2τn(d−1)f(g⋅0),g=(abcd).(C8)

    Here the blocks belong to g, not its inverse. This is a right-K-equivariant function, Ff(gk)=τn(kR)−1Ff(g). The connection and weight theorem establishes

    24(𝒟Ff)(σ(q))=s(n+4)/2Dl(Tnf),Ff(σ(q))=s(n+2)/2f(q).(C9)

    Left translation on the lifted space commutes with 𝒟, so it preserves the kernel and gives (C7). The connection and its curvature are included in that calculation.

    For unitarity, write r=|J|. Then

    s(q′)=s(q)/r2,dv(q′)=r−8dv(q),‖|J|−2τn(J−1)v‖2=r−2n−4‖v‖2.

    These factors cancel exactly against the weight sn−2 in the change of variables. Hence (C7), and therefore (C3), are unitary. Strong continuity follows first on compactly supported continuous functions in the ambient weighted L2 space by change of variables, then by density and unitarity; the closed invariant kernel inherits it. Nonzeroness follows from the constant Vn-valued functions.

    Irreducibility and discrete series#

    Theorem DS3. Irreducibility by compact averaging [1, §§2.1–3.4]

    For each n≥2, the representation on ℬn, equivalently on ℋn, is irreducible. The group translates of the constant Vn have dense span.

    DS3 · version 1

    Uses DS2.

    Proof of Theorem DS3[1, §§2.1–3.4]

    The compact action is

    (πn(a,b)f)(q)=τn(b)f(a−1qb).(C10)

    Average over the first factor, with Haar probability measure:

    (PLf)(q)=∫Sp⁡(1)f(a−1q)da=f(0).(C11)

    For q≠0, this is the spherical mean of the harmonic function f over the sphere of radius |q|; for q=0 it is immediate. Thus PL is the orthogonal projection onto the constant Vn. The second compact factor acts irreducibly on those constants by τn.

    Let M be a nonzero closed G-invariant subspace. Choose f∈M and a point p with f(p)≠0. Transitivity and the invertible fibre multiplier give a translate πn(g)f nonzero at 0. Averaging that vector by PL gives a nonzero constant in M. Its second-compact-factor orbit spans the entire constant Vn, so M contains every constant.

    If M were proper, unitarity would make M⟂ another nonzero closed invariant subspace. The same argument would put all constants in M⟂, a contradiction. Therefore the representation is irreducible. Equivalently, the translates of its constant Vn span a dense subspace.

    Theorem DS4. A closed embedding into the regular representation [1, §§2–3]

    For n≥2, the representation belongs to the discrete series of Sp⁡(1,1). For any nonzero v∈Vn, let cv be its constant function and put (𝒲vf)(g)=⟨πn(g−1)f,cv⟩, with inner product linear in the first argument. This map has closed image in L2(G) and satisfies

    ‖𝒲vf‖L2(G)2=‖v‖2(n+1)Cn‖f‖ℬn2

    for the Haar measure specified in the proof.

    DS4 · version 1

    Uses DS2, DS3.

    Proof of Theorem DS4[1]

    Fix Haar measure by

    ∫GΦ(g)dg=∫B∫KΦ(σ(q)k)dks(q)−4dv(q),∫Kdk=1

    in this disintegration convention. The base measure is invariant. Let v∈Vn be nonzero and cv its constant function. Use a complex inner product linear in the first argument. Harmonic mean values and the probability measure give

    ⟨f,cv⟩ℬn=⟨f(0),v⟩Vn.

    The matrix-coefficient map

    (𝒲vf)(g)=⟨πn(g−1)f,cv⟩=⟨Ff(g),v⟩(C12)

    intertwines πn with left translation on functions on G.

    Schur orthogonality for the finite-dimensional irreducible τn gives

    ∫K|⟨τn(kR)−1u,v⟩|2dk=‖u‖2‖v‖2n+1.

    Since Ff(σ(q))=s(n+2)/2f(q), we obtain the exact norm identity

    ‖𝒲vf‖L2(G)2=‖v‖2(n+1)Cn‖f‖ℬn2.(C13)

    Thus a fixed scalar multiple of 𝒲v is an isometric embedding with closed invariant image in L2(G). Together with irreducibility, this proves that ℬn and ℋn are discrete series.

    For normalized v, the inverse constant in (C13) is n(n2−1)/π2 for the specified Haar measure. Its vanishing at the endpoint is consistent with the divergence proved in H4; it is not used as a substitute for that argument.

    Compact types and explicit vectors#

    Theorem DS5. The complete compact decomposition [1, Thm. 3.9]

    For n≥2,

    ℋn|K≃⨁ˆm≥0Vm⊠Vm+n.

    Every type occurs once. Coordinate degree m has dimension (m+1)(m+n+1), and the constants form the unique minimal type V0⊠Vn. The same polynomial-kernel calculation holds for n=1 and supplies the compact types of the Hardy space.

    DS5 · version 1

    Uses DS2, Clebsch–Gordan and spherical harmonic decomposition.

    Proof of Theorem DS5[1, Thm. 3.9]

    Let 𝒫m,n be the degree-m homogeneous polynomials in the real coordinates of q, valued in Vn, satisfying Dl(Tnf)=0. Harmonicity puts this space inside

    SHm(ℝ4)⊗Vn≃Vm⊠(Vm⊗Vn).

    Here the scalar spherical harmonics on S3≃Sp⁡(1) carry the standard left/right type Vm⊠Vm.

    For the right factor set Rjf=∂qejf, where e0=1,e1=i,e2=j,e3=k. On degree-m functions R0=mI. The infinitesimal action is dτn(ej)+Rj for j=1,2,3, so its Casimir is

    CR=−n(n+2)−m(m+2)+2∑j=13dτn(ej)Rj.

    The equation forces the cross term to be −mn. To check this without an implicit coordinate change, put 𝐓f=(fX,fY)t and write the flat equation as

    ∑i=03ι(ei)𝐓(∂if)=0.

    Multiply on the left by ι(q‾) and contract a column (A,B)t to XA+YB. The result is

    0=∑idτn(q‾ei)∂if=∑j=03dτn(ej)Rjf.(C14)

    The last equality follows from q‾ei=∑j⟨qej,ei⟩ej. The j=0 term is nmf, proving the assertion. Consequently CR acts as

    −(m+n)(m+n+2).

    The Clebsch–Gordan decomposition of Vm⊗Vn has distinct Casimir values and contains Vm+n once. Hence 𝒫m,n lies in Vm⊠Vm+n. It is nonzero: the explicit seed

    fm(q;X,Y)=(t+ix)mXn(C15)

    is regular, because (∂t+i∂x)(t+ix)m=0. Since the candidate compact summand is irreducible and the kernel is K-invariant, its nonzero subspace is the whole summand. Thus

    𝒫m,n=Vm⊠Vm+n,dimℂ⁡𝒫m,n=(m+1)(m+n+1).

    Harmonic functions on the ball have homogeneous harmonic expansions. Their degrees are orthogonal on every sphere, and therefore for the radial measure dμn. The flat first-order equation holds separately on each homogeneous term. Parseval's identity on spheres and integration in the radius show that partial sums converge in the Hilbert norm. This proves the complete direct-sum decomposition in the theorem.

    Proposition DS6. Polynomial seeds and translated constants

    For n≥2 and m≥0, the vectors hm=(t+ix)mXn−1 span each compact type under K, have squared norm m!/(n+1)m, and the translates of constant spinors displayed in the proof have dense span.

    DS6 · version 1

    Uses DS1, DS3, DS5.

    Proof of Proposition DS6

    Under 𝒯n, the seed becomes the particularly simple quaternion-valued polynomial

    hm(q;X,Y)=(t+ix)mXn−1.

    Its squared norm is

    ‖hm‖2=m!(n+1)m,(n+1)m=(n+1)(n+2)⋯(n+m),(C16)

    by the beta integral. Its K-orbit spans the entire type of degree m. Constants (m=0) are the minimal type; the two highest-weight labels increase with m, also making this the unique minimum for the usual compact highest-weight norm.

    An additional supply of nonpolynomial examples comes directly from translated constants. For any p∈B and v∈Vn,

    fp,v(q)=s(p)(n+2)/2|1−p‾q|−2τn((1−p‾q)−1)v=πn(σ(p))cv(q).(C17)

    These are regular spinors of norm ‖v‖, and 𝒯nfp,v are corresponding Fueter-regular vectors. Their span is dense by DS3. For the norm, put u=t2+x2 and v=y2+z2. Euclidean measure becomes π2dudv after angular integration, so

    Cnπ2∫u+v<1um(1−u−v)n−2dudv=m!n!(m+n)!.

    For a translate, σ(p)−1 has c=−p‾/s(p) and d=1/s(p); substituting these blocks in C7 gives the displayed formula, and unitarity gives its norm.

    Corollary DS7. The first member as a pair of regular functions

    The n=2 representation is the space of pairs (h0,h1)=(a−jb,b−jc), with a,b,c complex functions on B, satisfying coefficientwise Fueter regularity and

    ‖h‖2=2π2∫B(|h0|2+|h1|2)dv<∞.

    Its minimal compact type has complex dimension three.

    DS7 · version 1

    Uses DS1, DS2, DS5, DS6.

    Proof of Corollary DS7

    For n=2, write

    f=a(q)X2+2b(q)XY+c(q)Y2,h=𝒯2f=(a−jb)X+(b−jc)Y,

    where a,b,c are complex-valued. Thus the representation consists exactly of pairs

    (h0,h1)=(a−jb, b−jc)

    that are componentwise Fueter-regular and satisfy

    ‖h‖2=2π2∫B(|h0|2+|h1|2)dv<∞.

    The measure is ordinary ball volume, up to normalization. The action is fully explicit:

    (Π2(γ)h)(q)=|J|−2J−1(h0(q′),h1(q′))ι(J−1)t.

    Constant pairs of this form constitute the three-dimensional complex minimal K-type. The pair (t+ix,0) is a nonconstant vector, of squared norm 1/3, in the next compact type of complex dimension 8. All formulas follow by inserting n=2 into the preceding definitions and theorems.

    Discussion

    This is the l=n, quaternionic-dimension-one family in [1], §§2–3. The compact-type calculation follows their Theorem 3.9; DS3–DS4 give direct arguments for irreducibility and square-integrability. The ordinary quaternion-valued endpoint is developed in The Fueter Hardy space, and the section calculation is in Connections and the Fueter equation. The local closed pairings lead to separate quotient questions.
