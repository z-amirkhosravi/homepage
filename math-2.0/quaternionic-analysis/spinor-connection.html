    Expository

    The nonflat bundle connection, its projected contribution, and the exact scalar weight relating the invariant and flat equations.

    Connections and the Fueter equation

    Discussion

    The section over the ball has a nonzero connection. After projecting to the first-order spinor operator, that contribution cancels the derivative of one specific scalar weight. This article supplies the calculation used by the representation constructions.

    The equivariant lift#

    Definition SC-D1. Section, lift, and directional derivatives

    B={q∈ℍ:|q|<1},s=1−|q|2,q=t+ix+jy+kz,K=Sp⁡(1)×Sp⁡(1).

    Write e0=1,e1=i,e2=j,e3=k, and distinguish the group Lie algebra element

    ξv=(0vv‾0)

    from the quaternion v. Use

    σ(q)=s−1/2(1qq‾1),Vn=ℂ[X,Y]n,n≥1.

    The polynomial representation and quaternion identification are

    τn(abcd)p(X,Y)=p(aX+cY,bX+dY),Tnp=pX−jpY.(L1)

    Here a quaternion acts through

    ι(t+ix+jy+kz)=(t+ixy+iz−y+izt−ix).

    Thus the complex column (A,B)t represents A−jB, and left quaternion multiplication has matrix ι(q). The first compact factor acts trivially on Vn. The lift from a smooth ball spinor to a right-equivariant group function is

    F(g)=|d|−2τn(d−1)f(g⋅0),g=(abcd).(L2)

    These blocks belong to g itself. The equivariance is F(gk)=τn(kR)−1F(g) for k=diag⁡(kL,kR)∈K.

    At g=σ(q), the lower-right entry is the positive real quaternion s−1/2. Since τn(r)=rnI for real r>0, (L2) gives

    U(q):=F(σ(q))=s(n+2)/2f(q),f(q)=s−(n+2)/2U(q).(L3)

    For a smooth equivariant function F, write (ξvF)(g)=ddεF(gexp⁡(εξv))|0. Define the projected operator

    24𝒟F=∑v∈{1,i,j,k}vTn(ξvF).

    The factor 24 is an operator normalization and does not change its kernel. The projection v⊗p↦vTnp is K-equivariant: the polynomial chain rule intertwines the tangent action v↦avb−1 and p↦τn(b)p with the corresponding target action. Consequently 𝒟 is well-defined on the homogeneous bundle and commutes with left translations.

    The nonflat connection#

    Proposition SC1. Connection, curvature, and the changing frame cf. [1, §§2.1–3.4]

    For U=F∘σ, the relevant compact connection is

    A=dq‾q−q‾dq2s,dA+A∧A=−s−2dq‾∧dq.

    For Cv=12(v‾q−q‾v) one has

    (ξvF)(σ(q))=s∂vU+dτn(Cv)U.

    SC1 · version 1

    Uses SC-D1.

    Proof of Proposition SC1cf. [1, (2.8), Lemma 3.5, Prop. 3.6]

    Direct multiplication, including the derivative of s−1/2, gives

    σ−1dσ=(dqq‾−qdq‾2sdqsdq‾sdq‾q−q‾dq2s).(L4)

    The diagonal part is the compact connection of the reductive homogeneous bundle. Its second component, the one acting on Vn, is

    A=dq‾q−q‾dq2s.(L5)

    The Maurer–Cartan equation gives its curvature:

    dA+A∧A=−dq‾∧dqs2≠0.(L6)

    For example at q=0, evaluation on (∂t,∂x) is −2i. On Vn, n≥1, its action on Xn is −2inXn, also nonzero. Although A vanishes at the origin in this frame, it does not vanish throughout a neighborhood and its curvature does not vanish even at the origin.

    Consequently a global section, or the fact that the ball is contractible, does not justify replacing covariant derivatives by ordinary derivatives in this frame. A scalar change of frame adds an exact scalar one-form to the connection and does not remove this curvature. The cancellation below occurs only after projection to the particular first-order operator.

    For a smooth right-K-equivariant F, use right differentiation

    (ξvF)(g)=ddεF(gexp⁡(εξv))|0.

    Factor the path through the section:

    σ(q)exp⁡(εξv)=σ(qε)kε.

    Its projection to the ball has derivative

    q0′=v−qq‾v=sv.

    Evaluating (L4) on that tangent vector, and comparing with the off-diagonal Lie algebra element ξv, shows that the second compact component satisfies

    (k0′)2=−Cv,Cv=12(v‾q−q‾v)=sA(v).

    Right equivariance therefore contributes the plus sign in

    (ξvF)(σ(q))=s∂vU(q)+dτn(Cv)U(q).(L7)

    Indeed F(σ(qε)kε)=τn((kε)2−1)U(qε), whose fibre derivative is −dτn((k0′)2)U.

    For explicit comparison in real coordinates:

    C1=ix+jy+kz,Ci=−it+jz−ky,Cj=−iz−jt+kx,Ck=iy−jx−kt.

    The infinitesimal polynomial actions in (L1) are

    dτn(i)=i(X∂X−Y∂Y),dτn(j)=−Y∂X+X∂Y,dτn(k)=i(Y∂X+X∂Y).(L8)

    These formulas make the changing-frame contribution completely explicit.

    Projection and cancellation#

    Lemma SC2. The projected connection term cf. [1, §§2.1–3.4]

    For every homogeneous p∈Vn, n≥1,

    ∑vvTn(dτn(v‾q)p)=2(n+1)qTnp,∑vvTn(dτn(Cv)p)=(n+2)qTnp.

    SC2 · version 1

    Uses SC-D1, SC1.

    Proof of Lemma SC2cf. [1, (2.8), Lemma 3.5, Prop. 3.6]

    The cancellation identity in Liu–Zhang Lemma 3.5 becomes

    ∑v∈{1,i,j,k}vTn(dτn(v‾q)p)=2(n+1)qTnp,p∈Vn.(L9)

    Here dτn has been extended to all quaternion matrices; in particular dτn(r)=nrI for real scalars r.

    A direct proof avoids any assumption about a flat section. For a complex 2×2 matrix M, put

    RMp=(M11X+M21Y)pX+(M12X+M22Y)pY,𝐓p=(pXpY),E=X∂X+Y∂Y.

    Expansion of the four matrices ι(v) gives the differential-operator identity

    ∑vι(v)𝐓(Rι(v‾q)p)=2ι(q)(E+2)𝐓p.(L10)

    For example, with q=ζ+wj, the first component is

    2ζ(XpXX+YpXY+2pX)+2w(XpXY+YpYY+2pY);

    the second component has coefficients −w‾,ζ‾ in place of ζ,w. Since 𝐓p is homogeneous of degree n−1, E𝐓p=(n−1)𝐓p. This proves (L9) for every n, not just tested degrees.

    Now Cv=v‾q−⟨v,q⟩, so (L9) and ∑vv⟨v,q⟩=q give

    ∑vvTn(dτn(Cv)p)=(n+2)qTnp.(L11)

    Thus the connection term is not zero before weighting, even after projection.

    Theorem SC3. The section weight identifies the two kernels cf. [1, §§2.1–3.4]

    For n≥1, put U=s(n+2)/2f. Then

    24(𝒟F)(σ(q))=sDl(TnU)+(n+2)qTnU=s(n+4)/2Dl(Tnf).

    Thus the lift of SC-D1 identifies the equivariant group kernel with the ordinary Fueter equation for Tnf and proves covariance of the ball action.

    SC3 · version 1

    Uses SC1, SC2.

    Proof of Theorem SC3cf. [1, (2.8), Lemma 3.5, Prop. 3.6]

    Combining (L7) and (L11) proves

    24(𝒟F)(σ(q))=sDl(TnU)+(n+2)qTnU.(L13)

    Set β=(n+2)/2 and U=sβf. Since Dls=−2q, the product rule gives

    24(𝒟F)(σ(q))=sβ+1Dl(Tnf)=s(n+4)/2Dl(Tnf).(L14)

    The term −2βqTnf from differentiating the scalar weight cancels (n+2)qTnf exactly.

    For n≥1, the lift (L2) identifies smooth ball functions with the specified smooth right-K-equivariant functions, and

    𝒟F=0 on G⟺Dl(Tn(s−(n+2)/2F∘σ))=0 on B.(L15)

    The nonzero factor s(n+4)/2 in (L14) gives equivalence on the section. K-equivariance of the projected group operator extends vanishing from the section to all of G=σ(B)K. This is a smooth differential assertion; it makes no discrete-series claim at n=1. Conversely, every smooth right-equivariant F is recovered from its value on the section, since G=σ(B)K, so the lift is bijective on the specified smooth spaces. Its left translation becomes the ball action in DS-C1. Because the projected operator is left invariant, its kernel and hence the flat Fueter kernel are preserved by that action.

    Corollary SC4. The discrepancy for any other scalar weight cf. [1, §§2.1–3.4]

    For fα=sαU with any real α,

    24(𝒟F)(σ(q))=s−α[sDl(Tnfα)+(n+2+2α)qTnfα].

    The zeroth-order term disappears exactly at α=−(n+2)/2.

    SC4 · version 1

    Uses SC3.

    Proof of Corollary SC4cf. [1, (2.8), Lemma 3.5, Prop. 3.6]

    Insert U=s−αfα in SC3 and use Dls=−2q. The scalar weight does not flatten the connection: its derivative cancels this particular projected term.

    Proposition SC5. Direct differentiation of the full lift cf. [1, §§2.1–3.4]

    The lift also gives the independent formula

    (ξvF)(σ(q))=s(n+2)/2[s∂vf+dτn(v‾q)f−2(n+1)⟨v,q⟩f].

    SC5 · version 1

    Uses SC-D1, SC2.

    Proof of Proposition SC5cf. [1, (2.8), Lemma 3.5, Prop. 3.6]

    Put β=(n+2)/2. To see each contribution, write

    Hε=1+εq‾v+O(ε2),bεdε−1=q+εsv+O(ε2).

    Then the lift along the right path is

    F(σ(q)eεξv)=sβ|Hε|−2τn(Hε−1)f(q+εsv+O(ε2)).

    Differentiating yields

    (ξvF)(σ(q))=sβ[s∂vf−dτn(q‾v)f−2⟨v,q⟩f]=sβ[s∂vf+dτn(v‾q)f−2(n+1)⟨v,q⟩f].(L17)

    The equality uses q‾v+v‾q=2⟨v,q⟩ and the scalar degree n action. This agrees with the final derivative display in [1], printed p. 2991. Applying the projection and (L9) cancels the last two terms, proving (L14) up to the chosen operator normalization. It also agrees with (L7) after substituting U=sβf.

    Discussion

    The source of the lift and cancellation is [1], equation (2.8), Lemma 3.5 and Proposition 3.6. The proof here differentiates the moving frame explicitly. The resulting covariance is used in the discrete-series construction and in the Hardy endpoint.
