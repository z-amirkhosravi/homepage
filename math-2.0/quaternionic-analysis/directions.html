    Eisenstein sums and coefficient identities, separated from open research directions.

    From lattice sums to number theory

    Discussion

    The elliptic construction starts from an arbitrary full-rank lattice. Its coefficients supply a first connection to lattice Eisenstein sums. The precise statements below lead to representations and special values, followed by questions about arithmetic multiplication and periods.

    Higher derivatives and Eisenstein sums#

    For ν=(n1,n2,n3)∈ℕ03, set

    |ν|=n1+n2+n3,∂ν=∂x1n1∂x2n2∂x3n3,Gν=∂νG.

    Write ϵj for the multi-index with a single 1 in position j, for j∈{1,2,3}.

    Construction N-C1. Higher Eisenstein and elliptic sums

    Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice and let ν∈ℕ03 satisfy |ν|≥2. Form

    Eν(L)=∑λ∈L∗Gν(λ),℘L,ν(q)=∑λ∈LGν(q+λ)−Eν(L)(q∉L).

    Proposition N2. Convergence and periodicity of the higher sums

    For every full-rank lattice L⊂ℍ and every ν∈ℕ03 with |ν|≥2, the Eisenstein series in N-C1 converges absolutely. The defining series for ℘L,ν converges absolutely and locally uniformly on ℍ∖L. For every ω∈L and q∉L,

    ℘L,ν(q+ω)=℘L,ν(q).

    Proof of Proposition N2

    F2 gives decay |q|−3−|ν|, with exponent at least 5. The lattice-count estimate from the proof of E1 therefore proves absolute convergence, uniformly on compact sets away from the lattice for the translated sum. Absolute convergence permits reindexing λ↦λ+ω, which proves periodicity.

    Proposition N1. Coefficients at the singularity

    Let L⊂ℍ be a full-rank lattice and j∈{1,2,3}. Let hj be the smooth extension near 0 of ℘L,j−Gj supplied by E1. For every μ∈ℕ03 with |μ|≥1,

    ∂μhj(0)=Eμ+ϵj(L).

    Proof of Proposition N1

    Differentiate the convergent series for hj from E1 and evaluate at q=0. The subtracted constants disappear, leaving ∑λ∈L∗Gμ+ϵj(λ). This converges absolutely since |μ+ϵj|≥2.

    Corollary N3. Parity cancellation

    For every full-rank lattice L⊂ℍ and every ν∈ℕ03 with even |ν|≥2,

    Eν(L)=0.

    Proof of Corollary N3

    By F2, Gν is odd when |ν| is even. The terms at λ and −λ cancel, and absolute convergence from N2 justifies pairing them.

    Discussion

    These are foundational identities, with no novelty claim. They explain how higher Eisenstein sums enter the regular part of the elliptic construction. Among the absolutely convergent sums, order 2 vanishes by N3, so order 3 is the first order not eliminated by this parity argument. For related lattice sums and Laurent coefficients, see [1], §§4–5.

    Eisenstein representations#

    Discussion

    Varying the lattice produces Eisenstein functions on SL₄. Their irreducible module, inducing parameter, and compact types are established there with proofs. This lattice action differs from the Möbius action on regular functions in the discrete-series and Hardy constructions.

    Harmonic theta series#

    Discussion

    The kernel derivatives have harmonic polynomial numerators. Their convergent lattice sums admit a Mellin formula; even integral lattices give harmonic theta cusp forms and critical L-values. The cubic example identifies a specific coefficient with the value at five of the weight-six newform η(2τ)¹².

    The order-one sum and its proposed connection to quasi-periods still need a regularization theorem. Other arithmetic lattices, Hecke decompositions, and algebraic period normalizations remain open directions.

    Discussion. Open directions — no theorem asserted

    Multiplication and arithmetic lattices#

    Lattices with an action by elements of a quaternion algebra suggest transformation laws for the elliptic functions and quasi-periods. The action and its hypotheses need precise statements. A transformation identity and an independent arithmetic constraint are different conclusions.

    Period relations#

    The additivity of ηL is part of E3. Relating it to a residue or to integrals on ℍ/L requires further statements fixing normalization, orientation, and left/right regularity. The closed pairings and pullback identities supply local results; quotient cohomology remains open.

    The literature guide collects sources and further reading. These questions remain in the research notebook for investigation.

    The full-lattice Eisenstein sum in N-C1 is twice the positive-semilattice sum of [1, §4, eq. (8), p. 1018] at odd derivative order. The regularized first-derivative sum is [1, §4, eq. (12), p. 1019]. N1 uses real Taylor derivatives; the source’s expansion [1, eq. (13), p. 1020] uses Fueter polynomials.
