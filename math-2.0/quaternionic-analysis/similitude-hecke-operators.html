    Positive quaternionic similitudes, three normalizations, and an integral cohomology lattice.

    Similitude Hecke operators and integral eigenvalues

    Discussion

    The Hecke-equivariant period map extends from isometries to positive similitudes. There are several natural extensions of the central action. The extension compatible with the untwisted coefficient representation gives algebraicity; a different extension preserves integral coefficients on the integral Hecke semigroup and gives algebraic integers.

    SH1 specifies the normalizations, SH2 extends the cohomological comparison, and SH3 proves integrality. The central example in SH4 shows why integrality cannot be asserted independently of normalization and the chosen semigroup. The proofs apply to either spinor chirality, for weights n≥2.

    Positive similitudes and their adjoints#

    Let D/ℚ be a definite quaternion algebra, fix D⊗ℝ≃ℍ, and put H=diag⁡(1,−1). For α∈M2(D), let α∗ be its quaternionic conjugate transpose and put α†=H−1α∗H.

    Write

    G~+(ℚ)={α∈GL2⁡(D):α∗Hα=ν(α)H,ν(α)∈ℚ>0}.(1)

    Let G~+(ℝ) denote the group given by the same equation over ℍ with positive real multiplier.

    The subgroup of G~+(ℝ) with ν=1 is G=Sp(1,1). For α=(abcd) set

    αq=(aq+b)(cq+d)−1,J(α,q)=cq+d,gα=ν(α)−1/2α∈G,(2)

    using the positive square root. The scalar factors cancel in the fractional-linear formula, so α and gα have the same action on B.

    Fix a number field L⊂ℂ splitting D, a splitting D⊗L≃M2(L), and its entrywise representation RL:M2(D)→M4(L). Write

    WL=L4,Em,L=Symm⁡WL,ρ~m,L(α)=Symm⁡RL(α).

    Fix a complex isomorphism identifying this representation with the analytic block block representation Rm, extended to G~+(ℝ). For n≥2 put m=n−2. For a smooth spinor f:B→Vn, define

    (ℬαf)(q)=|J(α,q)|−2τn(ι(J(α,q)−1))f(αq).(3)

    For α∈G~+(ℝ) and n≥2, define

    f|nUα=f|ngα,f|nCα=ν(α)2ℬαf,f|nIα=ν(α)nℬαf,(4)

    where |n is the isometry slash action.

    Proposition SH1. Three Compatible Slash Normalizations

    For α∈G~+(ℝ) and n≥2, the three slash normalizations satisfy

    f|nUα=ν(α)(n+2)/2ℬαf,f|nCα=ν(α)−m/2(f|nUα),f|nIα=ν(α)m(f|nCα)=ν(α)m/2(f|nUα).(5)

    Each is a right action: (f|n∙α)|n∙β=f|n∙(αβ) for ∙∈{U,C,I}. Moreover,

    Ωf|nCα=Rm(α)−1α∗Ωf,Ωf|nIα=Rm(α†)α∗Ωf,α†=ν(α)α−1.(6)

    All three actions preserve regularity.

    SH1 · version 1

    Uses positive-similitude group, AC-C1, CP8.

    Proof of Proposition SH1

    The multiplier is multiplicative and gαβ=gαgβ. Also J(gα,q)=ν(α)−1/2J(α,q). The squared norm in (3) contributes ν(α) and the degree-n symmetric power contributes ν(α)n/2. This proves (5). The cocycle identity for J and multiplicativity of ν prove the right-action identities.

    Apply CP8 to gα. Since m=n−2,

    Rm(gα)−1=ν(α)m/2Rm(α)−1,gα∗Ωf=α∗Ωf.

    Multiplying by the scalar ν(α)−m/2 from (5) proves the first identity in (6). The relation α†α=ν(α)I follows from (1); taking its mth symmetric power proves the second identity. Both sides include the moving coefficient inclusion from AC-C1. No coefficient section is assumed to be parallel. Closedness commutes with constant coefficient maps and pullback, so AC3 proves regularity preservation. The U-action is a scalar multiple of the C-action.

    Discussion

    The label U denotes the unitary normalization inherited from the discrete-series model: positive central scalars act trivially. C uses the untwisted algebraic coefficient representation. I uses the algebraic extension ν−mρ~m, which agrees with Rm on the isometry group. Its inverse coefficient transport is Rm(α†). Its integrality is proved below, under explicit lattice hypotheses.

    Normalization Factor multiplying ℬαf Coefficient transport on α∗Ωf
    U ν(n+2)/2 νm/2Rm(α)−1
    C ν2 Rm(α)−1
    I νn Rm(α†)

    Each row extends exactly the same isometry action. The factors differ only when the multiplier is not one.

    Hecke correspondences and algebraicity#

    Let Γ⊂G(ℚ) be a torsion-free congruence lattice. For α∈G~+(ℚ) choose

    ΓαΓ=∐iΓαi.

    Write θ(α)=WαW, where W=(0110). The plus-space function is on the reflected group, as in CP-C1.

    Construction SH-D2. Similitude Double-Coset Operators

    For n≥2 and α∈G~+(ℚ), use the coset representatives chosen above. For ∙∈{U,C,I} define

    Tα∙,−f=∑if|n∙αi,Tα∙,+f+=∑if+|n∙θ(αi).(7)

    SH-D2 · version 1

    Well-definedness: SH2.

    Put Γα=Γ∩α−1Γα, Yα=Γα\B, and let p1,p2:Yα→X=Γ\B send [q] to [q] and [αq]. On cohomology with coefficients Em,L put

    TαC=(p1)∗ρ~m,L(α)−1p2∗,TαI=(p1)∗ρ~m,L(α†)p2∗=ν(α)mTαC,(8)

    where the constant matrices denote maps between the pulled-back coefficient systems and (p1)∗ is trace as a sum over sheets. The last equality in (8) follows from α†=ν(α)α−1, the degree m of the symmetric power, and linearity of trace. The coefficient transports and independence of coset representatives are justified in SH2.

    Theorem SH2. Similitude equivariance and algebraic eigenvalues

    For the data of SH-D2, (7) defines endomorphisms of 𝒮n±(Γ). The C- and I-operators in (8) are L-linear endomorphisms of

    HL=Hpar2(Γ,Em,L).

    For either chirality, the injective period map intertwines the corresponding operators. Every eigenvalue of a C-, I-, or U-operator on a nonzero cusp spinor is algebraic over ℚ.

    For any commuting family indexed by A⊂G~+(ℚ) and any nonzero simultaneous eigenform, the fields generated over L by its C- and I-eigenvalues are equal and finite over L. If these eigenvalues are denoted by λαC and λαI and the U-eigenvalue by λαU, then

    λαI=ν(α)mλαC=ν(α)m/2λαU.(9)

    For even n, the U-eigenvalues generate that same field over L.

    SH2 · version 1

    Uses SH1, SH-D2, CP7–CP9.

    Proof of Theorem SH2

    Conjugation by a rational similitude is a rational automorphism of the isometry group. Clearing denominators shows that it commensurates Γ. Thus p1,p2 are finite coverings. The coefficient maps in (8) intertwine the two Γα actions, since α†=ν(α)α−1. They are defined over L. Properness of both coverings and trace compatibility with compact support prove preservation of interior, hence parabolic, cohomology as in CP8.

    Every αi has multiplier ν(α), and every slash summand is a constant scalar multiple of f|ngαi. The latter is regular by SH1. Its lift is g↦Ff(gαig), so right central finiteness and moderate growth persist. With Δi=Γ∩αi−1Γαi, change of variables gives

    ∫Δi\G‖Ff(gαig)‖n2dg=[Γ:αiΔiαi−1]∫Γ\G‖Ff(g)‖n2dg.

    It is finite by CP1. Taking a common finite cover for the finitely many summands and using the triangle inequality proves finite norm for their sum. The right-action and coset identities prove Γ-automorphy and independence of representatives. CP1 then proves cuspidality.

    The trace calculation of CP8 applies unchanged, and (6) identifies it term by term with the spinor sum. Reflection gives the same calculation for the plus space: ν(θ(α))=ν(α) and θ intertwines the coefficient transports through Rm(W). The period map is injective by AC7 and reflection. The characteristic-polynomial and finite-dimensional Hecke-algebra arguments of CP9 therefore prove algebraicity and the finite-field assertion for C and I. Their fields are equal because their eigenvalues differ by nonzero rational factors ν(α)m. Equation (5) proves (9); its multiplier is algebraic, giving individual algebraicity for U as well. If n is even, then m/2 is an integer and the factors relating U and I are nonzero rational numbers, so their generated fields over L coincide.

    Discussion

    For even n, the U-eigenvalues also generate the same finite field, because m/2 is an integer. For odd n, the square roots in (9) can vary with α; the argument above gives individual algebraicity but does not supply a common finite field for the U-normalization. Neither semisimplicity nor multiplicity one is required for SH2.

    An integral coefficient lattice#

    Theorem SH3. Algebraic integers for integral similitudes

    Fix a maximal order 𝒪⊂D, and put

    G(𝒪)={γ∈M2(𝒪):γ∗Hγ=H},Δ𝒪+=M2(𝒪)∩G~+(ℚ).(10)

    Let Γ⊆G(𝒪) be a torsion-free congruence subgroup. Let 𝒪L be the ring of integers of L. There exists a finitely generated 𝒪L-submodule M⊂HL with M⊗𝒪LL=HL such that

    TαIM⊆M(α∈Δ𝒪+).(11)

    For every α∈Δ𝒪+, every eigenvalue of TαI,± on a nonzero regular cusp spinor is an algebraic integer. Equivalently, for α∈Δ𝒪+ the numbers ν(α)mλαC and ν(α)m/2λαU are algebraic integers. For a simultaneous eigenform of a commuting family in Δ𝒪+, all these eigenvalues lie in the ring of integers of one number field.

    SH3 · version 1

    Uses SH2, CP2, finite cellular cohomology with local coefficients.

    Proof of Theorem SH3

    Construct the coefficient lattice. Choose a full 𝒪L-lattice Λ0⊂WL and a ℤ-basis a1,…,a16 of M2(𝒪). Put

    Λ=∑j=116RL(aj)Λ0.

    It is finitely generated, torsion-free, and spans WL. It contains Λ0 because the order contains the identity. Products of basis elements are integral linear combinations of the basis, so the order preserves Λ. Let Λm⊂Em,L be the image of Sym𝒪Lm⁡Λ. It is a full lattice and is preserved by the symmetric power of every element of the order. No division by m! is used.

    To see that reduced traces and norms are integral, let x∈𝒪 act by left multiplication on the finite free abelian group 𝒪. Its integer characteristic polynomial annihilates x, so x is integral over ℤ. If x is noncentral, its degree-two minimal polynomial is its reduced characteristic polynomial; its coefficients are rational algebraic integers, hence integers. If x is central, it is a rational algebraic integer, and the same conclusion follows directly. Consequently quaternionic conjugation preserves the order: for x∈𝒪, x‾=trd⁡(x)−x and the reduced trace is integral. Hence α†=Hα∗H belongs to M2(𝒪) whenever α does. For γ∈G(𝒪), both γ and γ−1=γ† preserve Λ, so Λm defines an integral local system on X.

    The Hecke coefficient map is integral. If α∈Δ𝒪+, its multiplier is a positive integer: the first diagonal entry of α∗Hα is the difference of two integral reduced norms and equals ν(α). Every representative in ΓαΓ lies in M2(𝒪). Thus

    ν(α)mρ~m,L(α)−1=ρ~m,L(α†)

    preserves Λm. This gives a morphism of integral local systems from p2∗Λm to p1∗Λm. It need not be an isomorphism over 𝒪L. Pullback and trace are also integral; trace sums over sheets and does not divide by their number.

    Pass to integral parabolic cohomology. Define

    Hpar2(Γ,Λm)=ker⁡(H2(Γ,Λm)⟶⨁[ξ]H2(Γξ,Λm))

    and let M be its image in HL. The compact-core relative cohomology sequence identifies this kernel with the image of compactly supported cohomology, over 𝒪L as well as over L. Proper pullback, the integral coefficient morphism, and trace preserve that image. Therefore they preserve M.

    The finite cellular complexes for the compact core and its boundary show that M is finitely generated. Localization to L is exact, so it commutes with cohomology and with the kernel of restriction. It follows that M⊗L=HL. As a subgroup of an L-vector space, M is torsion-free; its underlying abelian group is therefore a finite-rank free ℤ-module. Each operator in (11) is represented on it by an integer matrix and satisfies a monic polynomial in ℤ[t]. The same polynomial annihilates its action on HL⊗Lℂ. By the injective Hecke comparison, every cusp-spinor eigenvalue is a root and is an algebraic integer. Finally SH2 supplies one finite number field for a simultaneous system; its integral elements form that field's ring of integers.

    Discussion

    The lattice argument includes primes dividing the quaternion discriminant or the level for the semigroup and group specified in (10). No exclusion of those primes is needed. For another arithmetic lattice or an adelic level defined by a different quaternionic lattice, the applicable criterion is that each adjoint coefficient transport preserve the chosen lattice; the matrix-order hypothesis in SH3 is one explicit sufficient condition.

    Why the normalization and semigroup matter#

    Proposition SH4. Central scalars and the minimal coefficient twist

    For t∈ℚ>0, the central double coset Γ(tI)Γ has one left coset, and on either cusp-spinor space

    TtIU=1,TtIC=t−m,TtII=tm.(12)

    If m>0 and the cusp-spinor space is nonzero, TpIC has the nonintegral eigenvalue p−m for every rational prime p, while Tp−1II has the same nonintegral eigenvalue.

    For the standard symplectic form J4=(0I2−I20) over ℚp, put Ap=diag⁡(p,p,1,1), so its multiplier is p. For an integer e, the map

    peSymm⁡(Ap−1)

    preserves Symm⁡ℤp4 if and only if e≥m.

    SH4 · version 1

    Uses SH1, SH-D2.

    Proof of Proposition SH4

    For α=tI, the action on B is the identity, J(α,q)=t, and ν(α)=t2. Equation (3) gives ℬtI=t−(n+2). Substitute in (4) to obtain (12). Rational algebraic integers are integers, so p−m is not integral when m>0.

    On a monomial with r factors from the first two basis vectors and m−r from the last two, Symm⁡(Ap−1) acts by p−r, for 0≤r≤m. Thus integrality is equivalent to e−r≥0 for every such r, or e≥m.

    Discussion

    The last assertion shows that νm is the smallest uniform integral power correcting the inverse coefficient transport on this standard local lattice. Combined with the degree-two factor ν2 in SH1, it explains the exponent n=m+2 in the integral spinor slash action. It is a statement about coefficient lattices; it does not exclude extra divisibility after taking a particular Hecke trace or passing to a particular cohomology group.

    The central example also separates two different assertions. Integral positive similitudes give algebraic integers in the I-normalization. The full rational similitude group contains inverses of integral central scalars, so it does not have the same blanket integrality property. No division by the double-coset degree is part of SH3.

    Published conventions and further comparisons#

    Discussion

    [1, §2, §§5.1–5.3] define adelic similitude forms with trivial central character, local Hecke operators, and additional rescalings for their spinor Euler factors. Such an Euler-factor normalization must be distinguished from both a unitary action and an integral coefficient action. The ball-model dictionary is AS3.

    The formulas above specify rational double-coset correspondences on a fixed arithmetic quotient. Matching them generator by generator with a full adelic Hecke algebra requires tracking the finite adelic components, the chosen quaternionic lattices at ramified primes, the direction of the correspondence, and the central character. A different normalization cannot inherit the integrality label without this comparison. Explicit eigenforms and the arithmetic of their periods remain useful next examples.

    Galois representations#

    The Langlands and Galois article explains the four-dimensional spin representation, its five-dimensional standard quotient construction, and the published existence theorem for general-type adelic constituents of weight n≥3. Its Satake normalization is explicit; identifying it with individual double-coset generators requires the adelic dictionary discussed above.
